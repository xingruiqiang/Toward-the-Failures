塞麦尔维斯：劝医生洗手，被关进疯人院的“母亲救星”

Semmelweis: The "Savior of Mothers" Who Told Doctors to Wash Their Hands—and Was Locked in an Asylum

——他死于自己一生对抗的那种感染

He died of the very infection he spent his life fighting.

2020年之后，全世界都学会了一件事：洗手。

After 2020, the whole world learned one thing: washing your hands.

在疫情最凶的那几个月，“勤洗手”成了各国卫生部门反复播放的头号建议。你可能想不到，这条今天连小学生都懂的常识，在150多年前，是一个医生用命换来的——他提出“医生先洗手再接生”，换来的是同行的排挤、失业、羞辱，最后被关进精神病院，死于感染。

In the worst months of the pandemic, "wash your hands often" became the number-one piece of advice broadcast again and again by health authorities around the world. You might not guess that this piece of common sense, which even a schoolchild understands today, was bought more than 150 years ago with a doctor's life—he proposed that "doctors should wash their hands before delivering a baby," and in return he got his colleagues' ostracism, unemployment, humiliation, and finally commitment to an insane asylum, where he died of infection.

更讽刺的是：他死于败血症，正是他一辈子想消灭的那种病。

More ironic still: he died of sepsis, the very disease he had spent his life trying to wipe out.

一、两个产房，两种命运

I. Two Maternity Wards, Two Fates

1818年，伊格纳兹·塞麦尔维斯出生在匈牙利布达（今布达佩斯）。1844年他拿到医学博士，1846年进入维也纳总医院，当上了第一产科诊所的助理医生。

In 1818, Ignaz Semmelweis was born in Buda, Hungary (now Budapest). In 1844 he received his doctorate in medicine, and in 1846 he entered the Vienna General Hospital as an assistant physician at the First Maternity Clinic.

维也纳总医院有两个产科诊所，就在同一栋楼里，设备差不多，时间安排也差不多。可有一个数字让塞麦尔维斯寝食难安：

The Vienna General Hospital had two maternity clinics in the same building, with similar equipment and similar schedules. But one number gave Semmelweis no peace:

- 第一诊所（由医生和医学生接生）：产妇死于“产褥热”的比例，高的时候在10%以上；

- The First Clinic (where doctors and medical students delivered the babies): the share of mothers who died of "childbed fever" reached more than 10 percent at its worst;

- 第二诊所（由助产士接生）：死亡率只有4%左右，有时更低。

- The Second Clinic (where midwives delivered the babies): the death rate was only about 4 percent, sometimes lower.

产妇们很快也发现了这件事。她们宁可在大街上生孩子，也不愿被送进第一诊所。有人跪下来求护士，别把自己分到第一诊所去。

The mothers soon found out about it too. They would rather give birth in the street than be sent to the First Clinic. Some knelt and begged the nurses not to assign them to the First Clinic.

一个新生命降临本该是喜事，在那栋楼里却像是一场抽签——抽到第一诊所，就是抽到了半张死亡通知。

The arrival of a new life ought to be a joy, but in that building it was like drawing lots—to draw the First Clinic was to draw half of a death notice.

二、一具尸体给出的答案

II. The Answer a Corpse Gave

塞麦尔维斯做过很多推测：通风问题？床位太挤？接生姿势不对？他一项项排除，都不成立。他甚至观察到，第一诊所里躺的时间越短的产妇，反而越容易死——这跟“环境差”的直觉完全相反。

Semmelweis made many guesses: poor ventilation? Too many beds crowded together? The wrong delivery position? He ruled them out one by one; none held up. He even observed that in the First Clinic, the shorter a mother stayed, the more likely she was to die—the exact opposite of the intuition that a poor environment was to blame.

1847年，一个意外点醒了他。他的同事、法医病理学家科莱奇卡（Jakob Kolletschka）在解剖尸体时，手指被学生的手术刀划伤了。不久，科莱奇卡出现了和产褥热产妇几乎一模一样的症状，很快去世了。

In 1847, an accident woke him up. His colleague, the forensic pathologist Jakob Kolletschka, cut his finger on a student's scalpel while performing an autopsy. Soon afterward Kolletschka developed symptoms almost identical to those of the mothers with childbed fever, and died quickly.

塞麦尔维斯脑子里“啪”地一下亮了：科莱奇卡死于解剖尸体时沾上的“尸体颗粒”；那些产妇，是不是也一样——是被医生们带进产房的？

A light snapped on in Semmelweis's mind: Kolletschka had died from the "cadaverous particles" he had picked up while dissecting a body; could those mothers be the same—infected by something the doctors carried into the delivery room?

因为在那个年代，医生和医学生上午要在解剖室解剖尸体，下午直接进产房接生，中间只是随便擦擦手。手上那股味道洗都洗不掉，他们自己都嫌弃，却说那是“尸体味”，丝毫不觉得它和产妇的生死有关。

For in those days doctors and medical students spent the morning dissecting corpses in the autopsy room and went straight into the delivery room in the afternoon, washing their hands only casually in between. The smell on their hands would not wash off; even they found it offensive, yet they called it merely "the smell of cadavers," never suspecting that it had anything to do with whether a mother lived or died.

塞麦尔维斯随即推出一个在当时看来近乎荒诞的要求：进产房前，必须用漂白粉溶液（含氯消毒液）洗手、刷手，还要清洗器械。

Semmelweis then issued a demand that seemed almost absurd at the time: before entering the delivery room, one must wash and scrub one's hands in a solution of chloride of lime (a chlorine disinfectant), and also clean the instruments.

结果堪称奇迹——第一诊所的产褥热死亡率，从10%以上一路掉到了1%左右。

The result was nothing short of miraculous—the childbed-fever death rate at the First Clinic fell from more than 10 percent all the way to around 1 percent.

三、“妇人屠杀的参与者”

III. "A Participant in the Slaughter of Women"

按理说，这是一个能拿诺贝尔奖的发现。但它撞上了一堵墙：自尊。

By any measure, this was a discovery worthy of a Nobel Prize. But it ran into a wall: pride.

在19世纪中期的医学界，医生是社会精英。说“产妇是被医生的脏手害死的”，等于当着全欧洲最有名望的产科权威的面，宣布他们是凶手。何况塞麦尔维斯拿不出“证据”——那时候细菌理论还没诞生，巴斯德证明微生物致病还要再等十几年。他只能凭现象推断有一种看不见的“尸体颗粒”，却没法让人在显微镜下看见它。

In the medical world of the mid-nineteenth century, doctors were social elites. To say that mothers were killed by doctors' dirty hands was, in front of the most celebrated obstetric authorities in Europe, to declare them murderers. And Semmelweis could produce no "proof"—the germ theory of disease had not yet been born, and Pasteur's demonstration that microorganisms cause disease was still more than a decade away. He could only infer from the evidence that there was some invisible "cadaverous particle," but he could not make anyone see it under a microscope.

他的顶头上司克莱因教授处处与他作对。合约到期后，医院拒绝续约。他连无薪的教师职位都申请不上，甚至被禁止为学生签发听课证明。一个发现了真相的人，在维也纳待不下去了。

His immediate superior, Professor Klein, opposed him at every turn. When his contract expired, the hospital refused to renew it. He could not even obtain an unpaid teaching post, and was even forbidden to issue certificates of attendance to students. A man who had discovered the truth could not remain in Vienna.

1849年，他回到故乡布达，接手一家医院的产科。他继续强制洗手，死亡率降到0.85%。

In 1849 he returned to his native Buda and took over the maternity department of a hospital. He continued to enforce handwashing, and the death rate fell to 0.85 percent.

1861年，他终于克服写作的恐惧，用德文出版了《产褥热的病因、概念与预防》——一本后来被称为“科学史上最具革命性的作品之一”的书。但当时的医学期刊几乎集体封杀了它，批评者把它贬为“来自布达佩斯一个大傻瓜的疯话”。

In 1861 he finally overcame his fear of writing and published, in German, The Etiology, Concept, and Prophylaxis of Childbed Fever—a book later called "one of the most revolutionary works in the history of science." But the medical journals of the day almost collectively blackballed it, and critics dismissed it as "the ravings of a big fool from Budapest."

塞麦尔维斯被激怒了。他开始一封封地写公开信反击，直接称那些反对他的产科医生是“妇人屠杀的参与者”。对方越刻薄，他回击越猛；他越不妥协，对方嘲讽得越凶。慢慢地，他开始酗酒，健康状况恶化，性情越来越偏执。

Semmelweis was enraged. He began writing open letter after open letter in reply, calling the obstetricians who opposed him outright "participants in the slaughter of women." The more cutting his opponents were, the harder he struck back; the more uncompromising he became, the more fiercely they mocked him. Gradually he began to drink heavily, his health declined, and his temper grew ever more paranoid.

四、他死在自己最懂的病上

IV. He Died of the Disease He Understood Best

1865年，塞麦尔维斯的精神状态已经很差。妻子和好友把他送进了维也纳的一家精神病院。

By 1865 Semmelweis's mental state had greatly deteriorated. His wife and a close friend had him committed to an insane asylum in Vienna.

他很快意识到自己被骗了，试图逃跑，与守卫发生激烈搏斗。守卫将他毒打一顿，用绳子捆起来关进小黑屋。约两周后——1865年8月13日——他死了，年仅47岁。

He soon realized he had been deceived, tried to escape, and struggled violently with the guards. The guards beat him severely, tied him up with rope, and locked him in a dark cell. About two weeks later—on August 13, 1865—he died, only forty-seven years old.

精神病院出具的尸检结论是“死于脓血症”。而常识告诉后来的人：他是被殴打后，右手伤口感染，死于败血症——正是产褥热同一类的感染。

The asylum's autopsy report gave the cause of death as "pyemia." But what later generations could see plainly was this: after being beaten, an infected wound on his right hand killed him—sepsis, the same class of infection as childbed fever.

一个为了阻止“伤口感染致命”而奔走了一辈子的人，最后死于伤口感染。

A man who had spent his whole life fighting to stop "infected wounds from killing people" died, in the end, of an infected wound.

他的葬礼很冷清，只有几个人到场。当地的医学期刊只发了一条简讯。他没有等到一个时代的道歉，因为那个时代根本不知道自己错在哪儿。

His funeral was bleak and sparsely attended; only a few people came. The local medical journal ran a single brief notice. He did not live to receive an apology from his age, because that age had no idea where it had gone wrong.

五、他死后，世界才听懂

V. Only After He Died Did the World Understand

转折发生在塞麦尔维斯死后不久：

The turning point came not long after Semmelweis's death:

- 1861年，巴斯德证实了微生物的存在；

- In 1861, Pasteur demonstrated the existence of microorganisms;

- 1867年，外科消毒法的创始人李斯特发表论文，用实验证明“不消毒是手术后感染的主因”；

- In 1867, Joseph Lister, the founder of surgical antisepsis, published a paper using experiments to show that "the lack of antisepsis is the main cause of postoperative infection";

- 之后的十年里，欧洲的术后死亡率从45%降到15%，挽救了亿万人的生命；

- Over the following decade, Europe's postoperative death rate fell from 45 percent to 15 percent, saving countless lives;

- 2005年，世界卫生组织把每年10月15日定为“世界洗手日”。

- In 2005, the World Health Organization designated October 15 each year as "Global Handwashing Day."

“尸体颗粒”终于在显微镜下被看清，它有个新名字：细菌。塞麦尔维斯当年凭经验和直觉看到的东西，被科学追认为真理。今天，匈牙利最著名的医科大学以他的名字命名，布达佩斯市中心的广场上立着他的雕像，他被称为“母亲们的救星”。

The "cadaverous particles" were at last seen clearly under a microscope, and they had a new name: bacteria. What Semmelweis had seen through experience and intuition was posthumously confirmed by science as truth. Today, Hungary's most famous medical university bears his name, his statue stands in a square in central Budapest, and he is called "the savior of mothers."

在他的日记里，有这样一句近乎预言的话：

In his diary there is a line that reads almost like a prophecy:

回首往事，我只能期待有一天终将消灭这种产褥感染，并用这样的快乐来驱散我身上的哀伤。

Looking back on the past, I can only hope that one day this childbed infection will at last be abolished, and that such joy will dispel the sorrow within me.

六、致2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

塞麦尔维斯的故事，是“对的人，生在了错的认知里”。

Semmelweis's story is that of the right man born into the wrong understanding.

他不是没能力，也不是不够努力。他做对了一切——观察、对比、验证、坚持——但因为他无法在当时的科学框架下“证明”自己，他的正确就变成了他的罪。这个世界常常惩罚那些比常识早到一步的人：因为你说的话，别人听不懂。

It was not that he lacked ability, nor that he did not try hard enough. He did everything right—observation, comparison, verification, persistence—but because he could not "prove" himself within the scientific framework of the day, his being right became his crime. The world often punishes those who arrive one step ahead of common sense: what you say, others cannot understand.

他留给今天两个提醒。

He left two reminders for us today.

第一，当你的结论冒犯了权威，先别急着怀疑自己。塞麦尔维斯的死亡率数据是硬的：10%对4%，这不是观点之争，是生死之别。很多时候，被否定的不是你的道理，而是别人不愿承认的错。

First, when your conclusion offends authority, don't rush to doubt yourself. Semmelweis's mortality data were hard: 10 percent versus 4 percent. This was not a dispute of opinions but the difference between life and death. Often what is being rejected is not your reasoning but an error others refuse to admit.

第二，别让愤怒吃掉你自己。塞麦尔维斯晚年的悲剧，一半是同行逼的，一半是他自己越打越孤、越孤越愤、最后连表达方式都变了形。真理你可以坚持，但方式得选对——否则你会成为那个“对得只剩下愤怒”的人。

Second, don't let anger eat you alive. The tragedy of Semmelweis's later years was half forced on him by his colleagues, and half his own doing—the more he fought, the more isolated he became; the more isolated, the angrier; until at last even his way of expressing things was deformed. You can hold fast to the truth, but you must choose the right way—otherwise you become the person who is "right and nothing but angry."

150年后，无数人靠“洗手”活了下来。他们不知道他叫什么，但他们每一次把手洗干净，都是在替他完成那句没说完的话。

A hundred and fifty years later, countless people have survived by "washing their hands." They do not know his name, but every time they wash their hands clean, they are finishing that unfinished sentence for him.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

8. “塞麦尔维斯被关进疯人院是同行报复的直接结果”——更准确的说法是：长期被排挤、事业受挫，加之他本人晚年情绪与行为显著异常（今人多推测与痴呆或晚期梅毒等疾病有关），最终由妻子与好友送医。文中将其处理为“长期排挤+本人精神恶化”的共同结果，未简化为单一阴谋。

8. "Semmelweis was locked in an asylum as the direct result of his colleagues' revenge"—a more accurate account is this: long-term ostracism and career setbacks, together with his own markedly abnormal mood and behavior in later years (now often thought to be related to conditions such as dementia or late-stage syphilis), led his wife and a close friend to have him committed. The article treats it as the combined result of "long-term exclusion plus his own mental deterioration," without reducing it to a single conspiracy.

9. “他死于精神病院的殴打，是明确的谋杀”——不完全可考。权威传记（如Britannica）的表述为：他在精神病院被守卫殴打、关押，死因是右手伤口感染所致，而该伤“可能”（possibly）来自被殴。因此“因殴伤感染而死”是主流说法，但“殴打是否为其唯一死因”存在推测成分，文中已注明。

9. "He died from a beating in the asylum; it was clear murder"—not fully verifiable. Authoritative biographies (such as Britannica) state that he was beaten and confined by the guards in the asylum, and that the cause of death was an infection of a wound on his right hand, an injury that "possibly" resulted from the beating. Thus "died of an infection from a beating" is the mainstream account, but whether the beating was his sole cause of death involves an element of conjecture, as noted in the article.

10. “他是凭直觉提出的'尸体颗粒'就是细菌”——他本人并未观察到细菌，只是从现象推断存在一种可经手传播的致病物质；细菌理论由巴斯德等人后来建立，二者不能等同。

10. "The 'cadaverous particles' he proposed on intuition were bacteria"—he himself never observed bacteria; he merely inferred from phenomena the existence of a disease-causing substance that could be transmitted by hand. The germ theory was later established by Pasteur and others, and the two cannot be equated.
