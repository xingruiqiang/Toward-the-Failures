Chapter 093 · Part 5

林昭：北大才女，用一生守住一句真话

Lin Zhao: A Peking University Prodigy Who Kept One True Sentence All Her Life

——“刀在口上之日。”

— “The day the knife lies over the mouth.”

← 返回主页 Home第五辑　走向未知的人93 / 100约 2989 字 · 2989 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，北大未名湖边，每年都有人悄悄放下几朵花。那是一个叫林昭的女生待过的地方。

In 2026, by the shore of Weiming Lake at Peking University, people quietly lay down a few flowers every year. It is a place where a young woman named Lin Zhao once lived and studied.

她曾是北大中文系新闻专业的学生，是江苏省考分第一的才女，是同学们口中的“林姑娘”。她年轻时热烈、真诚，为了理想愿意付出一切。

She had been a student in the journalism program of Peking University's Department of Chinese, a gifted young woman who placed first in Jiangsu Province in the entrance examinations, and “Miss Lin” to her classmates. In her youth she was ardent and sincere, ready to give up everything for her ideals.

1957年，她因为当众为一个同学说话，被划为“右派”。此后，她的人生急转直下。1968年，她在上海被处决，三十六岁。

In 1957, because she spoke up for a classmate in public, she was labeled a “rightist.” After that, her life plunged sharply downward. In 1968 she was executed in Shanghai. She was thirty-six.

十二年后，上海市高级人民法院撤销原判，宣告她无罪。

Twelve years later, the Shanghai Higher People's Court set aside the original verdict and declared her not guilty.

一、“林姑娘”

I. “Miss Lin”

1932年12月16日，林昭出生在苏州，原名彭令昭。她的母亲许宪民早年参加过革命，父亲彭国彦曾是苏州吴县的县长。

Lin Zhao was born in Suzhou on December 16, 1932. Her original name was Peng Lingzhao. Her mother, Xu Xianmin, had taken part in the revolution in her early years, and her father, Peng Guoyan, had once been the magistrate of Wu County in Suzhou.

她从小就受到进步思想的影响。十几岁时，她就秘密参与了反对国民党统治的地下活动。1949年，她考进苏南新闻专科学校，决心投身到新中国的建设中去。

From childhood she was influenced by progressive ideas. In her early teens she secretly took part in underground activities against Nationalist rule. In 1949 she was admitted to the Southern Jiangsu School of Journalism, determined to devote herself to building the new China.

1954年，她以江苏省文科第一名的成绩，考入北京大学中文系新闻专业。

In 1954, she entered the journalism program of Peking University's Department of Chinese, having placed first in Jiangsu Province in the liberal arts examinations.

在北大，她是活跃的、明亮的。她参加北大诗社，做《北大诗刊》的编辑，后来还成了学生文艺刊物《红楼》的编委。同学们叫她“林姑娘”。

At Peking University she was lively and radiant. She joined the university's poetry society, worked as an editor of the Peking University Poetry Journal, and later became a member of the editorial board of the student literary magazine Honglou (Red Building). Her classmates called her “Miss Lin.”

她读大量的书，抱着一摞线装书从图书馆走出来的样子，是很多人对她的记忆。

She read enormously, and many people remember her walking out of the library with an armful of traditional thread-bound books.

二、1957年的那个夜晚

II. That Night in 1957

1957年的反右运动中，北大的一个学生张元勋贴出了大字报，随即成为被批判的焦点。

During the Anti-Rightist Campaign of 1957, a Peking University student named Zhang Yuanxun put up a big-character poster and immediately became the focus of criticism.

在那个夏天的某个晚上，一场批判会在北大东门外的马路上进行，气氛激烈。这时，林昭跳上一张桌子，为张元勋说了话。

One evening that summer, a criticism session was held on the road outside the university's east gate, and feelings ran high. At that moment Lin Zhao jumped up onto a table and spoke in Zhang Yuanxun's defense.

她问：今天这个会，是演讲会还是斗争会？如果是斗争会，那就不需要斗争了——张元勋有什么地方值得你们一斗？我们也说过要欢迎别人提意见，现在人家提了，为什么又要发火？

She asked: Is this meeting today a lecture or a struggle session? If it is a struggle session, then there is nothing to struggle over—what about Zhang Yuanxun is worth your struggle? We said we welcomed criticism from others. Now someone has offered it, so why get angry?

人群中有人怒吼：你是谁？你叫什么名字？

Someone in the crowd roared: Who are you? What is your name?

她回答：你凭什么问我？我叫林昭。“林，双木之林；昭，刀在口上之日。” 她说，今天刀在口上也好，刀在头上也好，既然来了，就不考虑了。

She answered: By what right do you ask me? My name is Lin Zhao. “Lin, the lin of two trees; Zhao, the zhao with a knife above a mouth.” She said that today, whether the knife lay at her mouth or over her head, she had come, and she would not think twice.

那天深夜，她在未名湖畔对张元勋说了一段话，大意是：这或者是一个悲壮的祭坛，或者会流血，但愿不流血。

Late that night, by the shore of Weiming Lake, she said something to Zhang Yuanxun to the effect that this might be a tragic and solemn altar, or blood might be shed—though she hoped it would not be.

这几句话，成了她一生的缩影。

Those few sentences became the epitome of her whole life.

三、“说出常识就是反革命”

III. “To Speak Common Sense Was to Be a Counter-revolutionary”

林昭被划为“右派”。这个身份的代价是：她再也没能毕业。

Lin Zhao was labeled a “rightist.” The price of that label was that she never managed to graduate.

在当时的北大，被划为右派的人，几乎所有人都做了检讨。但林昭没有。她不但不检讨，还在会上公开反驳。有人问她是什么观点，她回答：我的观点很简单，就是人人要平等、要自由、要和睦，不要这样咬人。

At Peking University in those days, almost everyone labeled a rightist made a self-criticism. Lin Zhao did not. Not only did she refuse to make one, she openly argued back at the meetings. When someone asked what her views were, she answered: My view is very simple—that everyone should be equal, should be free, should live in harmony, and should not tear at one another like this.

她的一位同班同学后来回忆说：平心而论，林昭那时说的，其实都是常识。 但那位同学说了一句更痛的话——“但我们那时处于历史的低谷，把常识说出来，就是反革命。”

A classmate of hers later recalled: In all fairness, what Lin Zhao said back then was simply common sense. But the same classmate added something more painful— “Yet we were in a trough of history, and to speak common sense aloud was to be a counter-revolutionary.”

被划为右派后，林昭曾一度陷入很深的痛苦。她也哭过、失眠过、想过自杀。但她慢慢从那种痛苦里站了起来，开始认真思考：这不是我一个人的命运问题。

After being labeled a rightist, Lin Zhao fell for a time into deep anguish. She wept, she could not sleep, she thought of ending her life. But slowly she rose out of that pain and began to think seriously: this was not a matter of my fate alone.

她在北大、人民大学经历过劳动改造，也在甘肃等地与朋友保持着联系。她把自己省下的粮票寄给困难的友人，有人收到过七张五斤的全国通用粮票，靠着这些粮票撑过了最难的两个多月。

She went through labor reform at Peking University and at Renmin University, and kept in touch with friends in Gansu and elsewhere. She mailed the grain coupons she had saved to friends in difficulty; one of them received seven five-jin national grain coupons and got through the hardest two months or so on them.

一个自身难保的人，还在把口粮分给别人。

A person who could not save herself was still sharing her rations with others.

四、被捕与狱中

IV. Arrest and Prison

1960年10月，林昭因涉嫌参与地下刊物《星火》，在苏州被捕。

In October 1960, Lin Zhao was arrested in Suzhou on suspicion of involvement with the underground publication Spark (Xinghuo).

据后来的调查，她与《星火》的直接关系有限，她被捕的一个直接原因，是那份刊物刊登了她的一首长诗。但那已经不重要了——她进了上海的第一看守所。

According to later investigations, her direct connection with Spark was limited; one immediate reason for her arrest was that the publication had printed a long poem of hers. But that no longer mattered—she was taken to Shanghai's No. 1 Detention Center.

1962年，她一度被允许保外就医。但她并没有“安分下来”。她继续写作、表达。随后，她再次被捕，此后被长期关押在上海提篮桥监狱。

In 1962 she was at one point allowed out on medical parole. But she did not “settle down.” She continued to write and to speak out. She was then arrested again, and afterward held for a long period in Shanghai's Tilanqiao Prison.

在狱中，她写下了大量材料。她坚持自己的信念，狱中条件极其恶劣，她的身体也一天天垮下去。1965年，她被判处有期徒刑二十年。

In prison she wrote a great deal. She held to her convictions; conditions in the prison were extremely harsh, and her health declined day by day. In 1965 she was sentenced to twenty years' imprisonment.

她本可以活下来。

She could have lived.

但1968年4月，她的案件被改判死刑。据记载，改判的原因之一，是她始终不肯认罪。

But in April 1968 her sentence was changed to death. According to the records, one reason for the change was that she refused to the end to confess.

五、1968年4月29日

V. April 29, 1968

1968年4月29日，林昭在上海被枪决。她三十六岁。

On April 29, 1968, Lin Zhao was shot in Shanghai. She was thirty-six.

这一天，离她十八岁那年兴奋地告诉同学“我已经参加了组织”的日子，过去了二十年。

That day came twenty years after the day, at eighteen, when she excitedly told a classmate, “I have already joined the organization.”

她的死，给家人带来了毁灭性的打击。她的父亲，在她被捕后自杀；她的母亲，精神受到严重创伤，1975年在上海去世。

Her death struck her family a devastating blow. Her father took his own life after her arrest; her mother suffered severe psychological trauma and died in Shanghai in 1975.

一个原本体面的知识分子家庭，就这样散了。

A once respectable intellectual family simply fell apart.

六、“这是一次冤杀无辜”

VI. “This Was a Wrongful Killing of an Innocent”

1976年之后，中国开始系统平反历史上的冤案。

After 1976, China began systematically to redress wrongful cases from its history.

1980年8月22日，上海市高级人民法院作出判决，撤销对林昭的原判，宣告她无罪。判决书里有一句结论，只有六个字：

On August 22, 1980, the Shanghai Higher People's Court handed down a ruling that set aside the original verdict against Lin Zhao and declared her not guilty. The judgment contained a conclusion of only six characters:

“这是一次冤杀无辜。”

“This was a wrongful killing of an innocent.”

这六个字，是当时最直白、也最沉痛的一份自我纠错。它没有绕圈子，没有含糊其辞——它直接承认：我们杀错了人。

Those six characters were the most blunt and most sorrowful act of self-correction of that time. They beat around no bush and hedged nothing—they admitted outright: we killed the wrong person.

后来，林昭的老师、同学和亲友在北京为她举行了悼念会。2004年，她的衣冠冢在苏州灵岩山下落成。

Later, her teachers, classmates, relatives, and friends held a memorial gathering for her in Beijing. In 2004 a cenotaph was completed for her at the foot of Lingyan Hill in Suzhou.

历史给了她清白，但历史还不了她一条命。

History gave her back her good name, but history cannot give her back her life.

这六个字之所以重要，是因为它代表了一种态度：不回避，不含糊，不把错误说成“时代的局限”就轻轻放下。 一个愿意直接说出“我们冤杀了无辜”的判决，才配得上“纠错”这两个字。这比任何颂词都更有力量。

Those six characters matter because they stand for an attitude: not to evade, not to hedge, not to dismiss an error lightly by calling it “the limitations of the times.” Only a judgment willing to say outright “we wrongly killed an innocent” deserves the name of correction. That carries more force than any eulogy.

七、写给2026年的我们

VII. To Us in 2026

林昭的故事，最让人久久不能平静的地方是：她其实什么都没做“错”。

What lingers most about Lin Zhao's story is this: she had in fact done nothing “wrong.”

她只是替一个被围攻的同学说了话，只是相信“人人平等、和蔼、不要咬人”这些最朴素的道理，只是不肯在一个要求所有人认错的场合里说一句假话。

She had only spoken up for a classmate who was under attack; she had only believed in the plainest of truths—that people should be equal, kind, and should not tear at one another; she had only refused to say a single false word in a setting where everyone was required to admit error.

在今天，这些话都是我们的常识。 而在她的年代，常识成了罪证。

Today these words are our common sense. In her time, common sense became evidence of a crime.

这提醒我们：常识需要用制度去保护。 如果没有人保护“说真话”这件事，那么任何一个时代，都可能把最普通的良知，判成最重的罪。

This reminds us: common sense needs to be protected by institutions. If no one protects the act of speaking the truth, then any era can convict the most ordinary conscience of the gravest crime.

2026年的我们，生活在一个可以说真话的时代。这个“可以”，是林昭们用命垫出来的。所以我们纪念她，不只是为了哀悼，更是为了记住：不要把“说出常识”这件事，当成理所当然。 它需要有人愿意站出来，也需要社会有能力接住站出来的人。林昭的故事里，最遗憾的，就是当年既没有人能保住她，也没有一个机制能拦住那次错误。后来那份“宣告无罪”的判决，与其说是在救她，不如说是在提醒后来的人：制度的纠错能力，才是对个体最大的保护。

We in 2026 live in an age where the truth can be spoken. That “can” was paid for with the lives of Lin Zhao and people like her. So we remember her not only to mourn, but to keep in mind that “speaking common sense aloud” should not be taken for granted. It requires people willing to stand up, and it requires a society able to catch those who do. The saddest thing in Lin Zhao's story is that no one could save her at the time, and no mechanism could stop that error. The later ruling that “declared her not guilty” was less a rescue of her than a reminder to those who came after: the capacity of institutions to correct their own errors is the greatest protection an individual can have.

史料来源与辨析

Sources and Notes

1. 生平基础事实：林昭，原名彭令昭，1932年12月16日生于苏州，1968年4月29日在上海被处决，年36岁。为确证史实。

1. Basic biographical facts: Lin Zhao, originally named Peng Lingzhao, born in Suzhou on December 16, 1932, executed in Shanghai on April 29, 1968, aged 36. Confirmed historical fact.

2. 家庭：母亲许宪民早年参加革命；父亲彭国彦曾任吴县县长。为通行记载。其父在女儿被捕后自杀，为确证史实。

2. Family: her mother, Xu Xianmin, joined the revolution in her early years; her father, Peng Guoyan, served as magistrate of Wu County. Generally accepted record. Her father took his own life after his daughter's arrest; confirmed historical fact.

3. 考入北大：1954年以江苏省文科第一名成绩考入北京大学中文系（新闻专业）。为通行记载。

3. Admission to Peking University: in 1954 she entered the Department of Chinese (journalism program) at Peking University with the top score in Jiangsu Province in the liberal arts. Generally accepted record.

4. 1957年“跳上桌子为张元勋辩护”：这一情节的描述，主要来自多年后胡杰等人对当年北大同学（如张元勋、陈爱文等）的采访记录，以及独立纪录片《寻找林昭的灵魂》中的口述材料。辨析：属于“当事人多年后的回忆”，细节可能有所出入，但“林昭为张元勋当众辩护、因而被划为右派”这一基本事实，为多方印证。

4. The 1957 “jumping onto a table to defend Zhang Yuanxun”: this account comes mainly from interviews conducted years later by Hu Jie and others with Peking University classmates of the time (Zhang Yuanxun, Chen Aiwen, and others), as well as oral testimony in the independent documentary In Search of Lin Zhao's Soul (Xunzhao Lin Zhao de linghun). Evaluation: it rests on “the recollections of those involved many years later,” so details may vary, but the basic fact that “Lin Zhao defended Zhang Yuanxun in public and was labeled a rightist as a result” is corroborated from several quarters.

5. 被捕与判刑：1960年因《星火》案被捕；1965年被判有期徒刑20年；1968年4月改判死刑并执行。为通行记载。

5. Arrest and sentencing: arrested in 1960 in connection with the Spark case; sentenced to 20 years' imprisonment in 1965; sentence changed to death and carried out in April 1968. Generally accepted record.

6. 平反：1980年8月22日，上海市高级人民法院撤销原判，宣告林昭无罪，判决结论中有“这是一次冤杀无辜”的表述。为确证史实。

6. Redress: on August 22, 1980, the Shanghai Higher People's Court set aside the original verdict and declared Lin Zhao not guilty; the conclusion of the judgment stated that “this was a wrongful killing of an innocent.” Confirmed historical fact.

7. 表述原则：本文严格依据中国司法机关1980年的正式平反结论、以及国内公开媒体（如《中国青年报》2004年对纪录片《寻找林昭的灵魂》的相关报道）的叙述框架展开。文中不采用境外政治化、对抗性的叙述框架，不渲染受刑细节，聚焦于“坚持真话—被冤杀—国家正式平反昭雪”这一主线。

7. Principle of presentation: this essay follows strictly the formal 1980 redress conclusion of China's judicial authorities and the narrative framework of open domestic media (such as China Youth Daily's 2004 coverage of the documentary In Search of Lin Zhao's Soul (Xunzhao Lin Zhao de linghun)). It does not adopt an overseas politicized or confrontational narrative framework and does not dwell on the details of punishment or execution, focusing instead on the main line: holding to the truth—being wrongly killed—formal state redress and exoneration.

8. 关于其在狱中写下的具体文字内容：来源较为复杂，部分材料由境外出版物整理。本文不引用具体文本（因其版本与可靠性存在争议），仅作概括性陈述。

8. On the specific texts she wrote in prison: the sources are relatively complex, and some materials were compiled by overseas publications. This essay does not quote specific texts (since their editions and reliability are disputed) and offers only general statements.

9. 2004年苏州灵岩山衣冠冢落成，为通行记载。

9. The cenotaph at Lingyan Hill in Suzhou was completed in 2004; generally accepted record.
