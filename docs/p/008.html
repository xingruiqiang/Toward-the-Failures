Chapter 008 · Part 1

牛顿：那个在瘟疫里躲回乡下的“早产儿”

Newton: The "Premature Baby" Who Fled the Plague Back to the Countryside

——他一生都在退，却退出了一整个新世界

— He retreated all his life, and in retreating brought a whole new world into being

在2026年的今天，我们提起牛顿，脑海里浮现的是一尊大理石像：戴着假发、身着长袍、手握《原理》，是画册里那个正襟危坐的“科学之神”。可如果你真的走进1642年那个冬天的英格兰农舍，你会看到一个完全不同的人——一个早产的、瘦小得能塞进一只奶杯的婴儿；一个被母亲“丢下”的孤儿；一个终身未娶、脾气古怪、把大半辈子花在炼金术和神学上的怪人。

In 2026, when we speak of Newton, a marble statue rises in the mind: wig on head, robe on back, the Principia in hand—the solemnly seated "god of science" of the picture books. But if you truly walked into that English farmhouse in the winter of 1642, you would see an entirely different person: a premature, tiny baby small enough to fit inside a quart mug; an orphan "abandoned" by his mother; an eccentric who never married, whose temper was difficult, and who spent the greater part of his life on alchemy and theology.

他一生都在躲。躲瘟疫，躲人群，躲争吵，躲女人，躲整个喧嚣的世界。

All his life he was hiding. Hiding from plague, from crowds, from quarrels, from women, from the whole clamorous world.

而正是这个总在“退”的人，退出了现代科学的整个地基。

And it was precisely this man who was always "retreating" who retreated out the entire foundation of modern science.

一、一个本不该活下来的孩子

I. A Child Who Should Not Have Survived

1642年12月25日（旧历）的圣诞节清晨，英格兰林肯郡伍尔索普村的一间农舍里，一个男婴出生了。他早产，瘦小得惊人。

On the morning of Christmas Day, December 25, 1642 (Old Style), a baby boy was born in a farmhouse in the village of Woolsthorpe, in Lincolnshire, England. He was premature, and astonishingly small.

多年以后，牛顿向亲戚转述过他母亲当年的一句话——这孩子刚生下来时，小得“能装进一只一夸脱的杯子里”。（这句话是牛顿本人后来转述的家人口述，并非当时医生的记录，但流传极广。）

Years later, Newton repeated to a relative something his mother had once said—that at birth the child was small enough "to fit into a quart mug." (The remark is Newton's own later retelling of a family account, not a physician's record from the time, but it has circulated very widely.)

更糟的是，他的父亲——也叫艾萨克·牛顿，一个不识字的自耕农——在他出生前三个月就去世了。他一出生，就是个没有父亲的遗腹子。

Worse still, his father—also named Isaac Newton, a yeoman farmer who could not read or write—had died three months before he was born. He came into the world a posthumous child without a father.

当时来帮忙的两位邻家妇人，在半路上坐下歇脚，说：不用急着赶去，这孩子八成等我们回去就已经没气了。

Two neighboring women who had been called to help stopped on the way to rest and said there was no need to hurry—by the time they got back, the child would very likely have breathed his last.

这个“几乎活不下来”的孩子，后来活到了84岁。

This child who "almost did not survive" went on to live to eighty-four.

可他活下来的方式，和别人不一样。他三岁那年，母亲汉娜改嫁给一位年长的牧师，搬去邻村，把他留给了外祖母抚养。一个被“丢下”的孩子，慢慢学会了一件事：不指望任何人，只待在自己的脑子里。

But the way he survived was unlike anyone else's. When he was three, his mother, Hannah, remarried an older clergyman and moved to a neighboring village, leaving him to be raised by his grandmother. A child who had been "left behind" slowly learned one thing: to count on no one, and to live only inside his own mind.

他后来在私人笔记里写下过极狠的话——他“威胁要把继父和亲生母亲连同房子一起烧掉”。这句让后世研究者都错愕的话，藏着一个被遗弃的孩子心底，那块冻得最硬的冰。

He later wrote something savage in his private notebooks—that he "threatened to burn the house down with his stepfather and his own mother inside it." That line, which has startled later scholars, holds, at the bottom of an abandoned child's heart, the hardest-frozen ice.

二、他连“当个农民”都失败了

II. He Failed Even at Being a Farmer

少年牛顿在格兰瑟姆的国王中学读书，寄宿在一位药剂师家里——这大概是他一生痴迷配方与化学的起点。他在学校图书馆的窗台上刻下自己的名字，那个签名至今还在。

As a boy, Newton attended the King's School in Grantham and lodged with an apothecary—probably the beginning of a lifelong fascination with formulas and chemistry. He carved his name into a windowsill of the school library, and that signature is still there today.

可他并不是什么“神童”。他成绩平平，只是爱动手：做风车模型，做水钟，做日晷。邻居们甚至靠“牛顿的日晷”来判断时辰。

But he was no "prodigy." His marks were middling; he simply loved to make things with his hands—windmill models, water clocks, sundials. Neighbors even told time by "Newton's sundial."

十七岁那年，家里把他从学校拽了回来。母亲第二次守寡，想让他回来务农、继承庄园。他去了，喂羊、下地——然后成了全村最糟糕的农夫。

At seventeen, his family pulled him out of school. His mother, widowed a second time, wanted him to come back and farm, to inherit the estate. He went—tending sheep, working the fields—and became the worst farmer in the whole village.

他在田里看书，忘了干活；让他去集市学做买卖，他躲在树丛后头算数学。有一次舅父起了疑心，悄悄跟去，发现外甥正躺在草地上，盯着一道数学题看得入神。

He read books in the fields and forgot to work; sent to market to learn the trade, he hid behind a thicket and did mathematics. Once his uncle grew suspicious and followed him secretly, only to find his nephew lying on the grass, lost in a math problem.

是舅父和校长一起劝服了他母亲：这孩子不是种地的料，送他念书吧。

It was his uncle and the schoolmaster together who persuaded his mother: the boy was not cut out for farming—send him to school.

1661年，他进了剑桥大学三一学院，身份是“减费生”（subsizar）——要给富裕同学端饭跑腿的穷学生，靠做杂役换学费。

In 1661 he entered Trinity College, Cambridge, as a "subsizar"—a poor student who waited on wealthier classmates, serving at table and running errands to earn his fees.

一个连当农民都不及格的人，就这样被推上了另一条路。

And so a man who could not even pass as a farmer was pushed onto a different road.

三、瘟疫来了，学校关了，他的“奇迹年”却到了

III. The Plague Came, the School Closed, and His "Miracle Year" Arrived

1665年，一场大瘟疫席卷伦敦。剑桥大学关闭，所有人撤离。

In 1665, a great plague swept London. Cambridge shut down and everyone withdrew.

牛顿也回到了伍尔索普——回到了那个后来被他称为“一生中最富有创造力”的地方。

Newton too returned to Woolsthorpe—back to what he later called "the most creative period of my life."

接下来两年（1665—1666），一个二十三四岁的年轻人，独自待在乡下农舍里，一个人，一支笔，做出了三件改变世界的事：

Over the next two years (1665–1666), a young man of twenty-three or twenty-four, alone in a country farmhouse, with no one but himself and a pen, did three things that changed the world:

他发展出了“流数法”——也就是后来的微积分；

He developed the "method of fluxions"—later known as the calculus;

他用三棱镜把白光分解成彩色光谱，揭开了颜色之谜；

He used a prism to split white light into a spectrum of colors, unlocking the mystery of color;

他看着院子里一只苹果落地，开始想：让苹果落地的那股力，会不会和把月亮留在轨道上的力，是同一股力？

He watched an apple fall in the garden and began to wonder: could the force that pulls an apple to the ground be the very same force that holds the moon in its orbit?

（顺便要澄清：苹果砸中脑袋，是后人添的戏。牛顿晚年确实向传记作者讲过，他是看到苹果下落而受到启发——但没有证据说苹果砸到了他头上，他自己也从没这么说过。）

(A clarification in passing: the apple striking his head is a later embellishment. In his old age Newton did tell a biographer that he had been inspired by seeing an apple fall—but there is no evidence that the apple hit him on the head, and he himself never said so.)

两年，一个人，三件事。数学、光学、力学——现代科学的三根柱子，都在这间乡下的屋子里打了桩。

Two years, one man, three things. Mathematics, optics, mechanics—the three pillars of modern science were all sunk into the ground in that country house.

可他在做什么？他谁也没见，什么也没发表。因为瘟疫，世界“隔离”了他。而恰恰是这份隔离，给了他一生中最自由的两年。

And what was he doing? Seeing no one, publishing nothing. Because of the plague, the world had "isolated" him. And it was precisely that isolation that gave him the freest two years of his life.

两年后他回到剑桥，又过了二十多年，才在别人一再催促和资助下，把《自然哲学的数学原理》（1687）拿出来。替他奔走、掏钱印书的，是天文学家埃德蒙·哈雷。

Two years later he returned to Cambridge, and it was more than twenty years after that before, at others' repeated urging and with others' money, he brought out the Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy (1687). The one who ran errands for him and paid to print the book was the astronomer Edmond Halley.

四、他其实是个“吵架成瘾”的难相处的人

IV. He Was in Fact a Difficult, Quarrel-Addicted Man

我们总爱把伟人塑造成圣人。牛顿不是。他是科学史上最有名的“难相处的人”之一。

We love to turn great men into saints. Newton was not one. He is among the most famously "difficult" people in the history of science.

1672年，他向皇家学会递交第一篇论文，讲光的颜色，主张“光是粒子”。这直接顶撞了皇家学会创始人之一——罗伯特·胡克的波动说。两人从此结怨。

In 1672 he submitted his first paper to the Royal Society, on the color of light, arguing that "light is a particle." This ran directly against the wave theory of Robert Hooke, one of the Society's founders. From then on the two were at enmity.

1676年，牛顿在给胡克的一封信里，写下一句后来印遍全世界教科书的话：

In 1676, in a letter to Hooke, Newton wrote a line that would later be printed in textbooks all over the world:

“如果我看得更远，那是因为我站在巨人的肩上。”

"If I have seen further, it is because I have stood on the shoulders of giants."

这句话，后世当作谦逊的典范。但不少研究者认为，它写给身材矮小、驼背的胡克，未必没有一点暗讽。而胡克如今连一张可靠的肖像都没留下——这笔账常被人算在牛顿头上（此说流传很广，但并无确证）。

Posterity has taken this as a model of humility. But many researchers believe that, addressed as it was to the short, hunchbacked Hooke, it may not have been entirely free of a sly barb. And Hooke today has not even one reliable portrait left—a loss often laid at Newton's door (the claim is very widespread, but there is no proof).

他还和莱布尼茨为“微积分到底是谁发明的”打了半辈子官司，让这位对手在争议与冷落中度过了晚年。

He also spent half a lifetime in litigation with Leibniz over "who really invented the calculus," leaving his rival to pass his last years amid controversy and neglect.

他更是造币厂的执法者。1696年他出任皇家造币厂督办，后来升任厂长。他极其认真地追查假币制造者，把他们一个个送上绞架。一个改写了宇宙观的人，同时是伦敦街头最冷酷的执法官之一。

He was also a lawman of the Mint. In 1696 he became Warden of the Royal Mint, later rising to Master. He pursued counterfeiters with the utmost seriousness and sent them to the gallows one by one. The man who rewrote our picture of the cosmos was at the same time one of the coldest law enforcers on the streets of London.

五、晚年的牛顿：炼金术与神学

V. Newton in His Later Years: Alchemy and Theology

比造假币更让后人意外的，是他私底下真正投入的东西。

What surprised posterity even more than counterfeiting were the things he was privately devoted to.

牛顿留下的手稿里，关于炼金术和神学的篇幅，比关于物理和数学的还多。他几十年如一日地钻研炼金术，寻找传说中的“哲人石”；他写下上百万字的神学笔记，研究《圣经》预言，甚至推算过世界末日。

In the manuscripts Newton left behind, the pages on alchemy and theology outnumber those on physics and mathematics. For decades on end he delved into alchemy, seeking the fabled "philosopher's stone"; he wrote more than a million words of theological notes, studied biblical prophecy, and even calculated the date of the end of the world.

在同时代的正统学者眼里，这简直是“走火入魔”“江郎才尽”。一个用数学统治了天体运行的人，晚年怎么沉迷于把铅变成金子的幻想？

In the eyes of the orthodox scholars of his day, this was sheer "obsession" and "decline of talent." How could a man who governed the motions of the heavens with mathematics sink, in his later years, into the fantasy of turning lead into gold?

但换个角度看：牛顿从不觉得自己在做“两件事”。他研究引力，是想读懂上帝写在自然这部大书上的字；他研究炼金术，也是一样。上帝、自然、宇宙的秩序，在他眼里本就是同一件事。

But seen another way: Newton never thought he was doing "two things." When he studied gravity, he was trying to read the words God had written in the great book of nature; when he studied alchemy, it was the same. God, nature, and the order of the cosmos were, to his mind, one and the same thing.

他不是“老糊涂了”。他只是始终在追问同一个问题：这一切的规则，究竟是谁定下的。

He was not "losing his mind." He was simply asking the same question all along: who, in the end, laid down the rules of all this?

1727年3月，84岁的牛顿在伦敦去世，葬于威斯敏斯特教堂。他留下过一段著名的自我评价：

In March 1727, at eighty-four, Newton died in London and was buried in Westminster Abbey. He left behind a famous estimate of himself:

“我不知道世上的人怎样看我。但我自己觉得，我不过是一个在海边玩耍的孩子，偶尔捡到几块光滑的石子、几片美丽的贝壳，而真理的大海，仍在我面前一片未知。”

"I do not know how the world may see me. But to myself I seem to have been only a boy playing on the seashore, now and then finding a smoother pebble or a prettier shell, while the great ocean of truth lay all undiscovered before me."

六、结语：致每一个“不合群”的人

VI. Conclusion: To Everyone Who "Doesn't Fit In"

牛顿这一生，几乎每一步都在“失败”：

Almost every step of Newton's life was a "failure":

他是早产儿，被断定活不下来；

He was premature, given up for dead;

他是遗腹子，被母亲丢给外祖母；

He was a posthumous child, left by his mother to his grandmother;

他不会种地，是全家眼里的“废物”；

He could not farm, and was the "good-for-nothing" in the family's eyes;

他不合群，一辈子没结婚，几乎把朋友都吵成了敌人；

He did not fit in, never married, and quarreled nearly every friend into an enemy;

他大半生缩在乡下和实验室里，研究着当时没人懂的炼金术和神学。

He spent the greater part of his life holed up in the countryside and the laboratory, studying alchemy and theology that no one then understood.

换成今天的说法：他社交失败，事业“不务正业”，感情空白，晚年“迷信”。任何一个焦虑的父母，大概都不会希望孩子长成他那样。

Put in today's terms: he was a social failure, his career was "not his proper business," his love life was blank, and he grew "superstitious" in old age. Any anxious parent would probably not wish a child to grow up like him.

可正是这个“不合群的人”，在所有人都撤走的瘟疫之年，独自把那扇门推开了。

Yet it was precisely this "man who did not fit in" who, in the plague year when everyone had withdrawn, pushed that door open all alone.

所以，2026年，如果你也觉得自己是个“怪人”——不爱应酬，不合群，被催着“合群”“成家”“像个正常人”；如果你也觉得热闹是他们的，你只喜欢一个人待着，钻研点什么——

So, in 2026, if you too feel you are an "odd one"—no taste for socializing, out of step with the crowd, pushed to "fit in," "settle down," "be like a normal person"; if you too feel that the bustle belongs to them, and all you want is to be alone and dig into something—

请记住：历史上改变世界最多的那类人，往往正是最不合群的那一种。

then remember: the kind of people who have changed the world most are often exactly the kind who fit in least.

那个“站在巨人肩上”的人，一生最伟大的成就，恰恰发生在全世界都躲开他的时候。

The man who "stood on the shoulders of giants" achieved his greatest work at the very moment when the whole world was keeping its distance from him.

Appendix: Sources and Notes

主要史料

Primary Sources

1. 《艾萨克·牛顿爵士生平回忆录》（威廉·斯蒂克利，1752年）——牛顿晚年亲述“看到苹果落地”的出处；也是“站在巨人肩上”书信背景的早期记录来源。

1. Memoirs of the Life of Sir Isaac Newton (William Stukeley, 1752)—the source for Newton's own late-life account of "seeing the apple fall"; also an early record of the background to the "shoulders of giants" letter.

2. 牛顿书信（1676年致罗伯特·胡克）——“站在巨人肩上”原文出处。

2. Newton's letter to Robert Hooke (1676)—the source of the original "shoulders of giants" passage.

3. 《自然哲学的数学原理》（1687年，在哈雷推动与资助下出版）。

3. The Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy (1687, published at Halley's urging and expense).

4. 牛顿私人笔记本（约1659年）——含威胁烧掉继父与母亲的记载。

4. Newton's private notebook (c. 1659)—containing the threat to burn his stepfather and mother.

5. 剑桥大学图书馆“朴茨茅斯手稿集”——含牛顿大量炼金术与神学手稿。

5. The "Portsmouth Papers" at Cambridge University Library—containing a great many of Newton's alchemical and theological manuscripts.

Dates and Facts

6. 生于儒略历1642年12月25日（格里高利历1643年1月4日），卒于1727年3月31日（格里高利历）。1696年任皇家造币厂督办，1699年后任厂长；1703年任皇家学会会长；1705年受封爵士；葬于威斯敏斯特教堂。

6. Born December 25, 1642 (Julian calendar; January 4, 1643, Gregorian), died March 31, 1727 (Gregorian). In 1696 he became Warden of the Royal Mint, and after 1699 its Master; in 1703 he became President of the Royal Society; in 1705 he was knighted; he is buried in Westminster Abbey.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “苹果砸中头”——后世讹传。牛顿只说“看到苹果下落”。

7. "The apple struck him on the head"—a later false report. Newton said only that he "saw an apple fall."

8. “一夸脱杯”——牛顿本人向亲戚转述的家人口述，非医学记录。

8. "A quart mug"—Newton's own retelling to a relative of a family account, not a medical record.

9. “站在巨人肩上”的谦逊含义——写给驼背矮小的胡克，是否含暗讽，学界有争论。

9. The humble meaning of "standing on the shoulders of giants"—whether, addressed to the short and hunchbacked Hooke, it carried a veiled barb remains a matter of scholarly debate.

10. “胡克画像被牛顿处理掉”——流传很广，但无确证，本文以“此说无确证”表述。

10. "Hooke's portrait was disposed of by Newton"—widely circulated but unproven; this chapter states it as "a claim for which there is no proof."
