张小龙：一个人写出Foxmail，最后把它“送”了出去

Allen Zhang: One Man Wrote Foxmail, Then "Gave It Away"

——微信诞生前，他在一个“不被看见”的角落里坐了整整五年

—Before WeChat Was Born, He Sat in an "Unseen" Corner for Five Long Years

2026年，很多人有一种隐痛：明明能力不差，却不在核心圈里；做的事不被理解，说了也没人听；公司里最热闹的项目跟自己无关，自己像个“被边缘化”的人。

In 2026, many people carry a quiet ache: their abilities are hardly lacking, yet they are not in the inner circle; the work they do goes ununderstood, and no one listens even when they speak; the liveliest projects in the company have nothing to do with them, and they feel like someone who has been "marginalized."

有一个人，也有过这样一段日子。他一个人写出了中国最有名的免费邮箱软件，却因为“不赚钱”被人接手；他加入一家大公司后，负责一个被当作“鸡肋”的项目，做了好几年，默默无闻。

There was a man who lived through days like that too. All by himself he wrote China's most famous free email program, only to have it taken over because it "made no money." After joining a big company, he was put in charge of a project everyone treated as a chicken rib—worth neither keeping nor throwing away—and he labored on it for years in obscurity.

直到2010年的某个深夜，他给老板发了一封邮件，说想做一个新产品。那个产品，后来叫微信。

Then, late one night in 2010, he sent his boss an email saying he wanted to build a new product. That product would later be called WeChat.

他叫张小龙。

His name was Allen Zhang.

一、一个人，一个软件

I. One Man, One Piece of Software

1969年，张小龙出生在湖南邵阳。1994年，他从华中科技大学（原华中理工大学）电信系硕士毕业。

Allen Zhang was born in Shaoyang, Hunan, in 1969. In 1994 he received a master's degree in telecommunications from Huazhong University of Science and Technology (then called Huazhong University of Science and Technology).

1997年，他开始写一个免费邮箱客户端——Foxmail。这是一个几乎由他一个人完成的软件：一个人设计、一个人编码、一个人维护。它体积小、运行快，很快在中国网民中积累了庞大的用户量，一度是很多人上网必装的软件之一。

In 1997 he began writing a free email client—Foxmail. It was software he built almost entirely alone: one man designing, one man coding, one man maintaining. Small in size and fast to run, it quickly built up a huge user base among Chinese internet users, and for a time it was one of those programs many people installed as soon as they went online.

但 Foxmail 是免费的。

But Foxmail was free.

张小龙几乎不靠它赚钱，也没有清晰的商业化路径。他是一个典型的技术理想主义者：在意产品本身的优雅，不太在意怎么把它变成生意。

Allen Zhang made almost no money from it, and had no clear path to commercialization. He was a classic technological idealist: he cared about the elegance of the product itself, and little about how to turn it into a business.

2000年，博大公司以约1200万元收购了 Foxmail。张小龙随之加入博大。

In 2000 the company Boda acquired Foxmail for about 12 million yuan. Allen Zhang joined Boda along with it.

2005年，腾讯收购了博大，张小龙和 Foxmail 一起进入腾讯。后来有人说，这是Foxmail“被送了出去”——一个他自己一个人做出来的产品，最终归入了一家大公司。

In 2005 Tencent acquired Boda, and Allen Zhang went into Tencent together with Foxmail. People later said that Foxmail had been "given away"—a product he had made all by himself, ending up in the hands of a large corporation.

二、被“边缘化”的五年

II. Five Years of Being "Marginalized"

进入腾讯后，张小龙被安排负责QQ邮箱。

After joining Tencent, Allen Zhang was put in charge of QQ Mail.

这个开局并不顺利。当时的QQ邮箱体验很差，被用户吐槽得厉害。张小龙在这个项目上，经历了他在腾讯最艰难的一段适应期——从单打独斗的独立开发者，变成要管理一个团队、要理解一家大公司的运作方式。

The start was not smooth. QQ Mail was a poor experience at the time, and users complained about it bitterly. On this project Allen Zhang went through his hardest period of adjustment at Tencent—from a lone-wolf independent developer to someone who had to manage a team and understand how a large company worked.

后来，他带领团队把QQ邮箱做了出来，成了国内口碑最好的邮箱之一。但在腾讯内部，邮箱始终不是一个“核心战场”。

In time he led his team to build QQ Mail into one of the best-reviewed email services in China. But inside Tencent, email was never a "core battlefield."

于是，有几年时间，张小龙和他所在的广州研发中心（广研），在腾讯内部处于一种“边缘”状态：不显眼，不被重点投入，也不被太多人关注。他后来的一些访谈里，能感觉到那种“不被看见”的处境。

So for several years, Allen Zhang and the Guangzhou R&D Center where he worked existed in a "marginal" state within Tencent: inconspicuous, not a priority for investment, and not much noticed by anyone. In some of his later interviews, you can sense that condition of being "unseen."

这段时间，对一个有能力的人来说，是最消磨人的。

For a capable person, that kind of stretch is the most wearing of all.

三、2010年那封深夜邮件

III. That Late-Night Email in 2010

转折发生在2010年。

The turning point came in 2010.

那一年，一款叫Kik的移动通讯应用在海外爆红。张小龙看到了机会。据流传较广的说法，他在一个深夜给马化腾发了邮件，提出腾讯应该做一款移动端的通讯产品。马化腾同意了，项目给了广研。

That year a mobile messaging app called Kik became a sensation overseas. Allen Zhang saw an opportunity. According to a widely circulated account, he sent Pony Ma an email late one night proposing that Tencent build a mobile messaging product. Pony Ma agreed, and the project went to the Guangzhou R&D Center.

2011年1月21日，微信上线。

On January 21, 2011, WeChat went live.

接下来的事情，几乎是中国互联网史上最著名的一段“逆袭”：微信在短短几个月内用户破亿；它做了“摇一摇”“附近的人”，做了朋友圈，做了公众号，做了微信支付，最后变成了一个超过十亿人使用的“生活方式”。

What followed is one of the most famous "comeback" stories in the history of the Chinese internet: within just a few months WeChat passed a hundred million users; it introduced "Shake" and "People Nearby," then Moments, then Official Accounts, then WeChat Pay, and finally became a "way of life" used by more than a billion people.

而做出微信的，正是那个在内部被“边缘化”了几年的团队。

And the team that built WeChat was precisely the one that had been "marginalized" inside the company for years.

四、他到底在想什么

IV. What He Was Actually Thinking

张小龙是一个很“反主流”的产品人。他有几句被反复引用的话：

Allen Zhang is a deeply "anti-mainstream" product person. A few of his lines get quoted again and again:

“好的产品，是用完即走的。”

"A good product is one you use and then leave."

“让用户用完即走，是他效率的体现，也是对他时间的尊重。”

"Letting users finish and leave is the mark of their efficiency, and a respect for their time."

“微信是一个工具，用完就可以离开。”

"WeChat is a tool; when you're done, you can leave."

在一个所有产品都在拼命争夺用户时长、制造上瘾的时代，说出“用完即走”这句话，几乎是一种逆流。它背后的意思是：不要用产品去绑架用户，而要帮用户解决问题。

In an era when every product is desperately fighting for users' time and engineering addiction, saying "use it and leave" is almost swimming against the current. The idea behind it: don't hold users hostage with your product—help them solve problems.

他也以“克制”著称。微信长期坚持不做过多的商业化、不开屏广告、不滥用推送，这在商业上甚至被批评为“不够激进”。

He is also known for "restraint." WeChat long insisted on not over-commercializing, not running splash-screen ads, and not abusing push notifications—a stance that was even criticized, in commercial terms, as "not aggressive enough."

一个安静、内向、爱读书、爱讲哲学的技术人，最后做出了十亿级的产品。这件事本身，就打破了“做产品必须外向、必须善交际、必须懂营销”的刻板印象。

A quiet, introverted technologist who loved reading and loved talking about philosophy ended up building a product on the billion-user scale. That fact alone breaks the stereotype that to build products you must be outgoing, must be good at socializing, and must understand marketing.

五、“克制”的代价

V. The Cost of "Restraint"

微信并不是没有争议。它的巨大成功，也带来了大量的批评：有人担心它让人沉迷，有人质疑它的内容生态，有人讨论它的规则边界。张小龙对这些问题的态度，始终是那套“工具论”——他认为微信的价值在于提高效率，而不是占用用户的时间。

WeChat is not without controversy. Its enormous success also brought a great deal of criticism: some worried it was addictive, some questioned its content ecosystem, some debated the limits of its rules. Allen Zhang's attitude toward these issues has always been that same "tool theory"—he believes WeChat's value lies in raising efficiency, not in consuming users' time.

有一个细节很能说明他的风格：微信上线多年，始终坚持不做开屏广告，对商业化保持相当程度的克制。这在商业上未必是“最优解”——它放弃了大量唾手可得的收入。有批评者说，这是一种“不够进取”。

One detail says a lot about his style: for years after launch, WeChat consistently refused splash-screen ads, keeping a considerable restraint on commercialization. Commercially this is not necessarily the "optimal solution"—it gave up a great deal of easy revenue. Some critics called it "not ambitious enough."

但换个角度看，这也是一种“失败”——在商业效率这个维度上，微信主动放弃了很多东西。 而它换来的，是另一种东西：用户长期的、近乎本能的信任。

But looked at another way, this is also a kind of "failure"—on the dimension of commercial efficiency, WeChat voluntarily gave up a great deal. What it got in return is something else: users' long-term, almost instinctive trust.

张小龙后来讲过一个观点，大意是：产品经理不该把自己当成“流量的收割者”，而该把自己当成“用户时间的守护者”。在一个人人都在抢时长的时代，这是一种很孤独的坚持。

Allen Zhang later made a point to the effect that a product manager should not see himself as a "harvester of traffic" but as a "guardian of users' time." In an era when everyone is grabbing for screen time, this is a very lonely kind of persistence.

也许正是这种“不肯把用户当资源”的克制，才让微信在十多年后，仍然是那个“可以放心交给家人用”的产品。

Perhaps it is precisely this restraint—this refusal to treat users as a resource—that has kept WeChat, more than a decade on, still the product you can "hand to your family without worry."

六、致2026：关于“蛰伏”和“准备好了”

VI. To 2026: On "Lying Low" and Being "Ready"

2026年，我们被“存在感焦虑”困住。别人都在聚光灯下，自己在角落里；别人的项目是重点，自己的项目没人看。很多人因此怀疑：我是不是被放弃了？

In 2026 we are trapped by "visibility anxiety." Everyone else is in the spotlight while we sit in a corner; other people's projects are priorities while ours go unseen. Many people start to wonder: Have I been given up on?

张小龙的经历提供了一个很不一样的答案：

Allen Zhang's experience offers a very different answer:

“边缘”可能是最好的位置。

"The margins" may be the best position of all.

在广研那几年，他没有被 KPI 追着跑，不用参加无数的会议，不用在内部竞争中消耗自己。他有时间读书、思考，有时间把一个产品想透。当机会来的时候，他已经“准备好了”——不是准备好了一个创意，而是准备了十几年做产品的功力。

During those years at the Guangzhou R&D Center, he was not chased around by KPIs, did not have to attend endless meetings, did not have to burn himself out in internal competition. He had time to read, to think, to think a product all the way through. When the opportunity came, he was already "ready"—not ready with an idea, but ready with more than a decade of product craft.

所以真正的关键，不是“有没有人看见我”，而是“在被看见之前，我有没有把自己练到能被托付一件大事的程度”。

So the real question is not "does anyone see me," but "before I am seen, have I trained myself to the point where a great undertaking could be entrusted to me?"

微信不是一夜之间做出来的，它是一个在角落里坐了五年、写过十年免费软件的人，等来的那一次机会。

WeChat was not built overnight. It was the one chance that came to a man who had sat in a corner for five years and written free software for ten.

对我们这代人来说，也许最该学的不是“如何快速出头”，而是——

For our generation, perhaps the thing most worth learning is not "how to rise fast," but—

在你还没被看见的时候，别把自己荒废了。

While no one sees you yet, don't let yourself go to waste.

还有一个细节值得一说：微信上线时，市场上已经有一款先发的同类产品（小米的米聊）。微信并没有抢到“第一”的先手。它能反超，靠的不是更快，而是把产品打磨到“连不太会用智能手机的人也能上手”。这背后是张小龙那套很少被说出口的标准：如果一个功能需要说明书，那它其实已经失败了。 这大概就是一个“被边缘化”的人，用最笨的方式，做出来的最有力的东西。

One more detail is worth mentioning: when WeChat launched, a similar first-mover product already existed on the market (Xiaomi's MiTalk). WeChat did not grab the "first-mover" advantage. What let it overtake the field was not speed, but polishing the product until "even people who aren't very good with smartphones can pick it up." Behind that is a standard of Allen Zhang's that he rarely voiced aloud: if a feature needs an instruction manual, it has already failed. This is, perhaps, the most powerful thing a "marginalized" man built in the clumsiest possible way.
