Chapter 011 · Part 1

苏格拉底：被投票处死的“牛虻”

Socrates: The "Gadfly" Voted to Death

——他一辈子没写过一行字，却成了西方哲学的起点

—He never wrote a single line, yet he became the starting point of Western philosophy

在2026年的今天，说起苏格拉底，是美术馆里的石膏半身像，是《雅典学园》里那个指着天、和柏拉图争论的老头。可如果你真的走进公元前五世纪末的雅典集市，你会看到一个完全不同的人：一个相貌丑陋、穿着旧袍、赤着脚、整天在街上拉住别人抬杠的中年男人。他不种地，不经商，不顾家，还特别爱问那些让人下不来台的怪问题。

In 2026, when people speak of Socrates, they mean the plaster bust in an art museum, the old man in The School of Athens pointing at the sky and arguing with Plato. But if you actually walked into the Athenian marketplace at the end of the fifth century BC, you would meet a completely different person: an ugly middle-aged man in an old cloak, barefoot, who spent all day stopping people in the street to pick arguments with them. He did not farm, did not trade, did not look after his household, and had a particular fondness for asking the sort of awkward questions that leave people with nowhere to hide.

按照当时任何一个正常雅典人的标准，他都很难被归入“正经人”。

By the standards of any normal Athenian of the time, he was hard to classify as a "respectable person."

而正是这个“不正经的闲人”，成了整个西方哲学的起点——代价是，他被自己城邦的一场民主投票，判了死刑。

And it was precisely this "disrespectable idler" who became the starting point of all Western philosophy—at the cost of being sentenced to death by a democratic vote of his own city-state.

一、一个丑得出名的“闲人”

I. An Idler Famous for Being Ugly

苏格拉底约公元前470年生于雅典。父亲是石匠，母亲是助产婆。

Socrates was born in Athens around 470 BC. His father was a stonemason, his mother a midwife.

他的相貌，据柏拉图《会饮篇》里阿尔喀比亚德的描述，是有名的不好看：塌鼻、凸眼、挺着肚子。可他的身体却异常强健，据说冬天能赤脚行走，战时能忍饥耐寒。

His looks, as Alcibiades describes them in Plato's Symposium, were famously unattractive: flat nose, bulging eyes, and a protruding belly. Yet his body was remarkably strong; it was said he could walk barefoot in winter and endure hunger and cold in wartime.

他不办学校，不收学费，也不写东西——一个字都不写。他唯一的“事业”，就是走到人多的地方（集市、体育馆、街边），拉住人聊天。

He ran no school, charged no fees, and wrote nothing—not a single word. His only "career" was to go where the crowds were—the marketplace, the gymnasium, the street corner—and pull people into conversation.

他的妻子赞西佩以脾气暴躁闻名。家里穷，他不管；孩子他不管；生计他不管。用今天的眼光看，他大约是个“不工作、不顾家、只会抬杠”的中年失败者。

His wife, Xanthippe, was famous for her temper. The family was poor, and he paid it no mind; the children he paid no mind; the livelihood he paid no mind. By today's measure, he was roughly a middle-aged failure who "doesn't work, doesn't care for his family, and only knows how to argue."

二、神谕说他是“最聪明的人”，他却说“我只知道我一无所知”

II. The Oracle Called Him the "Wisest Man," Yet He Said, "I Only Know That I Know Nothing"

据柏拉图《申辩篇》记载，苏格拉底的朋友凯勒丰，曾去德尔斐的阿波罗神庙问神谕：有没有比苏格拉底更聪明的人？女祭司的回答是：没有。

According to Plato's Apology, Socrates' friend Chaerephon once went to the oracle at the Temple of Apollo in Delphi and asked whether there was anyone wiser than Socrates. The priestess answered: no.

苏格拉底听到这话，大为困惑。他的反应很反常：“我明明不聪明，神为什么要这么说？”

When Socrates heard this, he was greatly puzzled. His reaction was odd: "I am plainly not wise—why would the god say such a thing?"

于是他去了一趟“调查”：他去找那些公认聪明的人——政治家、诗人、工匠——逐个交谈。结果他发现，这些人其实并不真懂，却都自以为懂。

So he set out on an "investigation": he went to the people generally considered wise—politicians, poets, craftsmen—and talked with them one by one. What he found was that they did not really understand, yet each believed he did.

他由此悟出：神谕的意思也许是——人中最聪明的，恰恰是那个深知自己一无所知的人。

From this he concluded: perhaps the oracle meant that the wisest among men is precisely the one who knows deeply that he knows nothing.

从这以后，他形成了他那套著名的“助产术”式提问法：不灌输结论，只不断追问，逼着对方自己发现自己不知道。他母亲是助产婆，他便说自己是“精神的助产士”——知识不在我这儿，我只是帮你把它生出来。

From then on he developed his famous "midwifery" method of questioning: not to hand down conclusions, but to keep asking until the other person discovered for himself what he did not know. His mother had been a midwife, so he called himself a "midwife of the spirit"—knowledge is not mine to give; I only help you bring it to birth.

三、“牛虻”：雅典这匹懒马身上的那根刺

III. The "Gadfly": The Sting on Athens, That Lazy Horse

他把自己比作神派给雅典的一只“牛虻”——叮咬一匹又大又懒的马，让它别睡着的那根刺。

He compared himself to a "gadfly" sent by the god to Athens—the sting that bites a great, lazy horse to keep it from dozing off.

在《申辩篇》里，他说：这个城邦像一匹高头大马，因为太大、太懒，需要有只牛虻来叮它，才能保持清醒。雅典人受不了他，正是因为这根刺扎得又准又痛。

In the Apology he said: this city is like a great thoroughbred, and because it is so large and so lazy, it needs a gadfly to sting it to stay awake. The Athenians could not bear him precisely because the sting went in so accurately, and hurt so much.

他要教的东西，也不是天文地理，而是一个问题：一个人，该如何过好这一生。

What he wanted to teach was not astronomy or geography, but a single question: how should a person live a good life?

他留下过一句被引用了两千多年的话：

He left behind a line quoted for more than two thousand years:

“未经审视的人生，是不值得过的。”

"The unexamined life is not worth living."

四、公元前399年：一场死刑投票

IV. 399 BC: A Vote on the Death Penalty

雅典刚刚在伯罗奔尼撒战争中惨败给斯巴达，民主制一度被“三十僭主”的暴政推翻，全城惊魂未定。

Athens had just suffered a disastrous defeat by Sparta in the Peloponnesian War; the democracy had for a time been overthrown by the tyranny of the Thirty, and the whole city was still shaken.

而三十僭主的头目克里提亚斯，和那位毁誉参半的政治人物阿尔喀比亚德，都曾是苏格拉底身边来往密切的人。这成了他被人“清算”的祸根。

And the leader of the Thirty, Critias, along with the politically controversial Alcibiades, had both been close associates of Socrates. This became the root of the reckoning against him.

最终，三个人把他告上了法庭：美勒托、安尼图斯、吕孔。罪名是：不敬城邦所奉的神、引进新神、败坏青年。

In the end, three men brought him to court: Meletus, Anytus, and Lycon. The charges were: not acknowledging the gods the city acknowledged, introducing new deities, and corrupting the young.

审判由一个五百人的陪审团进行。第一轮投票，判他有罪。

The trial was conducted by a jury of five hundred. In the first round of voting, he was found guilty.

按雅典惯例，定罪后还有第二轮投票，被告可以自己提出一个较轻的刑罚（比如罚金、流放），求陪审团宽恕。朋友们劝他服个软、求个情——他偏不。他甚至在法庭上说：我做的是对城邦最有好处的事，你们真要奖励我，该请我吃城邦的荣誉餐才对。

By Athenian custom, after a conviction there was a second vote, in which the defendant could propose a lighter penalty for himself (a fine, exile) and beg the jury's mercy. His friends urged him to soften, to plead—he refused. He even said in court: what I do is the greatest good for the city; if you really wanted to reward me, you should give me the honor of dining at public expense.

这番话，彻底激怒了陪审团。第二轮投票，多数人主张：死刑。

Those words enraged the jury completely. In the second vote, the majority called for: death.

他本可以逃。朋友们连越狱的钱和路子都安排好了（见柏拉图《克里同篇》）。他拒绝了。理由是：我这一辈子都在守法，不能到老了，自己破坏它。

He could have escaped. His friends had arranged both the money and the route for a prison break (see Plato's Crito). He refused. His reason: all my life I have kept the law; I cannot, in my old age, be the one to break it.

公元前399年，苏格拉底在狱中饮下毒堇汁，从容而死，享年约七十岁。

In 399 BC, Socrates drank the hemlock in prison and died calmly, at about the age of seventy.

五、他凭什么成了“失败者”的代表

V. Why He Became the Emblem of the "Failure"

苏格拉底的一生，用任何世俗标准衡量，都谈不上成功：

Measured by any worldly standard, Socrates' life could hardly be called a success:

他没写过一个字，没留下一部著作；

He wrote not a single word, left behind no book;

他没当过官（只轮值担任过一次议事会主席）；

He never held office (he served only once, by rotation, as presiding officer of the Council);

他没能挽救自己的城邦（雅典先败于斯巴达，又杀了他）;

He could not save his city-state (Athens first lost to Sparta, then killed him);

他最后被自己城邦的民主程序，投票判了死刑。

In the end he was sentenced to death by vote, through the democratic procedure of his own city-state.

在雅典人眼里，他就是那个“把年轻人带坏的人”。

In the eyes of the Athenians, he was simply "the man who corrupted the young."

可两千五百年过去，那个判他死刑的城邦，我们今天几乎只记得一件事——它杀死了苏格拉底。而他教出了柏拉图，柏拉图教出了亚里士多德，这一条线，撑起了整个西方思想史。

Yet two and a half thousand years later, of that city-state which sentenced him to death, we today remember almost only one thing—that it killed Socrates. And he taught Plato, Plato taught Aristotle, and this single line of succession held up the whole history of Western thought.

六、结语：致2026年，每一个“明知徒劳还去追问”的人

VI. Conclusion: To Every Person in 2026 Who Keeps Asking, Knowing It Is Futile

2026年，我们被算法投喂，被舆论裹挟，被一堆“标准答案”规训着。很多人不再问“为什么”——因为问了显得不合群、不成熟、不识趣。你只要照做就行。

In 2026, we are fed by algorithms, swept along by public opinion, disciplined by a pile of "standard answers." Many people no longer ask "why"—because asking makes you look like an outsider, immature, a killjoy. You just do as you're told.

苏格拉底是一只牛虻。牛虻不被马喜欢，但马需要它。

Socrates was a gadfly. Horses do not like gadflies, but horses need them.

他问的那些问题，当时没人给得出答案；他自己也说“我不知道”。可正是这些“没有答案的追问”，成了后来一切学问的起点。

The questions he asked had no answers then; he himself said, "I don't know." Yet it was precisely these "questions without answers" that became the starting point of all later learning.

他被投票处死了。但那场投票，只决定了当时；而追问这件事，从那一刻起，就再也没有停下来过。

He was voted to death. But that vote decided only that moment; and the asking of questions has never stopped since.

所谓失败，不过是那一刻的判决。而一个人愿意在所有人都沉默的时候，继续问下去——这大概是唯一不会失败的事。

So-called failure is nothing but the verdict of a single moment. And for a person to be willing, when everyone else is silent, to keep asking—that is perhaps the only thing that cannot fail.

Appendix: Sources and Notes

主要史料

Primary Sources

1. 柏拉图《申辩篇》《克里同篇》《斐多篇》《会饮篇》——苏格拉底审判、狱中拒逃、临终及相貌描述的主要来源。

1. Plato, Apology, Crito, Phaedo, Symposium—the principal source for Socrates' trial, his refusal to escape from prison, his final hours, and the description of his appearance.

2. 色诺芬《回忆苏格拉底》《申辩》——另一位学生的平行记载，风格更朴素。

2. Xenophon, Memorabilia and Apology—a parallel account by another pupil, plainer in style.

3. 阿里斯托芬喜剧《云》（前423年）——把苏格拉底写成“思想所”里的诡辩家；注意：这是比审判早二十四年的喜剧夸张，却反映了当时公众对苏格拉底的负面印象。

3. Aristophanes' comedy The Clouds (423 BC)—it portrays Socrates as a sophist in a "thinkery"; note that this comic exaggeration predates the trial by twenty-four years, yet it reflects the negative public impression of Socrates at the time.

4. 第欧根尼·拉尔修《名哲言行录》——后世汇编，含生平轶事，需甄别。

4. Diogenes Laertius, Lives and Opinions of Eminent Philosophers—a later compilation containing biographical anecdotes, to be used with caution.

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

5. 约生于公元前470年（一说前469年），卒于公元前399年。前399年因“不敬神”与“败坏青年”被五百人陪审团判罪，饮毒堇汁而死。

5. Born around 470 BC (some say 469 BC), died 399 BC. In 399 BC, convicted by a jury of five hundred on charges of "impiety" and "corrupting the young," and put to death by drinking hemlock.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

6. “苏格拉底问题”：苏格拉底本人无著作，一切形象皆出自柏拉图、色诺芬、阿里斯托芬的记述，三者差异颇大；柏拉图笔下的苏格拉底有多少是“历史上的苏格拉底”，学界至今争论。

6. The "Socratic problem": Socrates himself wrote nothing, so every image of him derives from the accounts of Plato, Xenophon, and Aristophanes, which differ considerably; how much of Plato's Socrates is "the historical Socrates" is still debated among scholars.

7. 本文不给出“定罪票数”的具体数字——柏拉图和色诺芬均未留下确切票数，后世流传的数字可靠性存疑。

7. This chapter gives no specific figure for the "conviction vote"—neither Plato nor Xenophon left an exact count, and the numbers that have come down to us are of doubtful reliability.
