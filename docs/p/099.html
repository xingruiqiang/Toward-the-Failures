郭永怀：飞机坠毁前，他和警卫员抱在了一起

Guo Yonghuai: Before the Plane Crashed, He and His Guard Held Each Other

——“如果我能把所学的知识用来报效祖国，那才是我最大的光荣。”

— “If I can use the knowledge I have learned to serve my country, that will be my greatest honor.”

2026年，“两弹一星”元勋的名字，大多已被人们熟知。但在这些名字里，有一个人格外特殊——他是唯一一位在核弹、导弹、人造卫星三个领域都做出重要贡献的科学家。

In 2026, most of the names of the founding fathers of the “Two Bombs, One Satellite” program are widely known. But among these names, one man stands out as especially unusual: he is the only scientist to have made important contributions in all three fields—nuclear weapons, missiles, and artificial satellites.

他叫郭永怀。

His name was Guo Yonghuai.

1968年12月5日，他乘坐的飞机在北京坠毁。搜救人员在残骸中，发现了两具紧紧抱在一起的遗体：一具是他，一具是他的警卫员。他们中间，护着一个装有绝密数据的公文包。 公文包完好无损。

On December 5, 1968, the plane he was aboard crashed in Beijing. Among the wreckage, rescue workers found two bodies locked in a tight embrace: one was him, the other his guard. Between them they had shielded a briefcase containing top-secret data. The briefcase was intact.

一、从山东农村到加州理工

I. From a Shandong Village to Caltech

1909年4月4日，郭永怀出生在山东荣成的一个农民家庭。

Guo Yonghuai was born on April 4, 1909, to a farming family in Rongcheng, Shandong.

他靠着自己的努力，一路读了出来。1935年，他从北京大学物理系毕业。1940年，他远渡重洋，先到加拿大多伦多大学，1941年获得硕士学位，随后进入美国加州理工学院。

Through his own efforts, he studied his way up, step by step. In 1935 he graduated from the Department of Physics at Peking University. In 1940 he crossed the ocean, going first to the University of Toronto in Canada, where he received his master's degree in 1941, and then to the California Institute of Technology in the United States.

在那里，他的老师是世界著名的空气动力学家冯·卡门。他的同学和合作者中，有一位后来同样大名鼎鼎的人——钱学森。

There, his teacher was the world-renowned aerodynamicist Theodore von Kármán. Among his classmates and collaborators was a man who would become equally famous—Qian Xuesen.

郭永怀和钱学森合作，在跨声速空气动力学领域做出了重要成果，提出了“上临界马赫数”的概念。 这个成果，对后来的高速飞行器设计有重要意义。

Working with Qian Xuesen, Guo Yonghuai achieved important results in the field of transonic aerodynamics, proposing the concept of the “upper critical Mach number.” These results would carry great significance for the later design of high-speed aircraft.

1946年，他成为美国康奈尔大学的教授。他在美国的生活，是安稳而体面的。

In 1946 he became a professor at Cornell University. His life in the United States was secure and comfortable.

二、一次特殊的路

II. An Unusual Road

1956年，郭永怀决定回国。

In 1956, Guo Yonghuai decided to return to China.

那个年代，从美国回中国，不是一件容易的事。当时美国对涉及敏感领域的中国学者有诸多限制。为了顺利回国，郭永怀做了一件今天看来很有勇气的事：他把自己多年积累的研究手稿，一把火烧掉了。

In those years, going back to China from the United States was not an easy thing. At the time, the United States placed many restrictions on Chinese scholars working in sensitive fields. To get home without trouble, Guo Yonghuai did something that today looks like a considerable act of courage: he burned all the research manuscripts he had accumulated over the years.

他不想在途中被搜查、被扣留，也不想给别人留下借口。一个人愿意烧掉自己半生的心血，只为了能够回到自己的国家。

He did not want to be searched or detained along the way, and he did not want to give anyone a pretext. A man was willing to burn the work of half a lifetime, just so that he could return to his own country.

回国后，他担任中科院力学研究所的副所长，并参与组建中国科学技术大学的相关院系。

After returning, he became deputy director of the Institute of Mechanics at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, and took part in establishing the relevant departments at the University of Science and Technology of China.

三、他参加了三件大事

III. He Took Part in Three Great Undertakings

郭永怀回国后，投入到中国最关键的几项国防科研工作中。

After he came home, Guo Yonghuai threw himself into some of China's most critical defense research programs.

第一是核武器。 他参与了中国原子弹和氢弹的研制，在力学、结构、爆炸力学等关键问题上，提供了重要的理论支持。

The first was nuclear weapons. He took part in the development of China's atomic and hydrogen bombs, providing important theoretical support on key problems in mechanics, structures, and explosion mechanics.

第二是导弹。 他在空气动力学和飞行力学方面的专长，直接服务于中国的导弹研制。

The second was missiles. His expertise in aerodynamics and flight mechanics served China's missile development directly.

第三是人造卫星。 他参与了相关项目的论证和前期工作。

The third was artificial satellites. He took part in the feasibility studies and early work on the relevant projects.

在“两弹一星”的全部领域里，他是极少见的“全都沾、全都贡献”的人。 这不是因为他“什么都懂”，而是因为——那个年代，中国的科学家太少，能扛事的人更少。一个人，往往要顶几个人用。

In every area of the “Two Bombs, One Satellite” program, he was one of the rare people who “touched all of them and contributed to all of them.” This was not because he “knew everything,” but because in that era China had too few scientists, and even fewer who could carry responsibility. One person often had to do the work of several.

四、1968年12月5日

IV. December 5, 1968

1968年12月5日，郭永怀在青海执行任务后，乘飞机返回北京。

On December 5, 1968, after carrying out a mission in Qinghai, Guo Yonghuai boarded a plane back to Beijing.

飞机在北京机场附近坠毁。机上人员大部分遇难。

The plane crashed near Beijing's airport. Most of those aboard were killed.

当搜救人员赶到现场时，他们看到了一个让他们终生难忘的场景： 两具遗体紧紧抱在一起，已经烧焦，难以分开。人们用力把他们分开后，发现他们中间护着一个公文包。

When the rescue workers reached the site, they saw a scene they would never forget: two bodies gripped in a tight embrace, already charred, so hard to pull apart that it took force. When they were finally separated, a briefcase was found shielded between them.

公文包里，是刚刚获得的、关于热核导弹试验的绝密数据。 因为被两个人的身体护住，公文包里的文件完好无损。

Inside the briefcase were top-secret data on a thermonuclear missile test, just obtained. Because the two men's bodies had shielded it, the documents inside were completely intact.

和他抱在一起的，是他的警卫员牟方东。这两个人，在生命最后的几秒钟里，用一个拥抱，保住了那些数据。

The man holding him was his guard, Mou Fangdong. In the last few seconds of their lives, these two people saved those data with a single embrace.

这个细节，后来成为中国科学史上最沉重、也最动人的一幕。

This detail would become the heaviest and most moving scene in the history of Chinese science.

1968年12月27日，郭永怀被追认为革命烈士。1999年，他被追授“两弹一星功勋奖章”。

On December 27, 1968, Guo Yonghuai was posthumously recognized as a revolutionary martyr. In 1999 he was posthumously awarded the “Two Bombs, One Satellite” Meritorious Medal.

五、那一代人的“选择”

V. The “Choice” of That Generation

郭永怀的故事，如果只是“壮烈牺牲”，反而会把人看浅。

If Guo Yonghuai's story were only about a “heroic sacrifice,” we would end up seeing him too shallowly.

真正值得想一想的，是他为什么会走到那架飞机上。

What is really worth thinking about is why he came to be on that plane at all.

他本可以在美国做他的教授，过体面的日子。他选择了回国，为此烧掉了自己的手稿。回国后，他一头扎进那些不能对家人说的工作里，常年奔波在戈壁、高原和试验场之间。

He could have stayed in the United States as a professor and lived a comfortable life. He chose to return home, and burned his manuscripts for it. After returning, he plunged into work he could not speak of to his family, spending years on the move between the Gobi, the high plateaus, and the test sites.

1968年那架飞机上的他，已经是五十九岁的老人。 他完全可以在北京待着，让别人去跑。但他没有。

The man on that plane in 1968 was already fifty-nine years old. He could easily have stayed in Beijing and let others make the trip. But he did not.

那一代科学家身上，有一种今天很难复制的逻辑：他们不是在“选择一份工作”，而是在“选择一件事，然后把自己交出去”。 这种“交出去”的程度，包括时间、健康、家庭生活，最后，也包括生命。

That generation of scientists carried a logic that is hard to reproduce today: they were not “choosing a job,” they were “choosing a cause, and then giving themselves over to it.” The degree of that “giving over” included time, health, family life—and in the end, life itself.

六、他留下的东西

VI. What He Left Behind

郭永怀最直接的贡献，是中国自己的核力量与导弹能力。 在一个曾经被核讹诈的年代，这些东西意味着国家的安全底线。

Guo Yonghuai's most direct contribution was China's own nuclear force and missile capability. In an age when the country had once been threatened with nuclear blackmail, these things were the baseline of the nation's security.

但更深的，是他和那一代人共同留下的东西：在最困难的条件下，中国也可以做出世界级的事情。

But deeper still is what he and his generation left behind together: proof that under the hardest conditions, China could also do world-class work.

他没有留下回忆录，没有留下大量的公开言论。他知道，他做的事不能公开。他留下的，是一个公文包，两具抱在一起的遗体，和一份“完好无损”的绝密数据。

He left no memoir, no large body of public statements. He knew that what he did could not be made public. What he left was a briefcase, two bodies in an embrace, and a set of top-secret data “intact and undamaged.”

这些，就是全部了。

That is all of it.

他的妻子李佩，后来也一直留在力学所工作。有人说，郭永怀这一代人，把一生拆成了三份：一份给了国家，一份给了科学，还有一份——远远不够——才留给家人。剩下的，交给了一个公文包。

His wife, Li Pei, would go on working at the Institute of Mechanics for the rest of her life. Some say that Guo Yonghuai and his generation divided their lives into three parts: one part given to the country, one part to science, and one part—far too small—left for their families. What remained was handed over to a briefcase.

七、写给2026年的我们

VII. To Us in 2026

郭永怀在生命最后的那一刻，做了一个选择：抱住那个包。

In the last moment of his life, Guo Yonghuai made a choice: he held on to that briefcase.

这个选择不是临时做的。它是他回国那天烧掉手稿时、是他一次次奔赴试验场时、是他五十九岁还坐上那架飞机时，早就“预演”过无数遍的。

This choice was not made on the spur of the moment. He had “rehearsed” it countless times already—on the day he decided to return, when he burned his manuscripts; when he set off for the test sites again and again; when, at fifty-nine, he still boarded that plane.

一个人平时怎么做事，决定了他最后一刻会怎么选。

How a person does things in ordinary times decides how he will choose in his final moment.

2026年的我们，很少会遇到那种“最后一秒”的抉择。但我们每天都在做小的选择：这件事我是认真做还是敷衍过去，这个责任我是担起来还是往别人身上推。

We in 2026 rarely face that kind of “last-second” choice. But every day we make small ones: whether to do this thing carefully or just go through the motions, whether to take on this responsibility or push it onto someone else.

郭永怀提醒我们：一个人真正的品格，是由他平时做的无数个不起眼的选择，一点点堆出来的。 到了那个决定性的时刻，你不会有时间思考，你只会按照你平时的样子去做。

Guo Yonghuai reminds us: a person's true character is built up, little by little, out of the countless unremarkable choices he makes in ordinary life. When the decisive moment comes, you will have no time to think; you will simply act the way you always have.

他抱住了那个包。因为他这一辈子，就是这样一个人。

He held on to that briefcase. Because his whole life, that was the kind of person he was.

也正因如此，他没有“最后一刻的英雄行为”——他只是在最后一刻，做了他本来就会做的事。 这大概就是这本书想说的：真正的品格，不是偶尔的高光，而是无数个日常选择积累起来的一种“默认反应”。郭永怀的默认反应，是护住那份数据；护住那份数据的那个动作，就是他一生的浓缩。

And precisely for that reason, he had no “last-minute act of heroism”—in the final moment he simply did what he would always have done. This is probably what this book wants to say: true character is not an occasional highlight, but a kind of “default response” accumulated out of countless everyday choices. Guo Yonghuai's default response was to shield those data; and that act of shielding them was his whole life condensed into a single movement.

1999年，当“两弹一星功勋奖章”被追授给他时，他已经离开这个世界三十一年了。三十一年里，中国从一穷二白，走到了拥有完整国防体系。这份沉甸甸的“今天”，地基里，就有他当年抱在怀里、用身体护住的那份数据。 历史不是靠某一个人推动的，但有些人的名字，你一旦记住，就再也没法把他们从这条线上划掉。

In 1999, when the “Two Bombs, One Satellite” Meritorious Medal was posthumously awarded to him, he had already been gone from this world for thirty-one years. In those thirty-one years, China went from utter poverty to possessing a complete national defense system. In the foundation of this weighty “today” lies that set of data he once held in his arms and shielded with his body. History is not moved forward by any single person, but some names, once you remember them, can never again be struck from that line.

他去世后，国际小行星中心把一颗小行星命名为“郭永怀星”。在天空中，他有了一个永远不会熄灭的位置。而在人间，他留下的，是一个关于“一个人最后的动作”的回答——那个动作不是挥手，不是逃开，而是抱住一件比自己更重要、也更需要被交出去的东西。

After his death, the Minor Planet Center named an asteroid the “Guo Yonghuai Star.” In the sky he has a place that will never go dark. And here on earth, what he left is an answer to the question of “a person's final action”—an action that was not a wave, not a flight, but an embrace around something more important than himself, something that needed even more to be handed on.
