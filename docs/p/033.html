杜甫：被秋风吹破茅屋的“诗圣”

Du Fu: The “Poet Sage” Whose Thatched Hut Was Broken by the Autumn Wind

——他生前不是“诗圣”，只是个到处投奔、总在赶路的倒霉人

—In his lifetime he was no “Poet Sage,” only an unlucky man forever seeking shelter, forever on the road

2026年，“诗人”这个身份在很多人眼里已经有点远了。但在中国，有一个人的诗句，被反复抄在课本上、印在春联里、挂在墙上——“会当凌绝顶”“安得广厦千万间”“润物细无声”。

In 2026, the identity of “poet” already feels a little remote to many people. Yet in China there is one man whose lines are copied again and again into textbooks, printed on Spring Festival couplets, hung on walls—“I shall climb to the very summit,” “Could I but get ten thousand spacious mansions,” “It moistens all things, softly, without a sound.”

这个人，被后世称为“诗圣”。可他自己活着的时候，是一个连家都安不下、孩子被饿死、四处求人接济、最后病死在一条小船上的中年男人。

This man is remembered by later ages as the “Poet Sage.” But while he was alive, he was a middle-aged man who could not keep a home together, whose child starved to death, who went everywhere begging others for help, and who in the end died of illness aboard a small boat.

他叫杜甫。他的“失败”，整整持续了一生，一直到他死后几十年，才被后世重新发现。

His name was Du Fu. His “failure” lasted an entire lifetime, and only decades after his death was he rediscovered by later generations.

一、一个屡考不中的“读书人”

I. A “Scholar” Who Failed the Examinations Again and Again

712年，杜甫出生在河南巩县，一个世代做官的家庭。他年轻时候其实很“野”：二十来岁游历吴越、齐赵，纵马射猎，写过“放荡齐赵间，裘马颇清狂”这样豪气的句子。

In 712, Du Fu was born in Gongxian, Henan, into a family that had produced officials for generations. In his youth he was in fact quite “wild”: in his twenties he traveled through Wu and Yue, through Qi and Zhao, galloping on horseback and hunting with bow and arrow, and he wrote such spirited lines as “Carefree among Qi and Zhao, in furs and on fine horses, light and reckless of heart.”

他也想做官。735年，他二十四岁，去洛阳考进士，落榜。此后他一直在长安一带活动，写诗投给权贵，希望有人举荐。

He too wanted to be an official. In 735, at the age of twenty-four, he went to Luoyang to sit the jinshi examination and failed. After that he stayed mostly in the region of Chang’an, writing poems to present to the powerful, hoping someone would recommend him.

747年，唐玄宗下诏，让天下有才能的人进京考试。杜甫满怀希望地去了。结果那一届一个人都没录取——当权宰相李林甫向皇帝报告说，这是“野无遗贤”，意思是所有贤才早就被朝廷收尽了，民间一个都没有。

In 747, Emperor Xuanzong issued an edict summoning all the talented men of the realm to the capital for examination. Du Fu went, full of hope. In the end, not a single person was taken that year—the chancellor then in power, Li Linfu, reported to the emperor that “no worthy talent had been left in the wilds,” meaning that the court had long since gathered up every last worthy man and not one remained among the common people.

杜甫就这样被“零录取”了一次。

And so Du Fu was, for once, part of an examination with “zero admissions.”

直到755年，四十四岁的他才得到一个职位：右卫率府兵曹参军——一个管钥匙、看兵甲的小官。这年冬天，他去奉先县探家，一路走一路想。可他推开家门听到的，是自己的小儿子已经被饿死了。

It was not until 755, when he was forty-four, that he won a post: Adjutant of the Right Guards’ Command—a minor official in charge of keys and of keeping watch over armor and weapons. That winter he went home to Fengxian County to visit his family, thinking all the way along the road. But when he pushed open the door, what he heard was that his youngest son had already starved to death.

他把这件事写进了诗里。一个父亲，在诗里写自己孩子饿死——这种痛，后来构成了杜甫全部作品最深的底色。

He wrote this into his poetry. A father, writing in a poem that his own child had starved to death—that pain would later form the deepest undertone of all Du Fu’s work.

二、被时代碾过的人

II. A Man Crushed by His Age

刚当上小官没多久，安史之乱爆发（755年）。

Not long after he took up his minor post, the An–Shi Rebellion broke out (755).

潼关失守，长安沦陷。杜甫在逃难途中被叛军抓住，困在长安。他看见的是“国破山河在，城春草木深”——长安城的春天还在，可一切都变了。

Tong Pass fell, and Chang’an was taken. Fleeing the chaos, Du Fu was seized by the rebel army and trapped in Chang’an. What he saw was “The state is shattered, yet the hills and rivers remain; spring comes to the city, and the grass grows deep”—the spring of Chang’an was still there, but everything had changed.

后来他冒险逃到凤翔，见了新即位的唐肃宗，被授为“左拾遗”——一个可以给皇帝提意见的谏官。这是他一生离权力最近的一次。可他很快就因为替一位获罪的宰相说话，触怒了皇帝，被贬到华州去当司功参军。

Later he risked his life to flee to Fengxiang, where he met the newly enthroned Emperor Suzong and was appointed “Left Reminder”—a remonstrating official who could offer the emperor advice. It was the closest he ever came to power in his life. But he soon offended the emperor by speaking up for a chancellor who had been condemned, and was demoted to Huazhou to serve as Adjutant of Merit.

759年，他彻底失望了，辞掉官职，带着家人往西走。他先在秦州、同谷一带流浪，穷到靠挖野菜、捡橡子活命。年末，他终于到了成都。

In 759, utterly disillusioned, he resigned his office and headed west with his family. He wandered first around Qinzhou and Tonggu, so poor that he survived by digging wild herbs and gathering acorns. At the end of the year he finally reached Chengdu.

三、成都草堂：一生中最安稳的几年

III. The Chengdu Thatched Cottage: His Most Peaceful Years

760年，在朋友的帮助下，杜甫在成都西郊的浣花溪边，盖起了一座茅草屋——这就是后来天下闻名的“杜甫草堂”。

In 760, with the help of friends, Du Fu built a thatched hut beside the Huanhua Stream on the western outskirts of Chengdu—this was the later world-famous “Du Fu Thatched Cottage.”

这是他一生中难得的安稳时光。他种地、种药、写诗。《春夜喜雨》《江畔独步寻花》这些看起来清亮的诗，多写于此时。

These were the rare peaceful years of his life. He farmed, planted medicinal herbs, wrote poems. Such seemingly bright and clear poems as “Happy Rain on a Spring Night” and “Walking Alone by the Stream to Admire the Flowers” were mostly written at this time.

但安稳很短暂。761年秋天，一场大风把他的茅屋吹破，屋漏如麻，孩子在雨夜里冻得睡不着。他在那一夜写下了那首著名的《茅屋为秋风所破歌》——最动人的不是他抱怨自己的屋顶，而是他最后喊出的一句：

But the peace was short-lived. In the autumn of 761, a great wind tore his thatched roof apart, the leaks came thick as hemp, and the children were too cold to sleep through the rainy night. That night he wrote the famous “Song of My Thatched Hut Broken by the Autumn Wind”—and the most moving part is not his complaint about his own roof, but the line he cried out at the end:

安得广厦千万间，大庇天下寒士俱欢颜。

“Could I but get ten thousand spacious mansions, to shelter all the poor scholars under heaven and make them all smile!”

他自己的屋顶破了，他想到的却是天下所有没有房子的人。这一句，把他的整个人格立住了。他不是一个只会哭自己苦的人——他把自己的苦，变成了对别人的疼。

His own roof was broken, yet what he thought of was everyone under heaven without a house. That one line establishes his whole character. He was not a man who could only weep over his own suffering—he turned his own suffering into tenderness for others.

四、最后十年，一直在路上

IV. The Last Ten Years: Always on the Road

764年，他的好友严武镇守成都，举荐他做了“检校工部员外郎”——后人称他“杜工部”，就是从这里来的。

In 764, his good friend Yan Wu, then commanding Chengdu, recommended him for the post of “Supernumerary Director of the Ministry of Works”—it is from this that later ages called him “Du of the Ministry of Works.”

可765年严武死后，杜甫失去了依靠，只能再次带着家人离开成都。此后五年，他漂泊在夔州、江陵、潭州一带，身体越来越差，日子越来越难。

But when Yan Wu died in 765, Du Fu lost his support and had to leave Chengdu again with his family. For the next five years he drifted about Kuizhou, Jiangling, and Tanzhou, his health growing steadily worse and his days steadily harder.

770年冬天，他在从潭州（今长沙）往岳阳去的湘江小船上，病死了，享年五十九岁。

In the winter of 770, aboard a small boat on the Xiang River, on the way from Tanzhou (present-day Changsha) toward Yueyang, he died of illness, at the age of fifty-nine.

他没能回到他心心念念的长安，也没能回到他略得安稳的成都。

He never made it back to the Chang’an he longed for, nor back to the Chengdu where he had found a little peace.

五、“百年歌自苦，未见有知音”

V. “A Lifetime of Songs, All Bitter; No Kindred Soul Have I Met”

杜甫活着的时候，诗名其实并不大。

While Du Fu was alive, his poetic reputation was in fact not great.

当时的诗坛，最红的是李白、王维这些人。杜甫的诗，被认为又苦、又慢、又沉重。他自己也清楚这一点。他在诗里写过一句极孤独的话：

In the poetry circles of the time, the brightest stars were Li Bai, Wang Wei, and their like. Du Fu’s poems were thought bitter, slow, and heavy. He himself knew this clearly. In one poem he wrote a line of utter loneliness:

百年歌自苦，未见有知音。

“A hundred years of singing, all my own sorrow; no kindred soul have I met.”

意思很清楚：我写了一辈子，一个真正懂我的人都还没遇到。

The meaning is plain: I have written all my life, and I have still not met a single person who truly understood me.

可历史跟他开了一个巨大的玩笑。他死后，从唐代中后期开始，白居易、元稹、韩愈一批人开始极力推崇他；到了宋代，他被正式推上“诗圣”的位置。他那些当年“没人读”的诗，成了中国文学史上最被反复研究的文本之一。“诗史”这个称号，也只给了他一个人。

But history played an enormous joke on him. After his death, beginning in the mid-to-late Tang, a group of men—Bai Juyi, Yuan Zhen, Han Yu—began to champion him with all their might; by the Song dynasty he was formally raised to the position of “Poet Sage.” Those poems that “no one read” in their day became one of the most repeatedly studied bodies of text in the history of Chinese literature. The title “Poet Historian,” too, was given to him and to no one else.

六、致2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

杜甫的一生，几乎把“世俗意义的失败”凑齐了：考试落榜、做官被贬、孩子饿死、一生漂泊、晚年病死异乡。

Du Fu’s life assembled almost every item on the list of “failure in the worldly sense”: he failed the examinations, was demoted in office, lost a child to starvation, wandered his whole life, and died of illness in a foreign land in his old age.

但他留下了一个不一样的答案。

But he left behind a different kind of answer.

第一，他从不把自己写成一个受害者。他确实苦，可他写诗时不只写“我苦”，他写的是“我们苦”——是士兵、是农夫、是被征去当兵的老妇人、是天下所有没房住的人。他把个人的痛，换算成了共情的能力。这是把“失败”变成“养分”的方式。

First, he never wrote himself as a victim. He did indeed suffer, but when he wrote poems he did not write only “I suffer”—he wrote “we suffer.” Of soldiers, of farmers, of an old woman conscripted into the army, of everyone under heaven with no roof over their heads. He converted his personal pain into a capacity for empathy. This is how he turned “failure” into “nourishment.”

第二，他的价值，是在他死后才被认出来的。这不是安慰，是事实。他用自己的例子告诉我们：一个人的作品能走多远，和他生前红不红，可以是两件事。

Second, his worth was recognized only after his death. This is not a consolation; it is a fact. By his own example he tells us: how far a person’s work can travel, and whether he was famous in his lifetime, can be two entirely different things.

所以，如果你此刻正在过一段很窄的日子——工作不顺、没人认可、看不到出路——请记住成都西郊那间被秋风吹破的茅屋。那个破了屋顶的人，在一个漏雨的夜里，想的不是自己，而是“天下寒士”。

So if you are right now going through a narrow stretch of days—work not going well, no one giving you credit, no way out in sight—remember that thatched hut on the western outskirts of Chengdu that was torn open by the autumn wind. The man whose roof was broken, on a night of leaking rain, was not thinking of himself, but of “all the poor scholars under heaven.”

他输了整整一辈子，却赢过了几乎所有人。

He lost for an entire lifetime, and yet he outlasted almost everyone.

Appendix: Sources and Notes

2. 《旧唐书》《新唐书》中的杜甫传记。

2. The biographies of Du Fu in the Jiu Tangshu (Old Book of Tang) and the Xin Tangshu (New Book of Tang).

3. 元稹《唐故工部员外郎杜君墓系铭》等对其诗歌地位的最早高度评价。

3. Yuan Zhen’s “Epitaph for the Late Mr. Du, Supernumerary Director of the Ministry of Works of the Tang,” and other early high appraisals of his poetic standing.

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

4. 生于712年（河南巩县），卒于770年冬（湘江舟中，一说潭州至岳阳途中），享年59岁。

4. Born 712 (Gongxian, Henan); died in the winter of 770 (aboard a boat on the Xiang River; one account says on the way from Tanzhou to Yueyang), aged 59.

5. 735年考进士落第；747年应制举（因李林甫操纵而“零录取”）；755年任右卫率府兵曹参军；757年任左拾遗，后贬华州；759年弃官西行；760年建成都草堂；765年离蜀漂泊。

5. Failed the jinshi examination in 735; sat the imperially summoned examination in 747 (from which, through Li Linfu’s manipulation, “not a single candidate was taken”); appointed Adjutant of the Right Guards’ Command in 755; appointed Left Reminder in 757, later demoted to Huazhou; resigned office and went west in 759; built the Chengdu thatched cottage in 760; left Shu to wander in 765.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

6. “杜甫之子被饿死”——见其《自京赴奉先县咏怀五百字》“所愧为人父，无食致夭折”等句，是其自述，属可信的一手材料；但具体时间、年龄等细节无法完全确证，文中按自述呈现。

6. “Du Fu’s son starved to death”—see his “Five Hundred Words of Reflection on the Road from the Capital to Fengxian,” with lines such as “Ashamed to be a father, unable to feed him, so he died young.” This is his own account, a credible first-hand source; but the exact time, the child’s age, and other details cannot be fully confirmed, and are presented in the text as he himself related them.

7. “李林甫'野无遗贤'致本次考试零录取”——见《资治通鉴》相关记载，是史书所载事件；但将杜甫个人落第完全归因于这一事件，属后人（含杜甫自述）的关联解释，文中表述为“就这样被'零录取'了一次”。

7. “Li Linfu’s ‘no worthy talent left in the wilds’ caused the examination to take no one”—see the relevant record in the Zizhi Tongjian (Comprehensive Mirror for Aid in Government); this is an event recorded in the histories. But attributing Du Fu’s personal failure entirely to it is a later connecting interpretation (including Du Fu’s own), and the text simply states that “and so he was, for once, part of an examination with ‘zero admissions.’”

8. “杜甫生前诗名不显”——这是学界主流判断（其诗在他生前未获广泛推崇），但并非“完全无人知”，当时已有少数人称赏。文中已用“诗名并不大”“一个真正懂我的人都还没遇到”等相对表述，未绝对化。

8. “Du Fu’s poetic fame was not prominent in his lifetime”—this is the mainstream scholarly judgment (his poetry did not win wide admiration before his death), but it was not “entirely unknown”; a few people did appreciate him at the time. The text uses relative wording such as “his poetic reputation was not great” and “I have still not met a single person who truly understood me,” without making it absolute.

9. “诗史”“诗圣”的称号——均为后世（唐代中后期至宋代）所加，非其生前自称，文中已明确。

9. The titles “Poet Historian” and “Poet Sage”—both were conferred by later ages (from the mid-to-late Tang through the Song) and were not self-adopted in his lifetime; the text makes this clear.
