林肯：破产、失恋、连输八场，51岁才当上总统

Lincoln: Bankrupt, Brokenhearted, Eight Losses in a Row—President at Fifty-One

——他一生大部分时间，是个标准的“loser”

— For most of his life, he was a textbook "loser"

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者24 / 100约 2727 字 · 2727 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，如果你打开任何一个求职或创业论坛，看到最多的就是一串问题：我35岁了还没成事，是不是废了？我第三次创业又失败了，还要不要再来一次？

In 2026, open any job-hunting or startup forum and the questions you see most are a string of the same ones: I'm thirty-five and still haven't made it—am I finished? I failed at my third startup—should I try once more?

如果这些问题有个历史答案，那个答案很可能长着亚伯拉罕·林肯的脸。

If these questions have a historical answer, that answer very likely wears the face of Abraham Lincoln.

在成为美国历史上最受敬仰的总统之前，林肯的前半生几乎写满了失败：经商破产、竞选连任失败、未婚妻去世、精神崩溃卧床半年、连续多次落选公职。他51岁才当选总统，当了四年多就被暗杀。可就是这个人，在最黑暗的时刻守住了美国。

Before he became the most admired president in American history, Lincoln's first half of life was almost a full page of failure: a business that went bankrupt, a lost bid for reelection, a fiancée who died, a breakdown that kept him in bed for six months, and one defeat after another in races for public office. He was fifty-one when he was elected president, and a little more than four years later he was assassinated. And yet it was this man who held America together in its darkest hour.

他的故事说明一件事：一个人的“履历”，和他能不能做成事，往往没有关系。

His story makes one thing clear: a person's "résumé" and his ability to get things done often have nothing to do with each other.

一、木屋里长大的孩子

I. A Child Raised in a Log Cabin

1809年2月12日，林肯出生在肯塔基州一间小木屋里。父亲是贫苦农民，母亲几乎是文盲。他一生接受的正规教育，加起来不到一年。

On February 12, 1809, Lincoln was born in a small log cabin in Kentucky. His father was a poor farmer, his mother all but illiterate. His formal education over his whole life added up to less than a year.

买不起算术书，他就借来一本，用信纸大小的纸片一行行抄下来，再用麻线缝合，做成自己的“课本”。他做过船员、土地测绘员、杂货店员、邮局职员，什么活都干过。开杂货店的时候，趁着没顾客，他就埋头看法律书，硬是把自己读成了伊利诺伊州的执业律师。

Unable to afford an arithmetic book, he borrowed one, copied it out line by line on sheets the size of letter paper, sewed them together with hemp thread, and made himself a "textbook." He worked as a boatman, a land surveyor, a grocery clerk, a postmaster—he did every kind of work. While running a general store, whenever there were no customers he would bury himself in law books, and by sheer persistence he read his way into becoming a licensed attorney in Illinois.

一个连小学都没读完的人，成了律师。这件事本身，就是他后半生的底色：他从不是靠天赋赢，他是靠不肯停。

A man who never even finished grade school became a lawyer. That fact in itself is the ground note of the rest of his life: he never won by talent; he won by refusing to stop.

二、那份“失败清单”

II. That "List of Failures"

关于林肯的失败，流传最广的是一份长长的清单。它大致是这样的（这里用通行的通俗版本）：

The most widely circulated account of Lincoln's failures is a long list. It goes roughly like this (I use the common popular version here):

- 22岁，经商失败，欠债累累；

- At 22, his business failed and left him deep in debt;

- 23岁，竞选州议员落选，工作也丢了；

- At 23, he lost his race for the state legislature and lost his job as well;

- 24岁，借钱再经商，年底破产，还了十几年债；

- At 24, he borrowed money to go into business again, went bankrupt at the end of the year, and spent more than a decade paying off the debt;

- 25岁，订婚；

- At 25, he became engaged;

- 26岁，未婚妻在婚礼前病逝；

- At 26, his fiancée fell ill and died before the wedding;

- 27岁，精神崩溃，卧床六个月；

- At 27, he had a breakdown and lay in bed for six months;

- 28岁，竞选州议会议长落选；

- At 28, he lost his race for speaker of the state legislature;

- 34岁，竞选国会议员落选；

- At 34, he lost his race for the U.S. Congress;

- 37岁，当选国会议员（这是他早期少有的胜仗），两年后寻求连任又失败；

- At 37, he was elected to Congress (one of the few victories of his early years), and two years later his bid for reelection failed;

- 45岁，竞选参议员落选；

- At 45, he lost his race for the Senate;

- 47岁，争取副总统提名，得票不到一百张；

- At 47, he sought the vice-presidential nomination and won fewer than a hundred votes;

- 49岁，再度竞选参议员，再度落败；

- At 49, he ran for the Senate again and lost again;

- 51岁，当选美国总统。

- At 51, he was elected President of the United States.

这份清单在网上被复制了无数次，细节其实有不小争议（见文末辨析）。但它指向的核心事实是确凿的：林肯在前半生反复地输，反复地爬起来。

This list has been copied countless times online, and its details are in fact considerably disputed (see the notes at the end). But the core fact it points to is solid: in the first half of his life, Lincoln lost again and again, and got up again and again.

其中伤他最深的，是那位女士的离去。朋友们后来回忆，那段时间他不吃不喝，整日在河边和树林里游荡，嘴里念叨着“大雪和冷雨落在她坟上的样子”。有朋友担心他会自杀。他躺了半年，然后爬了起来，继续竞选，继续失败，继续再爬起来。

What wounded him most deeply was the loss of that young woman. Friends later recalled that in that time he would not eat or drink, and wandered all day by the river and in the woods, murmuring about "the way the heavy snow and cold rain fall on her grave." Some friends feared he would kill himself. He lay there for half a year—and then rose, and kept campaigning, kept losing, kept rising again.

三、1858：一场“输了却很值”的辩论

III. 1858: A Debate That Was "Lost but Worth It"

1858年，林肯与道格拉斯竞选伊利诺伊州参议员。两人进行了七场公开辩论，围绕的核心问题是：奴隶制到底该不该被允许向新领地扩张。

In 1858 Lincoln ran against Douglas for a Senate seat from Illinois. The two held seven public debates, centered on one question: should slavery be allowed to expand into the new territories?

这七场辩论把林肯的口才和逻辑展现得淋漓尽致，让他从“西部的乡下律师”变成全国瞩目的人物。但最后，他还是输了——因为当时参议员由州议会选举，而民主党在州议会占多数，把票投给了道格拉斯。

Those seven debates displayed Lincoln's eloquence and logic to the fullest, turning him from "a country lawyer from the West" into a figure the whole nation watched. But in the end he still lost—because senators were then elected by the state legislature, and the Democrats held the majority there and cast their votes for Douglas.

林肯输给了制度，不是输给了道理。

Lincoln lost to the system, not to the argument.

但正是这七场“输掉”的辩论，让全党记住了他。两年后，共和党需要一位能代表整个北方的总统候选人——1860年，林肯以51岁的年纪，当选为美国总统。

But it was precisely those seven "lost" debates that made the whole party remember him. Two years later the Republican Party needed a presidential candidate who could represent the entire North—and in 1860, at the age of fifty-one, Lincoln was elected President of the United States.

他输掉的那场竞选，成了他通往白宫的入场券。有些失败，是把名字播出去的过程。

The race he lost became his ticket into the White House. Some failures are the process of getting your name out into the world.

四、他赢了战争，却没活过胜利

IV. He Won the War but Did Not Live Past the Victory

1861年3月，林肯就任总统。一个月后，南北战争爆发。

In March 1861 Lincoln took office as president. A month later the Civil War broke out.

这场战争远比所有人预想的艰难。北军在人力和物力上占优，却屡屡在战场上失利。林肯频繁更换指挥官——他任命过麦克莱伦、伯恩赛德、胡克，一个个都让他失望，最后才找到了格兰特。他一边承受战事不利的压力，一边面对内阁的掣肘、舆论的嘲讽和反战的声浪。

The war was far harder than anyone had expected. The North held the advantage in manpower and material, yet lost on the battlefield again and again. Lincoln changed commanders frequently—he appointed McClellan, Burnside, and Hooker, and each disappointed him, until at last he found Grant. All the while he bore the pressure of a failing war, the obstruction of his own cabinet, the mockery of public opinion, and the swell of antiwar feeling.

1863年，他签署《解放宣言》，宣布叛乱各州的奴隶获得自由。同年11月，他在葛底斯堡发表那篇只有两百多字的演说，重新定义了这场战争的意义。1865年，他成功连任，北方即将赢得战争。

In 1863 he signed the Emancipation Proclamation, declaring the slaves in the rebelling states free. That November he delivered at Gettysburg an address of barely more than two hundred words that redefined the meaning of the war. In 1865 he won a second term, and the North was about to win the war.

然后，1865年4月14日晚，林肯在华盛顿福特剧院看戏时，被一名南方奴隶主支持者开枪击中，次日凌晨去世。他在内战结束（4月9日李将军投降）后仅仅几天，就倒下了。

Then, on the evening of April 14, 1865, while Lincoln was watching a play at Ford's Theatre in Washington, a supporter of the Southern slaveholders shot him; he died in the early hours of the next day. He fell only days after the Civil War ended (General Lee surrendered on April 9).

这个让美国重新统一的人，没能活到重建美国的那一天。

The man who made America one again did not live to see the day America was rebuilt.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

把林肯放进这本书，不是因为他最后成功了，而是因为他是在无数次失败之后，才开始赢的。

Lincoln belongs in this book not because he succeeded in the end, but because he began to win only after countless failures.

他的一生对今天的我们，至少有三个提醒。

His life holds at least three reminders for us today.

第一，你现在的处境，不能定义你的上限。他35岁的时候，是一个破产过、失恋过、竞选一次次落败的乡村律师。没有人，包括他自己，能在那时看出他会成为什么人。正因为看不出来，所以你也别急着给自己盖章。

First, your present situation cannot define your ceiling. At thirty-five he was a country lawyer who had gone bankrupt, lost a sweetheart, and lost one election after another. No one, including himself, could have seen then what he would become. Precisely because it could not be seen, you should not be in a hurry to stamp a verdict on yourself either.

第二，“输”和“错”是两回事。1858年那场参议员竞选，他输在了制度设计上，但他的主张是对的。不是每一次失败都意味着你做错了，有时候只是时间、规则或运气没站在你这边。真正的判断标准，是你有没有说出你真正相信的话。

Second, "losing" and "being wrong" are two different things. In that 1858 Senate race he lost because of the way the system was designed, but his position was right. Not every failure means you did something wrong; sometimes it is simply that time, the rules, or luck were not on your side. The real measure is whether you said what you truly believed.

第三，决定一个人走多远的，是他被击倒之后爬起来的速度。林肯没有什么天赋异禀，他最大的本事是“还不放弃”。用他自己的话说，他有一种“坚韧不拔、百折不挠、勇往直前的使命感”。

Third, what decides how far a person goes is how fast he gets up after being knocked down. Lincoln had no remarkable gift; his greatest skill was "not giving up yet." In his own words, he had a sense of mission that was firm, unyielding, and ever pressing forward.

他51岁才当总统，56岁被暗杀。看起来生命很短、启动很晚。但就是这短短几年，他做出了足以让两百年后的人仍然记得他的事。

He became president at fifty-one and was assassinated at fifty-six. His life looks short, his start very late. But in those few short years he did things that keep people remembering him two hundred years later.

起步晚不可怕，怕的是你在第37岁那年就认了命。

A late start is not what is frightening; what is frightening is giving in at thirty-seven.

Appendix: Sources and Notes

主要史料

Primary Sources

3. Chronologies of Lincoln's life (including records of his campaigns, bankruptcies, and public offices), in the Library of Congress, the major Lincoln biographies, and authoritative Chinese-language sources.

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

4. 生于1809年2月12日（肯塔基州），卒于1865年4月15日（华盛顿，遇刺次日），享年56岁。

4. Born February 12, 1809 (Kentucky); died April 15, 1865 (Washington, the day after he was shot), aged 56.

5. 1834年当选伊利诺伊州议员；1847—1849年任联邦众议员一届；1858年竞选参议员落败；1860年当选总统，1864年连任。

5. Elected to the Illinois legislature in 1834; served one term in the U.S. House of Representatives, 1847-1849; lost the Senate race in 1858; elected president in 1860 and reelected in 1864.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

6. “林肯8次竞选失败、2次破产、未婚妻去世”等流行清单——细节需谨慎。诸如“1832年落选”“1843年竞选国会议员落选”“1848年连任失败”“1855/1858年参议员落选”“1856年副总统提名仅得不足100票”等，多数有史料支持；但清单中的具体年份、次数在不同中文版本间存在出入（如“经商破产”的次数与还债年限有“16年/17年”等不同说法），文中已说明是“通行的通俗版本”，未将其作为精确年表使用。

6. The popular list—"Lincoln lost eight elections, went bankrupt twice, lost his fiancée," and so on—needs to be handled with care in its details. Items such as "lost in 1832," "lost a congressional race in 1843," "lost reelection in 1848," "lost Senate races in 1855/1858," and "won fewer than 100 votes for the vice-presidential nomination in 1856" are for the most part supported by historical sources; but the specific years and counts in the list vary among different Chinese versions (for example, the number of business bankruptcies and the years spent repaying debt are given variously as "16 years / 17 years"). The main text states that this is the "common popular version" and does not use it as a precise chronology.

7. “未婚妻安娜·拉特利奇（Ann Rutledge）”——此恋情的历史真实性存在争议。1930年代以后的部分史家认为，相关叙述多来自其友人威廉·赫恩登晚年的回忆，证据不甚可靠。文中采用“流传甚广但细节存争议”的处理，保留了情感冲击力，同时避免坐实为定论。

7. "His fiancée Ann Rutledge"—the historical reality of this romance is disputed. Since the 1930s some historians have held that the accounts come largely from the late-life recollections of his friend William Herndon, and that the evidence is not very reliable. The main text treats it as "widely circulated, but with disputed details," preserving its emotional force while avoiding setting it down as settled fact.

8. “林肯有严重抑郁症”——他确有长期的忧郁与“疑病症”（hypochondria）记录，但现代以“临床抑郁症”直接诊断历史人物需谨慎，文中表述为“精神崩溃/深陷忧郁”。

8. "Lincoln suffered from severe depression"—he does have records of long-standing melancholy and "hypochondria," but diagnosing a historical figure directly with "clinical depression" in modern terms requires caution; the main text words it as "a breakdown / deep melancholy."
