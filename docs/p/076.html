李贺：因为父亲的名字，他一生不许考进士

Li He: Forbidden All His Life to Sit the Imperial Examination Because of His Father's Name

——“天若有情天亦老。”

— "If Heaven had feelings, Heaven too would grow old."

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂76 / 100约 3037 字 · 3037 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，很多年轻人会抱怨“出身决定命运”。我们抱怨的是资源、是人脉、是起点。

In 2026, many young people complain that "your origins decide your fate." What we complain about is resources, connections, starting points.

但在一千二百多年前，有一个人，因为自己父亲的名字，被制度性地剥夺了参加最重要考试的权利。他叫李贺。

But more than twelve hundred years ago, there was a man who, because of his own father's name, was institutionally stripped of the right to sit the most important examination. His name was Li He.

他写诗写到呕心沥血，被称为“诗鬼”。他一生只活了27岁。他留下的诗句，一千多年后还在被引用——包括那句被后人反复改造的“天若有情天亦老”。

He wrote poetry until he vomited his heart's blood, and was called the "Poet Ghost." He lived only twenty-seven years. The lines he left behind are still quoted more than a thousand years later—including the one later generations have endlessly remade, "If Heaven had feelings, Heaven too would grow old."

一、一个荒唐的避讳

I. An Absurd Taboo

790年，李贺出生在福昌（今河南宜阳）。他的家族号称是唐宗室郑王的后代，但这个身份到他那辈，早已名存实亡，家道清贫。

In 790, Li He was born in Fuchang (present-day Yiyang, Henan). His family claimed descent from the Tang imperial clan, from the Prince of Zheng, but by his generation that status existed in name only, and the household was poor.

李贺从小就是个奇才。据说他七岁就能写诗，尤其擅长乐府。他个子细瘦，眉毛连成一线，手指很长，走起路来像个随时会被风吹倒的人。

Li He was a prodigy from childhood. It is said that by the age of seven he could write poetry, and he was especially skilled at yuefu. He was slight and thin, his eyebrows grew in a single line, his fingers were very long, and he walked like a man who might be blown over by any gust of wind.

他十几岁时，文学前辈韩愈听说了他的才华，亲自来看他，让他当场作诗。李贺提笔就写下《高轩过》，韩愈大为惊叹。有韩愈这样的文坛领袖赏识，按常理，李贺的前程应该一片光明。

When he was in his teens, the literary elder Han Yu heard of his talent, came in person to see him, and asked him to compose a poem on the spot. Li He took up his brush and at once wrote "Gao Xuan Guo" ("The Tall Carriage Passes By"), and Han Yu was astonished. With the recognition of a literary leader like Han Yu, by any normal reckoning, Li He's future should have been bright.

但李贺参加科举的前夜，出事了。

But on the eve of Li He's sitting the imperial examination, something happened.

他的父亲名叫“李晋肃”。“晋”与“进”同音。按照当时极度严苛的避讳制度，既然父亲名字里有“晋”，那么儿子就不能去考“进士”。这个逻辑，在今天看来荒谬到可笑，但在当时，它是一道可以断送人一生的门槛。

His father's name was Li Jinsu. "Jin" (晋) had the same sound as "jin" (进) in jinshi, the "advanced scholar" degree. Under the extremely strict taboo system of the time, since the father's name contained "Jin," the son could not sit for the jinshi examination. To us today this logic is absurd to the point of being laughable, but at the time it was a threshold that could ruin a man's whole life.

韩愈为此专门写了一篇《讳辩》，痛斥这种荒谬的避讳，为李贺鸣不平。他写道：父亲名字里有“晋”，儿子就不能考进士；那么如果父亲名字里有“仁”，儿子是不是连做人都不行了？

For this Han Yu wrote a piece specifically, "On the Taboo of Names" (Hui Bian), fiercely denouncing this absurd taboo and speaking out on Li He's behalf. He wrote: if a father's name contains "Jin" (晋), the son cannot sit for the jinshi examination; then if a father's name contains "Ren" (仁, "benevolence"), should the son not even be allowed to be a person (人, also pronounced ren)?

韩愈的辩词犀利绝伦，可它没能改变制度。 李贺最终还是被挡在了进士考场之外。

Han Yu's argument was incomparably sharp, but it could not change the system. Li He was ultimately barred from the jinshi examination hall.

二、一个从九品的小官

II. A Minor Official of the Ninth Rank

被挡住升迁正途的李贺，只能靠宗室身份，经人推荐做了个“奉礼郎”——一个从九品的小官，负责在朝会祭祀时安排位次、喊礼。

Barred from the proper path of advancement, Li He could only rely on his imperial-clan status and, on someone's recommendation, take the post of fengli lang—a minor official of the ninth rank, responsible for arranging ranks and calling out the ritual at court assemblies and sacrifices.

这个职位，对李贺这样的人来说，是一种羞辱。他能写出震烁古今的诗句，却要在礼仪场合里，替别人站队、喊口令。

For a man like Li He, this post was a humiliation. He could write lines that shook the ages, yet in ceremonial settings he had to line people up and call out the orders.

他做了三年，终于辞官回乡。

He held the post for three years, then finally resigned and went home.

三、呕出心来的诗人

III. The Poet Who Vomited Out His Heart

李贺的写诗方法，在唐代诗人里是独一份的。他母亲后来跟人讲：这孩子每天一早就骑上驴出门，身上背一个破口袋，想到好句子就立刻记下来扔进口袋里，晚上回来，口袋常常是满的。他把白天攒下的句子摊开，整理成诗。

Li He's method of writing poetry was unique among Tang poets. His mother later told others: this boy would ride out on his donkey first thing every morning, carrying a worn bag, and the moment a good line came to him he would write it down and drop it in the bag; when he came home at night, the bag was often full. He would spread out the lines he had gathered during the day and work them into poems.

母亲看到他这样，常常心疼地说：“是儿要当呕出心乃已尔！”（这孩子，是非要把心吐出来才肯罢休啊！）

Seeing him like this, his mother would often say with a pang: "This boy will not rest until he has vomited out his very heart!" (This child will not stop until he has spat out his heart!)

“呕心”这个词，就是从李贺这里来的。

The phrase "vomiting the heart" comes from Li He.

他的身体本就不好。长期的苦吟、清贫、加上仕途无望的郁结，让他很早就衰颓下去。他的诗里反复出现死亡、鬼魂、墓地、铜人、老去的天，那不是他刻意要“吓人”，而是一个年轻人真切地感受到自己的寿命正在流逝。

His health was poor to begin with. Long years of agonized versifying, poverty, and the pent-up bitterness of a hopeless career made him decline early. Death, ghosts, graveyards, bronze figures, an aging heaven recur again and again in his poems; this was not a deliberate attempt to "frighten people," but a young man's keen sense that his own span of life was running out.

他的代表作

His Representative Works

《雁门太守行》里那两句：

Two lines from "Yan Men Tai Shou Xing" ("Song of the Governor of Yanmen"):

黑云压城城欲摧，甲光向日金鳞开。

Black clouds press the city, the city is about to fall; / Armor gleams in the sun, golden scales opening.

《李凭箜篌引》里写音乐：

In "Li Ping Kong Hou Yin" ("Li Ping Plays the Konghou"), he writes of music:

昆山玉碎凤凰叫，芙蓉泣露香兰笑。

Jade of Kunlun shatters, the phoenix cries; / Lotuses weep dew, fragrant orchids laugh.

还有那首《金铜仙人辞汉歌》，他写下了一千多年后被反复引用的名句：

And in "Jin Tong Xian Ren Ci Han Ge" ("The Bronze Immortal Bids Farewell to Han"), he wrote a famous line quoted again and again for more than a thousand years:

衰兰送客咸阳道，天若有情天亦老。

Faded orchids see the traveler off on the Xianyang road; / If Heaven had feelings, Heaven too would grow old.

“天若有情天亦老”——上天如果有感情，也会因为悲伤而衰老。 这七个字，把“人情之苦”推到了一个极致。

"If Heaven had feelings, Heaven too would grow old"—if Heaven had feelings, it too would age from sorrow. These seven characters push the "suffering of human feeling" to its utmost extreme.

四、“诗鬼”是怎么来的

IV. How the "Poet Ghost" Got His Name

后人给李贺起了一个称号，叫“诗鬼”。

Later generations gave Li He the title "Poet Ghost."

这个“鬼”字，不是骂他。它说的是他诗里那种阴冷、诡艳、幽暗的气质。他写马、写剑、写美人、写神仙，但写给它们的光，总是带着一种黄昏或深夜的色调。他尤其喜欢写“铜”——铜人、铜驼、铜镜，那种金属的、冰冷的、属于历史的质感。

The word "ghost" is not an insult. It describes the cold, strange, gorgeous, dark quality of his poetry. He wrote of horses, swords, beauties, immortals, but the light he cast on them always had the tone of dusk or deep night. He was especially fond of "bronze"—bronze figures, bronze camels, bronze mirrors—that metallic, cold texture belonging to history.

在唐诗的谱系里，李白是“诗仙”，杜甫是“诗圣”，李贺是“诗鬼”，三个人各占一个极端。 “仙”是向上飞，“圣”是向下扎根，“鬼”则是往幽暗处去。

In the genealogy of Tang poetry, Li Bai is the "Poet Immortal," Du Fu the "Poet Sage," and Li He the "Poet Ghost," each occupying one extreme. The "immortal" flies upward, the "sage" roots downward, and the "ghost" goes toward the dark.

他也把乐府的写法推到了极致。他的诗常有长短不齐的句式和强烈的音乐性，后人评他的乐府“如百家锦衲，五色眩目”。这种风格，直接影响了后世一大批诗人，晚唐的李商隐、温庭筠，乃至更晚的一些“奇诡”一路的写作者，都从他这里取过火。

He also pushed the art of yuefu to its extreme. His poems often have lines of uneven length and a strong musicality; later critics described his yuefu as "like a patchwork robe of a hundred houses, dazzling with five colors." This style directly influenced a whole host of later poets; Li Shangyin and Wen Tingyun of the late Tang, and even later writers on the "strange and uncanny" path, all took fire from him.

五、他为什么还在被读

V. Why He Is Still Read

李贺的诗，今天读起来仍然“不像古人写的”。

Li He's poetry still reads today as though "not written by an ancient."

他不写“人生如梦”这种现成的感慨，他写具体的、怪诞的、带着体温的意象：老去的天、哭的兰花、碎掉的玉、会笑的凤凰。他把抽象的情绪，变成了可以触摸的东西。 这种手法，其实非常“现代”。

He does not write ready-made sentiments like "life is a dream"; he writes concrete, grotesque images that carry body heat: an aging heaven, weeping orchids, shattered jade, a phoenix that laughs. He turns abstract emotion into something you can touch. This technique is in fact very "modern."

他也写过边塞与战争：

He also wrote of the frontier and of war:

男儿何不带吴钩，收取关山五十州。

Why should a man not take up the Wu hook sword, / And win back the fifty provinces of the passes and mountains?

请君暂上凌烟阁，若个书生万户侯？

Pray, step for a moment up to the Lingyan Pavilion— / How many scholars there are made marquises of ten thousand households?

这四句里有一种被压抑到极点的爆发力。一个被剥夺了前途的人，偏偏写出了最锋利的报国之志。 这种“反差”，正是李贺的魅力所在。

In these four lines there is an explosive force repressed to its limit. A man stripped of his future wrote, of all people, the sharpest ambition to serve his country. This "contrast" is precisely where Li He's charm lies.

后世的李商隐、温庭筠，乃至现代一些以“奇诡意象”著称的诗人，都明显受到他的影响。有评论者说，唐诗到李贺，像是开出了一条旁支的、幽暗的、但也极其绚烂的路。

Later Li Shangyin, Wen Tingyun, and even some modern poets known for "strange and uncanny imagery" were clearly influenced by him. One critic said that in Tang poetry, Li He opened up a side branch—dark, but also extraordinarily brilliant.

六、27岁

VI. Twenty-Seven

大约在816年，李贺在故乡病逝，年仅二十七岁。

Around 816, Li He died of illness in his native place, only twenty-seven years old.

关于他的死，后人编出了许多传说，比如“天帝召他去天上写文章”。这些都是想象，不是事实。真正可以确定的是：一个中国文学史上最奇诡、最天才的诗人之一，在27岁就熄灭了。

About his death, later generations invented many legends, such as that "the Heavenly Emperor summoned him to write in heaven." These are imagination, not fact. What can truly be established is this: one of the strangest and most brilliant poets in the history of Chinese literature was extinguished at twenty-seven.

他没有等到属于自己的时代，也没有等到科举给他的公道。

He did not live to see an age that belonged to him, nor to see the examination system grant him justice.

五、写给2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

李贺的遭遇，今天读来依然让人愤怒。它不是“怀才不遇”那四个字能概括的——他是被一条荒唐的规则，从制度上判了“终身禁赛”。

Li He's fate still makes one angry to read today. It cannot be summed up in the four words "a talent unrecognized"—he was given, by an absurd rule, an institutional sentence of "a lifetime ban."

但更让人敬畏的是他的反应。他没有去求情，没有去钻营，也没有消沉到底。他把所有的不甘，都变成了诗。他知道自己活不长，所以他拼了命地写，写得连母亲都看不下去。

But what is even more awe-inspiring is his response. He did not go begging for leniency, did not scheme for advantage, nor sink into despair to the end. He turned all his resentment into poetry. He knew he would not live long, so he wrote desperately, wrote until even his mother could not bear to watch.

他用27年，写出了很多人一辈子也写不出的密度。

In twenty-seven years he wrote with a density many people cannot achieve in a lifetime.

2026年的我们，也许不会再遇到“父亲名字里有晋字就不能考进士”这种事。但我们依然会遇到各种看不见的“避讳”——学历的门槛、出身的标签、行业的偏见。

We in 2026 may no longer encounter something like "if your father's name contains the character Jin, you cannot sit for the jinshi exam." But we still encounter all kinds of invisible "taboos"—the threshold of academic credentials, the label of one's origins, the prejudices of an industry.

李贺给出的答案是：当规则把你挡在门外的时候，你至少还能选择，用剩下的时间，做出连规则都无法否认的东西。

The answer Li He gives is this: when the rules shut you outside the door, you can at least still choose to use the time that remains to make something the rules themselves cannot deny.

他留给后世的，其实还有一个更尖锐的问题：一个社会用什么样的规则，能决定一个人的一生？ 李贺被挡在门外的理由，荒唐到连韩愈都忍不住要写文章骂。可就是在这样的荒唐面前，一个最有可能成为大官的人，被迫成了一个只能写诗的“边缘人”。而恰恰是这个“边缘人”，成了唐诗里不可替代的那一个。

What he left to later ages is in fact also a sharper question: by what kind of rules may a society decide a person's whole life? The reason Li He was shut outside the door was so absurd that even Han Yu could not help writing an article to curse it. And yet before just such absurdity, a man most likely to become a high official was forced to become a "marginal man" who could only write poetry. And it is precisely this "marginal man" who became the irreplaceable one in Tang poetry.

六、附记

VI. A Postscript

李贺的诗集，由他自己生前编过，也由后人整理过。杜牧为他写过序，李商隐为他写过小传。两位晚唐最重要的人物，都为这个只活了27岁的前辈作记，这本身说明：在那个时代，真正懂诗的人，是知道李贺有多重的。 遗憾的是，这份“懂”，没能改变他生前的处境——他依然是在贫病和失意里走到尽头的。这也是这本书记下他的原因：有些人的价值，从来不是当时的评价能承载的。

Li He's collection of poems was compiled by himself in his lifetime and also arranged by later hands. Du Mu wrote a preface for him, and Li Shangyin wrote a short biography of him. That two of the most important figures of the late Tang should both write records for this elder who lived only twenty-seven years shows in itself: in that age, those who truly understood poetry knew how much Li He weighed. Sadly, that "understanding" could not change his circumstances while he lived—he still came to the end of his road in poverty, illness, and disappointment. This is also why this book records him: the worth of some people can never be carried by the judgment of their own time.

一千二百年过去了，那条“不许考进士”的规矩，早已无人记得。但“天若有情天亦老”这句话，仍然被一代又一代的人引用、改写。时间最终给出了答案：当年那个被制度挡在门外的年轻人，比那条制度活得长久得多。

Twelve hundred years have passed, and the rule that "forbade him to sit the jinshi exam" is long forgotten by everyone. But the line "If Heaven had feelings, Heaven too would grow old" is still quoted and rewritten by generation after generation. Time has finally given its answer: the young man once shut outside the door by the system has outlived that system by far.

Sources and Notes

1. 生平基础事实：李贺（字长吉），790年生于福昌（今河南宜阳），约816年卒，年27岁。为确证史实。

1. Basic biographical facts: Li He (courtesy name Changji), born in Fuchang (present-day Yiyang, Henan) in 790, died around 816, aged twenty-seven. Considered confirmed historical fact.

2. 家世：自称唐宗室郑王之后，至其父辈已家道中落。为通行记载。

2. Family background: he claimed descent from the Prince of Zheng of the Tang imperial clan, but by his father's generation the family had already declined. A commonly accepted account.

3. 避讳不得应进士举：因其父名“李晋肃”，“晋”与“进”同音，被阻于进士科考。韩愈作《讳辩》为其辩白。为确证史实。辨析：此事的具体细节（如是谁最先提出避讳、李贺是否曾“已报名后被举报”）在史料中记载不一，本文只陈述其核心事实。

3. Barred by taboo from sitting the jinshi examination: because his father was named Li Jinsu, and "Jin" (晋) was homophonous with "jin" (进), he was blocked from the jinshi examination. Han Yu wrote "On the Taboo of Names" (Hui Bian) to argue in his defense. Considered confirmed historical fact. Analysis: the specific details (such as who first raised the taboo, or whether Li He had already registered and was then reported) are recorded inconsistently across the sources; this book states only the core facts.

4. 曾任奉礼郎：为从九品小官。为确证史实。

4. He once held the post of fengli lang: a minor official of the ninth rank. Considered confirmed historical fact.

5. “呕心”典故：出自李商隐《李贺小传》等记载，为其母所言。李商隐此文写于李贺去世后，属后人追记，细节可能带有文学加工，但“李贺苦吟”这一基本形象得到多方印证。

5. The "vomiting the heart" anecdote: from Li Shangyin's "A Short Biography of Li He" and other accounts, spoken by his mother. Li Shangyin wrote this after Li He's death; it is a retrospective record by a later hand, and the details may carry literary embellishment, but the basic image of "Li He agonizing over his verses" is corroborated from several quarters.

6. 享年27岁：为通行说法。其具体卒年与享年，史料存在一定模糊性，本文采用最常见的“约816年、27岁”。

6. Age at death of twenty-seven: the commonly accepted account. The exact year of death and age at death are somewhat ambiguous in the sources; this book adopts the most common version, "around 816, aged twenty-seven."

7. “天帝召其作记”等传说：出自李商隐《李贺小传》，属神话式记载，非史实，本文明确不作为事实采用。

7. Legends such as "the Heavenly Emperor summoned him to write a record": from Li Shangyin's "A Short Biography of Li He," a mythic account, not historical fact; this book explicitly does not treat it as fact.
