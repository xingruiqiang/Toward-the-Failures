丘吉尔：全校倒数、被骂“过气政客”，60岁后才成为救星

Churchill: Last in His Class, Dismissed as a "Has-Been"—and the Savior Only After Sixty

——他人生最辉煌的那六年，从65岁才开始

— The most glorious six years of his life began only at sixty-five

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者26 / 100约 3340 字 · 3340 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，很多人在意的是“35岁危机”。到了35岁还没混出来，就好像人生已经进入了倒计时。

In 2026, what many people worry about is the "crisis at thirty-five." If you haven't made it by thirty-five, it feels as though your life has already begun its countdown.

可历史上有一个人的故事，专门用来反驳这句话。他小时候是全校最差的学生之一，考军校考了三次才进；中年时主导过一场惨败的战役，被迫下台；他一生中最常被贴的标签是“过气政客”“鲁莽”“不稳定”。直到65岁那年，他才成为一国的首相，然后在人类最黑暗的六年里，成了那个不肯投降的声音。

But history holds one man whose story seems made to refute that. As a boy he was among the worst students in his whole school, and it took him three tries to get into military college. In middle age he led a campaign that ended in catastrophe and was forced out of office. The labels most often pinned on him were "has-been," "reckless," "unstable." Only at sixty-five did he become a nation's prime minister—and then, through the darkest six years in human history, he became the voice that would not surrender.

他叫温斯顿·丘吉尔。他用一生证明：有些人的巅峰，不在前半程。

His name was Winston Churchill. His whole life proved one thing: for some people, the summit does not come in the first half of the journey.

一、一个“问题儿童”

I. A "Problem Child"

1874年11月30日，丘吉尔出生在牛津郡的布伦海姆宫——一座公爵家族的宫殿。他生在一个显赫到不能再显赫的贵族家庭：祖父是马尔巴罗公爵，父亲伦道夫·丘吉尔当过财政大臣，母亲是美国金融家的女儿。

On November 30, 1874, Churchill was born at Blenheim Palace in Oxfordshire—the palace of a ducal family. He was born into an aristocratic family of the most illustrious kind: his grandfather was the Duke of Marlborough, his father, Randolph Churchill, had served as Chancellor of the Exchequer, and his mother was the daughter of an American financier.

但显赫的家世，并没有给他一个温暖的童年。他是早产儿，父亲忙于政治，母亲沉溺于社交，他几乎是由保姆爱维莉丝太太带大的。多年后，他还说过，自己是从“远处”爱戴并敬仰父亲的，两人之间从未有过他渴望的那种亲密谈话。

But a grand lineage did not give him a warm childhood. He was born prematurely; his father was consumed by politics, his mother by society, and he was raised almost entirely by his nanny, Mrs. Everest. Years later he would say that he had loved and admired his father "from a distance," and that the two had never had the intimate talks he longed for.

在学校里，他是让老师最头疼的那种学生：顽皮、贪吃、成绩差，屡屡挨罚。他尤其讨厌数学和拉丁文——偏偏这两门是当时“好学生”的硬指标。1888年进入哈罗公学后，他的成绩迅速滑到全校倒数。他偏科偏得厉害：英语和历史出众，拉丁文和数学几乎交白卷。

At school he was the kind of pupil teachers dreaded: mischievous, greedy, poor at his studies, and constantly punished. He particularly detested mathematics and Latin—precisely the two subjects that marked out a "good student" in those days. After entering Harrow in 1888, his marks quickly slid to the very bottom of the school. He was wildly lopsided: outstanding in English and history, while in Latin and mathematics he all but handed in blank papers.

1893年，父亲决定送他进军校。他考桑赫斯特皇家军事学院，考了三次才勉强通过，以第95名的成绩进了骑兵专业（因为骑兵科的入学要求相对低，这在当时还让家里觉得有些丢脸）。

In 1893 his father decided to send him to military college. He sat the entrance exam for the Royal Military College, Sandhurst, and barely passed on his third attempt, entering the cavalry with a rank of ninety-fifth (the cavalry's entrance requirements were relatively low, which at the time was a small embarrassment to the family).

可进了军校，他像换了一个人：军事训练、历史、写作、演讲，他样样投入，毕业时成绩排到了全校第8名。

But once inside the military college he seemed a different person: drill, history, writing, public speaking—he threw himself into all of it, and graduated eighth in his class.

他不是笨，他是那种“在不合适的评价体系里显得笨”的人。

He was not stupid; he was the sort of person who only looks stupid inside the wrong system of judgment.

二、靠一场越狱进入政坛

II. He Entered Politics Through an Escape

毕业后的丘吉尔，先是当军官，同时当战地记者。他人生第一次“出圈”，是在南非。

After graduating, Churchill served as an officer while also working as a war correspondent. His first real brush with fame came in South Africa.

1899年，他作为记者随英军参加英布战争，被俘。他在狱中设法越狱，靠一个当地英国侨民的帮助，逃到了英国领事馆。这件事在英国引起轰动，“越狱英雄”的名声把他送进了政坛。1900年，他当选议员，开始了长达六十多年的政治生涯。

In 1899, reporting with the British army in the Boer War, he was captured. In prison he managed to escape and, with the help of a local British émigré, made his way to the British consulate. The episode caused a sensation in Britain, and the fame of the "escaping hero" carried him into politics. In 1900 he was elected to Parliament, beginning a political career that would last more than sixty years.

可他的政治生涯，几乎就是一部“起落起落起落”的连续剧。

Yet his political career was almost a serial drama of rise and fall, rise and fall.

他说过自己的追求：“只要哪里打仗，就去参军；仗打好了，就搞政治。”这种直白，也注定了他在政治里的不讨喜——他两度更换党派，树敌无数，被同僚认为“机会主义”“不可靠”。

He once described his ambition: "Wherever there is fighting, I go to the army; when it is over, I go into politics." Such plainness all but guaranteed he would never be liked in politics—he switched parties twice, made countless enemies, and was seen by his colleagues as "opportunistic" and "unreliable."

三、一场战役，把他打下深渊

III. One Campaign Plunged Him into the Abyss

1915年，时任海军大臣的丘吉尔，力主发动一场大胆的战役：派舰队打通达达尼尔海峡，攻占加里波利半岛，从侧翼打击德国和奥匈帝国。

In 1915, Churchill, then First Lord of the Admiralty, pushed hard for a bold campaign: send a fleet to force the Dardanelles, seize the Gallipoli peninsula, and strike at Germany and Austria-Hungary from the flank.

结果，这场战役成了一场巨大的灾难。盟军陷入僵局，伤亡惨重（其中澳新军团损失尤为惨烈）。丘吉尔被各方猛攻，被免去海军大臣之职，政坛地位跌入谷底。他一度辞去政府职务，亲自去了法国前线，在战壕里当营长。

The result was a catastrophe. The Allies became deadlocked with appalling casualties (the ANZAC forces suffered especially heavily). Churchill was savaged from every side, stripped of the Admiralty, and his political standing hit rock bottom. For a time he resigned from the government and went to the front in France himself, commanding a battalion in the trenches.

一个曾经前途无量的政治明星，因为一个错误的判断，几乎把自己搭了进去。此后二十多年，他在内阁的名声始终是“判断力不稳、行事鲁莽”。

A political star once thought to have an unlimited future had, through a single misjudgment, nearly destroyed himself. For the next twenty years and more, his reputation in the cabinet never shed the tag of "unsteady judgment and reckless action."

四、荒野岁月：他喊了十年，没人听

IV. The Wilderness Years: For Ten Years He Shouted and No One Listened

1929年，工党上台，丘吉尔离开了政府。从1929年到1939年，是他政治生涯中最灰暗的十年，被他自己称为“在野岁月”。

In 1929 Labour came to power and Churchill left the government. The decade from 1929 to 1939 was the bleakest stretch of his political life, what he himself called his "wilderness years."

这十年里，他大部分时间在写作（写出了《世界危机》《我的早年生活》和他祖先马尔巴罗公爵的传记），偶尔画画，也去美国拜访政要。而在议会里，他做的事几乎只有一件：警告大家防备德国。

Through those ten years he spent most of his time writing (producing The World Crisis, My Early Life, and a biography of his ancestor the Duke of Marlborough), painted now and then, and traveled to America to meet leading figures. In Parliament, he did almost one thing only: warn everyone to prepare against Germany.

当整个英国还沉在“一战之后再不会有大战”的和平主义气氛里、主张裁军的时候，他是议会里极少数坚持反对裁军、并警告希特勒正在撕毁《凡尔赛条约》的人。1936年希特勒重占莱茵非军事区，1938年德国吞并奥地利、慕尼黑协定逼捷克割让苏台德——丘吉尔几乎每一次都站出来反对绥靖政策，说“我们已经遭到一次完全、彻底的失败”。

While all of Britain was still sunk in the pacifist mood that "there will never be another great war after the last one," and calling for disarmament, he was one of the very few in Parliament who kept opposing disarmament and warning that Hitler was tearing up the Treaty of Versailles. When Hitler remilitarized the Rhineland in 1936, when Germany annexed Austria in 1938 and the Munich Agreement forced Czechoslovakia to cede the Sudetenland—Churchill stood up almost every time to oppose appeasement, saying, "We have sustained a total and unmitigated defeat."

回应他的是什么？是议会里的一片抗议声和嘘声。他甚至在选区被保守党部启动过弹劾动议。在很多人眼里，他是一个“战争贩子”“过气的老人”。

And what came back? A chorus of protest and jeers in the House. His own constituency Conservative association even moved a motion against him. To many, he was a "warmonger," a "spent old man."

他是对的，但对的人，往往要等到灾难发生，才被听进去。

He was right. But the one who is right often has to wait for disaster before anyone hears him.

五、1940：65岁，他在最黑暗的时刻上任

V. 1940: At Sixty-Five, He Took Office in the Darkest Hour

1939年9月1日，二战爆发。张伯伦政府无力应对，1940年5月8日，议会通过了不信任动议。5月10日——同一天德军入侵法国——国王召见丘吉尔，命他组阁。

On September 1, 1939, the Second World War began. Chamberlain's government could not cope, and on May 8, 1940, the Commons passed a motion of no confidence. On May 10—the same day German troops invaded France—the King summoned Churchill and asked him to form a government.

此时，他已经65岁。

He was sixty-five.

上任第三天，他站在下议院，说出了那段著名的话：

On his third day in office, standing before the House of Commons, he spoke the famous words:

我没有别的，只有热血、辛劳、眼泪和汗水献给大家……你们问：我们的目的是什么？我可以用一个词来答复：胜利，不惜一切代价去争取胜利。

"I have nothing to offer but blood, toil, tears and sweat. … You ask: what is our aim? I can answer in one word: victory—victory at all costs."

在法国即将投降、英国几乎独自面对纳粹的时刻，正是这个被嘲笑了十年的“过气政客”，用语言和意志把整个国家的恐惧，变成了“绝不投降”：

At the moment when France was about to surrender and Britain stood almost alone against the Nazis, it was this "has-been," mocked for a decade, who turned a whole nation's fear into "never surrender"—through words and will:

我们将战斗到底……我们绝不投降。

"We shall fight on to the end. … We shall never surrender."

后来的敦刻尔克大撤退、不列颠空战，英国扛住了。历史学家们现在普遍认为，1940年那几个月，如果没有丘吉尔那股近乎顽固的意志，英国很可能走上与纳粹妥协的道路。

In the Dunkirk evacuation and the Battle of Britain that followed, Britain held. Historians now generally agree that in those months of 1940, without Churchill's almost obstinate will, Britain would very likely have taken the road of compromise with the Nazis.

更有意思的是结局：1945年，战争赢了，英国人民却在选举中把丘吉尔选下了台。他坦然接受，引用了一句希腊名言，大意是“对伟人最好的回报，就是让他在胜利之后安静地回到平凡”。1951年，他再次出任首相，1953年获得诺贝尔文学奖，1955年因健康退休。

More interesting still is the ending: in 1945 the war was won, and the British people voted Churchill out. He accepted it calmly, quoting a Greek maxim to the effect that "the best reward for a great man is to let him return quietly to private life after victory." In 1951 he became prime minister again, in 1953 he won the Nobel Prize in Literature, and in 1955 he retired on grounds of health.

六、致2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

丘吉尔的一生，几乎是给“35岁焦虑”和“成名要趁早”这两句话，写的一份反驳材料。

Churchill's life is almost a rebuttal brief against the sayings "anxiety at thirty-five" and "make your name early."

他给出的第一个提醒是：评价体系错了，不代表你不行。他在哈罗是倒数，在拉丁文和数学面前是个差生；但他对历史、英语和军事的敏锐，在那套标准里根本显示不出来。你要做的，是找到那个能显示你真实才能的场地——就像他进了军校之后，忽然成了优等生。

The first reminder he leaves: a wrong yardstick does not mean you're no good. He was at the bottom at Harrow, a poor pupil in the face of Latin and mathematics; but his instincts for history, English, and soldiering could not show up at all under that standard. What you have to do is find the arena where your real gifts can show—just as, once he entered the military college, he suddenly became a top student.

第二个提醒是：被孤立、被嘲笑，不能证明你错了。从1915年加里波利之败，到1930年代被斥为“过气政客”，人们给他的标签从未友好过。可正是这个“不被喜欢的人”，在最关键的时刻说出了正确的话、做出了正确的决定。当所有人都在说“这不可能”的时候，你得有胆量替未来说话。

The second reminder: being isolated and mocked does not prove you are wrong. From the Gallipoli disaster of 1915 to being derided as a "has-been" in the 1930s, the labels people gave him were never kind. Yet it was precisely this "unlikable man" who, at the crucial moment, said the right words and made the right decision. When everyone is saying "this is impossible," you must have the courage to speak for the future.

第三个提醒，也是最重要的：巅峰没有年龄限制。他真正改变世界的那几年，从65岁才开始。在那之前，他失败过、被排挤过、被全世界当成一个不识时务的老头。但正是那几十年的积累和坚持，让他在那一刻有底气站在所有人面前说：我们要战斗到底。

The third reminder, and the most important: the summit has no age limit. The years in which he truly changed the world began only at sixty-five. Before that he had failed, been pushed aside, and been taken by the whole world for an old man out of step with his times. But it was exactly those decades of accumulation and persistence that gave him the standing, in that moment, to face everyone and say: we shall fight to the end.

所以，别急着给自己的一生下结论。你可能只是还没走到1940年5月10日。

So do not be in a hurry to pass judgment on your own life. You may simply not have reached May 10, 1940 yet.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “丘吉尔在哈罗公学成绩全校倒数第一”——通行的说法是“长期处于学校最差的几名之内/排名靠后”，不同材料有“倒数第三”“在最低年级停留最久”等表述，“倒数第一”并非唯一确证说法。文中采用“全校倒数”的概括表述。

7. "Churchill was last in the whole school at Harrow"—the customary statement is that he "long ranked among the worst few, near the bottom," and different sources give "third from last," "spent the longest time in the lowest form," and so on; "last of all" is not the only attested account. This chapter uses the general phrasing "at the bottom of the school."

8. “丘吉尔考军校考了三次”——通说他两次落榜、第三次通过（1893年，第95名，骑兵科）。文中表述为“考了三次才勉强通过”。

8. "Churchill sat the military college exam three times"—the standard account is that he failed twice and passed on the third attempt (1893, ranked ninety-fifth, cavalry). This chapter phrases it as "barely passed on his third attempt."

9. “加里波利战役完全是他一个人的责任”——不完全。1915年间该行动由战时内阁与军方高层共同推动；战后调查委员会的报告主要归咎于首相与陆军大臣，认为丘吉尔的责任“不是那么重大”。中文通俗叙述常把责任集中在他身上，文中处理为“他力主该计划、并因此承担了主要政治后果”。

9. "The Gallipoli campaign was entirely his own responsibility"—not quite. Through 1915 the operation was pushed jointly by the War Cabinet and the military high command; the postwar commission of inquiry laid most blame on the prime minister and the Secretary of State for War, holding that Churchill's responsibility was "not so grave." Popular Chinese accounts often concentrate the blame on him; this chapter treats it as "he championed the plan and bore the main political consequences for it."

10. “丘吉尔是完美英雄”——他本人也确有争议（如对罢工、殖民与种族问题的立场等，后世评价分歧较大）。本文只取其“晚成”与“在危机中坚持”的一条线，不作全面人格评判。

10. "Churchill was a flawless hero"—he was himself genuinely controversial (his positions on strikes, colonialism, race, and other matters, on which later assessments diverge widely). This chapter takes only the single thread of his "late achievement" and his "persistence in crisis," and makes no full judgment of his character.
