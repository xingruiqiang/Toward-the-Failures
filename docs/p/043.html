韩信：从别人胯下爬过去的“胯夫”

Han Xin: The “Crotch-Crawler” Who Passed Beneath Another Man’s Legs

——他忍受了最大的羞辱，打下了大半个汉朝，最后死在皇宫里

—He endured the deepest humiliation, conquered more than half of the Han empire, and died in the imperial palace

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者43 / 100约 2588 字 · 2588 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，我们谈“尊严”，很多时候指的是“不能被当众羞辱”。被人骂一句、被人当众下不来台，都足以成为一个人辞职、翻脸的理由。

In 2026, when we talk about “dignity,” we usually mean “not being humiliated in public.” A single insult, one moment of being put on the spot in front of others, is enough reason for a person to quit a job or cut someone off.

而在两千两百年前，有一个人，被一个市井无赖当众拦住，说：你要么拔剑刺我，要么从我胯下爬过去。

Yet twenty-two hundred years ago, a man was stopped in public by a street thug who said to him: either draw your sword and run me through, or crawl between my legs.

他选了后者。他趴下身子，从对方的裤裆下面，爬了过去。满街的人哄笑，从此给了他一个外号：“胯夫”。

He chose the latter. He lowered himself and crawled under the man’s legs. The whole street roared with laughter, and from then on they gave him a nickname: “the Crotch-Crawler.”

他叫韩信。后来，他成了汉朝开国最会打仗的将军——然后，他被杀在了皇宫里。

His name was Han Xin. He went on to become the finest general of the founding of the Han dynasty—and then he was killed inside the imperial palace.

一、一个连饭都吃不上的人

I. A Man Who Could Not Even Feed Himself

韩信是淮阴人，早年很穷。

Han Xin was from Huaiyin, and in his early years he was very poor.

《史记》里写他：既没有好的品行，不能被推举做官；又不会做买卖谋生。用今天的话说，他既没有“关系”，也没有“技能”，甚至连能种的地都没有。

The Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian) describes him this way: he had no good reputation that might earn him recommendation to office, and he had no head for trade to make a living. In today’s terms, he had neither “connections” nor “skills”—he did not even own a plot of land to farm.

他只能到处蹭饭。他曾经在一位亭长家里吃了好几个月，亭长的妻子烦了，故意提前吃饭、不再给他留饭。他一怒之下，和对方断绝关系，从此连蹭饭的地方都没有了。

He could only scrounge meals wherever he could. For months he ate at the home of a district headman, until the man’s wife grew tired of it and began eating early on purpose, no longer leaving any food for him. Enraged, he broke off with them—and after that he had not even a place to scrounge a meal.

他在城下钓鱼，饿得不行。河边一位洗衣的老妇人（漂母）看他可怜，把自己带的饭分给他吃。他一连几十天都靠她接济。他很感动，说：我将来一定会重重报答你。

He fished at the foot of the city wall, starving. An old woman washing clothes by the river (the piaomu, the “washerwoman”) took pity on him and shared her own food with him. For dozens of days he survived on her charity. He was deeply moved and said: one day I will repay you richly.

老妇人生气地说：你一个男子汉大丈夫，连自己都养不活；我是可怜你才给你饭吃，难道是图你报答吗？

The old woman replied angrily: You are a grown man and you cannot even support yourself. I gave you food out of pity—do you think I did it for your repayment?

这是他人生中最狼狈的时刻之一：连一位洗衣妇，都在为他感到羞愧。

This was one of the most wretched moments of his life: even a washerwoman was ashamed on his behalf.

二、那个决定命运的“胯下之辱”

II. The “Humiliation Beneath the Legs” That Decided His Fate

淮阴的屠户里，有个年轻人拦住他，当众说：

Among the butchers of Huaiyin, a young man blocked his way and said in front of everyone:

“你虽然长得高大，喜欢佩剑，其实是个胆小鬼。”然后又说：“你要不怕死，就拔剑刺我；要是怕死，就从我裤裆里爬过去。”

“You may be big and tall and fond of wearing a sword, but you are a coward at heart.” Then he added: “If you are not afraid to die, draw your sword and run me through; if you are afraid to die, crawl between my legs.”

韩信盯着他看了很久——然后，俯下身子，从那个人的胯下爬了过去。

Han Xin stared at him for a long while—then bent down and crawled between the man’s legs.

满街的人都笑他，认为他胆小如鼠。

The whole street laughed at him, taking him for a coward.

这件事，《史记》里只记了几行字。但它后来成了中国历史上最著名的“忍辱”典故之一。人们反复讨论：韩信当时在想什么？

The Shiji records this in only a few lines. Yet it became one of the most famous tales of “enduring humiliation” in Chinese history. People have debated it endlessly: what was Han Xin thinking at that moment?

也许答案就在他后来的行动里：他不想把命，浪费在一个毫无价值的街头冲突上。

Perhaps the answer lies in what he did afterward: he did not want to throw his life away on a worthless street brawl.

三、他先后被两个老板无视

III. Overlooked by Two Masters in Turn

秦末大乱，韩信先投奔了项梁，后来又跟着项羽。他多次向项羽献计，项羽不用他。

When the Qin dynasty collapsed into chaos, Han Xin first joined Xiang Liang and later followed Xiang Yu. Again and again he offered Xiang Yu his plans, and Xiang Yu would not use him.

于是他转投刘邦。可到了刘邦手下，他也只是个小官，照样没被重视。他甚至因为犯事差点被斩。同案的人被杀，轮到他时，他大喊了一句，大意是：“汉王不是要夺天下吗？为什么要杀壮士！”

So he switched to Liu Bang. But under Liu Bang he was only a minor official, still ignored. He even came close to execution for an offense. When his companions in the case were beheaded and it was his turn, he shouted, in effect: “Is the King of Han not out to seize the empire? Why kill a brave man?”

刘邦的谋士夏侯婴听了觉得他不凡，救了他，并把他推荐给刘邦。刘邦给了他一个管粮草的官（治粟都尉）——还是没看出他的价值。

Liu Bang’s adviser Xiahou Ying heard him, thought him extraordinary, saved his life, and recommended him to Liu Bang. Liu Bang gave him a post in charge of grain supplies (Commissioner of Grain)—and still failed to see his worth.

韩信彻底失望了，逃走了。

Thoroughly disillusioned, Han Xin fled.

然后就有了那个著名的故事：萧何月下追韩信。

And then came the famous story: Xiao He chasing Han Xin under the moonlight.

丞相萧何听说韩信跑了，来不及报告刘邦，连夜骑马去追。追回来之后，他郑重地对刘邦说：韩信是“国士无双”，如果大王想争天下，非用他不可。

When Chancellor Xiao He heard that Han Xin had fled, he had no time to report to Liu Bang; he mounted and rode after him by night. Once he had brought him back, he told Liu Bang solemnly: Han Xin is “a scholar without peer in the realm” (guoshi wushuang); if the king wants to contend for the empire, he must use him.

刘邦最终采纳了萧何的建议，为韩信设坛、举行隆重的拜将仪式——一个刚来的、甚至逃跑过的年轻人，一夜之间成了汉军的大将军。

Liu Bang finally took Xiao He’s advice, built an altar for Han Xin, and held a grand ceremony to install him as general—and a young man who had only just arrived, who had even once run away, became overnight the Grand General of the Han army.

四、他真正的才华：一人定天下

IV. His True Genius: One Man Who Settled the Empire

韩信的价值，在他当上大将军之后彻底爆发。

Han Xin’s worth burst forth in full once he became Grand General.

- 他提出“明修栈道，暗度陈仓”，帮刘邦夺回三秦；

- He proposed the stratagem of “openly repairing the plank road while secretly crossing at Chencang,” helping Liu Bang retake the Three Qins;

- 他北上破魏、代、赵，最著名的就是背水一战：他把军队背靠河水列阵，逼将士拼死作战，以少胜多，大破赵军；

- He marched north and defeated Wei, Dai, and Zhao; his most famous victory was the Battle of the Back-to-the-River (beishui yizhan): he arrayed his troops with their backs to a river, forcing his soldiers to fight to the death, and with fewer men he routed the Zhao army;

- 他攻下齐国，在潍水之战中大败楚军；

- He took the state of Qi and crushed the Chu army at the Battle of the Wei River;

- 最后，在垓下，他指挥汉军十面埋伏，逼得项羽四面楚歌、乌江自刎。

- Finally, at Gaixia, he commanded the Han army’s ten-sided ambush, driving Xiang Yu to face “songs of Chu on every side” and take his own life at the Wu River.

汉朝的大半江山，是韩信打下来的。

More than half of the Han empire was conquered by Han Xin.

刘邦曾问他：像我这样的，能带多少兵？韩信答：陛下不过能带十万。刘邦又问：那你呢？韩信说：“臣多多而益善耳。”——我越多越好。

Liu Bang once asked him: how many troops could a man like me command? Han Xin answered: Your Majesty could command no more than a hundred thousand. Liu Bang asked again: And you? Han Xin said: “The more, the better” (chen duoduo er yi shan er)—as for me, the more the better.

这句话后来被浓缩成“韩信点兵，多多益善”。它是自信，也是他一生悲剧的伏笔。

This was later compressed into the saying “Han Xin counts his troops—the more the better.” It was confidence, and it was also the foreshadowing of his life’s tragedy.

五、“成也萧何，败也萧何”

V. “Made by Xiao He, Undone by Xiao He”

天下打完了，问题也来了。

Once the empire had been won, the problems began.

韩信的功劳太大、能力太强，刘邦对他越来越忌惮。他先是被从齐王改封为楚王，不久又被诬告谋反，贬为淮阴侯，留在京城，形同软禁。

Han Xin’s merits were too great and his abilities too strong, and Liu Bang grew ever more wary of him. First he was moved from King of Qi to King of Chu; soon after he was falsely accused of rebellion, demoted to Marquis of Huaiyin, and kept in the capital under what amounted to house arrest.

公元前196年，刘邦出征在外，吕后与萧何合谋，把韩信骗进长乐宫，杀之于钟室，并夷其三族。

In 196 BCE, while Liu Bang was away on campaign, Empress Lü and Xiao He conspired to lure Han Xin into the Changle Palace, where he was killed in the Bell Chamber and his clan was exterminated to the third degree.

“成也萧何，败也萧何”——当年是萧何把他追回来、捧上大将军之位；最后也是萧何，把他骗进了宫门，送上了死路。

“Made by Xiao He, undone by Xiao He”—it was Xiao He who had chased him back and raised him to the rank of Grand General; and in the end it was Xiao He who lured him through the palace gate and sent him to his death.

他约活了三十五岁左右。

He lived to be about thirty-five.

六、致2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

韩信的一生，是两个极端叠在一起：最深的羞辱，和最高的战功。

Han Xin’s life was two extremes laid one atop the other: the deepest humiliation and the highest military achievement.

第一，“胯下之辱”不是他丢的，是他攒的。他把那一口气咽下去，不是因为怯懦，而是因为他心里有一个更大的目标。如果当时他拔剑杀了那个屠夫，他大概率会被处死，世界上就再也没有“韩信”。有些气，值得忍；有些人，不值得陪。判断标准不是“面子有多大”，而是“你还有没有更重要的事要做”。

First, the “humiliation beneath the legs” was not something he lost—it was something he saved up. He swallowed that insult not out of cowardice but because he carried a larger goal within him. Had he drawn his sword and killed the butcher then, he would most likely have been executed, and there would have been no “Han Xin” in the world at all. Some insults are worth enduring; some people are not worth engaging. The test is not “how great is my face” but “do I still have something more important to do.”

第二，真正的才华，需要一个愿意看见你的人。韩信在项羽那里是废物，在刘邦那里是“国士无双”，他本人没有变，变的是有没有人识得他。如果你现在觉得自己被埋没，先别急着否定自己——很可能你只是还没遇到那个能“看见”你的人。

Second, true talent needs someone willing to see it. Han Xin was a nobody under Xiang Yu and “a scholar without peer” under Liu Bang; he himself had not changed—what changed was whether anyone recognized him. If you feel buried right now, do not rush to write yourself off—chances are you simply have not yet met the person who can “see” you.

第三，他的悲剧提醒我们：能力太强，也是一种风险。他用“多多益善”回答了皇帝的试探，也把自己推得更远。在复杂的处境里，清醒地判断“什么时候该收敛”，和“把事做成”同样重要。

Third, his tragedy reminds us that being too capable is itself a risk. He answered the emperor’s probing with “the more, the better,” and in doing so pushed himself further away. In complicated situations, judging clearly “when to hold back” is as important as “getting the job done.”

但无论如何——那个从别人胯下爬过去的年轻人，最终让整个天下，都从他面前走了过去。

But in the end—that young man who crawled beneath another’s legs made the whole empire, in time, pass before him.

Appendix: Sources and Notes

主要史料

Primary Sources

1. 《史记·淮阴侯列传》（韩信生平的最主要一手材料，含胯下之辱、萧何追韩信、背水一战、垓下之战等）。

1. Shiji, “The Biographies of the Marquis of Huaiyin” (Shiji: Huaiyin Hou Liezhuan)—the most important primary source for Han Xin’s life, including the humiliation beneath the legs, Xiao He’s pursuit of Han Xin, the Battle of the Back-to-the-River, and the Battle of Gaixia.

2. 《史记·高祖本纪》《史记·萧相国世家》中的相关记载。

2. Relevant passages in Shiji, “The Basic Annals of Emperor Gaozu” (Shiji: Gaozu Benji) and Shiji, “The Hereditary House of Chancellor Xiao” (Shiji: Xiao Xiangguo Shijia).

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

3. 生年不详（约公元前231年），卒于公元前196年，被吕后、萧何设计杀于长乐宫钟室，夷三族。

3. Year of birth unknown (c. 231 BCE); died in 196 BCE, killed by a plot of Empress Lü and Xiao He in the Bell Chamber of the Changle Palace, and his clan exterminated to the third degree.

4. 早年贫寒，受胯下之辱、漂母赠饭；后投项羽不用，转投刘邦，经萧何推荐登坛拜将；先后封齐王、楚王，后贬淮阴侯。

4. Poor in his early years; suffered the humiliation beneath the legs and received food from the washerwoman; later joined Xiang Yu and was not used, then switched to Liu Bang and, on Xiao He’s recommendation, was installed as general at the altar; was enfeoffed first as King of Qi, then King of Chu, and later demoted to Marquis of Huaiyin.

5. 参与指挥垓下之战，灭项羽；汉朝建立后被杀。

5. Took part in commanding the Battle of Gaixia and destroyed Xiang Yu; was killed after the Han dynasty was founded.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

6. “胯下之辱”——此记载见于《史记·淮阴侯列传》，是可靠的一手史料；但后世对其内涵（是“大勇”还是“怯懦”）有大量阐释与文学演绎，本文的解说是基于其后续行为的合理推断，非史料原文。

6. The “humiliation beneath the legs”—this account appears in Shiji, “The Biographies of the Marquis of Huaiyin,” and is a reliable primary source; but later ages produced a great deal of interpretation and literary elaboration as to its meaning (whether it was “great courage” or “cowardice”). The reading offered in this essay is a reasonable inference from his later conduct, not the wording of the source.

7. “韩信因谋反被杀”——需谨慎。吕后、萧何杀他的罪名是“谋反”，但该罪名的真实性与证据，历代史家多有质疑（司马迁对韩信谋反的记载本身就存在疑点，后世多认为其死更可能源于刘邦、吕后对其功高震主的忌惮）。文中表述为“被诬告谋反”。

7. “Han Xin was killed for rebellion”—handle with care. The charge on which Empress Lü and Xiao He killed him was “rebellion,” but historians across the ages have often questioned the truth of that charge and the evidence for it (Sima Qian’s own account of Han Xin’s rebellion contains doubtful points, and later ages generally held that his death more likely stemmed from Liu Bang’s and Empress Lü’s fear of a subject whose achievements overshadowed his sovereign). This essay phrases it as “falsely accused of rebellion.”

8. “漂母赠饭、胯下之辱、萧何月下追韩信”——前两事及萧何追韩信均见于《史记》，但故事的细节（如对话）带有叙事加工成分，宜视为《史记》所载的核心事件及其文学化叙述，而非逐字纪实的对话记录。

8. “The washerwoman’s gift of food, the humiliation beneath the legs, Xiao He chasing Han Xin by moonlight”—the first two and Xiao He’s pursuit of Han Xin all appear in the Shiji, but the details of the stories (such as the dialogue) bear the marks of narrative shaping and should be regarded as the core events recorded in the Shiji plus their literary narration, rather than verbatim records of actual conversation.

9. “韩信约活了35岁”——其生年史料未载，此说为后世推算（约前231年出生），不同推算有差异，文中已注“约”。

9. “Han Xin lived to about thirty-five”—his birth year is not recorded in the sources; this figure is a later reconstruction (born c. 231 BCE), and different reconstructions differ. The main text notes “about.”
