司马迁：受了宫刑的“太史令”

Sima Qian: The ‘Grand Astrologer’ Who Suffered Castration

——他遭受了那个时代最深的羞辱，然后把羞辱写成了“史家之绝唱”

— He endured the deepest humiliation of his age, and then turned that humiliation into ‘the supreme song of the historians’

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者40 / 100约 2603 字 · 2603 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，我们谈“职业尊严”“人格底线”。而“羞辱”这个词，在今天已经很少有它曾经的分量了。

In 2026 we talk about ‘professional dignity’ and ‘the line a person will not cross.’ And yet the word ‘humiliation’ today seldom carries the weight it once had.

两千多年前，有一个人，因为替一个战败投降的将军说了几句公道话，被处以宫刑——在那个时代，这是一种比死还难受的刑罚：不致命，却让人在宗族、社会面前彻底“不是人”。

More than two thousand years ago, a man was sentenced to castration for speaking a few fair words on behalf of a defeated general who had surrendered — in that age, a punishment harder to bear than death: not fatal, yet it left a man utterly ‘no longer human’ before his clan and his society.

他叫司马迁。他本来可以一死了之，用死来保住体面。可他选择活下来，因为还有一部书没写完。

His name was Sima Qian. He could have ended it all with death, and so preserved his dignity. But he chose to live, because a book remained unfinished.

那部书，叫《史记》。

That book was the Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian).

一、一个史官之家的使命

I. The Calling of a Historian’s House

约公元前145年，司马迁出生在左冯翊夏阳（今陕西韩城）。他的父亲司马谈，是汉武帝时期的“太史令”——掌管天文历法、记录历史的官职。

Sima Qian was born around 145 BCE in Xiayang, Zuofengyi (present-day Hancheng, Shaanxi). His father, Sima Tan, was ‘Grand Astrologer’ under Emperor Wu of Han — the office that oversaw astronomy and the calendar and kept the record of history.

他十岁开始读书，二十岁漫游天下：他南下游过江淮，去过会稽，看过屈原沉江的地方；北上到过齐鲁，探访孔子的遗迹；还去过楚汉相争的战场。他不是去游山玩水——他是在为将来写历史做实地考察。

He began his studies at ten, and at twenty set out to roam the realm: south along the Huai and the Yangtze, to Kuaiji, where he saw the place where Qu Yuan drowned himself; north to Qi and Lu, where he visited the traces of Confucius; and to the battlefields of the Chu–Han contention. He was not sightseeing — he was doing fieldwork for the history he would one day write.

父亲临终前，握着他的手，把一件心事交给了他。父亲说：我死后，你一定会继续做太史；做了太史，不要忘了我想写的那部书。从孔子算到今天四百多年，诸侯互相攻伐、史书散失，我心里一直放不下这件事。

As his father lay dying, he took Sima Qian’s hand and entrusted to him a burden long on his mind. He said: After I die, you will surely carry on as Grand Astrologer; once you hold that office, do not forget the book I meant to write. For more than four hundred years since Confucius, the feudal lords have warred among themselves and the historical records have scattered and been lost — this is a matter I have never been able to put down.

司马迁低下头，流着泪说：小子不敢忘。

Sima Qian bowed his head and, weeping, said: I, your son, dare not forget.

这份托付，成了他一生的使命。

That charge became the mission of his life.

二、李陵之祸：一封信改变一生

II. The Disaster of Li Ling: One Letter That Changed a Life

公元前108年，司马迁继任太史令。前104年，他又参与主持了“太初历”的改历工作——这是他作为官员最风光的一段日子。

In 108 BCE, Sima Qian succeeded to the post of Grand Astrologer. In 104 BCE he took part in directing the reform of the calendar known as the Taichu calendar — the most glorious period of his career as an official.

然后是公元前99年。

Then came 99 BCE.

这一年，名将李广的孙子李陵，率五千步兵出击匈奴，被八万骑兵围攻，苦战多日，箭尽粮绝，最后投降了。

That year Li Ling, grandson of the famous general Li Guang, led five thousand infantry against the Xiongnu, was surrounded by eighty thousand horsemen, fought on for days until his arrows were spent and his supplies exhausted, and at last surrendered.

消息传回长安，满朝震动。汉武帝大怒，群臣纷纷骂李陵。只有司马迁站了出来。

When the news reached Chang’an, the whole court was shaken. Emperor Wu was furious, and the ministers heaped abuse on Li Ling. Only Sima Qian stepped forward.

他说的话大意是：李陵平时孝顺父母、讲信义、廉洁谦让，是难得的将才；他这次以五千人对抗数万敌军，杀敌过万，已经尽力了；他之所以不死而降，很可能是想找机会再报效朝廷。

In substance he said: Li Ling was devoted to his parents, true to his word, honest and modest — a commander of rare quality; in this campaign he had pitted five thousand men against tens of thousands of the enemy and killed more than ten thousand of them, and had done all that could be asked of him; his choice to surrender rather than die was most likely made in the hope of finding a chance to repay the court once more.

这番话触怒了汉武帝。汉武帝认为司马迁是在影射当时带兵却无功而返的另一位将领（他的宠妃李夫人的哥哥李广利），于是把司马迁下了狱。

These words enraged Emperor Wu, who believed that Sima Qian was obliquely attacking another general who had led troops and returned without success (Li Guangli, brother of the emperor’s favorite consort, Lady Li), and he had Sima Qian thrown into prison.

按当时的法律，司马迁有两条路：一是交钱赎罪——可他家贫，拿不出那么多钱；二是接受宫刑，用受刑代替死刑。

Under the law of the time, Sima Qian had two choices: pay a fine to redeem his offense — but his family was poor and could not raise the sum — or accept castration, taking the punishment in place of the death sentence.

他选择了宫刑。

He chose castration.

他活了下来——但在那个时代，这是比死更难的一条路。

He lived — but in that age, it was a road harder than death.

三、“人固有一死”

III. ‘All Men Must Die’

受刑之后，司马迁被任命为中书令，表面上职位不低，但他的内心处在极深的煎熬里。他在给朋友任安的回信《报任安书》里，写下了那段让两千年来无数人读之泪下的话：

After the punishment, Sima Qian was appointed Palace Secretary (Zhongshuling), an office high in name; but inwardly he was in the deepest torment. In his letter of reply to his friend Ren An, the ‘Letter in Reply to Ren An’ (Bao Ren An Shu), he wrote the words that for two thousand years have moved countless readers to tears:

人固有一死，或重于泰山，或轻于鸿毛。

‘All men must die in the end; and a death may be weightier than Mount Tai, or lighter than a goose feather.’

他解释说，自己之所以忍辱偷生、接受这一切，是因为有一件事没有完成——那部书。

He explained that he had swallowed his shame and clung to life, accepting all of it, because one thing remained unfinished — the book.

他还列举了一长串先例，来给自己打气：周文王被囚而演《周易》；孔子受困而作《春秋》；屈原被放逐而赋《离骚》；左丘明失明而有《国语》；孙膑被挖去膝盖骨而修兵法……他说：

He also listed a long line of precedents to hearten himself: King Wen of Zhou was imprisoned and elaborated the Book of Changes (Zhouyi); Confucius met with hardship and composed the Spring and Autumn Annals (Chunqiu); Qu Yuan was exiled and wrote the ‘Li Sao’; Zuoqiu Ming lost his sight and produced the Discourses of the States (Guoyu); Sun Bin had his kneecaps cut out and revised the art of war… He said:

此人皆意有所郁结，不得通其道，故述往事，思来者。

‘All these men had something pent up in their hearts that they could not give free course to; and so they set down the past, and thought of those who would come after.’

意思是：这些人心里都积着一团化不开的东西，无处可放，于是就把往事写下来，留给后世的人。

The meaning is this: each of these men had something in his heart that would not dissolve and had nowhere to go; and so he wrote down the past and left it for later generations.

他把自己的苦难，放进了一个从古至今“失意者写史”的传统里。这一句话，本身就是一种自我拯救。

He placed his own suffering within a tradition, running from antiquity to his own day, of the disappointed writing history. That sentence was, in itself, a form of self-rescue.

四、一部“史家之绝唱”

IV. ‘The Supreme Song of the Historians’

出狱之后，司马迁把全部的心力，都投进了那部书。

After leaving prison, Sima Qian poured all his strength into that book.

他写成了《史记》——共一百三十篇，五十二万多字。它从传说中的黄帝，一直写到汉武帝太初年间，横跨约三千年。

He completed the Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian) — one hundred and thirty chapters, more than five hundred and twenty thousand characters. It runs from the legendary Yellow Emperor down to the Taichu era of Emperor Wu of Han, spanning some three thousand years.

它最了不起的地方，是它不再是干巴巴的“帝王家谱式”记录。司马迁：

Its greatest achievement is that it is no longer a dry, genealogical record of emperors and kings. Sima Qian:

- 为刺客、游侠、商人、医生、滑稽艺人都立了传——在他之前，这些人是不配进史书的；

- wrote biographies for assassins, wandering knights, merchants, physicians, and court jesters — before him, such people were not fit to enter the historical record;

- 他敢于批评当朝皇帝，写汉武帝好神仙、好征伐、好酷吏；

- dared to criticize the reigning emperor, writing of how Emperor Wu loved immortals, loved conquest, and loved harsh officials;

- 他写的每一个人，都有自己的脾气、选择与结局，而不是一个符号。

- made every person he wrote about a being with a temper, choices, and an ending of his own, rather than a mere cipher.

鲁迅后来用两句话评价它：“史家之绝唱，无韵之离骚”。

Lu Xun later summed it up in two lines: ‘the supreme song of the historians, a Li Sao without rhyme.’

而司马迁本人，写完书后大约在公元前86年前后去世（享年约六十岁），卒年与死因史书无载——连他自己的结局，都没有人替他记下来。

As for Sima Qian himself, he died around 86 BCE, after finishing the book (at about the age of sixty). The year and cause of his death are recorded nowhere in the histories — even his own ending went unrecorded by anyone.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

司马迁的故事，是关于“羞辱”和“完成”之间那道鸿沟的。

Sima Qian’s story is about the gulf between ‘humiliation’ and ‘completion.’

第一，他要做的，不是“忍”，而是“转”。他在《报任安书》里并没有把自己写成一个可怜的受害者，他把自己放进了一个更长的时间序列里：所有那些“意有所郁结”的人，最后都把委屈变成了作品。他选择不解释、不申辩，而是用一部书来回答一切。有些羞辱，只能用成就来还。

First, what he had to do was not to ‘endure’ but to ‘transform.’ In the ‘Letter in Reply to Ren An’ he did not write himself as a pitiable victim; he placed himself within a longer sequence of time: all those who had ‘something pent up in their hearts’ turned their grievances into works in the end. He chose not to explain, not to plead, but to answer everything with a book. Some humiliations can be repaid only with achievement.

第二，“苟活”有时候比“赴死”需要更大的勇气。在那样的处境下，死是容易的、体面的；活下来是难的、屈辱的。而他选择了难的这条路，理由只有一个：有件事还没做完。这提醒我们：当你被生活按在地上时，先问自己一句——我的那部“书”，完成了吗？

Second, ‘living on’ sometimes demands greater courage than ‘going to one’s death.’ In his situation, death was easy and honorable; to live was hard and shameful. And he chose the hard road for a single reason: something was not yet finished. This reminds us: when life has you pinned to the ground, first ask yourself one question — is my ‘book’ finished?

第三，他改写了“谁值得被记住”的标准。在他之前，历史是写给帝王将相的；在他之后，一个小人物、一个刺客、一个商人，也可能因为司马迁的一篇传记而被记两千年。他用自己的卑微，换来了无数卑微者的不朽。

Third, he rewrote the standard of ‘who deserves to be remembered.’ Before him, history was written for emperors, kings, and generals; after him, a nobody, an assassin, a merchant, might be remembered for two thousand years because of one biography by Sima Qian. With his own lowliness he purchased immortality for countless of the lowly.

他遭遇了那个时代最深的失去，却写出了那个时代最完整的记忆。

He suffered the deepest loss of his age, and yet wrote the most complete memory of that age.

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

4. 约生于公元前145年（左冯翊夏阳，今陕西韩城），约卒于公元前86年前后（卒年史无明确记载）。

5. 公元前108年继任太史令；前104年参与改历；前99年因李陵之祸下狱，受宫刑；此后任中书令，完成《史记》。

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “司马迁是李陵之祸被处宫刑的”——这是主流结论，但他获罪的具体缘由（是因“诬罔”之罪，还是因在与汉武帝的对答中被认定影射李广利）史料记载存在不同解读；文中采用“为李陵辩解而触怒汉武帝”的通行叙述。

7. ‘Sima Qian was sentenced to castration over the Li Ling affair’ — this is the mainstream conclusion, but the precise grounds for his offense (whether it was the crime of ‘deceiving the throne,’ or an imputation of oblique criticism of Li Guangli during his exchange with Emperor Wu) are variously interpreted in the sources; the text adopts the common account that he angered Emperor Wu by defending Li Ling.

8. “他受刑后担任了中书令”——据《汉书》等记载，他出狱后任中书令，此职在宫中近侍之列；但关于他受刑的具体时间（是否在入狱之后立即执行）、以及“以腐刑代死”的法律适用细节，学界仍有讨论，文中按通行说法呈现。

8. ‘After the punishment he served as Palace Secretary’ — according to the Hanshu and other records, he held the office of Zhongshuling after leaving prison, a post among the emperor’s close attendants in the palace. But the exact timing of the punishment (whether it was carried out immediately after his imprisonment), and the legal details of ‘substituting castration for death,’ are still debated among scholars; the text presents the common account.

9. “《报任安书》是写给任安的回信”——其性质与写作时间有研究讨论（一般认为写于任安因罪将死之前、司马迁晚年），但作为理解司马迁心境的第一手文本，其核心内容被广泛采信。

9. ‘The “Letter in Reply to Ren An” is a reply written to Ren An’ — its nature and date of composition are subjects of scholarly discussion (it is generally thought to have been written in Sima Qian’s later years, before Ren An’s death on a criminal charge), but as a first-hand text for understanding Sima Qian’s state of mind, its core content is widely accepted.

10. “左丘失明，厥有《国语》”——这句出自司马迁本人的《报任安书》（亦见《史记·太史公自序》），是他列举“受难者著书”传统时的一句表述；关于左丘明与《国语》《左传》的关联，学界争议极大（详见下一篇《左丘明》的辨析）。此处引用时已注明“他列举的先例”，未将其当作已被证实的史实。

10. ‘Zuoqiu Ming lost his sight, and there was the Guoyu’ — this line comes from Sima Qian’s own ‘Letter in Reply to Ren An’ (it also appears in the ‘Postface of the Grand Historian’ in the Shiji); it is one formulation in his list of the tradition of ‘sufferers who wrote books.’ The relation of Zuoqiu Ming to the Guoyu and to the Zuozhuan is fiercely debated among scholars (see the notes in the next essay, ‘Zuoqiu Ming’). In quoting it here it has been labeled as ‘the precedents he listed,’ and is not treated as established historical fact.
