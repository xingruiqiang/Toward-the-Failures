香农：定义“信息”的人，弄丢了记忆

Shannon: The Man Who Defined "Information" and Lost His Memory

——晚年他在走廊里骑独轮车、玩杂耍，被当成“江郎才尽”

— In his later years he rode a unicycle down the corridors and juggled, and was written off as a has-been

2026年，“信息”是这个星球上最基本的东西之一。每一次语音通话、每一张图片上传、每一次AI生成回答，背后都在进行一场关于“比特”的计算。而这一切通用语言的发明者，是一个后来骑着独轮车在麻省理工走廊里穿行、边骑边玩杂耍的怪人。

In 2026, "information" is one of the most fundamental things on this planet. Every voice call, every uploaded image, every answer generated by AI—behind each of them runs a calculation about "bits." And the inventor of this universal language was an odd man who would later ride a unicycle down the corridors of MIT, juggling as he went.

他叫克劳德·香农。他把“信息”变成了一门可以精确计算的科学，然后在人生后半段，他去做了一个更“没用”的人——玩机器的、耍杂耍的、造无用玩意儿的怪老头。学界一度以为他“江郎才尽”了。

His name was Claude Shannon. He turned "information" into a science that could be calculated exactly, and then, in the second half of his life, he set out to become something more "useless": a tinkerer, a juggler, a strange old man building pointless gadgets. For a time, academia assumed he had burned out.

最残酷的讽刺在后面：这位定义了信息本质的人，晚年患上了阿尔茨海默病，一点一点，丢失了自己的记忆。

The cruelest irony came later: the man who defined the nature of information developed Alzheimer's disease in his final years and, bit by bit, lost his memory.

一、一篇“史上最重要的硕士论文”

I. A Master's Thesis That Was "the Most Important Ever Written"

1916年，克劳德·香农出生在美国密歇根。他从小动手能力极强，崇拜爱迪生，还和爱迪生沾着点远亲关系。

Claude Shannon was born in Michigan in 1916. From childhood he was unusually good with his hands. He idolized Edison, and was even a distant relation of his.

1937年，21岁的香农在麻省理工写下了一篇硕士论文，题目叫《继电器与开关电路的符号分析》。他做的事听起来很抽象，但意义惊人：他把19世纪数学家布尔发明的“布尔代数”（一套只有0和1的逻辑），和电子电路里的“开与关”对应了起来。

In 1937, at twenty-one, Shannon wrote a master's thesis at MIT titled A Symbolic Analysis of Relay and Switching Circuits. What he did sounds abstract, but its significance was astonishing: he matched "Boolean algebra," a system of logic invented by the nineteenth-century mathematician Boole that consists of nothing but 0s and 1s, to the "on and off" of electronic circuits.

这一对应，等于给“用电路做逻辑运算”开了门。从这一天起，电路不再只是导电的铜丝，它可以“思考”了——你可以在电路上实现逻辑。今天所有数字电路的设计，都从这一篇论文开始。

That correspondence opened the door to "doing logic with circuits." From that day on, a circuit was no longer just copper wire carrying current; it could "think"—you could implement logic on it. Every digital circuit designed today begins with that thesis.

有人称它是“有史以来最重要的硕士论文”。1940年，他又拿下了数学博士。

Some call it "the most important master's thesis ever written." In 1940 he also earned a doctorate in mathematics.

二、1948年，他给“信息”定了量

II. In 1948, He Gave "Information" a Quantity

1941年，香农进入贝尔实验室。1948年，他发表了那篇改变世界的论文——《通信的数学理论》。

In 1941 Shannon joined Bell Labs. In 1948 he published the paper that changed the world—"A Mathematical Theory of Communication."

在这之前，“信息”是个模糊的日常词：信息多、信息少、信息重要。香农做了一件前人没人敢做的事：他把信息数学化了。

Before that, "information" was a vague everyday word: more information, less information, important information. Shannon did what no one before him had dared to do: he made information mathematical.

他提出：信息的多少，可以用“不确定性被消除的程度”来衡量。一条消息越出人意料，它携带的信息量就越大；如果一件事必然发生，它传递的信息量就是零。他为此引入了那个后来无处不在的单位——比特（bit）。

He proposed that the amount of information can be measured by how much uncertainty is removed. The more surprising a message is, the more information it carries; if something is bound to happen, the information it conveys is zero. For this he introduced the unit that has since been everywhere—the bit.

他还给出了一个著名的上限：在给定的噪声条件下，一条信道能以多快的速度可靠地传输信息（“信道容量”）。这个公式的含义是，只要没超过这个速度，再糟的信道也可以通过编码把错误几乎消干净。

He also gave a famous upper limit: given a certain level of noise, how fast a channel can transmit information reliably (the "channel capacity"). What the formula says is that as long as you stay below that rate, even a wretched channel can be encoded so that errors are all but wiped out.

从手机到卫星，从硬盘到Wi-Fi，从压缩算法到深度学习的损失函数——今天你几乎找不到哪个与信息相关的技术，不站在香农1948年的那篇论文上。有数学家说，他对数字时代的影响，相当于“字母的发明者对文学的影响”。

From phones to satellites, from hard drives to Wi-Fi, from compression algorithms to the loss functions of deep learning—today there is scarcely a technology related to information that does not stand on Shannon's 1948 paper. One mathematician said his influence on the digital age was comparable to "the influence of the inventor of the alphabet on literature."

三、他顺手开启了人工智能

III. He Also Set Artificial Intelligence in Motion

香农的“不务正业”，恰恰是另一条主线的开端。

Shannon's "neglect of his proper business" was precisely the start of another main line.

1950年，他造了一只叫“提修斯”（Theseus）的机械老鼠——它能在一个迷宫里摸索，一次一次地试错，最后记住正确路径。这是最早的“机器学习”装置之一，也是后来人工智能研究的原型。同一年，他还写了论文，讲怎么给计算机编程让它下国际象棋。

In 1950 he built a mechanical mouse named Theseus, which could grope its way through a maze, try and fail again and again, and in the end remember the correct path. It was one of the earliest "machine learning" devices and a prototype for later artificial intelligence research. That same year he also wrote a paper on how to program a computer to play chess.

1956年，他参与组织了著名的达特茅斯会议——这场会议，被公认为是“人工智能”作为一个独立学科诞生的标志。

In 1956 he helped organize the famous Dartmouth workshop—a meeting now widely regarded as the birth of "artificial intelligence" as a field of its own.

一个把信息变成科学的人，顺手给AI点了火。

A man who turned information into a science casually lit the fire of AI.

四、他主动“消失”了

IV. He Deliberately "Disappeared"

1958年，香农成为麻省理工的教授。也是从那时起，他开始把越来越少的精力放在“正经研究”上，转向了他真正喜欢的事情：

In 1958 Shannon became a professor at MIT. From then on, he began to put less and less energy into "serious research" and to turn to what he genuinely loved:

- 他在自家地下室里建了一个机械车间，做各种电动和机械的玩意儿：一列通往湖岸的缆车、一台用罗马数字运算的计算机（THROBAC）、一支会喷火的塑料小号、一个能自己复原魔方的装置；

- He built a machine shop in his basement and made all sorts of electric and mechanical contraptions: a cable car running down to the lake shore, a computer that worked in Roman numerals (THROBAC), a plastic trumpet that shot flames, and a device that could solve a Rubik's Cube on its own;

- 他做了“终极机器”——一个只有开关的盒子，你按下开关，盒子打开，伸出一只机械手把开关关掉，然后缩回去。它唯一的用途，就是否定你的操作；

- He made the "Ultimate Machine"—a box with nothing but a switch. You press the switch, the box opens, a mechanical hand reaches out, turns the switch off, and withdraws. Its only purpose is to negate what you did;

- 他痴迷杂耍，著名的场面是他骑着独轮车，在麻省理工的走廊里边骑边耍球，有时还踩着弹簧单高跷跳来跳去；

- He was obsessed with juggling. The famous image is of him riding a unicycle down the corridors of MIT while juggling balls, sometimes hopping about on a pogo stick;

- 他和爱德华·索普一起，造出了最早的“可穿戴计算机”——藏在鞋里，用来在轮盘赌上偷偷预测结果。

- Together with Edward Thorp he built one of the earliest "wearable computers"—hidden in a shoe, used to secretly predict the outcome of roulette.

1960年代，年近半百的香农逐渐淡出公众视野，甚至不再出席以他名字命名的信息论会议。很多人觉得，那个写出伟大理论的天才，已经“江郎才尽”了。

In the 1960s, approaching fifty, Shannon gradually withdrew from public view; he even stopped attending the information theory conferences named after him. Many people felt that the genius who had written that great theory had burned out.

他自己说过一句话，也许就是答案：

He once said something that may be the answer:

许多伟大的数学家，在年轻的时候就已经完成了生命中最重要的研究。

Many great mathematicians have completed their most important research while they were still young.

于他而言，重要的研究做完了，剩下的人生，他要留给自己玩的东西。

For him, the important research was done; the rest of his life he meant to keep for the things he enjoyed.

五、1985年，他突然出现在一个会议上

V. In 1985, He Suddenly Appeared at a Conference

1985年，英国布莱顿召开一场国际信息论研讨会。谁也没想到，久未露面的香农突然出现了。会场上的人一涌而上，围着他要签名。有人劝他到宴会现场讲几句，他简短地说了几句，然后从口袋里掏出三个球，开始表演杂耍。

In 1985 an international symposium on information theory was held in Brighton, England. No one expected the long-absent Shannon to appear. People at the venue surged toward him, crowding around for autographs. Someone urged him to say a few words at the banquet; he spoke briefly, then took three balls from his pocket and began to juggle.

一位在场的工程师感叹：“这简直就像牛顿出现在一场物理学会议上。”

An engineer who was there marveled: "It was as if Newton had turned up at a physics conference."

可是，此时香农的记忆力已经开始严重衰退。1990年代，他确诊了阿尔茨海默病——一种以“记忆丢失”为特征的疾病。这几乎是一种残忍的隐喻：那个用“信息”和“熵”定义了世界的科学家，正在经历他自己理论里最令人心碎的情形——存储的信息在丢失，而且没有纠错机制能救回来。

Yet by then Shannon's memory had already begun to fail badly. In the 1990s he was diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease—an illness defined by the loss of memory. It is an almost cruel metaphor: the scientist who defined the world with "information" and "entropy" was living out the most heartbreaking case in his own theory—stored information was being lost, and no error-correcting mechanism could bring it back.

2001年2月24日，香农去世，享年84岁。

On February 24, 2001, Shannon died, aged eighty-four.

六、致2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

香农的一生，看起来根本不“失败”——他被同行高度尊敬，MIT给了他教授席位，他还靠投资科技公司赚了不少钱。那为什么把他放进这本书？

Shannon's life looks anything but a "failure." He was deeply respected by his peers, MIT gave him a professorship, and he made a great deal of money investing in technology companies. So why is he in this book?

因为他代表了一种特别容易被误解的“失败”：他在人生后半段，选择去做“看起来毫无意义”的事，被主流视为退步。

Because he represents a kind of "failure" that is especially easy to misread: in the second half of his life he chose to do things that "seemed utterly meaningless," and the mainstream took it for decline.

在任何一个以产出衡量人的评价体系里，晚年的香农都是“不合格”的：不写论文了，不追热点了，不带团队冲顶会了，整天玩独轮车和杂耍。可恰恰是这个人，用他的前半生，给出了整个数字时代的地基；又用他的后半生，示范了什么叫“一个人可以只为好奇心活着”。

In any system that measures a person by output, the late Shannon was "not up to standard": no more papers, no chasing of the latest trends, no leading a team to storm the top conferences, just unicycles and juggling all day. And yet it was precisely this man who, with the first half of his life, laid the foundation of the entire digital age, and then, with the second half, showed what it means to live for curiosity alone.

他有一句话，值得每个被KPI追着跑的人抄下来：

He said one thing that is worth copying out for anyone chased down by KPIs:

我受到好奇心的驱动。我从不追求经济利益。我只是想知道，事物是怎么组合在一起的。

I was driven by curiosity. I never pursued financial gain. I simply wanted to know how things fit together.

这对2026年的我们，是很重要的一课。今天我们被效率、变现、流量、“有没有用”推着走，几乎所有的选择都要先问“这有什么用”。而香农给了一个相反的答案：真正改变世界的研究，往往开始于一个没人要求、也没人看好的问题。

For us in 2026, that is a very important lesson. Today we are pushed along by efficiency, monetization, traffic, and the question "is it useful?" Almost every choice must first answer "What is this good for?" Shannon gave the opposite answer: the research that truly changes the world often begins with a question that no one asked for and no one thought promising.

他被当成“江郎才尽”的那些年，其实是一个自由的人，最自在的那些年。

The years in which he was written off as a has-been were in fact the freest, most at ease years of a free man's life.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

8. “香农晚年被学界公认为'江郎才尽'”——这是一种流传的说法/外界观感，并非学界正式结论。事实是他在1960年代后有意识地减少公开学术活动、转向个人兴趣，他自己也说过“许多伟大数学家年轻时已完成最重要研究”。文中按“被当成/被误解为”处理，未表述为定论。

8. "Shannon was universally considered by academia to have burned out in his later years"—this is a circulating claim, or an outside impression, not a formal conclusion of the scholarly community. The fact is that after the 1960s he deliberately cut back his public academic activity and turned to personal interests, and he himself said that "many great mathematicians complete their most important research when young." The main text treats this as "was regarded as / was misread as," not as a settled judgment.

9. “香农发明了'比特'一词”——需注意：他是把“比特”（bit）这一术语与概念系统化、并用于信息度量的人；该词的语源来自“binary digit”的缩写，香农在1948年论文中正式采用并推广。文中表述为“引入那个无处不在的单位——比特”。

9. "Shannon invented the word 'bit'"—note: he was the one who systematized the term and the concept of the "bit" and applied it to the measurement of information; the word's etymology is as a contraction of "binary digit," and Shannon formally adopted and popularized it in his 1948 paper. The main text words it as "introduced that ubiquitous unit—the bit."

10. 关于他与爱德华·索普共同发明“可穿戴计算机（预测轮盘赌）”，为有据可查的记载；但“最早期可穿戴设备”的具体归属在技术史上有不同讨论，文中按“最早的之一”处理。

10. That he and Edward Thorp jointly invented a "wearable computer" (to predict roulette) is a documented account; but the precise attribution of the "earliest wearable device" has been debated in the history of technology, and the main text treats it as "one of the earliest."

11. “达特茅斯会议=人工智能的诞生地”为通俗说法，该会议被公认为AI作为学科的标志性起点，但并非唯一源头，文中已作“被公认”的限定。

11. "The Dartmouth workshop = the birthplace of artificial intelligence" is a popular shorthand; the meeting is widely recognized as the landmark starting point of AI as a discipline, but it was not the only source, and the main text has already qualified it with "widely regarded as."
