王小波：生前书难出版，死后“封神”的自由灵魂

Wang Xiaobo: A Free Spirit Who Could Hardly Get Published in Life and Was Canonized After Death

——“一个人只拥有此生此世是不够的，他还应该拥有诗意的世界。”

——"To possess only this life and this world is not enough; one should also possess a poetic world."

2026年，王小波已经是一个文化符号了。他的书长期畅销，他的话被做成无数张图片，在社交媒体上被一遍遍转发。年轻人引用他，说他“有趣”“清醒”“是一个真正自由的人”。

In 2026, Wang Xiaobo is already a cultural icon. His books have long been bestsellers; his words have been turned into countless images and forwarded over and over on social media. Young people quote him, saying he was "funny," "clear-eyed," "a truly free person."

但在他活着的时候，情况完全不是这样。

But while he was alive, things were entirely different.

他的小说，在大陆很难出版。他自己说，他的作品像是“被扔进了一个黑洞”。他在四十多岁的时候突然去世，死因是心脏病突发。他去世后，他的作品才开始被大量出版，然后迅速形成了一种文化现象——“王小波热”。

His fiction was very hard to publish on the mainland. He himself said his work seemed to have been "thrown into a black hole." He died suddenly in his forties, of a heart attack. Only after his death did his work begin to be published in large quantities, quickly forming a cultural phenomenon — "Wang Xiaobo fever."

他活着的时候，几乎没尝过“被广泛认可”的滋味。

In his lifetime, he scarcely had a taste of what it meant to be "widely recognized."

一、在云南插队的北京少年

I. A Beijing Boy Sent to the Countryside in Yunnan

1952年5月13日，王小波出生在北京。他的家庭是知识分子家庭。

On May 13, 1952, Wang Xiaobo was born in Beijing. His family was an intellectual one.

1969年，十七岁的他去了云南插队。那是一段极其艰苦的岁月：繁重的农活，单调的生活，精神上的压抑。他在那里待了几年，后来又在山东、在北京做过各种工作。

In 1969, at seventeen, he went to Yunnan as a "sent-down youth." Those were extremely hard years: heavy farm labor, a monotonous life, spiritual repression. He stayed there several years, and later took various jobs in Shandong and back in Beijing.

这段经历，后来成了他最重要的写作素材。 他小说里那些荒诞、苦中作乐、在压抑的环境里依然想要活得像个人的年轻人，都有他自己的影子。

This experience later became his most important source of writing material. The absurd, bittersweet young characters in his fiction — who, in a repressive environment, still want to live like human beings — all carry his own shadow.

1978年，他考进了中国人民大学，学的是贸易经济系的商品学专业。这不是他真正想学的东西。毕业后他留校任教，后来又去了美国，在匹兹堡大学读东亚研究。

In 1978, he was admitted to Renmin University of China, to study commodity science in the Department of Trade and Economics. It was not what he really wanted to study. After graduating he stayed on to teach, and later went to the United States to study East Asian studies at the University of Pittsburgh.

1988年，他回国，先后在北京大学和中国人民大学任教。

In 1988 he returned to China, teaching first at Peking University and then at Renmin University.

二、一个“不正经”的作家

II. An "Unserious" Writer

在学术圈里，王小波是个怪人。他不喜欢那套规范、严肃、端着的写法。他写小说，用的是极其口语化、极其幽默、甚至有点“痞”的语言。

In academic circles, Wang Xiaobo was an oddity. He disliked that regulated, solemn, self-important way of writing. In his fiction he used an extremely colloquial, extremely humorous, even slightly roguish language.

他最有名的是《黄金时代》。这部小说写的是“文革”时期，一个北京知青在云南插队时的经历。但它不是那种控诉式的“伤痕文学”。王小波的处理方式是：用一种近乎嬉皮笑脸的语气，去写最荒诞、最痛苦的事。

His most famous work is Huangjin Shidai (The Golden Age). The novel is about the experience of a Beijing sent-down youth in Yunnan during the Cultural Revolution. But it is not the accusatory "scar literature" of the period. Wang Xiaobo's approach was to write about the most absurd and painful things in a tone that was almost flippant and grinning.

他的逻辑是：既然这个世界的荒诞已经超过了想象，那就只能笑着把它写出来。

His logic was this: since the absurdity of this world has already exceeded the imagination, then you can only write it out laughing.

他还写《白银时代》《青铜时代》，也写杂文——《沉默的大多数》《我的精神家园》。他的杂文犀利、直接、不装，谈的是常识，用的是人人看得懂的话。

He also wrote Baiyin Shidai (The Silver Age) and Qingtong Shidai (The Bronze Age), as well as essays — Chenmo de Daduoshu (The Silent Majority) and Wo de Jingshen Jiayuan (My Spiritual Homeland). His essays were sharp, direct, and unpretentious; they discussed common sense, in words anyone could understand.

他反复讲的一个主题是：一个正常的社会，应该允许人做一个“有脑子”的人。

One theme he returned to again and again: a normal society should allow a person to be someone "with a mind of his own."

他还特别擅长拆穿那些“假大空”的说法。他不跟你争论高深的理论，他只用最平常的话问你：这件事，按常识看，是不是有问题？他把“讲道理”变成了一件轻松、有趣、甚至有点解气的事。 这就是为什么他的杂文能流传——它们不是训斥，更像是一个朋友坐下来，用大白话跟你说点实在的。

He was also especially good at puncturing "empty, grandiose" rhetoric. He would not argue high-flown theory with you; he would simply ask, in the most ordinary words: by common sense, is there something wrong with this? He turned "reasoning things out" into something light, enjoyable, even a little cathartic. That is why his essays traveled — they are not scoldings, but more like a friend sitting down to tell you something real in plain speech.

三、那个“发不出去”的困境

III. The Predicament of "Not Being Able to Get It Out"

但王小波的作品，在他生前很难出版。

But Wang Xiaobo's work was very hard to publish in his lifetime.

他的小说《黄金时代》最早是在台湾获奖并出版的——1991年，它获得了《联合报》文学奖的中篇小说大奖。但在大陆，出版一再受阻。

His novel Huangjin Shidai (The Golden Age) first won an award and was published in Taiwan — in 1991 it won the novella prize of the United Daily News literary awards. But on the mainland, publication was repeatedly blocked.

原因很复杂：他的写法太“不正经”，他的内容太“不主流”，他不愿意把作品改成别人期待的样子。

The reasons were complicated: his style was too "unserious," his content too "off the mainstream," and he was unwilling to revise his work into the shape others expected.

他一度几乎放弃了靠写作生活的想法。 1992年，他从人民大学辞职，成为自由撰稿人。这是一个非常冒险的决定——没有单位，没有稳定收入，全靠稿费。但写作对他来说，是不可放弃的。

At one point he nearly gave up the idea of living by his writing. In 1992 he resigned from Renmin University and became a freelance writer. It was a very risky decision — no work unit, no steady income, only manuscript fees. But writing was something he could not give up.

他一边给报刊写杂文（这是他能被发表的部分），一边继续写他的小说。他的杂文集后来成了很多人的“精神启蒙读物”，因为那些文章讲的是一个人人都能听懂的道理：别被大词唬住，用你自己的脑子想想。

He wrote essays for newspapers and magazines (this was the part of his work that could get published) while continuing to write his fiction. His essay collections later became a kind of "spiritual primer" for many people, because those articles made a point anyone could grasp: don't be cowed by big words — use your own head and think.

四、1997年4月11日

IV. April 11, 1997

1997年4月11日，王小波在北京的住处因心脏病突发去世。他四十五岁。

On April 11, 1997, Wang Xiaobo died of a sudden heart attack at his home in Beijing. He was forty-five.

他死的时候，他的重要作品，还没能在大陆正式出版。

At the time of his death, his important work had still not been formally published on the mainland.

他的妻子李银河当时正在英国。发现他时，他已经去世。据回忆，他死在电脑旁，电脑里还有没写完的稿子。

His wife, Li Yinhe, was in England at the time. By the time he was found, he had already died. According to recollections, he died beside his computer, which still held an unfinished manuscript.

一个写了那么多字、讲了那么多常识、被那么多后来的年轻人当作精神偶像的人，就这样在一个人的房间里，突然走了。

A man who had written so many words, spoken so much common sense, and been taken by so many later young people as a spiritual idol — just like that, alone in a room, he was suddenly gone.

五、死后的“王小波热”

V. The "Wang Xiaobo Fever" After His Death

王小波去世后，事情发生了戏剧性的变化。

After Wang Xiaobo's death, things changed dramatically.

他的作品开始被大量出版。《黄金时代》《沉默的大多数》《我的精神家园》一本接一本地印出来，越卖越好。他的名字迅速从一个小圈子里的“怪才”，变成了一个大众文化现象。

His work began to be published in great quantity. Huangjin Shidai (The Golden Age), Chenmo de Daduoshu (The Silent Majority), Wo de Jingshen Jiayuan (My Spiritual Homeland) — printed one after another, selling better and better. His name rapidly turned from a "quirky talent" known within a small circle into a mass cultural phenomenon.

“王小波热”这个词，就是在他死后才出现的。

The phrase "Wang Xiaobo fever" appeared only after his death.

为什么？也许是因为，他讲的那些东西——独立思考、反对盲从、拒绝说假话、用幽默对抗荒诞——在他活着的时候，还没有足够多的人准备好去理解。而在他死后，越来越多的人开始感到，他说的正是自己想说的。

Why? Perhaps because the things he talked about — independent thinking, standing against blind conformity, refusing to lie, using humor against absurdity — were not yet ready to be understood by enough people while he was alive. And after his death, more and more people began to feel that what he said was exactly what they themselves wanted to say.

他的读者越来越多，本质上是因为，越来越多的人变成了“沉默的大多数”。

His readers grew ever more numerous, essentially because more and more people had become "the silent majority."

这也是“死后封神”这件事最讽刺的地方：他不是“因为死了才被看见”，而是“时代终于走到了能够理解他的位置”。 他的死是一个突然的句号，但真正让他的作品被接受的，是此后十几年里，整个社会观念的缓慢松动。

This is also the most ironic thing about his "canonization after death": he was not "seen only because he died," but "the times had finally arrived at the point where they could understand him." His death was a sudden full stop, but what truly made his work acceptable was the slow loosening of the whole society's ideas over the decade and more that followed.

六、他到底留下了什么

VI. What, in the End, He Left Behind

王小波留下的东西，很难简单概括。

What Wang Xiaobo left behind is hard to sum up simply.

他留下的是一种态度：不装、不怯、不随大流。他谈问题从不引经据典，而是像一个正常人一样讲道理。他讨厌虚假的崇高，也讨厌廉价的犬儒。

He left an attitude: never pretend, never be afraid, never follow the crowd. In discussing any question he never cited the classics; he reasoned like an ordinary person. He detested fake loftiness, and cheap cynicism too.

他留下的是一种趣味：他证明了“严肃的思考”和“幽默的表达”可以不矛盾。一个人可以既认真，又好玩。

He left a sensibility: he proved that "serious thinking" and "humorous expression" need not contradict each other. A person can be both earnest and a delight.

他还留下了一种活法：一个人可以不进体制、不当大官、不挣大钱，就靠写自己想写的东西活着——虽然这条路他走得非常艰难。

He also left a way of living: a person can stay outside the system, hold no high office, make no great fortune, and live simply by writing what he wants to write — though he walked that road with great difficulty.

他最著名的一句话，也许就是那句“一个人只拥有此生此世是不够的，他还应该拥有诗意的世界”。

His most famous line is perhaps this: "To possess only this life and this world is not enough; one should also possess a poetic world."

七、写给2026年的我们

VII. To Us in 2026

王小波在今天被大量引用，其实是有风险的——因为他反对的那些东西，恰恰以新的形式回来了。

The fact that Wang Xiaobo is quoted so much today actually carries a risk — because the very things he opposed have come back in new forms.

他反对盲从，而今天我们更容易被算法和情绪带着走；他反对说假话，而今天的社交网络里，包装和表演成了常态；他强调“独立思考”，而今天我们接收信息的量，是前人的千百倍，但真正“自己想过”的比例，可能并没有提高。

He opposed blind conformity, yet today we are more easily led by algorithms and emotion; he opposed lying, yet on today's social networks packaging and performance have become the norm; he stressed "independent thinking," yet the amount of information we take in today is a thousand times that of earlier generations, while the proportion we have truly "thought through ourselves" may not have risen at all.

所以，读王小波，不该只是摘几句金句发朋友圈。他真正的意思是：你能不能不去迎合任何人，只对你自己心里的那个判断负责？

So reading Wang Xiaobo should not be merely a matter of plucking a few quotable lines to post on social media. What he really meant was: can you stop pandering to anyone, and answer only to the judgment in your own heart?

他活着的时候，市场没有给他机会。但时间给了他机会。这或许是所有“不被时代接受”的写作者，最想听到的一种安慰。

While he was alive, the market gave him no chance. But time gave him his chance. This is perhaps the consolation that every writer "not accepted by his time" most longs to hear.

还有一点：王小波的作品之所以能穿越时间，不是因为他讲了什么惊天动地的大道理。恰恰相反，他讲的都是“本来就应该这样”的常识——人应该有脑子，说话应该算数，不该为了迎合谁而扭曲自己。在一个常识经常被忘记的年代里，反复讲常识，本身就是一件了不起的事。 他讲了十几年，讲得轻松、好笑、不留情面。然后人们发现：他说的那些，我们其实一直都知道，只是很少有人愿意一直说。

One more point: the reason Wang Xiaobo's work can travel through time is not that he laid down some earth-shattering grand truth. On the contrary, everything he said was common sense about "how things ought to be anyway" — a person should have a mind, one's word should count, one should not twist oneself to please anyone. In an age when common sense is often forgotten, to keep repeating common sense is itself a remarkable thing. He did it for more than a decade, lightly, amusingly, without sparing anyone's feelings. And then people discovered: the things he said, we had known all along — only few had been willing to keep saying them.

他去世的那一年，他的作品才刚刚开始被更多人读到。此后二十多年，他的读者越来越多。有人说，这是因为“时代终于赶上了他”。我更愿意换一个说法：不是时代赶上了他，而是每一代新长大的人，都要重新遇到一次王小波——然后发现，自己心里那些说不出口的别扭，早就被他轻轻松松地写出来了。

In the year he died, his work was only just beginning to be read by more people. Over the twenty-plus years that followed, his readers grew ever more numerous. Some say this is because "the times finally caught up with him." I would rather put it another way: it is not that the times caught up with him, but that every newly grown generation must encounter Wang Xiaobo once again as if for the first time — and then discover that the awkwardness in their own hearts, which they could not put into words, had long ago been written out by him, with effortless ease.
