舒伯特：31岁饿着肚子死去的“歌曲之王”

Schubert: The "King of Song" Who Died Hungry at Thirty-One

——他一生写了六百多首歌，却连一顿饭都要拿乐谱去换

— He wrote more than six hundred songs in his lifetime, yet had to trade his sheet music for a single meal

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者31 / 100约 2583 字 · 2583 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，一首歌能不能火，几天内就有答案。播放量、热搜、榜单，所有的努力都被即时地“看见”。

In 2026, whether a song will catch on is answered within days. Play counts, trending lists, charts — every bit of effort is instantly "seen."

可一百多年前有一个人，写了六百多首艺术歌曲——“艺术歌曲之王”这个称号，今天写在他所有唱片的封面上。而他活着的时候，这些歌大多没被印出来，很多只在维也纳一小圈朋友之间流转。他穷到什么程度？据说，有一首作品的稿费，只值一顿饭。

But a little over a century ago there lived a man who wrote more than six hundred art songs — and today the title "King of Song" stands on the cover of every recording of his music. In his own lifetime, most of those songs were never printed; many circulated only among a small circle of friends in Vienna. How poor was he? It is said that the fee for one of his compositions was worth no more than a single meal.

他叫弗朗茨·舒伯特。他31岁就死了。去世后才慢慢被人发现：原来那个“没红”的年轻人，写下了西方音乐史上最动人的一批旋律。

His name was Franz Schubert. He was dead at thirty-one. Only after his death did people slowly discover that this young man who had never "made it" had written some of the most moving melodies in the history of Western music.

一、一个辞职去“当自由艺术家”的年轻人

I. A Young Man Who Quit His Job to Become a "Free Artist"

1797年，舒伯特出生在维也纳近郊。他父亲是一所小学的校长。17岁那年，为了免服兵役，他在父亲的学校里当了个教学助理，给低年级孩子上课，拿一点微薄的薪水。

Schubert was born in 1797 on the outskirts of Vienna. His father was the headmaster of a primary school. At seventeen, to be exempted from military service, he took a post as a teaching assistant at his father's school, teaching the younger children for a meager salary.

但他真正想做的是音乐。1818年，他辞掉了教职，做了一件在当时几乎等于“自绝生路”的事：当一个不依附于宫廷、不依附于贵族的“自由音乐家”。

But what he really wanted to do was music. In 1818 he gave up teaching and did something that at the time amounted almost to "cutting off his own livelihood": he became a "free musician," attached to neither court nor nobility.

在19世纪初的维也纳，音乐家通常靠三种活法：受雇于宫廷、受雇于教会，或者受雇于某个贵族。舒伯特三样都没有。他靠的是卖作品的微薄稿酬、偶尔的演出，以及——朋友的接济。

In Vienna at the start of the nineteenth century, a musician usually lived in one of three ways: employed by a court, employed by the church, or employed by some nobleman. Schubert had none of the three. He lived on the slender fees from selling his works, the occasional performance, and — the charity of friends.

他有一群关系极好的朋友，后人称“舒伯特圈子”。这群人凑在一起弹琴、唱歌、谈诗。舒伯特常常是那个弹钢琴的、把刚写完的歌当场合唱出来的人。他没有自己的房子，长期借住在朋友家；没有稳定的收入，常年入不敷出。

He had a very close group of friends whom later generations called the "Schubert circle." They would gather to play piano, sing, and talk about poetry. Schubert was usually the one at the keyboard, singing through a song he had just finished. He had no house of his own and lodged for long stretches with friends; he had no steady income and was perennially short of money.

二、他的产量高得不像话

II. His Output Was Almost Absurd

舒伯特的创作速度，近乎惊人。

Schubert's speed of composition was close to astonishing.

1815年，他18岁，这一年他写下了一百四十多首艺术歌曲，其中包括《魔王》。据说10月15日那一天，他一个人就写完了八首作品。

In 1815, at the age of eighteen, he wrote more than a hundred and forty art songs, among them "Erlkönig" (The Erl-King). It is said that on October 15 alone he completed eight works by himself.

他一生写了六百多首歌曲、多部交响曲、室内乐和钢琴作品。最著名的包括：《魔王》《鳟鱼》《死神与少女》《美丽的磨坊女》《冬之旅》，以及那部被称为《未完成》的第八交响曲。

Over his lifetime he wrote more than six hundred songs, several symphonies, and works of chamber and piano music. The best known include "Erlkönig" (The Erl-King), "Die Forelle" (The Trout), "Death and the Maiden," "Die schöne Müllerin" (The Fair Maid of the Mill), "Winterreise" (Winter Journey), and the Eighth Symphony known as the "Unfinished."

问题是：这些东西在他活着的时候，几乎没有产生什么影响。交响曲没怎么被演奏过，歌剧也没成功。他自己也不太擅长“推销”自己——同时代的贝多芬会主动经营自己的公众形象，舒伯特不会。有人形容他“太固执、太自我”。

The problem was that during his lifetime these works made almost no impact. His symphonies were rarely performed; his operas failed. Nor was he much good at "selling" himself — Beethoven, his contemporary, actively managed his public image; Schubert did not. Some described him as "too stubborn, too wrapped up in himself."

三、一个“没赶上好时候”的人

III. A Man Born at the Wrong Time

1814年，他爱上了一位纺织厂主的女儿特丽莎，但对方最后嫁给了一个面包师。这是他一生唯一一次真正的恋爱。后来，他把感情寄托在学生、女伯爵卡洛林身上，为她写了不少钢琴作品。

In 1814 he fell in love with Therese, the daughter of a textile-mill owner, but in the end she married a baker. It was the only real love of his life. Later he transferred his affections to a pupil, Countess Caroline, for whom he wrote a number of piano works.

1822年，命运给了他更重的一击：他开始与梅毒作斗争。这在当时是一种难以治愈、且被认为极度羞耻的病。他的健康从此一路恶化，情绪也越来越极端——朋友记载他长期在狂喜与深深的绝望之间反复。

In 1822 fate struck him a heavier blow: he began his struggle with syphilis. At the time this was a disease that was hard to cure and considered deeply shameful. His health declined steadily from then on, and his moods grew more and more extreme — friends recorded that he swung for long periods between exaltation and profound despair.

1828年3月26日，在纪念贝多芬逝世一周年的日子里，朋友们凑钱，为舒伯特办了他生前唯一一场个人作品音乐会。这场音乐会取得了巨大的成功。

On March 26, 1828, on the first anniversary of Beethoven's death, friends pooled their money to give Schubert the only concert of his own works held in his lifetime. It was a great success.

可这成功来得太晚了。八个月后，1828年11月19日，舒伯特病逝，年仅31岁。官方给出的死因是伤寒，但后人多认为，他的身体早已被梅毒（以及当时用来治疗梅毒的含汞药物）拖垮。

But the success came too late. Eight months later, on November 19, 1828, Schubert died of illness at just thirty-one. The official cause was typhoid fever, but later generations have largely held that his body had long since been worn down by syphilis — and by the mercury-based remedies then used to treat it.

按照他的遗愿，他被葬在他一生崇拜的偶像贝多芬的墓旁。两位伟大的音乐家，最终成了邻居。

In keeping with his last wishes, he was buried beside the grave of Beethoven, the idol he had revered all his life. The two great composers ended up as neighbors.

四、为什么他值得被写进“失败者”里

IV. Why He Deserves a Place Among the "Failures"

舒伯特的“失败”，不是“没才华”，而是才华与时代完全错位。

Schubert's "failure" was not a lack of talent; it was a complete mismatch between his talent and his age.

他的音乐太“私人”了：他写的是一个人独处时的感受，是诗与旋律之间的悄悄话。而在那个年代，公众要的是炫技、是大场面、是歌剧。他的作品，其实是写给“未来的人”听的。

His music was too "private": he wrote the feelings of a person alone, the whispered exchange between a poem and a melody. But in that era the public wanted virtuosity, spectacle, opera. His works were in fact written for "future listeners."

更心酸的是，他并不自知自己有多重要。他甚至不把自己当成一个“作曲家”，顶多算一个“喜欢音乐的人”。他一生贫困，连自己的大部分作品都没能亲眼看到出版。

More heartbreaking still, he had little sense of his own importance. He did not even regard himself as a "composer" — at most as "a man who loved music." He was poor all his life and never saw most of his own works published.

他死后，孟德尔颂、舒曼、李斯特、勃拉姆斯等一批作曲家陆续发掘并推介他的作品，舒伯特才被真正“发现”，并逐渐被公认为西方音乐史上最伟大的作曲家之一。

After his death, a succession of composers — Mendelssohn, Schumann, Liszt, Brahms — unearthed and championed his music. Only then was Schubert truly "discovered," and gradually recognized as one of the greatest composers in the history of Western music.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

舒伯特的故事，对活在流量时代的我们，几乎是一记直球。

Schubert's story is almost a fastball delivered straight to those of us living in the age of traffic and clicks.

我们太习惯用“有没有反馈”来给一件事打分：发出去的歌有没有人听、写的字有没有人看、做的项目有没有人买。舒伯特告诉你的是另一种可能：你做的事可能对，但反馈可能迟到很久，久到超出你的一生。

We are far too used to grading a thing by whether it gets a response: whether the song we release is heard, whether the words we write are read, whether the project we build is bought. What Schubert shows us is another possibility: what you are doing may well be right, but the response may arrive very late — so late that it outlasts your whole life.

他一生没等到自己音乐被广泛演奏的那一天，可今天全世界都在唱他写的旋律。他没赢在他的时代，他赢在了时间。

He never lived to see the day his music was widely performed, yet today the whole world sings the melodies he wrote. He did not win in his own age; he won in time.

对2026年的我们，有两句话。

For us in 2026, there are two things to say.

第一，别把“当下没人懂”直接翻译成“我做的没价值”。舒伯特那六百首歌，当时只是朋友聚会上的几把琴、几个人的合唱；一百年后，它们成了人类共有的财富。价值是长出来的，不是被即时点赞出来的。

First, do not translate "no one understands this right now" directly into "what I'm doing has no value." Those six hundred songs of Schubert's were, at the time, just a few instruments and a handful of voices at a friend's gathering; a hundred years later they became the shared wealth of humankind. Value is grown, not instantly liked into being.

第二，穷和难，不等于停。舒伯特最穷的几年，也是他产出最猛的几年。他没等到环境变好才动笔，他在环境最糟的时候写了最好的东西。

Second, being poor and being hard-pressed does not mean stopping. Schubert's poorest years were also his most productive. He did not wait for better conditions before putting pen to paper; he wrote his best work when conditions were at their worst.

如果你此刻正在做一件没人鼓掌的事，请记住：有些人的听众，还没出生。

If right now you are doing something no one applauds, remember this: some people's audience has not yet been born.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

9. “舒伯特死于伤寒”与“死于梅毒”——病历记载的官方/直接死因为伤寒（typhoid fever），但他自1822年起患梅毒，且长期使用含汞药物治疗，晚期症状与汞中毒相似。学界主流看法是：梅毒（及其治疗）严重损害了他的健康，而伤寒可能是最后的直接死因，但由于证据不足，无法作出确定诊断。文中已并列两种说法并注明“不确定”。

9. "Schubert died of typhoid" versus "Schubert died of syphilis" — the official/immediate cause recorded in the medical notes was typhoid fever, but he had suffered from syphilis since 1822 and had long taken mercury-based treatment, and his late symptoms resembled mercury poisoning. The mainstream scholarly view is that syphilis (and its treatment) seriously damaged his health, while typhoid may have been the final immediate cause; but the evidence is insufficient for a definitive diagnosis. The main text sets the two accounts side by side and marks them as uncertain.

10. “他的歌只换了一顿饭”——此为流传的说法，并无精确的文献账目支撑；但它反映了舒伯特生前稿酬极少、生活窘迫的真实状况，文中用“据说”限定。

10. "One of his songs was traded for a single meal" — this is a circulating claim with no precise documentary accounting behind it; but it reflects the reality of how little Schubert was paid and how straitened his life was. The main text qualifies it with "it is said."

11. “舒伯特一生只爱过一次/被拒绝”——关于特丽莎·葛罗伯的记载主要来自其友人回忆，细节难考；文中按“有记载的初恋”处理，未作绝对化表述。

11. "Schubert loved only once in his life / was rejected" — the accounts of Therese Grob come mainly from the recollections of friends, and the details are hard to verify. The main text treats the episode as a documented first love and does not state it absolutely.

12. “他死后才被出版”——需修正：他生前已有一批作品出版（约上百首），只是与他全部作品的总量相比只占很小部分，且交响曲、歌剧等大部头长期未获重视。文中已表述为“大部分作品生前未能出版/未能产生重要影响”，避免绝对化。

12. "His works were only published after his death" — this needs correction: a body of his works (roughly a hundred songs or more) was published during his lifetime, though it amounted to only a small fraction of his total output, and the large works such as symphonies and operas went long neglected. The main text phrases this as "most of his works were not published in his lifetime / did not make an important impact," avoiding absolutes.
