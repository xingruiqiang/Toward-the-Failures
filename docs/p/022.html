洛夫莱斯：写下人类第一段程序的“诗人女儿”

Lovelace: The Poet's Daughter Who Wrote Humanity's First Program

——她的名字，被遗忘了整整一百年

— Her name was forgotten for a full hundred years

← 返回主页 Home第一辑　生不逢时的先行者22 / 100约 2952 字 · 2952 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，程序员是一个庞大而日常的职业。你每天用的每一款软件、每一次搜索、每一段推荐算法，背后都有人在写代码。而“代码”这个东西，第一次被人想象出来，是在一台从未被造出来的机器上。

In 2026, programmer is a vast and everyday profession. Every piece of software you use each day, every search you run, every recommendation algorithm you scroll past—behind each one, someone is writing code. And "code" itself was first imagined on a machine that was never built.

写出它的，是一位生活在维多利亚时代的英国女性。她不是工程师，甚至不是职业科学家。她是拜伦勋爵的女儿，是一位伯爵夫人。她留下了一份算法，然后把这个星球上还没存在的机器，想象了整整一百年后的样子。

The person who wrote it was a British woman of the Victorian era. She was not an engineer, nor even a professional scientist. She was the daughter of Lord Byron, and a countess. She left behind an algorithm, and then imagined the machine this planet did not yet have as it would be a full century later.

然后，她的名字，被遗忘了整整一百年。

And then her name was forgotten for a full hundred years.

一、一个被母亲“治好了诗意”的孩子

I. A Child Whose Mother "Cured" Her of Poetry

1815年12月10日，奥古斯塔·艾达·拜伦出生在伦敦。她的父亲，是当时英国最著名的浪漫派诗人拜伦勋爵。但她的父母在她出生一个多月后就分居了，她从未真正认识自己的父亲。

On December 10, 1815, Augusta Ada Byron was born in London. Her father was Lord Byron, the most famous Romantic poet in England at the time. But her parents separated little more than a month after her birth, and she never truly knew her father.

她的母亲安娜贝拉，是个热衷数学的女性。她担心女儿会遗传拜伦家族那种“危险的诗性”“不羁的情绪”，于是做了一个决定：用数学、逻辑和科学，把女儿培养成一个理性的人。

Her mother, Annabella, was a woman devoted to mathematics. Fearing that her daughter would inherit the Byron family's "dangerous poetic temperament" and "unruly passions," she made a decision: to raise her daughter into a rational person through mathematics, logic, and science.

这个决定有点狠，但它意外地塑造了历史上第一位程序员：艾达从小学习数学、逻辑、音乐，在19世纪，这些学科对女性几乎是不开放的禁区。她的天赋惊人，也继承了父亲那种澎湃的想象力——只不过，她的想象力最后落在了机器上。

The decision was a little harsh, but it accidentally shaped the first programmer in history: Ada studied mathematics, logic, and music from childhood—subjects that in the nineteenth century were nearly closed territory for women. Her gifts were astonishing, and she had also inherited her father's surging imagination—except that her imagination came to rest, in the end, on machines.

1833年，18岁的艾达参加了一场聚会，遇到了查尔斯·巴贝奇。那一年，巴贝奇刚提出了“差分机”的设想——一台用齿轮和曲柄做计算的机械计算机。两人一见如故。此后几十年，这段忘年之交，成了计算机史最初的起点。

In 1833, at eighteen, Ada attended a party and met Charles Babbage. That year Babbage had just put forward his idea for the "Difference Engine"—a mechanical computer that did its calculations with gears and cranks. The two took to each other at once. Over the decades that followed, this friendship across the generations became the earliest starting point of the history of computing.

二、为一台不存在的机器写程序

II. Writing a Program for a Machine That Did Not Exist

1835年，艾达嫁给了威廉·金，后来丈夫受封为洛夫莱斯伯爵，她成了洛夫莱斯伯爵夫人。

In 1835, Ada married William King, who was later created Earl of Lovelace; she thus became the Countess of Lovelace.

1842年，意大利军事工程师梅纳布雷亚写了一篇介绍巴贝奇“分析机”的文章，是法文。1843年，艾达把它翻译成英文，发表在英国的《科学回忆录》上。巴贝奇读了之后，鼓励她加上自己的注解。

In 1842, the Italian military engineer Menabrea wrote an article in French introducing Babbage's "Analytical Engine." In 1843 Ada translated it into English and published it in the British journal Scientific Memoirs. After reading it, Babbage urged her to add notes of her own.

就是这个决定，让她名垂青史。

It was that decision that made her name immortal.

艾达的注解，篇幅比原文本身还长得多。其中最著名的一条（后人称“注G”），她详细写出了一套用分析机计算“伯努利数”的步骤——每一步都精确到机器该怎么操作。

Ada's notes ran far longer than the original article itself. In the most famous of them (later called "Note G"), she set out in detail a set of steps for computing the "Bernoulli numbers" on the Analytical Engine—each step precise down to how the machine was to be operated.

这份注解，被后世公认为人类历史上第一段真正意义上的计算机程序。

This note is recognized by later generations as the first genuine computer program in human history.

更惊人的是她的洞察。巴贝奇本人和其他人，大多只把分析机当作一台“高级计算器”，用来算数字。而艾达看到了更远的东西：她说，只要能用符号表示，机器就能操作它——它可以处理字母、符号，甚至可以作曲。

Even more striking was her insight. Babbage himself and others mostly regarded the Analytical Engine as a "high-grade calculator," good for crunching numbers. Ada saw further: she said that anything that could be represented by symbols, the machine could operate on—it could handle letters, symbols, and even compose music.

在一台机器还没被造出来的年代，她已经在描述一台“通用计算机”能做什么。她还提出了“循环”（重复一组指令）和子程序的思想——今天几乎所有编程语言里都在用的东西。

In an age when the machine had not yet been built, she was already describing what a "general-purpose computer" could do. She also put forward the ideas of the "loop" (repeating a set of instructions) and the subroutine—things used in nearly every programming language today.

三、一个天才的另一面

III. The Other Side of a Genius

艾达不是圣人，她的后半生充满坎坷。

Ada was no saint, and the second half of her life was full of hardship.

她从小体弱，十几岁时曾因一场怪病卧床近三年。她患有哮喘和胃病，长期服用含鸦片成分的镇痛药，渐渐成瘾。她一度执迷于赌马，想用数学去“战胜概率”，结果欠下巨额赌债——晚年甚至因为赌资问题，不得不典当家里的珠宝，一度陷入非常难堪的境地。

She was frail from childhood, and in her teens a strange illness kept her bedridden for nearly three years. She suffered from asthma and a stomach ailment, and long took opium-based painkillers, gradually becoming addicted. For a time she was obsessed with betting on horses, hoping to "beat probability" with mathematics, and ended up deep in gambling debts—in her later years she even had to pawn the family jewels to cover her gambling losses, sinking at one point into a deeply humiliating position.

她与巴贝奇也并非一直和睦，两人因资金、专利和“分析机该不该造”的问题，有过争执。

Her relationship with Babbage was not always harmonious either; the two quarreled over funding, patents, and whether the Analytical Engine should be built at all.

1852年11月27日，艾达因子宫颈癌去世，年仅36岁。临终前，她按照当时的风俗，被放血治疗，也没能挽回病情。她被安葬在诺丁汉，就在她素未谋面的父亲拜伦的墓旁——一对没有真正相处过的父女，最终以这种方式相邻。

On November 27, 1852, Ada died of cervical cancer at the age of only thirty-six. On her deathbed, following the custom of the day, she was treated by bloodletting, which could not save her. She was buried at Nottingham, beside the grave of the father she had never met, Byron—a father and daughter who had never truly been together, side by side at last in this way.

而她的那篇译文和注解，当时几乎没有引起科学界的注意。人们只当是一位伯爵夫人赶时髦的业余爱好。

And her translation and notes drew almost no attention from the scientific community at the time. People took them for nothing more than the fashionable amateur pastime of a countess.

四、一百年后，世界回来了

IV. A Hundred Years Later, the World Came Back

真正重新“发现”艾达，要等到二十世纪。

Ada would not be truly "rediscovered" until the twentieth century.

1940年代，图灵在构想最早的现代计算机时，读过她的注解，并受到启发。此后，随着计算机科学成为一门独立的学科，人们回头翻找历史，才发现那个在1843年就写下算法、还预言了“机器可以作曲”的人，是一位女性。

In the 1940s, while conceiving the earliest modern computers, Turing read her notes and was inspired by them. Later, as computer science became a discipline in its own right, people went back through the history and discovered that the person who had written an algorithm in 1843, and even predicted that "a machine could compose music," was a woman.

1953年，她那份“注G”被重新印出，被正式确认为世界上第一份计算机程序。1980年，美国国防部把历时多年研制的一种高级编程语言，命名为“Ada”。每年10月的第二个星期二，是“艾达·洛夫莱斯日”，专门用来鼓励更多女性投身科学、技术、工程和数学。

In 1953, her "Note G" was reprinted and formally recognized as the world's first computer program. In 1980, the U.S. Department of Defense named a high-level programming language, developed over many years, "Ada." The second Tuesday of every October is "Ada Lovelace Day," dedicated to encouraging more women to enter science, technology, engineering, and mathematics.

我们今天说“第一位程序员”，是后世的*追认*。在艾达活着的时候，没有任何人这样称呼她——她只是一个喜欢数学、爱想机器、又时常被病痛和债务困住的年轻女子。

Our calling her "the first programmer" today is a retroactive designation by later generations. While Ada was alive, no one called her that—she was simply a young woman who loved mathematics, loved to think about machines, and was often trapped by illness and debt.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

艾达的故事，是一种很特别的“失败”：她不是被谁打败的，她是生得太早，早到她做的一切都无处安放。

Ada's story is a very particular kind of "failure": she was not defeated by anyone. She was born too early—so early that everything she did had nowhere to belong.

她为一个不存在的机器写程序。那台分析机，直到她死后一百多年才在博物馆里被造出模型。她的代码，在一个连电都还没普及的年代，是彻头彻尾的“无用之物”。

She wrote programs for a machine that did not exist. The Analytical Engine would not even be built as a model in a museum until more than a century after her death. Her code, in an age when electricity itself was not yet widespread, was a "useless thing" through and through.

但正是这种“无用”，定义了什么叫前瞻。她那些注解里最珍贵的东西，不是“算伯努利数”的步骤，而是她那个念头——这东西不止会算数，它什么都可能做。

But it is precisely this "uselessness" that defines what it means to be far-sighted. The most precious thing in those notes of hers is not the steps for "computing the Bernoulli numbers" but the thought she had—this thing would not just do arithmetic; it might do anything.

这给今天的我们两句话。

This leaves us today with two things to say.

第一，想象力是可以比技术先到的。你不需要等到工具齐全，才能开始思考它的可能性。艾达留给世界的，其实是一个“想象力的许可证”：任何能被符号化的东西，机器都能替你操作一遍。

First: imagination can arrive before the technology. You do not need to wait until the tools are all in place before you begin to think about the possibilities. What Ada left the world was, in fact, a "license for the imagination": anything that can be symbolized, a machine can operate on for you.

第二，别用“有没有用”来评判一个人此刻做的事。她当年的注解被认为没用，她本人被认为是业余爱好者，她的名字被遗忘了整整一百年。但我们今天的每一次“自动运行”，都要回溯到她。

Second: do not judge what a person is doing right now by whether it is "useful." Her notes were thought useless at the time, she herself was thought an amateur, and her name was forgotten for a full hundred years. Yet every "automatic run" we perform today leads back to her.

所以，如果你此刻正在学一门冷门的东西，做一件看不出回报的事，或者写一段只有你自己觉得重要的东西——请记住艾达。她替一台不存在的机器写下了第一行程序，然后安静地等了这个世界一百年。

So if right now you are studying something obscure, doing something with no visible payoff, or writing a passage that matters only to you—remember Ada. She wrote the first line of a program for a machine that did not exist, and then quietly waited a hundred years for the world.

有些人做的不是当下的工作，是给未来预留的接口。

Some people are not doing the work of the present; they are leaving an interface open for the future.

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

6. “洛夫莱斯是公认的'第一位程序员'”——这是后世的追认，尤其经20世纪计算机史研究确立；她生前及19世纪无人如此称呼她，巴贝奇也未曾如此定位她。文中已明确其为“追认的称号”。

6. "Lovelace is the universally acknowledged 'first programmer'"—this is a retroactive designation by later generations, established especially by twentieth-century research into the history of computing; neither in her lifetime nor at any point in the nineteenth century did anyone call her this, and Babbage never positioned her as such either. The main text makes clear that it is a "retroactive title."

7. “她只是拜伦的女儿、一个业余爱好者”——与她实际贡献不符，但也需注意：她的数学工作是真实的，其注解的原创性与深度在不同学者间讨论颇多（如 Fuegi & Francis 2003 等曾有不同评价）。主流学界仍承认她“注G”的程序史地位与“机器可处理符号、可作曲”的前瞻判断。

7. "She was merely Byron's daughter, an amateur"—this does not match her actual contribution, but note too that her mathematical work was real, and the originality and depth of her notes have been much debated among scholars (Fuegi & Francis 2003, for instance, offered a different assessment). Mainstream scholarship still recognizes the place of her "Note G" in the history of programming and her far-sighted judgment that "a machine could process symbols and compose music."

8. 关于她晚年“赌马欠债”“鸦片成瘾”等，见诸多种传记与史料（如 ETHW、Historic UK），属有据可查的记载；但具体欠债数额、成瘾程度在不同材料间细节不一，文中按通说简述。

8. As for her later "gambling debts" and "opium addiction" and the like, these appear in numerous biographies and sources (such as ETHW and Historic UK) and are documented accounts; but the exact amount of the debts and the degree of the addiction vary in detail from one source to another, so the main text summarizes them according to the standard account.

9. “艾达·洛夫莱斯在1844年写了第一段程序”（部分英文资料如此表述）——具体年份应为1843年（译文与注解发表之年），文中取1843年。

9. "Ada Lovelace wrote the first program in 1844" (as some English-language sources put it)—the correct year should be 1843 (the year the translation and notes were published); this chapter follows 1843.
