海伦·凯勒：被认定“无法教育”的盲聋女孩

Helen Keller: The Deaf-Blind Girl Deemed “Uneducable”

——她一生被贴过两个标签：先是“没救的残废”，后是“太危险的激进分子”

— Her whole life she wore two labels: first “a hopeless cripple,” then “a dangerous radical”

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者29 / 100约 2544 字 · 2544 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，我们谈到残疾人保障、无障碍设计、特殊教育，会觉得很自然。可在一百多年前，一个既看不见、又听不见、还不会说话的孩子，在大多数人的判断里，是“没有未来”的。

In 2026, we take disability rights, accessible design, and special education for granted. But more than a century ago, a child who could neither see nor hear nor speak was, in the judgment of most people, someone with “no future.”

海伦·凯勒就是那个孩子。她十九个月大时失去了视力和听力，六岁时被周围的人判定“不可教育”。可她后来考上了大学、写了十几本书、走遍了全世界。更有意思的是：她后半生最大的“罪名”，不是残疾，而是她的观点——她公开支持社会主义、反战、妇女投票权，被当时的报纸说成是“危险的激进分子”。

Helen Keller was that child. She lost her sight and hearing at nineteen months, and by the age of six the people around her had declared her “uneducable.” Yet she went on to enter college, write more than a dozen books, and travel the world. More interesting still: the gravest charge against her in the second half of her life was not her disability but her opinions — she openly supported socialism, opposed war, and campaigned for women’s suffrage, and the newspapers of the day branded her a “dangerous radical.”

她一生收集了两种标签。两种都贴错了。

Her life gathered two labels. Both were affixed wrongly.

一、黑暗里那个不肯安静的孩子

I. The Child Who Would Not Be Quiet in the Dark

1880年，海伦·凯勒出生在美国亚拉巴马州。她本来是个健康、伶俐的孩子，可十九个月大时，一场高烧（可能是猩红热或脑膜炎）夺走了她的视力和听力。

Helen Keller was born in Alabama in 1880. She was a healthy, bright child, but at nineteen months a high fever — possibly scarlet fever or meningitis — took away her sight and her hearing.

一个既看不见又听不见的孩子，很快也不会说话了。她没有语言，就无法理解世界，也无法被世界理解。她后来回忆，那几年她像困在一片无边无际的黑雾里：她会用触觉分辨妈妈嘴唇的动作，知道别人在说话，却不知道他们在说什么。

A child who could neither see nor hear soon stopped speaking as well. Without language she could not understand the world, and the world could not understand her. She later recalled those years as being trapped in a boundless black fog: she could feel her mother’s lips moving and knew that people were speaking, but she did not know what they were saying.

她发过很多脾气，摔东西、尖叫。家人几乎要放弃，一度考虑把她送到残疾人收容机构去——在那个年代，“送走”往往就等于“一辈子关在里面”。

She threw many tantrums, smashing things and screaming. Her family came close to giving up and at one point considered sending her to an institution for the disabled — and in those days, to be “sent away” usually meant to be shut inside for life.

转机出现在1887年。这一年，家里请来了一位二十一岁的年轻老师——安妮·沙利文。沙利文自己也几乎失明，刚从一所盲人学校毕业。

The turning point came in 1887. That year the family hired a young teacher of twenty-one — Anne Sullivan. Sullivan herself was nearly blind and had just graduated from a school for the blind.

二、那一声“水”

II. That One Word, “Water”

沙利文做的第一件事，谁也没想到：她不在海伦手上直接“写字”，而是在她手心里，用拼写的方式，一个字一个字地拼给她。

The first thing Sullivan did was something no one expected: rather than “writing” letters directly on Helen’s hand, she spelled things out for her, letter by letter, into her palm.

海伦能摸到，但不明白这些动作是什么。整整几个星期，她是一个在黑暗里抓不到意义的影子。

Helen could feel the movements but did not understand what they were. For weeks on end she was a shadow in the dark, grasping at a meaning she could not catch.

直到那天，她们在院子里打水。清凉的水流过海伦的手。沙利文一边让水流过，一边在海伦的另一只手心里，反复拼出“water”这个词。

Then one day they were drawing water in the yard. The cool water flowed over Helen’s hand. As it ran across, Sullivan spelled the word “water” again and again into Helen’s other palm.

海伦后来回忆：就在那一刻，她“忽然想起了什么”，一种被遗忘的东西从她心底醒来。她懂了——流过手心的这个东西，就叫 water。

Helen later recalled that in that moment she “suddenly remembered something,” and a forgotten thing woke deep inside her. She understood — this thing flowing over her palm was called water.

那一天，她学会了那一天的第一个词，之后又学会了三十个词。她说，那一刻，“世界突然向我打开了”。

That day she learned her first word, and after that thirty more. She said that in that moment “the world suddenly opened to me.”

从那天起，海伦像一台超速运转的机器：用手摸嘴唇学说话，用手指读盲文，用触觉感受世界的每一处细节。她后来能用指尖“听懂”别人说话，靠的是摸对方嘴唇和喉咙的震动。

From that day on Helen ran like an over-revving machine: she learned to speak by feeling lips with her hand, read braille with her fingers, and sensed every detail of the world through touch. She could later “understand” other people’s speech through her fingertips, by feeling the vibrations of their lips and throats.

三、她把“不可能”读成了一所大学

III. She Read “Impossible” Into a University

1900年，海伦被拉德克利夫学院（哈佛的附属学院）录取。1904年，她以优异的成绩毕业，成为世界上第一位获得大学学位的盲聋人。

In 1900 Helen was admitted to Radcliffe College (Harvard’s affiliated college). In 1904 she graduated with honors, becoming the first deaf-blind person in the world to earn a university degree.

一个在六岁时还被认为是“不可教育”的孩子，用十四年时间，读完了大学。

A child still deemed “uneducable” at six finished a university education in fourteen years.

她还写了书。1903年出版的《我的一生》（The Story of My Life），让她成了全世界家喻户晓的名字。人们把她当成一个“奇迹”，一个“战胜了残缺”的榜样。

She wrote books, too. The Story of My Life, published in 1903, made her a household name around the world. People saw her as a “miracle,” a model who had “overcome her defects.”

但海伦很快就发现，这个“榜样”的框子，她不喜欢。

But Helen soon discovered that she did not like the box this “model” came in.

四、她不想只当一个“感人的故事”

IV. She Did Not Want to Be Only a “Moving Story”

成名之后，海伦没有去安享“励志偶像”的光环。她把精力投向了她真正关心的问题：

After she became famous, Helen did not go off to bask in the glow of being an “inspirational idol.” She turned her energy toward the questions she genuinely cared about:

- 她支持妇女争取投票权；

- She supported women’s suffrage;

- 她支持社会主义，公开为工人和被剥削者说话；

- She supported socialism and spoke out publicly for workers and the exploited;

- 她反对战争，反对军备竞赛；

- She opposed war and the arms race;

- 她参与创建美国公民自由联盟，支持有色人种争取权利的组织。

- She helped found the American Civil Liberties Union and supported organizations fighting for the rights of people of color.

在那个年代，一个盲聋女性说这些话，是“越界”的。当时的报纸开始反过来咬她。有评论说，她的激进言论是“被操纵”的结果，因为她听不见、看不见，所以她是被身边人“灌输”的。她的回应很沉着：她研究过、思考过，并且因此更加愤怒——她看得比很多人更清楚，真相是什么。

In that era, for a deaf-blind woman to say such things was “overstepping.” The newspapers turned on her. Some commentators said her radical remarks were the product of “manipulation” — because she could neither hear nor see, she had been “indoctrinated” by those around her. Her reply was composed: she had studied the questions, she had thought them through, and for that very reason she was all the angrier — she saw more clearly than most what the truth was.

她一生去过几十个国家，为盲人和聋人争取权益。她还推动了一个在当时几乎没人敢碰的话题：把“残疾人”当成完整的人，而不是需要被怜悯的“对象”。

In her lifetime she visited dozens of countries, fighting for the rights of the blind and the deaf. She also pushed a topic that almost no one dared touch at the time: treating “the disabled” as whole human beings rather than as “objects” of pity.

1968年，海伦·凯勒去世，享年87岁。

Helen Keller died in 1968, at the age of 87.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

海伦·凯勒的故事常常被讲成一个“奇迹”：一个残障女孩在老师的帮助下，变成了伟人。

Helen Keller’s story is usually told as a “miracle”: a disabled girl who, with her teacher’s help, became a great person.

但这只是故事的一半。另一半更重要，也更少人提——她在被世界“同情”之后，拒绝只做那个被同情的人。

But that is only half the story. The other half matters more and is mentioned far less — after the world had “pitied” her, she refused to be only the one who was pitied.

她完全可以就此当一个“励志传奇”，靠巡回演讲的掌声活一辈子。可她选择去做一个“不受欢迎”的人：说工人该被保护、说战争是错的、说女人该有投票权。她放弃了体制想给她的位置，去换取她说真话的自由。

She could easily have spent her life as an “inspirational legend,” living on the applause of the lecture circuit. Instead she chose to be an “unpopular” person: to say that workers deserved protection, that war was wrong, that women deserved the vote. She gave up the place the establishment wanted to grant her, in exchange for the freedom to tell the truth.

对2026年的我们，这留下两句话。

For us in 2026, this leaves two sentences.

第一，不要让别人对你的定义，变成你自己的上限。“残废”“不可教育”“危险分子”——这些标签都被贴在她身上，也都错了。真正定义她的，从来不是别人的判断，而是她每天多学的那几个词。

First, do not let other people’s definition of you become your own ceiling. “Cripple,” “uneducable,” “dangerous radical” — every one of those labels was stuck on her, and every one was wrong. What truly defined her was never anyone else’s judgment, but the few extra words she learned each day.

第二，被同情，不等于被尊重。她一生中最了不起的，或许不是考上大学，而是她拒绝用“可怜”去交换“被接纳”，坚持说出自己真正相信的话。

Second, being pitied is not the same as being respected. Perhaps the most remarkable thing in her life was not that she got into college, but that she refused to trade “pity” for “acceptance,” and insisted on speaking what she truly believed.

她说过一句很轻但很重的话，大意是：世界上最美的东西，不必看见、不必听见，也能感受到。这句话，是她在黑暗中摸索出来交给我们的。

She said something very light and very heavy, to the effect that the most beautiful things in the world need not be seen or heard to be felt. That sentence is what she groped toward in the dark and handed to us.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

6. “海伦·凯勒19个月时的高烧病因”——不同史料记载为猩红热或脑膜炎（一说“急性脑充血”），无法确证，文中已注明为“可能”。

6. “The cause of Helen Keller’s high fever at 19 months” — sources differ, recording scarlet fever or meningitis (one says “acute cerebral congestion”); it cannot be confirmed, and the text notes it as “possibly.”

7. “那一声'water'的顿悟”——这是海伦本人自传中的著名叙述，属第一人称回忆，学界对此类“顿悟式”叙事的文学加工成分有讨论；核心事实（在打水时首次理解“词—物”对应关系）被广泛采信，文中按其自述呈现。

7. “The epiphany at the word ‘water’” — this is the famous account in Helen’s own autobiography and is a first-person recollection; scholars have discussed the degree of literary invention in such “epiphany” narratives. The core fact (that she first grasped the word-thing correspondence while drawing water) is widely accepted, and the text presents it as she told it.

8. “她被报纸攻击为'危险分子'”——需注意，这主要是针对她社会主义与反战的政治立场，而非她的残疾；当时确有媒体（如《布鲁克林鹰报》等）质疑她的激进言论，甚至暗示她被操纵。文中据实处理，未将其简化为“因残疾受歧视”。

8. “She was attacked by the papers as a ‘dangerous element’” — note that this was aimed mainly at her socialist and anti-war political stance, not at her disability. Some media of the time (such as the Brooklyn Eagle) did question her radical remarks and even hinted that she was being manipulated. The text handles this faithfully and does not reduce it to “discrimination because of disability.”

9. “她差点被送进收容所”——有记载称童年时家人曾考虑将她送往盲人机构，并曾向机构咨询；具体细节（是否达成、时间）在不同材料间有差异，文中采用“一度考虑”的谨慎表述。

9. “She was nearly sent to an institution” — some accounts say her family considered sending her to an institution for the blind and consulted one; the details (whether it happened, and when) differ across sources, and the text uses the cautious phrasing “at one point considered.”
