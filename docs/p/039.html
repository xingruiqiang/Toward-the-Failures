徐霞客：被当成“游手好闲”的旅行家

Xu Xiake: The Traveler Mistaken for an Idler

——他不考功名、不当官，走遍大半个中国，手稿却险些在战火中烧光

— He never sat the examinations, never took office, walked across most of China — and his manuscript nearly burned in the flames of war

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者39 / 100约 2642 字 · 2642 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，“不务正业”这四个字，依然是一种很重的指责。一个年轻人不去考公、不去上班，整天“到处跑”，很难不被亲戚念叨。

In 2026, the words ‘not doing proper work’ still carry the weight of a serious accusation. A young person who does not sit the civil service exam, does not take a job, and spends every day ‘running around’ will hardly escape an earful from relatives.

可四百年前有一个人，就是顶着这样的眼光活了一辈子。他叫徐霞客。他家境不错，却一辈子不考科举、不做官，从二十二岁起出门，一走就是三十四年。在当时人眼里，他就是个“不事生产”的怪人。

But four hundred years ago, one man lived his whole life under exactly that kind of judgment. His name was Xu Xiake. Though his family was comfortably off, he never sat the imperial examinations and never held office; he left home at twenty-two and kept going for thirty-four years. To the eyes of his contemporaries, he was an oddity — a man who produced nothing.

而他留下的那部《徐霞客游记》，后来被钱谦益称为“千古奇书”。它记录的东西，比西方同类研究早了几百年。

And the book he left behind, the Xu Xiake Youji (The Travel Diaries of Xu Xiake), was later called by Qian Qianyi ‘a wonder book of all ages.’ What it recorded predates comparable Western studies by several hundred years.

他的“失败”是当时社会给的定价；他的价值，是时间给的。

His ‘failure’ was a price set by the society of his day; his worth was set by time.

一、他为什么不考功名

I. Why He Refused the Examinations

1587年，徐霞客出生在江苏江阴。他名弘祖，字振之，号霞客——“霞客”这个名字，后来比他本名更有名。

Xu Xiake was born in 1587 in Jiangyin, Jiangsu. His given name was Hongzu, his courtesy name Zhenzhi, and his sobriquet Xiake — a name that would come to be far better known than his formal one.

他家是当地的读书人家庭，有田产，日子过得去。按常理，他该走那条所有人都在走的路：读书、应考、做官。

His was a local family of scholars, with land and a decent living. By the ordinary logic of the time, he should have taken the path everyone took: study, sit the examinations, hold office.

但他没有。少年时他就“有壮游之志”，喜欢读地理、方志一类的书。他后来自己说过一段话，大意是：我宁可做一个穿着布衣、拄着竹杖、穿着草鞋的普通人，走遍河沙、登上昆仑，在绝域中题名——能与张骞、玄奘这些人并列，我死也无憾。

But he did not. As a youth he already ‘had the ambition for grand travels,’ and loved reading books on geography and local gazetteers. He later said, in substance: I would rather be an ordinary man in plain cloth, leaning on a bamboo staff and wearing straw sandals, to travel the rivers and sands, climb the Kunlun, and leave my name in lands beyond the frontier — if I could stand alongside the likes of Zhang Qian and Xuanzang, I would die without regret.

他心里那条路，从来不是官场，而是地图。

The road in his heart was never the corridors of office, but the map.

二、三十四年，一直在路上

II. Thirty-Four Years, Always on the Road

从二十二岁（约1609年）开始，徐霞客几乎把一生都花在了旅行上。

From the age of twenty-two (around 1609), Xu Xiake spent nearly his entire life traveling.

他不是那种舒适的“游山玩水”。他大多是徒步，常常只带一个仆人、一根竹杖、一双草鞋。他走的范围极广，足迹遍及今天的北京、河北、山东、河南、江苏、浙江、福建、山西、江西、湖南、湖北、广西、云南、贵州等十几个省份。

His was not the comfortable sort of ‘sightseeing among hills and waters.’ He mostly went on foot, often with nothing but a single servant, a bamboo staff, and a pair of straw sandals. His range was enormous: his footprints covered more than a dozen of today’s provinces — Beijing, Hebei, Shandong, Henan, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian, Shanxi, Jiangxi, Hunan, Hubei, Guangxi, Yunnan, and Guizhou.

路上有多难？史料里有很具体的记载：

How hard was the road? The historical sources record it in concrete detail:

- 他多次断粮，穷到“蔬米俱尽，而囊无一文”，只能拿朋友送的绸布，去村里换几筒米；

- He ran out of food again and again, at one point so poor that ‘the vegetables and rice were all gone and there was not a coin in his purse’; he had to trade silk cloth given by a friend for a few measures of rice in a village;

- 1637年，他在湘江上遇到强盗，船被劫，他自己光着身子跳水逃生，脱险后一无所有，只好向同乡借贷，才把旅程继续下去；

- In 1637, robbers fell upon him on the Xiang River; his boat was plundered, and he escaped by leaping into the water stark naked. Having lost everything, he had to borrow from fellow townsmen before he could continue his journey;

- 他还曾用一块官方的“马牌”支使村民为他和仆人抬轿——这件事在史料中也留下了争议，被认为有“扰民”之嫌。

- He once used an official horse-pass to order villagers to carry sedan chairs for him and his servant — a matter that remains disputed in the sources, seen as an imposition on the common people.

他不是圣人，他就是一个把旅行当成命的人。

He was no saint. He was simply a man for whom travel was life itself.

三、他到底发现了什么

III. What He Actually Discovered

如果徐霞客只是“走得远”，那他还不足以被称为“奇人”。真正了不起的，是他边走路边做研究。

If Xu Xiake had merely ‘traveled far,’ he would not deserve to be called a ‘remarkable man.’ What is truly extraordinary is that he did research as he walked.

- 他研究石灰岩地貌（喀斯特地貌）：他对溶洞的分布，对石钟乳、石笋、溶沟、石芽的成因，都有详细的考察与解释。在他所处的年代，世界上还没有人像他这样系统地研究过岩溶地貌——他是当之无愧的第一人。

- He studied limestone landforms (karst terrain): he gave detailed observation and explanation of the distribution of limestone caves and of the formation of stalactites, stalagmites, karren, and lapies. In his day, no one in the world had studied karst landforms as systematically as he did — he is indisputably the first.

- 他纠正了“岷山导江”：中国古代最权威的地理文献《尚书·禹贡》说“岷山导江”，认为长江的源头在岷山。徐霞客通过实地考察，指出长江真正的上源应该是金沙江，源出昆仑之南。

- He corrected the doctrine that ‘the Min Mountains give rise to the Yangtze’: the most authoritative geographical text of ancient China, the Yugong (Tribute of Yu) chapter of the Shangshu (Book of Documents), stated that ‘the Min Mountains give rise to the Yangtze,’ holding that the river’s source lay in the Min Mountains. Through field investigation, Xu Xiake pointed out that the true upper source of the Yangtze should be the Jinsha River, rising south of the Kunlun.

第二件事有多重要？《禹贡》是儒家经典，地位极高。徐霞客用脚步推翻经典，等于在学术上“犯上”。正因如此，他的游记在当时的学界并不受欢迎，甚至有人专门写文章驳斥他——清初学者全祖望还写了一篇《江源辨》来反对他的结论。

How important was this second achievement? The Yugong is a Confucian classic of the highest standing. To overturn a classic with one’s own footsteps amounted to academic insubordination. For this very reason, his travel diaries found little favor among the scholars of his day, and some even wrote essays to refute him — the early Qing scholar Quan Zuwang wrote a piece titled ‘A Discourse on the Source of the River’ to oppose his conclusion.

四、手稿的九死一生

IV. The Manuscript’s Narrow Escapes from Death

1640年，徐霞客在云南因足疾不能行走，被地方官用轿子送回江阴。第二年，1641年，他在家中去世，年五十六岁。

In 1640, unable to walk because of a foot ailment, Xu Xiake was carried home to Jiangyin in a sedan chair by local officials. The following year, 1641, he died at home at the age of fifty-six.

临终前，他把游记手稿托付给了家庭教师季梦良，希望他能整理成书。

On his deathbed, he entrusted the manuscript of his travel diaries to his family tutor, Ji Mengliang, hoping he would organize it into a book.

然后，灾难来了。1645年，清军南下，江阴爆发了惨烈的“江阴八十一日”抗清斗争。徐霞客的长子徐屺在战乱中遇难，《徐霞客游记》的原始手稿，大部分被烧毁在战火里。

Then disaster struck. In 1645, as Qing forces swept south, the ferocious ‘Eighty-One Days of Jiangyin’ resistance broke out. Xu Xiake’s eldest son, Xu Qi, died in the turmoil, and most of the original manuscript of the Travel Diaries was burned in the flames.

季梦良没有放弃。他和儿子连夜抄录，抢出了一份残本。后来，徐霞客的私生子李寄又四处寻访，从宜兴找到了一个更早的抄本，把散失的部分一点点补回来，重新编成《徐霞客游记》。

Ji Mengliang did not give up. He and his son copied through the nights and salvaged a damaged copy. Later, Xu Xiake’s illegitimate son, Li Ji, searched far and wide, found an earlier copy in Yixing, and piece by piece restored what had been lost, compiling the Travel Diaries anew.

即便如此，这部书直到1776年（乾隆四十一年），才由徐霞客的族孙徐镇首次雕版刊印——距徐霞客去世，已经过去一百三十多年。

Even so, the book was not printed from woodblocks until 1776 (the forty-first year of the Qianlong reign), when Xu Xiake’s kinsman Xu Zhen published it — more than a hundred and thirty years after Xu Xiake’s death.

我们今天能读到它，靠的是一连串几乎不可能发生的接续：一个托付、一次战火、一场抄书、一个私生子的寻访。

That we can read it today rests on a chain of nearly impossible continuities: a deathbed trust, a war, a campaign of copying, and the search of an illegitimate son.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

徐霞客的一生，是给所有“不走寻常路”的人的一份证明。

Xu Xiake’s life is a testament to everyone who refuses to walk the beaten path.

第一，他输了“社会的评分”，却赢了“时间的评分”。在他那个年代，一个不考功名、不当官、整天往外跑的人，就是“不务正业”。可四百年后，他被称为“千古奇人”，被和李时珍、徐光启、宋应星并列为明代的大科学家。评价标准会变，你做的事是不是真的有意义，不会变。

First, he lost the ‘score of his society’ but won the ‘score of time.’ In his era, a man who sat no examinations, held no office, and was always off traveling was simply ‘not doing proper work.’ Yet four hundred years later he is called ‘a marvel of all ages,’ ranked alongside Li Shizhen, Xu Guangqi, and Song Yingxing as one of the great scientists of the Ming. The standards of judgment change; whether what you do truly means something does not.

第二，真正的热爱，是愿意为它吃苦的。他不是在“打卡旅行”，他是在用自己的身体，丈量、记录、校正这个国家的地理。断粮、遇盗、跳江逃生，他都扛下来了。热爱不是“我喜欢”，而是“我愿意为它受这个罪”。

Second, true passion means being willing to suffer for it. He was not ‘checking off’ destinations; he measured, recorded, and corrected the geography of his country with his own body. Running out of food, meeting robbers, leaping into a river to survive — he bore it all. Passion is not ‘I like it’; it is ‘I am willing to endure this hardship for it.’

第三，你留下的东西，可能比你以为的更脆弱，也更顽强。他的稿子差点被一把火烧光，却靠几个人的接力活了下来。这提醒我们：如果你真的做出了重要的东西，请认真把它保存好、交代给值得托付的人。文明的传递，有时候就系在这样一些琐碎的决定上。

Third, what you leave behind may be more fragile — and more tenacious — than you think. His manuscript nearly burned to nothing, yet survived through the relay of a few hands. This reminds us: if you have truly made something important, take care to preserve it, and to entrust it to those who deserve the charge. The transmission of civilization sometimes hangs on small decisions like these.

他一辈子没做过一件“正经事”，却成了中国地理学史上绕不开的名字。

He never did a single ‘respectable thing’ in his life, yet he became a name no history of Chinese geography can pass over.

Appendix: Sources and Notes

3. Qian Qianyi, ‘A Life of Xu Xiake’; Pan Lei’s preface to the Xu Xiake Youji; Chen Hanhui’s ‘Epitaph for Xu Xiake.’

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

4. 生于1587年（南直隶江阴），卒于1641年（江阴），享年56岁。

4. Born 1587 (Jiangyin, Southern Zhili), died 1641 (Jiangyin), aged fifty-six.

5. 约22岁起出游，游历时间前后约34年；足迹遍及16省。

5. Began traveling at about age twenty-two; traveled for roughly thirty-four years in all; his footprints covered sixteen provinces.

6. 1640年在云南因足疾被送回江阴；手稿于1645年“江阴八十一日”战乱中大部分被焚；1776年由族孙徐镇首次刊印。

6. In 1640, brought back to Jiangyin from Yunnan because of a foot ailment; most of the manuscript burned in the warfare of the ‘Eighty-One Days of Jiangyin’ in 1645; first printed in 1776 by his kinsman Xu Zhen.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “他推翻了《禹贡》'岷山导江'、指出长江源出金沙江”——这是徐霞客最重要的地理发现之一，有游记原文为证；但需注意，他给出的“源出昆仑之南的金沙江”仍与现代地理学的正源判断（唐古拉山各拉丹东雪山）不同，他的贡献在于首次否定了岷山为江源、把上源指向金沙江，而非完全解决江源问题。

7. ‘He overturned the Yugong’s claim that “the Min Mountains give rise to the Yangtze” and identified the Jinsha River as the Yangtze’s source’ — this is one of Xu Xiake’s most important geographical discoveries, supported by the text of the travel diaries itself. Note, however, that his ‘Jinsha River, rising south of the Kunlun’ still differs from the modern determination of the true source (the Geladaindong snow peak in the Tanggula Mountains); his contribution was to deny for the first time that the Min Mountains were the source and to point the upper course toward the Jinsha, not to fully settle the question of the river’s source.

8. “他'扰民'（用马牌役使村民、因失鸡扣人等）”——这些细节见于其游记及相关记载，是真实存在的记录，说明他并非完美无瑕的道德楷模。文中如实保留，以免塑造一个过度完美的人物。

8. ‘He “imposed on the people” (using the horse-pass to conscript villagers for labor, docking a man’s pay over a lost chicken, and so on)’ — these details appear in his travel diaries and in related records and are genuine; they show that he was not a flawless moral paragon. They are retained faithfully in the text so as not to create an overly perfect figure.

9. “他一生不考功名、不当官”——基本属实（他未取得科举功名，亦未入仕）。但需注意其家庭背景较优、早年旅费部分来自其母亲所经营的织造收入，他的“自由”有一定的物质基础，并非纯粹意义上的“一无所有”。文中以“家境不错”作了交代。

9. ‘He never in his life sought examination honors or held office’ — this is essentially true (he obtained no examination degree and never entered official service). Note, however, that his family background was relatively well-off and that part of his early travel funds came from the weaving income managed by his mother; his ‘freedom’ had a material basis and was not ‘having nothing’ in the purest sense. The text conveys this with the phrase ‘his family was comfortably off.’

10. “游记是'完整流传'下来的”——不准确。原稿大部分在战火中焚毁，今本是靠“季本”“李介立本”等抄本辗转拼合、并经历代校订而成的残本（约60余万字），部分内容已永久散失。文中已明确写出这一点。

10. ‘The travel diaries have come down to us “complete”’ — this is inaccurate. Most of the original manuscript was destroyed in war; the present text is a damaged edition pieced together from such copies as the ‘Ji edition’ and the ‘Li Jieli edition’ and revised over the generations (roughly six hundred thousand characters), with some portions permanently lost. The text states this plainly.
