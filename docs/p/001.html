Chapter 001 · Part 1

爱迪生：那个咬住唱针的失聪者

Edison: The Deaf Man Who Bit Down on the Stylus

The age he lived in shoved him into adulthood, and then shoved him into being forgotten.

他被时代推搡着长大，又被时代推搡着遗忘。

The age he lived in shoved him into adulthood, and then shoved him into being forgotten.

1847年，俄亥俄州一个小镇，一个男孩降生。他后来会成为这个星球上最著名的发明家，但在那时，他只是个让老师头疼的孩子——问题太多，多得让人厌烦。三个月后，他被赶出校门，老师送给他一个标签，像烙铁一样烙了一生：“低能儿”。

In 1847, in a small town in Ohio, a boy was born. He would go on to become the most famous inventor on the planet, but at the time he was just a child who gave his teacher headaches—too many questions, too many to bear. Three months later he was driven out of the schoolhouse, and the teacher handed him a label that seared into him for life like a brand: "feeble-minded."

母亲在家中的地窖里教他读书。那里没有讲台，没有教鞭，只有一个孩子和一个不肯放弃他的女人。许多年后，当他已经拥有上千项专利，他说：“我的母亲造就了我。”

His mother taught him to read down in the family cellar. There was no lectern there, no pointer, only a child and a woman who refused to give up on him. Many years later, when he already held more than a thousand patents, he said: "My mother made me."

可命运从不轻易放过任何人。

But fate never lets anyone off easily.

青年时代，他在火车上卖报，顺便把一节行李车厢改成流动实验室。一次实验意外引发火灾，他被列车长扇聋了右耳，连人带仪器扔下车。那之后，他成了一个听不见世界的人——也成了一个更专注地倾听内心的人。

In his youth he sold newspapers on a train, and on the side turned a baggage car into a rolling laboratory. One experiment accidentally started a fire; the conductor struck him and deafened his right ear, then threw him off the train, instruments and all. After that he became a man who could not hear the world—and also a man who listened all the more intently to his own heart.

他一生都在失败。

He spent his whole life failing.

发明电灯时，他试验了1600多种材料。金属、草木、矿石、纤维，从邻居的钓鱼线到朋友的胡须，能试的都试了。灯丝亮了又灭，灭了又亮，助手们看着满地的残骸，说：我们一无所有了。

When he was inventing the electric light, he tested more than 1,600 materials. Metals, plants, minerals, fibers—from a neighbor's fishing line to a friend's whiskers, he tried everything that could be tried. The filament lit and went out, went out and lit again. His assistants looked at the wreckage strewn across the floor and said: We have nothing left.

他看着那些残骸，说：不，我们已经知道，这一千多种东西，都不能用来做灯丝。

He looked at the wreckage and said: No—we already know that these thousand-odd things cannot be used as a filament.

世人眼里的一千次失败，是他心里的一千次排除。

A thousand failures in the eyes of the world were a thousand eliminations in his mind.

但他不只是那个“永不放弃”的励志标本。他也有光的背面。

But he was not only that motivational specimen who "never gives up." He also had the dark side of the light.

为了在电流大战中击败对手特斯拉，他用交流电当众电死一头大象，只为证明那种电流足以致命。那场表演血腥、残忍，成为他一生抹不去的污点。有人说那是商人的狡诈，有人说那是偏执的疯狂。他只是沉默，继续埋头在他的实验室里——那个堆满失败的房间里。

To defeat his rival Tesla in the War of the Currents, he publicly electrocuted an elephant with alternating current, only to prove that such a current was lethal enough. The spectacle was bloody and cruel, a stain he could never wipe from his life. Some called it the cunning of a businessman, some called it the madness of obsession. He only kept silent and buried himself again in his laboratory—the room piled high with failures.

1879年10月21日，一盏灯亮了，持续燃烧了45个小时。

On October 21, 1879, a lamp lit up and burned for 45 hours.

那一刻，世界终于看见了他。可世界没看见的是，那45小时之后，他又用了整整十年，才让这盏灯真正照亮千家万户。那十年里，他继续破产、继续被嘲笑、继续在黑暗中一次次点亮、一次次熄灭。

In that moment, the world finally saw him. What the world did not see was that after those 45 hours, it took him a full ten more years before this lamp truly lit up thousands of homes. Through those ten years he kept going bankrupt, kept being mocked, kept lighting it and snuffing it out again and again in the dark.

他被困在寂静里，却为世界打开了声音。

He was trapped in silence, yet he opened up sound for the world.

听力受损这件事，来得太早，早到他几乎不记得完整的世界是什么样子。童年时的一场猩红热，或是一次被扇聋右耳的火车事故——说法不一，但结果一样：他成了一个在喧嚣中长大、却听不见喧嚣的人。

The damage to his hearing came too early, so early that he could hardly remember what a whole world looked like. A childhood bout of scarlet fever, or the train accident that deafened his right ear—the accounts differ, but the result was the same: he grew up amid the clamor yet could not hear it.

人们以为他会退缩到无声的角落里。他没有。他只是更用力地贴近这个世界——用另一种方式。

People assumed he would retreat into some silent corner. He did not. He simply pressed himself harder against the world—in another way.

1877年，他画下一张奇怪的图纸，交给机械师去做。那是一个圆筒，上面覆着锡纸，旁边有手柄和唱针。所有人都不知道这东西能干什么。他转动曲柄，对着圆筒唱了一首童谣：《玛丽有只小羊羔》。

In 1877 he drew a strange diagram and gave it to a machinist to build. It was a cylinder wrapped in tinfoil, with a handle and a stylus beside it. No one knew what the thing was for. He turned the crank and sang a nursery rhyme into the cylinder: "Mary Had a Little Lamb."

然后他退回去，把唱针拨回起点。再次转动。

Then he stepped back and set the stylus back to the start. And turned it again.

机器里传出一个声音，细弱、沙哑，但清晰可辨：他自己的声音。

A voice came out of the machine—thin, hoarse, but distinctly audible: his own voice.

整个世界惊呆了。人们称它为“会说话的机器”，以为是魔术，是骗局，是魔鬼的把戏。他们不知道，这个几乎听不见声音的人，发明了人类历史上第一台可以记录和重放声音的机器。

The whole world was stunned. People called it the "talking machine," took it for magic, for a hoax, for the devil's trick. They did not know that this man, who could barely hear a sound, had invented the first machine in human history capable of recording and replaying the voice.

他是怎么做到的？

How did he do it?

据说，调试留声机时，他用牙齿咬住唱针的金属杆，让振动的节律通过骨骼，直接传进内耳。他听不见空气中的声波，就让声音从骨头里走。那个画面有一种奇异的庄严：一个半聋的人，咬着一根针，像咬住命运的缰绳，硬生生把声音从虚空中拽了出来。

It is said that while adjusting the phonograph, he bit down on the metal rod of the stylus with his teeth, letting the rhythm of the vibrations travel through bone straight into his inner ear. If he could not hear sound waves in the air, he would let sound travel through his bones. There is a strange solemnity in that image: a half-deaf man, biting a needle as if biting the reins of fate, dragging sound out of the void by sheer force.

他的“缺陷”，反而成了他的通道。

His "defect" became his channel.

但命运并不因此对他仁慈。

But fate was not kind to him for that.

1890年代，他倾尽家财，在新泽西建起一座巨大的矿石加工厂。他发明了一套磁力选矿设备，能从低品位的铁矿砂里提取高纯度铁。那是一项雄心勃勃的工程——他想让美国东部那些废弃的贫矿重新活过来。

In the 1890s he poured his fortune into building a huge ore-processing plant in New Jersey. He invented a magnetic ore-separating system that could extract high-purity iron from low-grade iron sand. It was an ambitious project—he wanted to bring the abandoned poor mines of the eastern United States back to life.

他投入了几乎全部身家，甚至卖掉通用电气的股票。那座工厂像一座未来主义的城堡，巨大的机器日夜轰鸣，碾碎岩石，筛出铁矿。他相信这会是他一生最大的成就。

He staked almost everything he had, even selling his General Electric stock. The plant was like a futuristic castle, its enormous machines roaring day and night, crushing rock and sifting out iron ore. He believed it would be the greatest achievement of his life.

然后，明尼苏达州的梅萨比山脉发现了富铁矿。铁矿石价格暴跌，他的工厂一夜之间变成废墟。

Then rich iron ore was discovered in the Mesabi Range in Minnesota. The price of iron ore plummeted, and his plant turned into ruins overnight.

他破产了。59岁，身无分文，背负巨债。

He was bankrupt. Fifty-nine years old, penniless, drowning in debt.

人们以为他终于要倒下了。他只是沉默着，走进那片废墟，看着那些耗尽心血的机器，布满铁锈，像一头死去的巨兽。

People assumed he would finally fall. He merely walked in silence into the ruins and looked at those machines he had poured his heart and blood into, covered in rust like a dead giant beast.

然后他做了一件谁也想不到的事。

Then he did something no one could have expected.

他把矿石加工中研发的技术——研磨、粉碎、煅烧——全部转移到水泥生产上。他用那些废弃的机器，建起了一座水泥厂。后来，他用这种水泥，建造了低价、防火的房屋，试图解决贫民的住房问题。

He transferred all the technology he had developed in ore processing—grinding, crushing, calcining—to the making of cement. With those discarded machines he built a cement plant. Later, with this cement, he built low-cost, fireproof houses, trying to solve the housing problem of the poor.

从废墟里，他捡起了另一种可能。

Out of the ruins, he picked up another possibility.

后来有人问他，那次失败值不值得。他说：“值。我从那里学会了怎么做水泥。”

Later someone asked him whether that failure had been worth it. He said: "It was. I learned from it how to make cement."

这就是他：从无声里，他听见了声音；从废墟里，他建起了房屋。他不是不被击倒，而是每一次倒下后，他都换一个姿势，重新站起来。

That was him: out of silence he heard sound; out of ruins he built houses. It was not that he was never knocked down, but that after every fall he rose again in a different posture.

他一生留下1093项专利，也留下电死大象的污点。他不是完美的伟人，只是一个不肯停手的普通人。他的光芒，有一部分来自人性的阴影——正如那盏灯，在最黑暗的夜里，才最亮。

He left behind 1,093 patents in his lifetime, and also the stain of electrocuting an elephant. He was not a flawless great man, only an ordinary person who would not stop. Part of his brilliance came from the shadows of human nature—just as that lamp shone brightest on the darkest night.

他创办了现代意义上的实验室，把人类拖进电气时代。但真正让他不朽的，从来不是那些成功，而是每一次跌倒后，他都像没事人一样爬起来，拍拍土，说：再来。

He founded the laboratory in the modern sense and dragged humanity into the electrical age. But what truly made him immortal was never those successes; it was that after every fall he got up as if nothing had happened, dusted himself off, and said: Again.
