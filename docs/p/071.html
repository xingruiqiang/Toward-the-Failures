莫迪里阿尼：生前只卖出几幅画的“酒鬼”，死后画价以亿计

Modigliani: The 'Drunkard' Who Sold a Few Paintings in His Lifetime, Whose Work Now Fetches Hundreds of Millions

——“我要的是短暂而炽烈的一生。”

—'I wanted a short and blazing life.'

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂71 / 100约 3056 字 · 3056 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，世界各大拍卖行里，莫迪里阿尼的裸女画可以卖到上亿美元。他的《侧卧的裸女》曾在纽约拍出天价，成为艺术市场上最贵的画作之一。

In 2026, at the world's major auction houses, a Modigliani nude can sell for hundreds of millions of dollars. His Reclining Nude once fetched a record price in New York, becoming one of the most expensive paintings on the art market.

但在生前，他的一生几乎是一场持续的、彻底的失败。

But in his own lifetime, his life was almost one continuous, utter failure.

他在世35年，几乎没卖出过画。他住在一间月租极便宜的破公寓里，常年靠朋友接济，酗酒，吸大麻，身患当时无法治愈的肺结核。他唯一一次个人画展，开幕几小时就被警察勒令关闭。他死的那天，巴黎的报纸只发了一条很短的消息。

He lived only 35 years and sold almost no paintings. He lived in a shabby apartment with extremely cheap rent, depended year after year on the charity of friends, drank heavily, smoked hashish, and suffered from tuberculosis, which was incurable at the time. His one and only solo exhibition was shut down by the police a few hours after it opened. The day he died, the Paris newspapers ran only a very short notice.

第二天，他怀有身孕的恋人从五楼跳了下去。

The next day, his pregnant lover jumped from the fifth floor.

一、里窝那的犹太少年

I. A Jewish Boy in Livorno

1884年7月12日，阿梅代奥·莫迪里阿尼出生在意大利里窝那的一个犹太家庭。他的父亲是商人，家境一度不错，但后来生意失败，家道中落。

On July 12, 1884, Amedeo Modigliani was born into a Jewish family in Livorno, Italy. His father was a businessman, and the family was once comfortably off, but the business later failed and their fortunes declined.

莫迪里阿尼从小体弱。他患过严重的胸膜炎，后来又染上伤寒。母亲为了让他养病，带他四处旅行，也让他在博物馆里第一次接触到文艺复兴的艺术。他早早就知道自己要画画。

Modigliani was frail from childhood. He suffered a severe bout of pleurisy and later contracted typhoid. To help him recover, his mother took him traveling, and it was in museums that he first encountered Renaissance art. Early on, he knew he wanted to paint.

1902年，他进入佛罗伦萨的美术学院，随后又去了威尼斯。1906年，他做了一件改变一生的事：他只身来到巴黎。

In 1902, he entered the academy of fine arts in Florence, and afterward went on to Venice. In 1906 he did the one thing that changed his life: he came alone to Paris.

二、蒙马特与蒙帕纳斯的“浪荡子”

II. A 'Bohemian' of Montmartre and Montparnasse

二十世纪初的巴黎，是世界艺术的中心。毕加索、布拉克、阿波利奈尔、苏丁……所有后来改写美术史的人，几乎都挤在蒙马特和蒙帕纳斯那几片廉价的街区里。

Paris in the early twentieth century was the center of world art. Picasso, Braque, Apollinaire, Soutine… nearly everyone who would later rewrite art history was crowded into those few cheap quarters of Montmartre and Montparnasse.

莫迪里阿尼混在这个圈子里，却是最特别的一个。他长得极其英俊，说话带着意大利口音，穿得也比谁都讲究——即便穷到交不起房租，他也要把自己收拾得体面。他写诗，会引用但丁，喝酒喝到人尽皆知。

Modigliani moved within this circle, yet he was the most singular figure in it. He was strikingly handsome, spoke with an Italian accent, and dressed more carefully than anyone—even when he was too poor to pay his rent, he kept himself presentable. He wrote poetry, could quote Dante, and drank to the point of notoriety.

他也是这个圈子里最不“时髦”的一个。当时流行的是立体主义、是未来主义，大家都在把形象打碎、重组。莫迪里阿尼却坚持画人——画肖像，画裸体。他用的是最传统的题材，却画出了一种前所未见的形状。

He was also the least 'fashionable' member of that circle. The vogue at the time was Cubism, Futurism—everyone was breaking forms apart and reassembling them. Modigliani insisted on painting people—portraits, nudes. He took the most traditional subjects and painted them into a shape no one had seen before.

他的画里，人的脸被拉长，脖子变得纤细，眼睛是一对没有瞳孔的杏仁形——像是在凝视，又像是在看透。 那种忧郁而有尊严的美，成为他独一无二的签名。

In his paintings, the faces are elongated, the necks slender, the eyes a pair of pupil-less almond shapes—as if gazing, as if seeing through. That melancholy, dignified beauty became his unmistakable signature.

三、唯一的展览，被警察关掉了

III. His Only Exhibition, Shut Down by the Police

1914年前后，莫迪里阿尼一度把重心放在雕塑上。他想做出像非洲木雕和古希腊基克拉迪雕像那样的作品。但石料太贵，粉尘又加重了他本就脆弱的肺部，雕塑之路走不下去。

Around 1914, Modigliani turned his focus for a time to sculpture. He wanted to make works like African wood carvings and ancient Greek Cycladic figures. But stone was too expensive, and the dust aggravated his already fragile lungs; the path of sculpture led nowhere.

他回到了绘画，也越来越穷。他靠画肖像换一顿饭、换几杯酒。画商、朋友、模特，都成了他画里的人物——只是那些画几乎卖不出去。

He returned to painting and grew poorer and poorer. He painted portraits in exchange for a meal, for a few glasses of wine. Dealers, friends, models—all became figures in his paintings, though those paintings almost never sold.

1917年12月，画商贝尔特·韦伊为他在巴黎举办了他一生中唯一一次个人画展。展览上挂着他最著名的那些裸女画。结果开幕没过多久，警察就来了——理由是“有伤风化”，橱窗里的裸体画被勒令撤下，展览就此夭折。

In December 1917, the dealer Berthe Weill mounted in Paris the only solo exhibition of his life. It hung his most famous nudes. Not long after it opened, the police arrived—on grounds of 'offending public morals'—and the nudes in the window were ordered taken down; the exhibition was cut short.

他一生中唯一一次被世界看见的机会，持续了几个小时。

The only chance he ever had to be seen by the world lasted a few hours.

四、他身边那群人

IV. The Circle Around Him

莫迪里阿尼不是一个人在战斗。他身边聚着二十世纪初巴黎最出色的一批人。

Modigliani did not struggle alone. Around him gathered some of the finest people in early twentieth-century Paris.

诗人阿波利奈尔是他的挚友，两人一起泡咖啡馆、一起谈论艺术与诗歌。画家苏丁、基斯林、郁特里罗，都是他的伙伴。波兰诗人兹博罗夫斯基后来成了他的画商，也是他晚年最重要的经济支柱——很多时候，兹博罗夫斯基不是“卖画”，而是自己掏钱买下他的画，再给他送来房租和饭钱。

The poet Apollinaire was his closest friend; the two of them sat in cafés together and talked about art and poetry. The painters Soutine, Kisling, and Utrillo were his companions. The Polish poet Zborowski later became his dealer and the most important financial support of his last years—often Zborowski did not so much 'sell' the paintings as buy them with his own money and then bring Modigliani the rent and money for food.

莫迪里阿尼也给这些人画画。他画阿波利奈尔，画苏丁，画兹博罗夫斯基。那些肖像有一个共同的特点：他不美化任何人，但也没有贬低任何人。 每一张脸上，都有一种安静的、带点忧伤的尊严。这大概是因为，他自己就是这样一个人。

Modigliani also painted these people. He painted Apollinaire, Soutine, Zborowski. Those portraits share one trait: he flattered no one, yet he belittled no one either. Every face carries a quiet, faintly sorrowful dignity. Perhaps because he himself was such a person.

他混迹于最前卫的圈子，却从不真正参与任何一种“主义”。他不写宣言，不站队，不解释自己的画。他只是日复一日地画人。

He moved in the most avant-garde circles, yet he never truly joined any 'ism.' He wrote no manifestos, took no side, explained nothing about his paintings. He simply painted people, day after day.

五、珍妮

V. Jeanne

1917年，莫迪里阿尼遇到了珍妮·埃比泰尔纳。她是一位年轻的画家，出身中产家庭，比他小十四岁。两人很快相爱、同居。

In 1917, Modigliani met Jeanne Hébuterne. She was a young painter from a middle-class family, fourteen years his junior. The two soon fell in love and moved in together.

珍妮为他生了一个女儿。她的父母强烈反对这段关系，甚至与她断绝了往来。但珍妮始终没有离开他。她陪着他熬过最穷困的日子，照顾他日渐恶化的身体。

Jeanne bore him a daughter. Her parents fiercely opposed the relationship and even cut off contact with her. But Jeanne never left him. She stayed with him through his poorest days and cared for his steadily worsening health.

莫迪里阿尼为她画了无数张肖像。那些画里的珍妮，脖子长长的，眼神安静，带着一种几乎令人心碎的温柔。这些画，如今是艺术史上最有名的“爱情肖像”之一。

Modigliani painted countless portraits of her. In those paintings Jeanne has a long neck and quiet eyes, with a tenderness that is almost heartbreaking. Today these are among the most famous 'love portraits' in art history.

五、1920年1月24日

V. January 24, 1920

莫迪里阿尼的健康，早就到了极限。长年的酗酒、营养不良、加上未经治疗的肺结核，让他的身体千疮百孔。

Modigliani's health had long since reached its limit. Years of heavy drinking, malnutrition, and untreated tuberculosis had left his body riddled and worn.

1920年1月24日，他在巴黎的一家医院里去世，死因是结核性脑膜炎，年仅35岁。临终前，据说他还在念叨着意大利，念叨着他从没真正回去过的故乡。

On January 24, 1920, he died in a Paris hospital of tuberculous meningitis, only 35 years old. On his deathbed, it is said, he kept murmuring about Italy, about the homeland he had never really returned to.

第二天——也有记载说是两天后——珍妮·埃比泰尔纳从她父母家的五楼窗口跳下，当场身亡。她当时已经怀孕，腹中是他们的第二个孩子。她只有21岁。

The next day—some accounts say two days later—Jeanne Hébuterne jumped from a fifth-floor window of her parents' home and died on the spot. She was pregnant at the time, carrying their second child. She was only 21.

六、死后封神

VI. Canonized After Death

莫迪里阿尼去世后不到几年，情况就彻底反转了。

Within only a few years of Modigliani's death, the situation reversed completely.

他的画商兹博罗夫斯基开始系统地整理、推广他的作品。1920年代初，他的画价飞快上涨。到二十世纪中叶，他已经和梵高、毕加索一道，成为二十世纪艺术市场上最受追捧的名字。

His dealer Zborowski began systematically organizing and promoting his work. In the early 1920s, his prices rose rapidly. By the middle of the twentieth century he stood alongside Van Gogh and Picasso as one of the most sought-after names in the twentieth-century art market.

他生前换一顿饭的一张画，如今可以买下一栋楼。 这是一个关于“天才与市场错位”的最极端的样本。

A painting he once traded for a single meal could today buy an entire building. This is the most extreme example of the mismatch between genius and the market.

更耐人寻味的是：他的画风其实并不“前卫”。他画的裸体、肖像，题材都是几百年前就有的。真正让人着迷的，是他处理这些老题材的方式——那种把人物抽离出具体情境、只留下一个孤独剪影的抽象感。也许正因为这种“既古老又现代”，他的画才能在死后跨越时代，被一代代观众接住。

More intriguing still: his style was in fact not 'avant-garde.' The nudes and portraits he painted were subjects that had existed for centuries. What truly fascinates is the way he handled these old subjects—an abstraction that pulls the figure out of any specific situation and leaves only a solitary silhouette. Perhaps it is precisely this 'both ancient and modern' quality that allowed his paintings to cross eras after his death and be caught, generation after generation, by new viewers.

七、写给2026年的我们

VII. To Us in 2026

莫迪里阿尼的故事，最容易被讲成“梵高式的悲剧”：天才不被时代接受，死后才被封神。

Modigliani's story is most easily told as a 'Van Gogh–style tragedy': a genius rejected by his age, canonized only after death.

但如果我们只记住这一点，就把这个人读浅了。莫迪里阿尼的悲剧，不只是“时代辜负了他”。他也确实在自我毁灭——酗酒、放纵、拒绝治疗。他有一种近乎决绝的自我消耗，仿佛他只想把生命烧得足够亮，不在乎烧多久。

But if that is all we remember, we read the man too shallowly. Modigliani's tragedy was not only that 'his age failed him.' He was also, in truth, destroying himself—the drinking, the excess, the refusal of treatment. There was in him an almost resolute self-consumption, as if he wanted only to burn his life bright enough, and did not care how long it burned.

他留下的那句“短暂而炽烈的一生”，既是他的艺术宣言，也是他的死亡预告。

The phrase he left behind—'a short and blazing life'—was both his artistic manifesto and the forecast of his death.

2026年的我们，习惯了用“长期主义”“复利”来规划人生，习惯了计算投入产出。莫迪里阿尼提醒我们：世界上还有一种人，他们活着不是为了赢，而是为了把心里那个东西做出来。这种人往往活不长，也往往不被理解。

We in 2026 are used to planning our lives around 'long-termism' and 'compound interest,' used to calculating inputs and outputs. Modigliani reminds us that there is another kind of person in the world, one who lives not to win but to bring out the thing inside them. Such people often do not live long, and are often misunderstood.

我们可以不学他的活法。但我们至少应该承认：正是这种“不划算”的人，替人类保存了最珍贵的那一部分东西。

We need not live as he did. But we should at least acknowledge this: it is precisely such 'unprofitable' people who have preserved, on humanity's behalf, the most precious part of what we have.

史料来源与辨析 · Sources & Notes

史料来源与辨析

Sources and Notes

1. 生平基础事实：莫迪里阿尼（Amedeo Modigliani），1884年7月12日生于意大利里窝那，1920年1月24日卒于巴黎，年仅35岁。为确证史实。

1. Basic biographical facts: Modigliani (Amedeo Modigliani) was born on July 12, 1884 in Livorno, Italy, and died on January 24, 1920 in Paris at the age of 35. To confirm the record.

2. 早年经历：幼年多病（胸膜炎、伤寒），随母亲旅行并接触艺术；1902年入佛罗伦萨美术学院，1906年赴巴黎。为通行权威记载。

2. Early years: sickly in childhood (pleurisy, typhoid), traveled with his mother and encountered art; entered the Florence academy in 1902 and went to Paris in 1906. According to standard authoritative accounts.

3. 唯一一次个人画展：1917年12月由画商贝尔特·韦伊（Berthe Weill）举办，因裸体画被警方以“有伤风化”为由要求撤下。为确证史实（该展通常被称为其生前唯一一次个展）。

3. The only solo exhibition: mounted in December 1917 by the dealer Berthe Weill; its nudes were ordered removed by police on the grounds of 'offending public morals.' To confirm the record (the show is usually described as the only solo exhibition of his lifetime).

4. 与珍妮·埃比泰尔纳的关系：1917年相识，同居并育有一女。珍妮曾因其父母反对而与之关系紧张。为确证史实。

4. Relationship with Jeanne Hébuterne: they met in 1917, lived together, and had a daughter. Jeanne's relations with her parents were strained because of their opposition. To confirm the record.

5. 死因：结核性脑膜炎（tuberculous meningitis），与其长期未治愈的肺结核相关。为确证史实。

5. Cause of death: tuberculous meningitis, related to his long-untreated pulmonary tuberculosis. To confirm the record.

6. 珍妮之死：珍妮在莫迪里阿尼去世后随即跳楼身亡，时年21岁，当时怀有身孕。需要辨析的是时间：多数资料记为“次日（1月25日）”，也有资料记为“两天后（1月26日）”。本文如实并列，不武断。她腹中胎儿的具体情况，各资料记载亦有差异。

6. Jeanne's death: Jeanne died by jumping shortly after Modigliani's death, at the age of 21 and pregnant at the time. The timing requires careful consideration: most sources record it as 'the next day (January 25),' while others record it as 'two days later (January 26).' This text sets the two side by side as reported, without deciding between them. The details concerning her unborn child also differ from source to source.

7. 生前作品销售：他生前几乎未通过正常渠道售出作品，主要靠画商兹博罗夫斯基（Léopold Zborowski）及友人接济。此为通行记载。“生前只卖出一幅”这类说法，通常是对梵高的描述，套用于莫迪里阿尼并不准确——更准确的说法是“生前作品几乎无人问津”。

7. Sales of his work during his lifetime: he sold almost nothing through normal channels, relying mainly on his dealer Léopold Zborowski and the support of friends. This is the standard account. The claim that he 'sold only one painting in his lifetime' usually describes Van Gogh and is not accurate when applied to Modigliani—the more accurate statement is that 'his work drew almost no interest during his lifetime.'

8. 拍卖价格：其裸女系列作品在21世纪多次刷新拍卖纪录，此处仅作宏观陈述，不列举具体成交价，以免因市场波动而失真。

8. Auction prices: his series of nudes has repeatedly set auction records in the twenty-first century; only a general statement is made here, without citing specific sale prices, so as to avoid distortion caused by market fluctuation.
