Chapter 058 · Part 3

马化腾：做出QQ，却差点把它卖掉的人

Pony Ma: The Man Who Built QQ and Nearly Sold It

——一个“不爱说话、不爱社交”的人，做出了中国最大的社交软件

—A man who "didn't like to talk and didn't like to socialize" built China's largest social software.

2026年，很多年轻人做的第一件事，就是给产品找一个“能被买走的出口”：做不下去就卖给大厂，做不成就换个方向。这没错——活下去比面子重要。

In 2026, the first thing many young people do is look for an "exit for their product": if it can't keep going, sell it to a big company; if it can't be done, switch directions. There's nothing wrong with that—surviving matters more than saving face.

但有一个故事值得说说：二十多年前，有个人做出了中国最早的即时通讯软件，用户疯涨，却赚不到钱。他想过把它卖掉，也找过一些买家——结果没人愿意接。最后他只能自己扛下来。

But here is a story worth telling: more than two decades ago, a man built one of China's earliest instant-messaging programs. Its users exploded, yet it made no money. He thought about selling it and approached some potential buyers—but no one was willing to take it. In the end he had to carry it himself.

他叫马化腾。那个软件，后来叫QQ。

His name is Pony Ma. That software was later called QQ.

一、五个年轻人的公司

I. A Company of Five Young Men

1971年，马化腾出生在广东汕头（后随家人到深圳）。1993年，他从深圳大学计算机系毕业。

Pony Ma was born in Shantou, Guangdong, in 1971 (his family later moved to Shenzhen). In 1993 he graduated from Shenzhen University's computer science department.

毕业后的马化腾，先在寻呼行业做了几年技术工作。他性格内向、不爱社交、甚至有点“技术宅”——这一点后来成了外界理解他的关键。

After graduating, Pony Ma spent a few years in technical work in the paging industry. He was introverted, unsociable, and even something of a "tech geek"—a trait that later became key to how outsiders understood him.

1998年11月，他和张志东等五位伙伴一起，在深圳创办了腾讯。最初，他们做的是寻呼系统的软件。

In November 1998, he and five partners, including Zhang Zhidong, founded Tencent in Shenzhen. At first they made software for paging systems.

1999年2月，他们推出了一个叫OICQ的产品——一个模仿以色列ICQ的即时通讯工具。为了让中文用户用得顺手，他们把软件做得很小（当时中国的网络和电脑都很弱），把好友列表存在服务器上（这样换电脑也能登录），还加上了离线消息等功能。

In February 1999, they launched a product called OICQ—an instant-messaging tool modeled on Israel's ICQ. To make it smooth for Chinese users, they kept the software very small (China's networks and computers were weak in those days), stored the friend list on the server (so you could log in from a different computer), and added features like offline messages.

结果，用户量开始疯涨。

The result: the user numbers began to explode.

二、用户疯涨，钱却不来

II. Users Explode, but the Money Doesn't Come

但问题也来了：用户越多，服务器和带宽成本越高，而公司几乎不赚钱。

But problems came with it: the more users, the higher the server and bandwidth costs, while the company made almost no money.

那个年代，互联网的商业模式还没有跑通，即时通讯本身更是没人想清楚该怎么赚钱。腾讯的钱花得飞快，账上常常捉襟见肘。据说，最困难的时候，马化腾甚至考虑过把OICQ卖掉。

Back then the internet's business models had not yet been figured out, and for instant messaging in particular no one had worked out how to make money. Tencent burned through cash fast and its books were often stretched thin. It is said that at the hardest moment, Pony Ma even considered selling OICQ.

关于“想卖QQ”，流传着很多版本：有的说找过深圳电信局，有的说找过门户网站，报价大约在一百万上下，都没谈成——有的是对方看不上，有的是价格没谈拢，有的是马化腾自己又舍不得。

There are many versions of the "wanted to sell QQ" story: some say he approached the Shenzhen telecom bureau, others say a portal website, with a price of around one million, and none of the talks succeeded—sometimes the other side wasn't interested, sometimes they couldn't agree on a price, and sometimes Pony Ma himself couldn't bear to let it go.

需要说明的是，这个“想卖掉QQ”的细节，流传甚广，但具体经过各方说法不一，很难完整还原。可以确认的是：在最困难的那几年，腾讯确实面临过“卖掉产品”的抉择，而最终没有卖成。

It should be noted that this detail—"wanting to sell QQ"—has circulated widely, but accounts of exactly what happened differ, and it is hard to reconstruct in full. What can be confirmed is that in those hardest years Tencent did face a choice about "selling the product," and in the end it was not sold.

于是马化腾只能自己扛。

So Pony Ma had to carry it himself.

三、熬过互联网泡沫

III. Surviving the Internet Bubble

2000年前后，全球互联网泡沫破裂，无数公司倒闭。腾讯也在生死线上挣扎。

Around 2000, the global internet bubble burst and countless companies went under. Tencent too struggled on the edge of survival.

转机来自外部投资。2000年，IDG和香港盈科向腾讯投资约220万美元；2001年，南非的MIH集团收购了腾讯的股份，成为重要股东。这些钱，让腾讯熬过了最冷的冬天。

The turning point came from outside investment. In 2000, IDG and Hong Kong's PCCW invested about $2.2 million in Tencent; in 2001, South Africa's MIH group bought a stake and became a major shareholder. This money carried Tencent through its coldest winter.

2004年6月，腾讯在香港上市。

In June 2004, Tencent listed in Hong Kong.

此后，腾讯以QQ为基础，做游戏、做门户、做支付、做内容，逐步长成一家巨型的互联网公司。而QQ本身，成了几代中国人的共同记忆——那个“企鹅”图标，几乎出现在每一台中国电脑和每一部手机上。

After that, building on QQ, Tencent moved into gaming, portals, payments, and content, gradually growing into a giant internet company. And QQ itself became a shared memory for several generations of Chinese—the "penguin" icon appeared on almost every Chinese computer and every Chinese phone.

四、“小步快跑，试错迭代”

IV. "Small Steps, Quick Runs; Trial and Error, Iteration"

马化腾留下的，不只是一家公司，还有一套影响中国互联网的打法。

What Pony Ma left behind was not just a company but a playbook that shaped China's internet.

他常说的一个理念是“小步快跑，试错迭代”：不要指望一次做出完美产品，而是快速推出、观察用户反馈、不断修改。这套方法论，后来几乎成了中国互联网产品经理的“圣经”。

One idea he often cited was "small steps, quick runs; trial and error, iteration": don't expect to make a perfect product in one go, but ship it fast, watch how users respond, and keep revising. This methodology later became almost the bible of Chinese internet product managers.

腾讯也不是每件事都做成了。它做过搜索（搜搜，后来并入搜狗）、做过电商（拍拍，最终转手）、做过微博（腾讯微博，最终停运）——这些尝试大多没有成功。但马化腾的公司有一个特点：允许失败，但要求快速调整。

Nor did Tencent succeed at everything. It tried search (Soso, later merged into Sogou), e-commerce (Paipai, eventually sold off), and microblogging (Tencent Weibo, eventually shut down)—most of these attempts failed. But Pony Ma's company had one trait: it allowed failure, but demanded fast adjustment.

这一点，和他个人的性格很像：他不擅长演讲，不太会“画大饼”，看起来甚至有点“闷”。但他的产品能跑出来，靠的是对细节近乎偏执的打磨——据说他能对着一张界面的截图，逐像素地提修改意见。

This mirrors his own character: he was not good at giving speeches, not much for "painting grand visions," and even seemed a bit quiet and withdrawn. But his products could take off because of a near-obsessive polishing of details—it is said he could look at a screenshot of an interface and give revision notes pixel by pixel.

一个不爱社交的人做出了中国最大的社交产品，这看起来是个矛盾。但也许正因为他不擅长当面讲话，才那么在意“人在屏幕后面怎么交流”。

That a man who disliked socializing built China's biggest social product looks like a contradiction. But perhaps precisely because he was not good at talking face to face, he cared so much about "how people communicate from behind a screen."

五、2010年那场“被骂醒”的危机

V. The 2010 Crisis That "Scolded Him Awake"

腾讯也不是一直顺风。

Tencent did not always have smooth sailing either.

2010年，腾讯与奇虎360爆发了著名的“3Q大战”。双方一度要求用户在两者之间“二选一”，引发全国用户的强烈不满，也让腾讯承受了空前的舆论压力。

In 2010, Tencent and Qihoo 360 erupted into the famous "3Q War." At one point the two sides forced users to "pick one of the two," provoking intense nationwide anger and placing Tencent under unprecedented public pressure.

这场风波对马化腾的冲击很大。后来他多次提到，那是腾讯历史上最艰难的时刻之一，也是他个人压力最大的时期。它逼着这家习惯“什么都自己做”的公司去反思：一个企业到底应该做多大、边界在哪里。

The episode hit Pony Ma hard. He later said many times that it was one of the hardest moments in Tencent's history, and the period of greatest personal pressure in his life. It forced a company used to "doing everything itself" to reflect: how big should an enterprise be, and where are its boundaries?

此后，腾讯启动了“开放平台”战略，从“自己把每件事都做完”，转向“把流量和能力开放出来，与合作伙伴一起做”。腾讯的投资版图（电商、出行、内容等领域的诸多公司）大体形成于那之后。

Afterward, Tencent launched its "open platform" strategy, shifting from "doing everything itself" to "opening up its traffic and capabilities, and building alongside partners." Tencent's investment map (many companies in e-commerce, mobility, content, and other fields) largely took shape after that.

这是一次被全网声讨的“失败”，但它逼着腾讯想清楚了自己是谁——某种意义上，没有那场骂声，就没有后来的腾讯。

This was a "failure" denounced by the whole internet, but it forced Tencent to think clearly about who it was—in a sense, without that storm of criticism there would have been no Tencent of later years.

六、致2026：关于“没人接盘”的时候

VI. To 2026: On the Times When "No One Will Take It Off Your Hands"

2026年，创业和做产品的人，心里都有一个声音：“做不下去了就卖掉吧。”

In 2026, everyone building a startup or a product hears a voice inside: "If it can't keep going, just sell it."

这种想法不该被嘲笑——绝大多数创业者确实需要退路。但马化腾这段经历提供了一个具体的参照：当你最想卖掉的时候，可能恰恰是它最值钱、只是你还没找到变现方式的时候。

That idea shouldn't be mocked—the vast majority of founders do need a way out. But this chapter of Pony Ma's life offers a concrete reference: the moment you most want to sell may be precisely when it is worth the most, and you simply haven't found the way to turn it into money yet.

当然，这不是说“不卖就一定能成”。腾讯能活下来，靠的是几件事同时成立：用户是真的爱用这个产品（否则卖不掉也没人投）；投资人愿意在泡沫破裂时下注；团队在漫长的不赚钱期里没有散。

Of course, this is not to say "if you don't sell, you'll definitely succeed." Tencent survived because several things held true at once: users genuinely loved the product (otherwise it couldn't be sold, and no one would invest); investors were willing to bet during the bursting of the bubble; and the team did not fall apart through the long stretch without profits.

对我们来说，也许最实际的一课是：

For us, perhaps the most practical lesson is this:

在决定放弃之前，先问自己一句——我放弃的是“这件事”，还是“暂时赚不到钱”？ 如果是后者，也许它只是还没到时候。

Before deciding to give up, ask yourself one question—am I giving up on "this thing," or on "not making money for now"? If it's the latter, maybe it just isn't time yet.

马化腾的性格里还有一个容易被忽略的点：他不擅长讲故事，也不太会“造势”，在很长一段时间里，他被认为是“最不会做公关的互联网大佬”。但恰恰是他这种“闷”，让腾讯的产品能一直贴着用户走——因为他不把精力花在让人相信自己，而是花在让产品自己说话。对今天很多“会包装、不会做事”的团队来说，这可能是一个反向的提醒：热闹是一时的，用户手里的那个图标，才是真的。

There is one more easily overlooked thing in Pony Ma's character: he was not good at telling stories and not good at "building hype," and for a long time he was regarded as "the internet mogul worst at public relations." Yet it is precisely this reticence that let Tencent's products stay close to users all along—because he spent his energy not on making people believe in him, but on letting the product speak for itself. For the many teams today that are "good at packaging but bad at doing," this may be a reminder in reverse: the excitement is fleeting; the icon in the user's hand is what's real.

回头看，腾讯的故事其实很朴素：一个做得早、做得细的产品，一群在最困难时不肯散的人，加上两个愿意在最冷的时候下注的投资人。这几样东西，缺一样，今天可能就没有那只“企鹅”。而马化腾最难的地方，不是做出QQ，而是在没人愿意接盘的时候，选择继续把它做下去。

Looking back, Tencent's story is quite plain: a product built early and carefully, a group of people who refused to scatter in the hardest times, and two investors willing to bet in the coldest season. Take away any one of these, and that "penguin" might not exist today. And the hardest part for Pony Ma was not building QQ, but choosing to keep building it when no one was willing to take it off his hands.
