陈子昂：站在幽州台上痛哭的人，最后死在了县衙的牢里

Chen Zi'ang: The Man Who Wept on the Tower of Youzhou, and Died in a County Jail

——“前不见古人，后不见来者。念天地之悠悠，独怆然而涕下。”

— "I see no ancients before me, no comers after me. I think on the vastness of heaven and earth, and alone, in grief, my tears fall."

2026年的深夜里，很多人会在加班后突然感到一种巨大的空虚：我在做什么？我这么拼，到底为了什么？

Late at night in 2026, many people, after working overtime, are suddenly seized by a vast emptiness: What am I doing? What am I striving so hard for?

一千三百多年前，有一个人站在一座高台上，把这种感觉写成了二十二字。这首诗被后人称为“千古绝唱”，几乎每一个中国读书人都背过它。

More than thirteen hundred years ago, a man stood on a high terrace and caught that feeling in twenty-two characters. Later generations called the poem "a peerless song of the ages," and almost every Chinese schoolchild has memorized it.

写它的人，叫陈子昂。他生前两次入狱，最后冤死在县衙的牢里，年仅四十一二岁。

The man who wrote it was named Chen Zi'ang. In his lifetime he was imprisoned twice, and in the end he died wrongfully in a county jail, barely forty-one or forty-two.

一、从射洪富家子到长安进士

I. From a Rich Boy of Shehong to a Metropolitan Graduate of Chang'an

约661年，陈子昂出生在梓州射洪（今四川射洪）。他的家世不错，是当地有名的富户。

Around 661, Chen Zi'ang was born in Shehong of Zizhou (present-day Shehong, Sichuan). His family was comfortably off—one of the notable wealthy households of the region.

少年时代的陈子昂，是个标准的“侠少”——爱打抱不平，轻财好施，喜欢舞刀弄剑，不太读书。一直到十七八岁，他才突然醒悟，开始发奋读书。这种“浪子回头”的转变，在唐代文人里并不少见，但陈子昂转得格外彻底。

As a youth, Chen Zi'ang was a textbook "young knight-errant": he loved to champion the wronged, held wealth lightly and gave generously, delighted in sword and blade, and cared little for books. Only at seventeen or eighteen did he suddenly awaken and begin to study with all his might. Such a prodigal's turn was not uncommon among Tang men of letters, but Chen Zi'ang turned with exceptional thoroughness.

他后来去了长安。二十四岁左右，他考中进士。这一步，对一个来自蜀地的年轻人来说，意味着进入了帝国的核心。

He later went to Chang'an. At about twenty-four he passed the imperial examination. For a young man from Shu, this step meant entry into the very core of the empire.

那个摔琴的故事

The Story of the Smashed Lute

关于他初到长安时的困境，有一个流传很广的段子：他当时籍籍无名，有一天在街上看到有人高价卖一把胡琴，围观的人很多。陈子昂当众买下，并邀请众人第二天到家里听他弹琴。第二天，众人到齐，他却把琴当场砸碎，说：“我是四川陈子昂，写了上百篇诗文，却没人知道。弹琴是乐工的事，何必留心！”然后把诗文分发给众人。从此，他一夜成名。

About the difficulties of his early days in Chang'an there is a widely circulated anecdote: at the time he was unknown, and one day in the street he saw someone selling a huqin at a high price, with a large crowd looking on. Chen Zi'ang bought it on the spot before everyone and invited the crowd to his home the next day to hear him play. The next day, with all gathered, he smashed the instrument to pieces before them and said: "I am Chen Zi'ang of Sichuan. I have written a hundred pieces of prose and verse, and no one knows them. Playing the lute is a musician's business—why give it a thought!" Then he handed out his writings to the crowd. From that day he was famous overnight.

这个“摔琴”的故事，出自唐代笔记，属于典型的文人逸事，真伪难考。但它透露出一个信息：陈子昂刚到长安时，确实是个没人知道的年轻人。

This "smashing the lute" story comes from Tang-dynasty anecdote collections; it is a typical literati tale whose truth is hard to verify. But it reveals one thing: when Chen Zi'ang first arrived in Chang'an, he really was a young man no one knew.

二、两次从军，两次下狱

II. Twice a Soldier, Twice Imprisoned

陈子昂真正的政治主张，是“廉正”和“务实”。他做官后，屡次上书直陈时政，批评苛政，主张减轻百姓负担。他因此得到过武则天的赏识，被授予麟台正字，后来又做了右拾遗——所以后世称他“陈拾遗”。

Chen Zi'ang's real political principles were "integrity" and "practicality." After taking office he repeatedly submitted memorials speaking plainly about the affairs of the day, criticizing harsh governance, and urging that the burden on the people be lightened. For this he won the appreciation of Wu Zetian and was appointed Proofreader of the Lintai, and later Reminder of the Right (You Shiyi)—hence later ages called him "Chen the Reminder."

但他直言敢谏的性格，也给他招来了麻烦。他曾因“逆党”事件被牵连下狱。此后又两次从军边塞（二十六岁和三十六岁前后），对边防事务颇有见解。

But his blunt, forthright temper also brought him trouble. He was once implicated in a "treasonous faction" case and thrown into prison. Afterward he twice joined campaigns on the frontier (around ages twenty-six and thirty-six), and he had considerable insight into frontier defense.

他的仕途，始终在“被赏识”和“被排挤”之间摇摆。他不是那种能圆滑周旋的人。

His official career swung forever between "being appreciated" and "being squeezed out." He was not the kind of man who could smooth-talk his way through.

三、幽州台上的那一次痛哭

III. That Outburst of Weeping on the Tower of Youzhou

696年前后，契丹叛乱，武则天派弟弟武攸宜率军征讨。陈子昂以“参谋”的身份随军。

Around 696 the Khitan rebelled, and Wu Zetian sent her brother Wu Youyi to lead an army against them. Chen Zi'ang joined the campaign as a staff adviser.

武攸宜不懂军事，屡战屡败。陈子昂多次进谏，甚至请求带兵上前线，都被拒绝，最后还被贬为军曹。

Wu Youyi knew nothing of military affairs and lost battle after battle. Chen Zi'ang remonstrated repeatedly and even asked to lead troops to the front, but was refused every time, and at last was demoted to the post of a low-ranking military officer.

就是在这个时候，他登上了幽州台（今北京一带，相传为燕昭王招贤之所）。他想起当年燕昭王筑黄金台、礼贤下士的故事，想到自己生在这个时代，既看不到那样的明君，也等不到懂自己的人。

It was at this moment that he climbed the Tower of Youzhou (in the area of present-day Beijing, said to be the place where King Zhao of Yan had once called for worthy men). He thought of the old story of King Zhao of Yan building the Golden Terrace and honoring the wise and the humble, and of himself, born in this age, able neither to see such a wise ruler nor to await anyone who understood him.

于是他写下了那首《登幽州台歌》：

And so he wrote "On the Tower of Youzhou" (Deng Youzhou Tai Ge):

前不见古人，

I see no ancients before me,

后不见来者。

no comers after me.

念天地之悠悠，

I think on the vastness of heaven and earth,

独怆然而涕下。

and alone, in grief, my tears fall.

短短二十二字，没有一个生僻字，没有一句用典，却写尽了“一个人在这个世界上，前无古人可依、后无来者可知”的那种绝对孤独。它不是抱怨，而是一个人面对无限时空时，最诚实的渺小感。

In just twenty-two characters, with not a single obscure word and not a single allusion, he wrote out the absolute solitude of "a person in this world with no ancients behind to lean on and no comers ahead to be known by." It is not a complaint; it is the most honest sense of smallness a person feels before infinite time and space.

四、回到射洪，死在县衙

IV. Returning to Shehong, Dying in the County Office

698年（圣历元年）前后，陈子昂因为父亲年老，辞官回乡。不久，父亲去世。

Around 698 (the first year of Shengli), Chen Zi'ang resigned his office and went home because his father was old. Not long after, his father died.

就在他居丧期间，灾难来了。射洪县令段简，贪暴残忍，觊觎陈子昂家的钱财。据记载，他是受了权臣武三思的指使，罗织罪名，把陈子昂抓进了县衙的牢里。

While he was in mourning, disaster came. Duan Jian, the magistrate of Shehong, was greedy and cruel, and coveted the Chen family's wealth. By one account, he acted on the orders of the powerful minister Wu Sansi, framed a charge, and threw Chen Zi'ang into the county jail.

一个有资格在朝堂上议政的“右拾遗”，死在了一个小小县令手里。

A Reminder of the Right—a man entitled to debate policy in the imperial court—died at the hands of a petty county magistrate.

约702年，陈子昂冤死狱中，年仅四十一二岁。他的死，在当时的文坛引起了震动。后人为了纪念他，在射洪金华山修建了“陈子昂读书台”，历朝历代都有人去凭吊。杜甫专门写诗纪念他，韩愈说：“国朝盛文章，子昂始高蹈。”

Around 702, Chen Zi'ang died wrongfully in prison, barely forty-one or forty-two. His death shook the literary world of the day. To commemorate him, later generations built the "Chen Zi'ang Reading Terrace" at Jinhua Mountain in Shehong, and down through the dynasties people came to pay their respects. Du Fu wrote a poem especially in his memory; Han Yu said: "Our dynasty's letters flourished—it was Zi'ang who took the high road first."

五、他做了什么“了不起”的事

V. What "Great" Thing He Did

今天的人知道陈子昂，多半只知道那首《登幽州台歌》。但他在文学史上的真正贡献，其实更大。

People today who know Chen Zi'ang mostly know only "On the Tower of Youzhou." But his true contribution to literary history is in fact greater.

初唐时期，诗坛还被南朝以来的绮靡风气统治——诗写的都是风花雪月、辞藻堆砌。陈子昂第一个站出来，主张恢复“汉魏风骨”，要求诗要有真情实感，要有刚健的内容。

In the early Tang, the poetic world was still ruled by the ornate, decadent manner inherited from the Southern Dynasties—poems of wind and flowers, moon and snow, piled high with ornament. Chen Zi'ang was the first to step forward, advocating a return to the "vigor of Han and Wei," demanding that poetry carry genuine feeling and firm, robust substance.

他确立了“唐诗”该往哪里走的方向。 后来李白、杜甫、白居易这些人的成就，某种程度上都站在他开辟的路上。金元时期的评论家元好问甚至说：“论功若准平吴例，合著黄金铸子昂”——如果按论功行赏的规矩，应该给陈子昂铸一座金像。

He set the direction that "Tang poetry" should take. The achievements of Li Bai, Du Fu, Bai Juyi and the rest afterward stood, in a sense, on the road he opened. The Jin-Yuan critic Yuan Haowen even said: "If merit were reckoned by the precedent of the conquest of Wu, a golden statue of Zi'ang should be cast"—if reward followed true merit, a golden image ought to be made of Chen Zi'ang.

六、他写下的诗

VI. The Poems He Wrote

除了《登幽州台歌》，陈子昂还留下了《感遇》三十八首。这是一组规模很大的组诗，写生死、写时事、写边塞、写求仙，几乎涵盖了他对世界所有的看法。

Besides "On the Tower of Youzhou," Chen Zi'ang left behind the thirty-eight poems of "Stirred by What I Encounter" (Ganyu). This is a large-scale sequence, writing of life and death, of current affairs, of the frontier, of seeking immortality—covering almost every view he held of the world.

他还写过《蓟丘览古》等怀古之作。他在这些诗里反复追问同一个问题：为什么好人总是被埋没，为什么小人总是得势？ 这不是空谈，而是他用自己的仕途一笔一笔换来的结论。

He also wrote such meditations on the past as "Looking Back at Antiquity from Jiqiu" (Jiqiu Lange). Again and again in these poems he asks the same question: why are good men forever buried, and petty men forever in power? This was no idle talk, but a conclusion he bought with his official career, one stroke at a time.

在理论上，他写过《修竹篇序》，明确提出要“以风雅兴寄”、要“汉魏风骨”。这篇文章不长，却被后来的人当作唐代诗文革新的一份宣言。它相当于在一片浮华的风气里，替后来者立了一根标尺。

In theory he wrote the Preface to the Tall Bamboo Poems (Xiuzhu Pian Xu), clearly calling for "lodging feeling in the manner of the Airs and the Odes" (fengya) and for the "vigor of Han and Wei." The piece is not long, yet later readers took it as a manifesto for the reform of Tang letters. Amid a climate of glitter, it set a standard for those who came after.

今天的人在博物馆、在教科书里遇见陈子昂，多半只会背那二十二个字。但那二十二个字之所以能流传千年，恰恰是因为他前面还铺了那么多东西——他的失意、他的仕途、他一生都在证明却始终没被兑现的那些道理。

People today who meet Chen Zi'ang in a museum or a textbook mostly just recite those twenty-two characters. But the reason those twenty-two characters have lived a thousand years is precisely that he had laid so much beneath them—his disappointments, his official career, the truths he spent a lifetime proving and never saw fulfilled.

七、写给2026年的我们

VII. To Us in 2026

陈子昂的一生，是一个“理想主义者被现实碾碎”的故事。

Chen Zi'ang's life is the story of an idealist crushed by reality.

他想做的是改天换地的大事，得到的是一贬再贬；他想保全的是家里的老人，最后连自己都没能保全。他死得不体面，也不壮烈，就是被一个小官以莫须有的罪名，闷死在牢里。

What he wanted was to remake heaven and earth; what he got was demotion upon demotion. What he wanted to protect was the old man at home; in the end he could not even protect himself. His death was neither dignified nor heroic—simply smothered out in a jail cell by a petty official on a fabricated charge.

可就是这样一个“失败者”，写下了中国人一千多年来关于孤独最著名的一首诗。

And yet it was this very "failure" who wrote the most famous poem the Chinese have had for over a thousand years on the subject of loneliness.

2026年的我们，常常觉得“努力没有用”“改变不了什么”。陈子昂也改变不了什么——他没有阻止契丹的叛乱，没有扳倒武三思，甚至没能保护自己。但他改变了“诗应该怎么写”这件事，一改就是一千年。

We in 2026 often feel that "effort is useless," that "nothing can be changed." Chen Zi'ang could change nothing either—he did not stop the Khitan rebellion, did not bring down Wu Sansi, could not even protect himself. But he changed how poetry ought to be written, and that change lasted a thousand years.

有时候，一个人能在自己完全失控的人生里，留下一个谁也拿不走的东西，就已经赢了。

Sometimes, if a person can leave behind, in a life utterly beyond his control, one thing that no one can take from him, he has already won.

还有一点值得记住：他写那首《登幽州台歌》的时候，并不是在做一件“扬名立万”的事。他是在被贬、被晾、被无视的境地里，一个人爬上高台，对着天地发呆。他没有想到那二十二个字会流传千年，他只是想说一句真话。

One more thing is worth remembering: when he wrote "On the Tower of Youzhou," he was not doing something to "make his name." Demoted, set aside, ignored, he climbed a high terrace alone and stared blankly at heaven and earth. He never imagined those twenty-two characters would last a thousand years; he simply wanted to say one true sentence.

最好的作品，往往不是“写给世界”的，而是“写给自己的”。 世界恰好接住了它，那是世界的事，不是他的事。

The best work is often not written "for the world," but "for oneself." That the world happened to catch it is the world's business, not his.

这或许也是给所有“暂时不被看见”的人的一句安慰：你此刻写下、做出、坚持的东西，未必立刻有人喝彩。但只要它是真的，它就有机会，在某一天，穿越时间，被一个素不相识的人认真接住。

This may also be a comfort to everyone who is "for the moment unseen": what you write, make, or hold fast to now may not win applause at once. But so long as it is true, it may one day travel through time and be caught, with care, by a total stranger.

七、写给2026年的我们

VII. To Us in 2026

史料来源与辨析 · Sources & Notes

史料来源与辨析

Sources and Notes

1. 生平基础事实：陈子昂（字伯玉），约661年生于梓州射洪（今四川射洪），约702年卒，年约四十一二岁。为通行记载。其生卒年学界有不同考订（如661—702、659—700等），本文采用最通行的表述。

1. Basic facts of his life: Chen Zi'ang (courtesy name Boyu), born about 661 in Shehong of Zizhou (present-day Shehong, Sichuan), died about 702, aged roughly forty-one or forty-two. This follows the standard accounts. Scholars have proposed differing dates for his birth and death (e.g. 661–702, 659–700); this article adopts the most commonly accepted formulation.

2. 早年“侠少”、后发奋读书：为通行记载，见于《旧唐书》《新唐书》本传。

2. His early years as a "young knight-errant" and later devoted study: standard accounts, found in his biographies in the Old Book of Tang (Jiu Tangshu) and the New Book of Tang (Xin Tangshu).

3. “摔琴”成名故事：出自唐代笔记小说类文献，属文人逸事，真实性无法确证，本文明确标注为“传说”。

3. The "smashing the lute" story of his rise to fame: drawn from Tang-dynasty anecdote and tale collections; a literati's anecdote whose truth cannot be confirmed, and this article expressly labels it "legend."

4. 中进士与任职：约二十四岁中进士，历任麟台正字、右拾遗。为确证史实。

4. Passing the examination and holding office: he passed the imperial examination at about twenty-four and served successively as Proofreader of the Lintai and Reminder of the Right. This is confirmed historical fact.

5. 两次从军与征契丹：万岁通天元年（696年）随武攸宜征契丹，谏而不听，贬为军曹，登幽州台作《登幽州台歌》。为通行记载。《登幽州台歌》的确切成诗时间，学界亦有讨论。

5. His two military campaigns and the expedition against the Khitan: in the first year of Wansui Tongtian (696) he followed Wu Youyi against the Khitan, his remonstrances went unheeded, he was demoted to a junior military officer, and he climbed the Tower of Youzhou to write "On the Tower of Youzhou." This follows the standard accounts. Scholars have also discussed the precise date of the poem's composition.

6. 冤死狱中：据《旧唐书》等记载，射洪县令段简受权臣武三思指使，罗织罪名将其下狱，陈子昂死于狱中。为通行记载。辨析：段简的动机与幕后指使者，史料记载存在不同说法，本文采用最通行的“武三思指使”说，并保留“贪财构陷”这一层面。

6. His wrongful death in prison: according to the Old Book of Tang and other sources, Duan Jian, magistrate of Shehong, acting on the orders of the powerful minister Wu Sansi, framed a charge and jailed him, and Chen Zi'ang died in prison. This follows the standard accounts. A critical note: the sources differ as to Duan Jian's motive and as to who stood behind him; this article adopts the most common view, that Wu Sansi gave the order, while retaining the element of "framing him out of greed for his wealth."

7. 文学贡献：倡导“汉魏风骨”“风雅兴寄”，为初唐诗文革新的先驱；杜甫、韩愈、元好问等均有高度评价。为确证史实。元好问语出《论诗三十首》，原文为“论功若准平吴例，合著黄金铸子昂”。

7. Literary contribution: he championed the "vigor of Han and Wei" and "lodging feeling in the manner of the Airs and the Odes," and was a pioneer of the reform of early Tang letters; Du Fu, Han Yu, Yuan Haowen and others all rated him highly. This is confirmed historical fact. Yuan Haowen's words come from his "Thirty Poems on Poetry" (Lun Shi Sanshi Shou), the original reading being "if merit were reckoned by the precedent of the conquest of Wu, a golden statue of Zi'ang should be cast."
