柴可夫斯基：写下《天鹅湖》的人，一生怕被人知道真实的自己

Tchaikovsky: The Man Who Wrote Swan Lake and Spent His Life Fearing That His True Self Would Be Known

——“我的一生，都在躲避一件事。”

—"All my life I have been fleeing from one thing."

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂73 / 100约 3113 字 · 3113 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，全世界有无数孩子在学芭蕾，跳的是《天鹅湖》；每逢圣诞节，各大剧院的《胡桃夹子》一票难求。写下这两部音乐的人，是俄国人柴可夫斯基。他被公认为俄罗斯古典音乐最伟大的作曲家。

In 2026, countless children around the world are learning ballet by dancing Swan Lake; every Christmas, tickets to The Nutcracker at the great theaters are impossible to get. The man who wrote this music was the Russian Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky. He is widely acknowledged as the greatest composer of Russian classical music.

但在他活着的年代，他一直在逃离一样东西——他自己。

But in his own lifetime, he was always fleeing from one thing—himself.

他一生都在小心翼翼地藏着一个秘密。他结婚，是想“治好”自己；他逃婚，是发现自己治不好；他靠一位从未见面的富孀的资助活了十三年；他死的时候，成了一个至今没有定论的悬案。

All his life he carefully guarded a secret. He married in order to "cure" himself; he fled the marriage when he found he could not be cured; he lived for thirteen years on the patronage of a wealthy widow he never met; and when he died, he left behind a case that remains unsolved to this day.

一、从法律官员到音乐学院

I. From a Career in the Chancery to the Conservatory

1840年5月7日，彼得·伊里奇·柴可夫斯基出生在俄国沃特金斯克的一个矿业工程师家庭。他从小就对音乐极度敏感，据说听到一段旋律就会哭。家人并不觉得这有什么用。

On May 7, 1840, Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky was born in Votkinsk, Russia, into the family of a mining engineer. From early childhood he was exquisitely sensitive to music—it is said that hearing a melody could make him weep. His family did not see what use this could be.

按当时俄国上流社会的规矩，他进了圣彼得堡的法律学校，毕业后做了司法部的公务员。那份工作他做了几年，平淡无奇。

Following the conventions of upper-class Russia at the time, he entered the Imperial School of Jurisprudence in St. Petersburg, and after graduating became a civil servant in the Ministry of Justice. He held the post for several years, uneventfully.

1861年，二十一岁的他做了一个惊人的决定：辞去公职，去刚成立不久的圣彼得堡音乐学院学音乐。一个已经端上“铁饭碗”的年轻人，为什么要去做一个前途未卜的乐师？在当时的俄国，这不是“追求梦想”，而是“自毁前程”。

In 1861, at the age of twenty-one, he made a startling decision: he resigned his government post to study music at the newly founded St. Petersburg Conservatory. Why would a young man who already held an "iron rice bowl" choose to become a musician with no prospects? In Russia at that time, this was not "pursuing a dream" but "throwing away one's future."

他跟着安东·鲁宾斯坦学作曲。1866年，他去莫斯科，成了莫斯科音乐学院的和声学教师。

He studied composition under Anton Rubinstein. In 1866 he went to Moscow and became a teacher of harmony at the Moscow Conservatory.

二、写不出作品的那些年

II. The Years When He Could Not Write

柴可夫斯基起初并不顺利。他的第一部交响曲写得很痛苦，反复修改，几乎让他精神崩溃。他天性极度敏感，一次批评就能让他整夜失眠，一次冷场就能让他怀疑自己。

Tchaikovsky did not begin well. His First Symphony was agony to write, endlessly revised, and nearly broke his mind. He was by nature extremely sensitive: a single word of criticism could keep him awake all night, and a single chilly reception could make him doubt himself.

但他写得太快了。交响曲、协奏曲、歌剧、芭蕾、室内乐，他几乎没有停过。他靠作曲和教学勉强维生，长期处于焦虑和抑郁之中。

But he wrote too quickly. Symphonies, concertos, operas, ballets, chamber music—he hardly ever stopped. He scraped a living from composing and teaching, and lived for long stretches in anxiety and depression.

1876年，转机来了。一位富有的遗孀——娜杰日达·冯·梅克，通过中间人联系上他，表示愿意每年资助他一大笔钱，让他可以专心作曲。唯一的条件是：两人永远不见面。

In 1876, a turning point came. A wealthy widow, Nadezhda von Meck, contacted him through an intermediary and offered to support him with a large annual sum so that he could devote himself to composition. There was one condition: the two were never to meet.

这段奇特的关系持续了十三年。他们写了上千封信，谈论音乐、人生、信仰、孤独。梅克夫人称他为“我最亲爱的朋友”，但在街上遇见时，两人会彼此避开，连点头都不点。他们用一千多封信，维持了一段从未见面的爱情与友谊。

This strange relationship lasted thirteen years. They exchanged more than a thousand letters, discussing music, life, faith, and loneliness. Madame von Meck called him "my dearest friend," yet when they happened to meet in the street, they would avoid each other, not even nodding. With over a thousand letters, they sustained a love and friendship that never once met face to face.

三、那场只持续了几周的婚姻

III. The Marriage That Lasted Only a Few Weeks

1877年，柴可夫斯基做了一件让他后悔一生的事。

In 1877, Tchaikovsky did something he would regret for the rest of his life.

一位名叫安东尼娜·米柳科娃的年轻女性向他表达了爱慕。柴可夫斯基并不爱她。但当时他正处在巨大的精神煎熬中——他想通过结婚，来“解决”自己内心那个他无法面对的问题。他天真地以为，一个正常的家庭，可以把他“掰回来”。

A young woman named Antonina Miliukova declared her love for him. Tchaikovsky did not love her. But at the time he was in enormous spiritual torment—he hoped that by marrying he might "resolve" the problem within himself that he could not face. He naively believed that a normal family life could "set him straight."

婚礼之后，他几乎立刻崩溃了。婚后几周，他的精神接近失常，跑到莫斯科河边试图让自己得肺炎，甚至还去求医、想过自尽。最后，他逃离了妻子，逃到国外。

Almost immediately after the wedding he collapsed. Within weeks of the marriage his mind was close to breaking: he went to the Moscow River in an attempt to catch pneumonia, even sought medical help, and considered suicide. In the end he fled his wife and escaped abroad.

这场婚姻从一开始就不是关于爱情的，而是一次失败至极的自我治疗。

This marriage was never about love from the start; it was an attempt at self-treatment that failed utterly.

此后余生，他一直向妻子支付赡养费，但两人几乎没有再共同生活过。

For the rest of his life he continued to pay his wife an allowance, but the two hardly ever lived together again.

四、《天鹅湖》与《悲怆》

IV. Swan Lake and "Pathétique"

柴可夫斯基最重要的作品，几乎都诞生在这段长期的精神孤寂里。

Nearly all of Tchaikovsky's most important works were born in this long spiritual solitude.

1877年，他完成了《天鹅湖》——但这个版本的首演并不成功，后来经重新编排才成为经典。真正让他声名大噪的，是《叶甫盖尼·奥涅金》《第四交响曲》，以及后来的《睡美人》《胡桃夹子》。

In 1877 he completed Swan Lake—but the premiere of that version was not a success; only after being re-choreographed did it become a classic. What truly made his name were Eugene Onegin, the Fourth Symphony, and later The Sleeping Beauty and The Nutcracker.

1893年，他写出了第六交响曲。这部作品后来被称为《悲怆》。它的末乐章不是胜利，不是狂欢，而是一种缓慢的、近乎熄灭的下沉。很多人说，那是他写给自己的安魂曲。

In 1893 he wrote his Sixth Symphony. The work later became known as the "Pathétique." Its final movement is not triumph, not jubilation, but a slow, almost extinguishing descent. Many say it was the requiem he wrote for himself.

首演之后没几天，他就去世了。

Only days after the premiere, he was dead.

五、被突然切断的十三年

V. Thirteen Years Cut Off Without Warning

1890年，一件对柴可夫斯基打击极大的事发生了：梅克夫人突然中断了资助，也中断了通信。

In 1890, something happened that struck Tchaikovsky a heavy blow: Madame von Meck abruptly cut off both her patronage and their correspondence.

她的理由是“经济困难”，但这个说法并不完全可信。更可能的原因，是她在生命的最后阶段精神状态不佳，加上家庭变故与周边人的影响。无论原因是什么，对柴可夫斯基来说，这不只是收入的中断，更是那段维系了十三年的、最私密的情感联系，被单方面掐断了。

Her stated reason was "financial difficulty," but that explanation is not entirely credible. More likely, in the final stage of her life she was in poor mental health, compounded by family troubles and the influence of those around her. Whatever the reason, for Tchaikovsky this was not merely an interruption of income but the unilateral severing of the most intimate emotional bond of his life, one that had sustained him for thirteen years.

他失去的不是一笔钱，而是一个可以毫无保留地说话的对象。 对一个几乎无法对任何人坦露内心的人来说，这几乎等于关掉了最后一扇窗。

What he lost was not a sum of money but the one person to whom he could speak without reserve. For a man who could hardly bare his heart to anyone, it was almost like the last window being shut.

此后三年，他的创作依然丰沛，但他的孤独感明显加深。他写出了《胡桃夹子》，写出了第六交响曲。有人说，他是在完成了对自己一生的总结之后，才离开的。

In the three years that followed, his output remained abundant, but his loneliness deepened noticeably. He wrote The Nutcracker, he wrote the Sixth Symphony. Some say he departed only after completing a summing-up of his own life.

六、他究竟是怎么死的

VI. How Did He Actually Die?

1893年11月6日（公历），柴可夫斯基在圣彼得堡去世，年仅53岁。

On November 6, 1893 (New Style), Tchaikovsky died in St. Petersburg at the age of only fifty-three.

官方给出的死因是霍乱——据说他喝了一杯没有煮沸的水。这个说法在当时被广泛接受。

The official cause of death was cholera—he was said to have drunk a glass of unboiled water. This account was widely accepted at the time.

但是，一百多年来，另一个说法始终没有消失：他可能是因为同性恋身份即将被公开，而被一群同学组成的“荣誉法庭”逼迫自杀。 这个说法由苏联学者奥尔洛娃在1979年移居西方后提出，影响很大，被写进了很多传记和纪录片。

But for more than a century another account has never disappeared: that he may have been driven to suicide by a "court of honor" made up of former classmates, because his homosexuality was about to be made public. This account was put forward by the Soviet scholar Alexandra Orlova after she emigrated to the West in 1979; it has been highly influential and has found its way into many biographies and documentaries.

需要老实说的是：这个“荣誉法庭”说，至今没有找到直接的档案证据。 支持它的人引用各种间接材料，反对它的人则认为，霍乱说的证据链反而更完整。两派争论了几十年，没有一个能让所有人信服的答案。

It must be said honestly: no direct archival evidence for the "court of honor" theory has ever been found. Its supporters cite various indirect materials, while its opponents argue that the cholera account actually has the more complete chain of evidence. The two camps have argued for decades, and neither has produced an answer that convinces everyone.

可以确定的是：柴可夫斯基一生都在极力隐藏自己的性取向，他确实为此付出了巨大的精神代价。这个秘密，是他所有痛苦的底层背景之一。

What can be stated with certainty is this: Tchaikovsky spent his whole life striving to conceal his sexual orientation, and he truly paid a heavy psychological price for it. That secret was one of the underlying backdrops to all his suffering.

六、写给2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

柴可夫斯基的故事，是一个关于“不敢做自己”的故事。

Tchaikovsky's story is a story about being afraid to be oneself.

他用婚姻去掩盖，用远离去躲避，用一千多封信去维持一段安全距离的关系。他写出了最华美、最动人的音乐，可他自己在生活里，几乎没有一天是放松的。

He used marriage to cover things up, distance to evade them, and more than a thousand letters to sustain a relationship kept at a safe remove. He wrote the most gorgeous, most moving music, yet in his own life there was scarcely a single day when he was at ease.

今天，我们当然可以说，他活在一个远不如我们宽容的年代。但正因如此，我们更应该理解他：一个人的才华，可以在压抑中绽放；但一个人的生命，往往在压抑中被耗尽。

Today we can of course say that he lived in an age far less tolerant than our own. But precisely for that reason we ought to understand him all the better: a person's talent can blossom under repression, but a person's life is often used up by it.

他的音乐之所以动人，也许正因为它里面有太多说不出口的东西。那些华丽的旋律之下，藏着一个一生都在害怕被看见的人。

Perhaps his music moves us precisely because it holds so much that could not be spoken. Beneath those sumptuous melodies hides a man who spent his whole life afraid of being seen.

《悲怆》的最后一个音符落下去的时候，几乎是没有声音的。那也许就是柴可夫斯基想说、却始终不敢说出的那句话。

When the final note of the "Pathétique" falls, there is almost no sound at all. That may be the very sentence Tchaikovsky wanted to say but never dared to utter.

值得一提的是，他也是把俄罗斯音乐推向世界的关键人物。他访问过美国，在卡内基音乐厅指挥过自己的作品，让西方第一次认真看待来自俄国的交响乐。他一生都在躲避自己，却把整个民族的音乐，带到了世界的正中央。 这大概是他一生中最讽刺、也最辉煌的一件事。

It is worth noting that he was also a key figure in bringing Russian music to the world. He visited America and conducted his own works at Carnegie Hall, making the West take Russian symphonic music seriously for the first time. All his life he fled from himself, yet he carried the music of an entire nation to the very center of the world. This is perhaps the most ironic—and the most brilliant—thing about his life.

他去世后，他的音乐却一直在世界上活着。《天鹅湖》成了最著名的芭蕾舞剧之一，《胡桃夹子》成了每年圣诞节的固定节目，《悲怆》成了每一个指挥家都想驾驭的高峰。一个一生都在躲避自己、害怕被看清的人，最终反而是通过音乐，被全世界认识得一清二楚。这大概是最好的补偿——你不敢让人看的地方，你的作品替你说完了。

After his death, his music went on living in the world. Swan Lake became one of the most famous ballets, The Nutcracker a fixed feature of every Christmas, the "Pathétique" a summit every conductor wants to master. A man who spent his whole life fleeing himself and fearing to be seen clearly was in the end known completely, by the whole world, through his music. This is perhaps the finest compensation of all—the places you dare not let anyone see, your works finish speaking for you.

史料来源与辨析 · Sources & Notes

史料来源与辨析

Sources and Notes

1. 生平基础事实：彼得·伊里奇·柴可夫斯基，1840年5月7日生于沃特金斯克，1893年11月6日（公历；俄历10月25日）卒于圣彼得堡，年53岁。为确证史实。

1. Basic biographical facts: Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, born May 7, 1840, in Votkinsk, died November 6, 1893 (New Style; October 25, Old Style), in St. Petersburg, aged fifty-three. Established historical fact.

2. 早年：毕业于圣彼得堡法律学校，曾任司法部公务员，后辞职入圣彼得堡音乐学院学习。为确证史实。

2. Early years: graduated from the Imperial School of Jurisprudence in St. Petersburg, served as a civil servant in the Ministry of Justice, then resigned to study at the St. Petersburg Conservatory. Established historical fact.

3. 与梅克夫人的关系：1876年起由娜杰日达·冯·梅克提供资助，持续约十三年，双方约定永不见面，互通大量书信。为确证史实。“爱情”一说属后人解读，本人通信中并无此类明确表述，本文以“奇特的、保持距离的深厚情谊”定位。

3. Relationship with Madame von Meck: from 1876, Nadezhda von Meck provided patronage that lasted about thirteen years; the two agreed never to meet and exchanged a great many letters. Established historical fact. The notion of "love" is a later interpretation—no such explicit statement appears in their own correspondence—and this essay characterizes it as "a strange, deep, distanced bond."

4. 婚姻：1877年与安东尼娜·米柳科娃结婚，婚后数周即精神崩溃并出逃。为确证史实。关于他结婚的动机，一般认为是试图以婚姻“解决”自己的同性恋问题，这是研究者较为一致的解读。

4. Marriage: in 1877 he married Antonina Miliukova, and within weeks of the wedding suffered a breakdown and fled. Established historical fact. As for his motive for marrying, it is generally held that he was trying to "resolve" his homosexuality through marriage; this is a fairly consistent interpretation among scholars.

5. 死因：官方记载为霍乱（饮用未煮沸的水所致）。另一个重要说法：由苏联学者亚历山大·奥尔洛娃（Alexandra Orlova）于1979年提出，认为柴可夫斯基是被一个由同学组成的“荣誉法庭”逼迫服毒自杀，以免同性恋身份曝光。辨析：该说流传极广，但缺乏直接的档案证据，且与霍乱说的证据存在冲突。本文并列两说，明确指出前者是官方记载、后者是重要但存疑的推论，不采信单一定论。

5. Cause of death: officially recorded as cholera (caused by drinking unboiled water). Another important account: put forward in 1979 by the Soviet scholar Alexandra Orlova, holding that Tchaikovsky was forced to take poison by a "court of honor" of former classmates so that his homosexuality would not be exposed. Analysis: this account has circulated extremely widely, but it lacks direct archival evidence and conflicts with the evidence for the cholera account. This essay presents both accounts side by side, stating clearly that the former is the official record and the latter an important but doubtful inference, and does not endorse either as definitive.

6. 性取向：柴可夫斯基的同性恋身份，在当代研究中已被普遍承认（其本人通信与同时代人的记述可佐证）。但在其生前与苏联时期，这一点长期被官方叙事回避。

6. Sexual orientation: Tchaikovsky's homosexuality is now generally acknowledged in contemporary scholarship (supported by his own correspondence and accounts by contemporaries). Yet during his lifetime and throughout the Soviet period, this was long avoided in the official narrative.
