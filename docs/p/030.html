贝多芬：听不见声音的“乐圣”

Beethoven: The “Music Saint” Who Could Not Hear

——他在彻底的寂静里，写出了人类最欢乐的曲子

— In total silence, he wrote humanity’s most joyful music

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者30 / 100约 2365 字 · 2365 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，耳机是绝大多数人听音乐的入口。戴上它，世界就被关上，只剩声音。可如果有一天，连声音都没有了呢？

In 2026, headphones are how the vast majority of people enter music. Put them on and the world shuts out, leaving only sound. But what if one day even the sound were gone?

有一个人，从二十多岁起就一点点失去听觉，到四十多岁几乎完全聋掉。而他最伟大的一部作品——一部结尾唱的是“拥抱吧，亿万人民”的欢乐之歌——正是在他彻底听不见之后写出来的。

There was a man who began losing his hearing bit by bit from his twenties, and by his forties was almost completely deaf. And his greatest work — one whose finale sings a song of joy, “Embrace, ye millions” — was written precisely after he had gone completely deaf.

他叫路德维希·凡·贝多芬。他不是靠天赋战胜了命运，他是靠一种几乎残忍的倔强，在无声里造出了声音。

His name was Ludwig van Beethoven. He did not overcome fate through talent; he made sound out of silence through a stubbornness that was almost cruel.

一、一个“又矮又倔”的年轻人

I. A “Short and Stubborn” Young Man

1770年，贝多芬出生在德国波恩。他父亲是个宫廷乐师，脾气不好，一心想把儿子打造成第二个莫扎特，好拿他去换钱。小贝多芬常常在半夜被叫起来练琴——这不是浪漫的童年，而是被逼出来的童年。

Beethoven was born in Bonn, Germany, in 1770. His father was a court musician, ill-tempered, determined to mold his son into a second Mozart so he could sell him for money. The young Beethoven was often roused from bed at midnight to practice — not a romantic childhood, but a forced one.

1787年，十七岁的贝多芬去了维也纳，见到了他崇拜的莫扎特。据记载，莫扎特听完他弹奏后说过一句大意是“这个人将来会让世界谈论他”的话。

In 1787, at seventeen, Beethoven went to Vienna and met the Mozart he worshipped. According to the record, after hearing him play, Mozart said something to the effect that “this one will make the world talk about him.”

1792年，他又一次来到维也纳，跟着海顿学习，从此在那里定居，靠教琴、演出、出版作品谋生。他也慢慢成了维也纳最抢手也最“难搞”的钢琴家：又矮、又黑、又倔，弹起来却像有风暴。

In 1792 he came to Vienna again, studied under Haydn, and settled there for good, making his living by teaching, performing, and publishing his works. He gradually became Vienna’s most sought-after and most “difficult” pianist: short, dark, stubborn, and yet storm-like at the keyboard.

二、命运的宣判

II. Fate’s Sentence

1796年前后，贝多芬发现自己的耳朵出了问题。先是耳鸣，然后高音听不清，别人说话他得凑近，最后连钢琴的琴音也渐渐远去。

Around 1796 Beethoven noticed that something was wrong with his ears. First came tinnitus, then high notes grew indistinct, then he had to lean in when people spoke, and at last even the sound of the piano faded away.

对一个音乐家来说，这几乎等于判了死刑。

For a musician this was practically a death sentence.

1802年，他躲到维也纳郊外的小镇海利根施塔特，写下了一封留给弟弟的、从未寄出的信——后人称之为《海利根施塔特遗书》。在那封信里，他写下了自己的绝望：他一度想过结束生命，是“艺术”把他拉了回来。

In 1802 he withdrew to Heiligenstadt, a small town outside Vienna, and wrote a letter to his brothers that was never sent — later known as the Heiligenstadt Testament. In it he set down his despair: he had thought of ending his life, and it was “art” that pulled him back.

他写道，大意是：是艺术留住了我。在完成我该完成的使命之前，我不能离开。

He wrote, in effect: it was art that held me back. Before I complete the mission I am meant to complete, I cannot leave.

这封信，成了一个分水岭。此后的贝多芬，像是把整个世界都关在了门外，然后把自己完全塞进了音乐里。

That letter became a watershed. The Beethoven who followed seemed to shut the whole world outside the door, and then stuff himself entirely into music.

三、聋着的那个人，在写什么

III. What the Deaf Man Was Writing

从1802年到1827年，贝多芬失聪的过程持续了二十多年。到1819年前后，他已经几乎完全听不见，与人交谈要靠纸笔——他随身带着“谈话本”，别人把话写上去，他再口答。

From 1802 to 1827, Beethoven’s loss of hearing went on for more than twenty years. By around 1819 he could hardly hear at all, and conversing required pen and paper — he carried “conversation books,” in which others wrote their words and he answered aloud.

他的生活也越来越不合常理：他争夺侄子卡尔的监护权，把全部父爱和偏执都倾注在这个孩子身上；卡尔不堪重负，曾一度试图自杀。这些事让他在外人眼里更像个“疯子”。

His life grew increasingly irregular: he fought for custody of his nephew Karl, pouring all his fatherly love and obsession into the boy; Karl, unable to bear the burden, at one point tried to take his own life. These affairs made him look all the more like a “madman” to outsiders.

但就是在这段时间里，他写出了：

Yet it was during this period that he wrote:

- 一部部交响曲与四重奏，把音乐从“悦耳”推向了“思想”；

- Symphony after symphony and quartet after quartet, pushing music from “pleasing” toward “thought”;

- 尤其是《第九交响曲》——1824年5月7日首演。

- Above all, the Symphony No. 9 — premiered on May 7, 1824.

首演那天，女高音不得不轻轻拉起他的衣袖，让他转身——因为他在台上完全听不见身后观众的掌声。据说，当全场起立欢呼时，他没有任何反应。他看不见这个场面（他背对着观众），也听不见这个场面。

On the day of the premiere, the soprano had to gently tug his sleeve to make him turn around — because on stage he could not hear the applause behind him at all. It is said that when the whole hall rose to cheer, he showed no reaction. He could not see the scene (his back was to the audience), nor hear it.

一个聋子，写出了一首让全人类合唱的歌。

A deaf man had written a song for all humanity to sing.

四、他的“失败”是什么

IV. What His “Failure” Was

如果按世俗标准，贝多芬算非常成功：他名满欧洲，作品受人追捧，连歌德都对他另眼相看。

By worldly standards, Beethoven was a great success: famous throughout Europe, his works admired, even Goethe held him in special regard.

那他为什么可以被放进“失败者”的书里？

So why does he belong in a book about “failures”?

因为他的“失败”不在事业，而在身体本身。他一生最大的打击，不是没名气、不是没钱，而是他的手艺所依赖的那个器官，被一点点收走了。一个画家慢慢失明，一个歌手慢慢失声，一个音乐家慢慢失聪——这是最精准、也最残忍的一种剥夺。

Because his “failure” lay not in his career but in his body itself. His life’s greatest blow was not a lack of fame or money, but that the very organ his craft depended on was slowly taken from him. A painter slowly going blind, a singer slowly losing her voice, a musician slowly going deaf — this is the most precise and most cruel kind of deprivation.

而贝多芬的应对方式，是把自己关进工作里。他的朋友说他脾气越来越躁，可他的作品越来越深。1827年3月26日，他在维也纳去世。据记载，他临终前，天空闪过一道雷电，他举起手臂，握成拳。

And Beethoven’s way of coping was to shut himself up in his work. His friends said his temper grew ever more volatile, but his works grew ever deeper. On March 26, 1827, he died in Vienna. According to the record, as he lay dying a flash of lightning crossed the sky, and he raised his arm and clenched it into a fist.

后来的人研究他的遗骨与头发，发现他体内铅含量极高——这可能是他诸多疾病的一部分原因。

Later researchers studying his remains and hair found extremely high lead levels in his body — which may have been part of the cause of his many illnesses.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

贝多芬给今天的人，留了一句非常硬的话：当你的处境收走了你的条件，你唯一还剩下的，是你的意志。

Beethoven left people today a very hard-edged sentence: when your circumstances take away your conditions, the only thing you have left is your will.

他不是不怕。他在遗书里写得很清楚，他绝望过，想过死。但他最终选择了另一种反应：既然世界要把他关进寂静，那他就用寂静当画布。他不是没被击倒，他是被击倒之后，又爬起来，把最痛的那一刀，变成了最好的作品。

It was not that he was unafraid. In his testament he wrote plainly that he had despaired and thought of death. But he ultimately chose a different response: if the world was going to shut him into silence, then he would use silence as his canvas. He was not un-struck-down; he was struck down, and then rose again, turning the most painful blow into his finest work.

对2026年的我们，这有两层意思。

For us in 2026, this carries two meanings.

第一，不要等到“条件齐全”才动手。很多人总在等：等有钱了、等身体好了、等机会到了。贝多芬等不来他的耳朵，于是他用没有耳朵的方式去工作。你手上的条件不完美，这是常态，不是借口。

First, don’t wait until “all the conditions are in place” to begin. Many people are always waiting: for money, for better health, for the right opportunity. Beethoven could not wait for his ears, so he worked in a way that had no ears. The conditions in your hands are imperfect — that is the norm, not an excuse.

第二，痛苦不是勋章，但它可以是燃料。贝多芬最伟大的作品，不是在幸福里写出来的，是在绝望里写出来的。这不是说痛苦值得赞美——而是说，当痛苦不可避免地来了，你有权决定，把它变成废墟，还是变成火。

Second, suffering is not a medal, but it can be fuel. Beethoven’s greatest works were not written in happiness but in despair. This is not to say that suffering deserves praise — it is to say that when suffering comes unavoidably, you have the right to decide whether to turn it into ruins or into fire.

他在最深的寂静里，写下了最响亮的欢乐。这就是一个人能对命运做出的最漂亮的回答。

In the deepest silence he wrote the loudest joy. That is the most beautiful answer a person can give to fate.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “首演时观众掌声雷动、贝多芬毫无反应、被人拉转身”——这是流传极广的情节，细节（谁拉他、掌声持续多久）在不同回忆中略有出入，但“他因失聪而未能听到现场反响”是各方一致的记载，文中已用“据说”限定。

7. “At the premiere the audience burst into applause, Beethoven showed no reaction, and was turned around by someone” — this is an extremely widespread account; the details (who turned him, how long the applause lasted) vary slightly across recollections, but “he failed to hear the response because of his deafness” is consistent in all accounts, and the text qualifies it with “it is said.”

8. “莫扎特听完贝多芬弹奏后的评价”——相关记载见于早期传记（如奥托·扬等），但其真实性在音乐史学界有争议，属“存疑轶事”，文中已用“据记载”处理。

8. “Mozart’s assessment after hearing Beethoven play” — the relevant record appears in early biographies (such as Otto Jahn’s); its authenticity is disputed among music historians and counts as a “doubtful anecdote,” which the text marks with “according to the record.”

9. “贝多芬死因”——肝硬化等肝病是通行判断；近年对头发与遗骨的研究发现其体内/发中铅含量极高，被认为可能与他的多种病症有关，但不能据此断言“铅中毒就是唯一死因”。文中表述为“可能是一部分原因”。

9. “Beethoven’s cause of death” — cirrhosis and other liver diseases are the usual judgment; recent studies of his hair and remains found extremely high lead levels in his body and hair, thought to be possibly related to his various ailments, but this cannot be taken to assert that “lead poisoning was the sole cause of death.” The text puts it as “possibly part of the cause.”

10. “临终前举拳的动作”——见于其友人的回忆，属轶事性记载，未作绝对化处理。

10. “The gesture of raising his fist near the end” — found in a friend’s recollection, it is an anecdotal record and is not treated as absolute.
