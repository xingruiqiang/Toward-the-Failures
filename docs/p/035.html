蒲松龄：考了一辈子科举的“落第书生”

Pu Songling: The “Failed Scholar” Who Sat the Examinations All His Life

——他把一生的失意，写成了四百九十多个鬼故事

—He turned a lifetime of disappointment into more than four hundred and ninety ghost stories

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者35 / 100约 2199 字 · 2199 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，“考公上岸”“考研上岸”是无数年轻人的焦虑来源。一次失败就让人觉得前路灰暗。

In 2026, “landing a government post” and “getting into graduate school” are sources of anxiety for countless young people. A single failure can make a person feel that the road ahead is bleak.

而在三百多年前，有一个人，从十九岁一直考到七十一岁，几乎一辈子都在落榜。他叫蒲松龄。他考不上功名，就在路边（按传说）摊开茶水，听人讲奇闻异事，然后把它们写成了《聊斋志异》——一部被翻译成几十种语言、在世界范围内流传的中国文言短篇小说巅峰之作。

And yet more than three hundred years ago there was a man who sat the examinations from the age of nineteen until the age of seventy-one, failing almost his entire life. His name was Pu Songling. Unable to win scholarly honors or office, he set out a table of tea by the roadside (so the legend goes), listened to people tell strange tales and marvels, and then wrote them into Liaozhai Zhiyi (Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio)—a pinnacle of classical Chinese short fiction, translated into dozens of languages and read across the world.

他一生没考上，却因为“没考上”这件事本身，成了一个传奇。

He never once passed the examinations—and yet it was precisely that “never passing” that made him a legend.

一、天才的开局，和被卡住的一生

I. A Brilliant Start, and a Life That Stalled

1640年，蒲松龄出生在山东淄川（今淄博）的一个没落读书人家庭。

In 1640, Pu Songling was born into a declining scholarly family in Zichuan, Shandong (present-day Zibo).

他的开局其实非常好：十九岁那年，他参加县、府、道三级考试，连夺三个第一，主考官是当时的名家施闰章，非常欣赏他的文章。所有人都觉得，这个年轻人前途无量。

His start was in fact remarkably good: at nineteen he sat the three-tier examinations—county, prefecture, and circuit—and took first place in all three. The chief examiner was the celebrated writer Shi Runzhang, who admired his essays greatly. Everyone believed the young man had a boundless future.

然后，命运跟他开了一个长达半个世纪的玩笑。

Then fate played a joke on him that lasted half a century.

从十九岁开始，他一次次去考举人，一次次落榜。他足足考了八次乡试（一说更多），全部落空。直到七十一岁那年，他才“援例”补了一个“岁贡生”——这在科举体系里，基本是一个给年老考生的安慰性功名。

From the age of nineteen, he went again and again to sit the provincial examination for the juren degree, and again and again he failed. He sat the provincial examination no fewer than eight times (some accounts say more), and every attempt came to nothing. Only at seventy-one did he manage, “by precedent,” to be granted the title of “senior tribute student” (suigongsheng)—which in the examination system was essentially a consolation honor for aged candidates.

一个二十岁被所有人看好的天才，考到七十岁，才拿到一个安慰奖。

A genius whom everyone had favored at twenty sat the examinations until he was seventy, and came away with a consolation prize.

二、当塾师，养家，写书

II. Tutoring, Keeping a Family, Writing Books

落榜的人也要活。为了养家，蒲松龄做了四十多年的家庭教师（塾师），长期在别人家里坐馆教书，离妻别子。

A man who failed still had to live. To support his family, Pu Songling spent more than forty years as a household tutor (shushi), teaching for long stretches in other people's homes, away from wife and children.

他家里很穷。他的妻子刘氏贤惠，独自在家上养老人、下带小孩，住在一处荒凉的老屋边上，据说夜里狼会进院子，她就整夜不睡纺线。家里有一点好吃的，都给蒲松龄留着——有时留久了，都坏了。

His family was very poor. His wife, née Liu, was virtuous and capable, looking after the elders and raising the children all by herself, living beside a desolate old house—it is said that wolves would come into the courtyard at night, and she would stay up spinning thread through the dark hours. If there was anything good to eat at home, it was kept for Pu Songling—sometimes kept so long that it went bad.

也正是在这漫长的、清苦的岁月里，他做了一件事：写作。

And it was in those long, frugal, hard-won years that he did one thing: he wrote.

大约从四十岁前后开始，他利用一切空闲，收集、记录、加工各种奇闻异事：狐鬼花妖、爱情与背叛、贪官与冤案、科举下的荒唐与悲凉。他的朋友张笃庆劝过他，说你写这些“谈空说鬼”的东西，会耽误考举人。他不听。

From around the age of forty, he used every spare moment to collect, record, and rework all kinds of strange tales and marvels: fox-spirits and flower-fairies, love and betrayal, corrupt officials and wrongful cases, the absurdity and sorrow beneath the examination system. His friend Zhang Duqing once advised him that writing such “empty talk and ghost stories” would hold him back from passing the provincial examination. He paid no heed.

大约在五十岁前后，《聊斋志异》基本成形，此后继续增补，前后历时四十余年，共收录四百九十余篇。

Around the age of fifty, Liaozhai Zhiyi had essentially taken shape; he continued to add to it afterward, over a period of more than forty years all told, until it held more than four hundred and ninety pieces.

三、关于那个“摆茶摊”的传说

III. On the Legend of the “Tea Stall”

关于《聊斋志异》，流传最广的一个故事是：蒲松龄为了搜集素材，在村外的大路旁摆了一个免费茶摊，谁来讲一个新鲜故事，就请他喝茶；他听完回家，再加工成小说。

The most widely told story about Liaozhai Zhiyi is this: to gather material, Pu Songling set up a free tea stall beside the main road outside the village, and whoever would tell him a fresh story was treated to tea; he would listen, go home, and then rework it into fiction.

这个故事很动人，但它大概率不是真的。

The story is very moving, but it is most likely not true.

这个说法出自清代邹弢的笔记《三借庐笔谈》。鲁迅早就分析过：蒲松龄穷到家里有时连锅都揭不开，常年在外当塾师教书，哪有闲工夫、闲钱去摆茶摊、搭上茶叶和烟草，一坐几十年？

The claim comes from a Qing-dynasty notebook, Sanjie Lu Bitan, by Zou Tao. Lu Xun analyzed it long ago: Pu Songling was so poor that at home there were times he could not even put a pot on the stove, and he spent his years away from home teaching as a tutor—where would he have found the leisure, or the spare money, for a tea stall and all that tea and tobacco, sitting there for decades on end?

《聊斋志异》的真正素材来源，根据研究，大致有三路：一是听别人讲；二是从古代笔记、志怪、传奇中取材——有研究者统计，其中大约有一百篇是改写自古人作品（比如一个几十、上百字的古代小故事，被他铺衍成一篇精彩的小说）；三是他自己凭想象虚构。

According to scholarship, the true sources of Liaozhai Zhiyi fall roughly into three kinds: first, hearing others tell stories; second, drawing on ancient notebooks, tales of the strange, and chuanqi romances—one researcher counted that about a hundred of the pieces were reworked from earlier writers (a small ancient tale of a few dozen or a few hundred characters, for instance, that he expanded into a brilliant story); and third, inventing outright from his own imagination.

那个“路边茶摊”，更像是一个后人为了解释“他怎么写出这么好的故事”而编织的美丽想象。

That “roadside tea stall” is more like a lovely invention woven by later people to explain “how he could write such good stories.”

四、他一生没等到自己的书出版

IV. He Never Lived to See His Book Published

更让人唏嘘的是，蒲松龄生前没能看到《聊斋志异》刊印出版。

What is more poignant still: in his lifetime Pu Songling never saw Liaozhai Zhiyi printed and published.

书写成了，可他穷，没有能力把它送去刻印。他只能眼睁睁看着手稿在朋友之间传抄。他写过一首诗抒发这种心境，大意是：“《志异》书成共笑之，布袍萧索鬓如丝。”

The book was finished, but he was poor and had no way to send it to the carvers. He could only watch as the manuscript circulated in hand-copied form among his friends. He wrote a poem to express the feeling, in substance: “The Tales completed, all men laugh at it; in thin cloth I dwindle, my temples like silk thread.”

《聊斋志异》最早的刻本，是在他去世五十多年之后（清乾隆年间）才出现的。

The earliest printed edition of Liaozhai Zhiyi appeared more than fifty years after his death (in the Qianlong era of the Qing).

1715年，蒲松龄在家中去世，享年七十六岁。他一生没中过举，没做过官，没富过，甚至连自己的书都没印出来。

In 1715, Pu Songling died at home, aged seventy-six. In all his life he never passed the provincial examination, never held office, never grew rich, and never even saw his own book printed.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

蒲松龄的故事，对每一个“考试失败、前途渺茫”的人，都有话要说。

Pu Songling's story has something to say to everyone who has “failed an examination and sees no prospects.”

第一，“落榜”这件事，可以是人生的岔路口，也可以是人生的主干道。他考了一辈子没中，但恰恰是这份“考不上”，让他有了几十年里大量的时间、心境与“看透科举”的愤怒——而这些，全都写进了《聊斋》。那些故事里对考官、对官场、对读书人丑态的讽刺，正是从他自己一次次落榜的切身体会里长出来的。他的失败，成了他最好的素材。

First: “failing” can be a fork in the road of a life, or it can be the main road itself. He sat the examinations his whole life and never passed—and yet it was precisely that failure that gave him, across the decades, the abundant time, the frame of mind, and the anger of a man who had seen through the examination system—and all of it went into Liaozhai. The mockery those stories aim at examiners, at officialdom, at the ugly faces of scholars grew directly out of the felt experience of his own repeated failures. His failure became his best material.

第二，你的价值，不取决于那个决定你“上不上岸”的考场。科举没有给蒲松龄功名，但时间给了他更高的位置：今天，谁记得那一届山东的举人叫什么？可是所有人都记得《聊斋志异》。

Second: your worth does not depend on that examination hall which decides whether you “make it ashore.” The examinations gave Pu Songling no honors, but time gave him a far higher place: today, who remembers the name of any of that year's Shandong juren? Yet everyone remembers Liaozhai Zhiyi.

他一生都在被“规则”淘汰，最终却用作品，淘汰了那个规则。

All his life he was eliminated by “the rules”—and in the end, through his work, it was he who eliminated the rules.

Appendix: Sources and Notes

主要史料

Primary Sources

1. 蒲松龄《聊斋志异》及其《聊斋自志》。

1. Pu Songling's Liaozhai Zhiyi and its preface, the “Liaozhai Zizhi.”

2. 蒲松龄诗文（含“《志异》书成共笑之”等自述性诗作）。

2. Pu Songling's poems and prose (including self-referential verses such as “‘The Tales completed, all men laugh at it’”).

3. 张笃庆等友人劝其专注科举的诗文记录。

3. Records of poems and prose in which friends such as Zhang Duqing urged him to concentrate on the examinations.

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

4. 生于1640年6月5日（山东淄川），卒于1715年2月25日，享年76岁。

4. Born June 5, 1640 (Zichuan, Shandong); died February 25, 1715, aged 76.

5. 19岁（约1658年）县、府、道三试第一；此后屡试乡试不中；1710年（71岁）援例补贡生。

5. Took first place in the county, prefecture, and circuit examinations at 19 (about 1658); thereafter repeatedly failed the provincial examination; granted the tribute-student rank by precedent in 1710 (aged 71).

6. 《聊斋志异》约历时四十余年写成，收文四百九十余篇；最早刻本约在乾隆年间（他去世五十年后）出现。

6. Liaozhai Zhiyi was written over roughly more than forty years and contains over four hundred and ninety pieces; the earliest printed edition appeared about the Qianlong era (fifty years after his death).

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “蒲松龄摆茶摊、请人讲故事”——此为传说，很可能不实。其出处为清代邹弢《三借庐笔谈》，鲁迅在《小说旧闻钞》中已指出其“最为无稽”。文中明确标注为传说，并给出实际素材来源（口传、改写前人作品、虚构）。

7. “Pu Songling set up a tea stall and asked people to tell him stories”—this is legend and very likely untrue. Its source is the Qing writer Zou Tao's Sanjie Lu Bitan; Lu Xun, in Xiaoshuo Jiuwen Chao, already called it “the most baseless of all.” The text clearly labels it as legend and gives the actual sources of material (oral transmission, reworkings of earlier writers, invention).

8. “乡试连考八次全部落榜”——不同材料对其次数与年限的记载略有差异（“八次”“十次”等说法并存）。文中采用“八次（一说更多）”的谨慎表述。

8. “He sat the provincial examination eight times and failed every time”—different materials record his number of attempts and years of sitting somewhat differently (accounts of “eight times” and “ten times” coexist). The text adopts the cautious phrasing “eight times (some say more).”

9. “71岁才补贡生”——此为援例补授的“岁贡生”（相当于晚年的安慰性功名），与举人、进士不同；文中已说明其性质。

9. “He was only made a tribute student at 71”—this is the “senior tribute student” (suigongsheng) granted by precedent (in effect a consolation honor for old age), distinct from the juren and jinshi degrees; the text explains its nature.

10. “《聊斋志异》成书在五十岁前后”——成书与增补的具体时间线在不同研究中存在差异，文中用“大约……基本成形”“此后继续增补”的方式表述，避免绝对化。

10. “Liaozhai Zhiyi was completed around the age of fifty”—the precise timeline of its completion and later additions differs among studies; the text phrases it as “had essentially taken shape around…” and “continued to add to it afterward,” avoiding absolutes.
