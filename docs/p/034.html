苏轼：越贬越远的“东坡居士”

Su Shi: The “Dongpo Recluse,” Exiled Ever Farther Away

——他一生被贬了三次，一次比一次远，却一次比一次通透

—Exiled three times in his life, each time farther from the capital, yet each time seeing more clearly

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者34 / 100约 2458 字 · 2458 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，“被优化”“被调岗”“被发配到边缘部门”，是很多人最怕听到的事。仿佛只要离开了中心，人生就走下坡了。

In 2026, “being made redundant,” “being reshuffled to another post,” “being banished to some backwater department”—these are among the things people most dread to hear. As if the moment you leave the center, your life is on the way downhill.

有一个人，专门用他的一生反驳这句话。他叫苏轼。他在朝廷里三次被贬，一次比一次远：先是黄州，再是惠州（广东），最后干脆被贬到了海南岛上的儋州——在那时候，那几乎等于“发配到天涯海角”。

There is one man who devoted his entire life to refuting that sentence. His name was Su Shi. Three times he was exiled from the court, each time farther than the last: first Huangzhou, then Huizhou (in Guangdong), and finally all the way to Danzhou on the island of Hainan—which in those days was almost the same as “banishment to the ends of the earth.”

可他越贬越活，越远越通透。他在黄州写出了《念奴娇·赤壁怀古》和前后《赤壁赋》，在惠州写出“日啖荔枝三百颗”，在儋州开办学堂。他一生大部分好作品，都是在他“失意”的那些年写出来的。

Yet the more he was exiled, the more alive he became; the farther he was sent, the clearer he saw. At Huangzhou he wrote “Nian Nu Jiao: Memories of the Past at Red Cliff” and both the first and second “Rhapsodies on the Red Cliff”; at Huizhou he wrote “Three hundred lychees a day”; at Danzhou he founded a school. Most of the good work of his life was written during those years of his “disappointment.”

他不是被贬倒的，他是被贬出来的。

He was not broken by exile—he was made by it.

一、21岁就红遍京城的天才

I. A Genius Who Won the Capital at Twenty-One

1037年，苏轼出生在四川眉山。父亲苏洵、弟弟苏辙，都是后来的大文人。他母亲程氏亲自教他读书。

In 1037, Su Shi was born in Meishan, Sichuan. His father, Su Xun, and his younger brother, Su Zhe, were both great men of letters in later years. His mother, née Cheng, taught him to read with her own hands.

1057年，二十一岁的苏轼和弟弟一同进京参加科举。主考官是文坛领袖欧阳修。欧阳修读到一份考卷，拍案叫绝，本以为是自己的学生曾巩写的（为避嫌疑只给了第二名）。放榜后才知是苏轼——他当场说了一句大意是：“老夫我应该让路，让这个人出头”的话。

In 1057, at the age of twenty-one, Su Shi went to the capital with his brother to sit the imperial examinations. The chief examiner was the leader of the literary world, Ouyang Xiu. Reading one of the papers, Ouyang Xiu struck the table in admiration, and took it to be the work of his own student Zeng Gong (and to avoid suspicion gave it only second place). Only when the results were posted did he learn that it was Su Shi’s—and he said on the spot something to the effect that “this old man ought to step aside and let this man have his head above the rest.”

一夜之间，苏轼名满京城。

Overnight, Su Shi’s name filled the capital.

二、乌台诗案：从云端跌入大牢

II. The Crow Terrace Poetry Case: From the Clouds into Prison

如果人生就这样顺下去，苏轼可能会是个太平的官员、一个风雅的文人。

If his life had simply continued smoothly, Su Shi might have been a placid official and an elegant man of letters.

但1079年，出事了。

But in 1079, something happened.

这一年，他被调任湖州，照例给皇帝上了一封谢表。表中一些话，被新党的人挑出来，指为“讥讽朝政”“对皇帝不敬”。他被从湖州抓走，押回京城，关进御史台的监狱——因为御史台树上常落乌鸦，人称“乌台”，这件事就叫“乌台诗案”。

That year he was transferred to Huzhou, and as was customary he submitted a letter of thanks to the emperor. Certain phrases in it were picked out by members of the New Faction and denounced as “slandering the government” and “showing disrespect to the emperor.” He was seized from Huzhou, hauled back to the capital, and thrown into the prison of the Censorate—and because crows often settled in the trees of the Censorate, it was called the “Crow Terrace.” This affair came to be known as the “Crow Terrace Poetry Case.”

他在狱中被关了四个多月，一度以为自己会被处死，甚至写好了给弟弟的绝命诗。最后，在多方营救下（包括政敌王安石也出面说了话），他被免死，贬为黄州团练副使——一个没有实权、还被监视的闲职。

He was held in prison for more than four months, at one point believing he would be put to death, and he even wrote a farewell poem for his brother. In the end, through the rescue efforts of many (including his political enemy Wang Anshi, who also spoke up), he escaped the death penalty and was demoted to Deputy Military Commissioner of Huangzhou—an idle post with no real authority, and with a watch kept on him.

那一年，他四十二岁。

That year, he was forty-two.

三、黄州：把“闲”活成了“传奇”

III. Huangzhou: Turning “Idleness” into a “Legend”

黄州，是苏轼人生的转折点。

Huangzhou was the turning point of Su Shi’s life.

他在城东一块坡地上开荒种田，给自己取了个号——“东坡居士”。从此，“苏东坡”这个名字，比他本名还响。

On a slope of wasteland east of the city he broke ground and planted crops, and gave himself a sobriquet—“Dongpo Recluse.” From then on, the name “Su Dongpo” rang louder than his original name.

在黄州的几年，是他创作的高峰。他写出了：

His years at Huangzhou were the peak of his creativity. He wrote:

- 《念奴娇·赤壁怀古》——“大江东去，浪淘尽，千古风流人物”；

- “Nian Nu Jiao: Memories of the Past at Red Cliff”—“The great river flows east; its waves have washed away, through all the ages, the gallant men of old”;

- 《前赤壁赋》《后赤壁赋》——把人生的无常，写成了一种旷达；

- “First Rhapsody on the Red Cliff” and “Second Rhapsody on the Red Cliff”—turning life’s impermanence into a kind of spacious ease;

- 《定风波》——“一蓑烟雨任平生”；

- “Ding Feng Bo”—“In a straw cloak, through mist and rain, I live my life as it comes”;

- 还有《寒食帖》，后来被称为“天下第三行书”。

- and the “Cold Food Observance” (Hanshi Tie), later called “the third finest running-script calligraphy under heaven.”

他在城外东坡上种地，自己动手盖房（取名“雪堂”），研究怎么把便宜的五花肉炖得好吃——后来人们管那道菜叫“东坡肉”。

He farmed the east slope outside the city, built a house with his own hands (naming it the “Snow Hall”), and studied how to stew cheap pork belly until it tasted good—later people called that dish “Dongpo pork.”

在最失意的地方，他活出了最从容的样子。

In the place of his greatest disappointment, he lived out his most unhurried self.

四、再贬惠州，三贬儋州

IV. Exiled Again to Huizhou, Then to Danzhou

1085年，旧党重新掌权，苏轼被召回京城，一路升到很高的位置。但他不站队——他既批评新党的激进，也批评旧党一味守旧，两头都得罪。

In 1085, the Old Faction returned to power, and Su Shi was recalled to the capital, rising all the way to very high office. But he would not take sides—he criticized the radicalism of the New Faction and also the Old Faction’s blind clinging to the past, offending both.

1094年，他再次被贬，这一次是惠州（今广东）。年近六十，被发配到当时还很荒僻的岭南。他倒好，写下“日啖荔枝三百颗，不辞长作岭南人”——吃得开心，住得自在。

In 1094, he was exiled once more, this time to Huizhou (present-day Guangdong). Nearly sixty, he was banished to what was then still the remote, wild region of Lingnan. He took it well enough, and wrote, “Three hundred lychees a day—I would not mind staying forever in Lingnan”—eating happily and living at ease.

1097年，政敌觉得他过得太舒服了，又把他往更远的地方贬——儋州，海南岛。在宋代，放逐海南是仅次于满门抄斩的重罚。

In 1097, his political enemies decided he was living too comfortably, and exiled him to a still more distant place—Danzhou, on the island of Hainan. In the Song dynasty, banishment to Hainan was a punishment second only to the extermination of one’s entire family.

可苏轼在儋州做了什么？他办学堂、教书、带着当地人挖井、劝农。他在这里教出了海南历史上第一批举人。他临走时写：“九死南荒吾不恨，兹游奇绝冠平生。”——我在这荒蛮之地九死一生，我不后悔，这一趟游历，是我一生中最奇绝的经历。

But what did Su Shi do at Danzhou? He founded a school, taught students, led the local people in digging wells, and urged them on in farming. Here he trained the first juren degree-holders in Hainan’s history. On the eve of his departure he wrote: “Nine times over I faced death in the southern wilds, and I do not regret it; this journey was the strangest and grandest of my life.”—In this savage land I came within a hair of death again and again, and I have no regrets; this whole journey was the most extraordinary experience of my life.

五、最后：他没能回家

V. At the End: He Never Made It Home

1100年，宋徽宗即位大赦天下，苏轼获准北归。他一路往北走，1101年走到常州，病倒了，不久去世，享年六十四岁。

In 1100, Emperor Huizong took the throne and proclaimed a general amnesty, and Su Shi was permitted to return north. He traveled north the whole way, and in 1101 reached Changzhou, where he fell ill and soon died, at the age of sixty-four.

他终究没能回到他心心念念的故乡眉山，也没能再见一眼京城。可他留下的，是两千多首诗、三百多首词、一大批散文和书画。

In the end he never made it back to his beloved hometown of Meishan, nor did he ever see the capital again. But what he left behind was more than two thousand poems, over three hundred ci lyrics, and a great body of prose, calligraphy, and painting.

他后来成了中国文化里一个非常特殊的符号：人们爱他的才华，更爱他的态度——那种无论被扔到哪里，都能把日子过出滋味、把苦难过成风景的能力。

He later became a very special symbol in Chinese culture: people love his talent, and love even more his attitude—that capacity, no matter where he was thrown, to draw flavor out of his days and turn hardship into scenery.

六、致2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

苏轼的故事，是给所有“被边缘化的人”的一封信。

Su Shi’s story is a letter to everyone who has been “pushed to the margins.”

他的一生，几乎每一次重大转折都是“往下走”：被贬、再贬、再再贬。可他每次都能在更低的地方，找到更高的自己。

In his life, almost every major turning point was a “step downward”: exiled, exiled again, exiled yet again. Yet each time, in a lower place, he found a higher self.

对2026年的我们，有两句话。

For us in 2026, there are two things to say.

第一，“被贬”的那几年，可能才是你真正开始创作的那几年。注意一件事：苏轼最好的作品，不是在京城做高官时写的，是在黄州、惠州、儋州这些“流放地”写的。失去位置，反而给了他时间和心境。很多时候，被拿走的不是机会，是枷锁。

First, those years of “exile” may be exactly the years when you truly begin to create. Note one thing: Su Shi’s best works were not written while he held high office in the capital, but at Huangzhou, Huizhou, and Danzhou—those “places of banishment.” Losing his position gave him, instead, the time and the frame of mind. Often what is taken from you is not an opportunity, but a set of shackles.

第二，你无法决定境遇，但你能决定用什么姿态去接。他在海南岛不是熬日子，是办学堂；在惠州不是怨天尤人，是吃荔枝。环境可以限住你的位置，但它限不住你的选择。

Second, you cannot decide your circumstances, but you can decide the posture with which you meet them. On Hainan he did not simply endure his days—he founded a school; at Huizhou he did not curse heaven and blame others—he ate lychees. Circumstances can confine your position, but they cannot confine your choices.

“一蓑烟雨任平生”——这不是嘴硬，这是一种被生活反复验证过之后，长出来的底气。

“In a straw cloak, through mist and rain, I live my life as it comes”—this is not mere bravado; it is a kind of confidence grown only after being tested by life again and again.

Appendix: Sources and Notes

主要史料

Primary Sources

2. 《宋史·苏轼传》。

2. “Biography of Su Shi” in the Songshi (History of Song).

3. 乌台诗案相关史料（乌台诗案档案、宋人笔记），以及苏轼黄州、惠州、儋州时期作品。

3. Sources relating to the Crow Terrace Poetry Case (archives of the case, notes by Song writers), together with Su Shi’s works from his Huangzhou, Huizhou, and Danzhou periods.

生卒与事实

Dates and Facts

4. 生于1037年1月8日（眉州眉山），卒于1101年8月24日（常州），享年64岁。

4. Born January 8, 1037 (Meishan, Meizhou); died August 24, 1101 (Changzhou), aged 64.

5. 1057年进士及第；1079年乌台诗案，贬黄州；1085年回朝；1094年贬惠州；1097年贬儋州；1100年遇赦北归。

5. Passed the jinshi examination in 1057; the Crow Terrace Poetry Case in 1079, exiled to Huangzhou; recalled to court in 1085; exiled to Huizhou in 1094; exiled to Danzhou in 1097; pardoned and allowed to return north in 1100.

6. “东坡居士”之号，源于黄州城东的耕地名“东坡”。

6. The sobriquet “Dongpo Recluse” derives from “Dongpo” (East Slope), the name of the farmland east of Huangzhou city.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “欧阳修说'我当让他出人头地'”——欧阳修的推崇见诸记载（他曾高度赞赏苏轼文章），但具体措辞（“老夫当避路，放他出一头地也”）见于宋人笔记，属流传引语，未必是其原话，文中以“大意是”处理。

7. “Ouyang Xiu said, ‘I should let him rise above the crowd’”—Ouyang Xiu’s admiration is on record (he praised Su Shi’s writings highly), but the exact wording (“This old man should step aside and let him have a head above the rest”) appears in notes by Song writers and is a widely circulated quotation that may not be his actual words; the text handles it with “to the effect that.”

8. “东坡肉是苏轼亲手创制”——民间普遍将这道菜归于苏轼（与其《猪肉颂》等文字相关），但菜名与做法在后世流传中不断演变，“亲自发明”的说法难以确证，文中按其研究烹饪、与民间传说相关联的方式表述。

8. “Dongpo pork was created by Su Shi himself”—folk tradition widely attributes this dish to Su Shi (linked to writings such as his “Ode to Pork”), but the name and preparation of the dish evolved over the generations, and the claim that he “invented it himself” cannot be confirmed; the text presents it in terms of his studying cookery and being associated with the popular legend.

9. “在儋州教出海南第一批举人”——苏轼在儋州办学授徒、对当地文教影响甚大，为其生平事迹；但“第一批举人”的具体归属与数量，史料记载不完整，文中采用通行说法并保持概括。

9. “He taught the first juren degree-holders of Hainan while at Danzhou”—Su Shi founded a school and taught students at Danzhou, and his influence on local education and culture was great, which is a matter of his biography; but the specific attribution and number of the “first juren” are incompletely recorded in the sources, and the text adopts the common account while keeping it general.

10. “乌台诗案险些处死他”——他确有被处死的风险，监狱中曾写绝命诗；但最终因多方营救（含王安石、太后等）而免除死罪。文中已如实呈现“险死—获救—贬黄州”的过程。

10. “The Crow Terrace Poetry Case nearly cost him his life”—he did indeed face the risk of execution, and wrote a death poem in prison; but in the end, through the efforts of many to save him (including Wang Anshi and the empress dowager), the death sentence was averted. The text faithfully presents the sequence of “near death—rescue—exile to Huangzhou.”
