稻盛和夫：进了一家将倒闭的公司，连跳槽都没跳成

Kazuo Inamori: He Joined a Company That Was Going Under—and Couldn't Even Manage to Quit

——他年轻时最想离开的地方，最后成了他一生事业的起点

— The place he most wanted to leave as a young man became, in the end, the starting point of his life's work

← 返回主页 Home第三辑　在废墟上重建的人47 / 100约 2633 字 · 2633 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，“入职了一家快要倒闭的公司”是很多毕业生最怕的开局。公司欠薪、老员工跑光、同期进来的人一个个辞职——这种局面，足以让人立刻开始改简历。

In 2026, "I joined a company that was about to go under" is the opening many graduates fear most. Unpaid wages, veteran employees gone, one new hire after another quitting—a situation like that is enough to send anyone straight to revising their résumé.

而日本有一位被称为“经营之圣”的人，年轻时就遇到了一模一样的处境。他进的那家公司，正处在破产边缘，工资发不出，同事纷纷离开。他也想走，甚至一度快走成了——结果因为一个很偶然的原因，没走成。

Yet in Japan there was a man known as the "sage of management" who, in his youth, faced exactly this same predicament. The company he joined was on the brink of bankruptcy, wages could not be paid, and his colleagues left one after another. He wanted to leave too, and at one point nearly did—but for one quite accidental reason, he did not.

那个“没走成”，成了他一生的转折点。他叫稻盛和夫。他从那家快倒的公司出发，后来创办了两家世界500强企业，又在78岁高龄，把破产的日本航空重新救活。

That "didn't leave" became the turning point of his life. His name was Kazuo Inamori. Starting out from that nearly failed company, he went on to found two Fortune Global 500 businesses, and at the age of 78 he revived a bankrupt Japan Airlines.

一、一个“不够聪明”的年轻人

I. A Young Man Who "Wasn't Clever Enough"

1932年，稻盛和夫出生在日本鹿儿岛。他小时候成绩并不突出，升学考试也不顺——初中升高中落榜过，大学也不是什么名校（鹿儿岛大学工学部）。

In 1932, Kazuo Inamori was born in Kagoshima, Japan. As a child he was not an outstanding student, and his entrance exams did not go smoothly—he failed the exam to move up from middle school to high school, and the university he attended was no prestigious place (the Faculty of Engineering at Kagoshima University).

毕业后，正赶上日本经济不景气，找工作非常难。他报考了多家公司，屡屡碰壁。最后，靠老师介绍，他才进了京都的一家做绝缘子的公司——松风工业。

After graduating, he entered the workforce just as Japan's economy was in a slump, and finding a job was extremely hard. He applied to company after company and was turned down again and again. In the end, through an introduction from a teacher, he got into a Kyoto firm that made insulators—Shofu Industries.

二、一家发不出工资的公司

II. A Company That Couldn't Pay Its Wages

松风工业当时的状况，可以用“在倒闭的边缘”来形容：

Shofu Industries at the time could be described as "on the verge of collapse":

- 公司连年亏损，经营困难；

- The company had run at a loss for years and was struggling to stay afloat;

- 工资经常不能按时发放，员工的士气极低；

- Wages were often not paid on time, and employee morale was extremely low;

- 宿舍破旧不堪，条件很差。

- The dormitory was dilapidated and conditions were very poor.

和稻盛同期进入公司的年轻人，一个接一个辞职走了。他也待不下去了。他和另一位同事一起报考了别的机会——据他本人的叙述，他们当时想考的是自卫队的干部候补生学校，并且通过了考试。只要家里把户口簿寄过来，他就能离开松风工业，去当一名军官。

The young men who joined the company at the same time as Inamori quit one after another. He, too, could no longer stand it. Together with a colleague he applied for another opportunity—by his own account, they were trying for the Self-Defense Forces' officer candidate school, and they passed the exam. All he needed was for his family to send him his household register, and he could leave Shofu Industries and become a military officer.

结果，户口簿一直没来。后来他才知道，是他的哥哥认为他“好不容易才进了公司，不该这么快又走”，故意没有把户口本寄给他。

In the end, the household register never came. Later he learned that his older brother, thinking he had "finally gotten into a company and shouldn't leave again so soon," had deliberately not sent it.

于是，他走不成了。

And so, he could not leave.

（关于这段经历，不同资料在“他想跳槽去哪儿”“为什么没走成”的细节上略有差异，但“想走、没走成、最后留下”这一段，是他本人反复讲述过的关键转折。）

(Sources differ slightly on the details here—where he wanted to jump ship to, and why he failed to leave—but the arc of "wanting to go, failing to go, and finally staying" was a key turning point he himself recounted many times.)

三、想通了：既然走不了，那就干出个样子

III. Making Up His Mind: If He Couldn't Leave, He Would Prove Himself

既然走不了，稻盛和夫做了一个决定：不再抱怨，把所有精力都投进研究。

Since he couldn't leave, Kazuo Inamori made a decision: he would stop complaining and pour all his energy into research.

当时公司有一个技术难题——一种叫“镁橄榄石”的新型陶瓷绝缘材料，很难做出来。这个方向当时几乎没人攻克，公司里也没人愿意碰。稻盛和夫主动接了下来。

At the time the company had a technical problem on its hands—a new type of ceramic insulating material called "forsterite," which was very hard to produce. Almost no one had cracked it, and no one in the company wanted to touch it. Inamori volunteered to take it on.

他干脆把锅碗瓢盆和被褥都搬进了实验室，住在里面做试验。他日夜钻研，最终攻克了这个材料的合成技术。

He simply moved his pots and pans and his bedding into the laboratory and lived there while running his experiments. Working day and night, he eventually mastered the synthesis of this material.

这一项技术突破，让快要倒闭的松风工业起死回生，也让他自己在业内有了名声。

That single technical breakthrough brought the nearly bankrupt Shofu Industries back from the dead, and earned him a name in the industry as well.

他人生真正的起点，不是那家好公司，而是那家“烂公司”里他主动接下的那个难题。

The true starting point of his life was not some good company, but the problem he volunteered to take on inside that "bad company."

四、两家世界500强

IV. Two Fortune Global 500 Companies

1959年，27岁的稻盛和夫和几位同事一起，创办了京都陶瓷（京瓷，Kyocera）。

In 1959, at the age of 27, Kazuo Inamori and several colleagues founded Kyoto Ceramics (Kyocera).

京瓷从一个小小的陶瓷作坊起步，靠着他那套独特的经营理念——“阿米巴经营”（把公司拆成一个个独立核算的小组，让每个员工都像经营者一样思考）和“敬天爱人”的哲学，逐渐成长为世界级的精密陶瓷与电子元件企业，进入世界500强。

Kyocera started as a tiny ceramics workshop and, on the strength of his distinctive set of management ideas—"amoeba management" (splitting the company into small, independently accounted units so that every employee thinks like a manager) and the philosophy of "respect heaven, love people"—gradually grew into a world-class maker of precision ceramics and electronic components, and entered the Fortune Global 500.

1984年，日本电信市场开放，他又创办了第二电电（DDI，后来合并为KDDI），进入通信行业，再次做成一家世界500强企业。一个人，创办了两家世界500强——这在全世界都极为罕见。

In 1984, when Japan's telecommunications market opened up, he founded Daini Denden (DDI, later merged into KDDI), entered the communications industry, and built yet another Fortune Global 500 company. One man, two Fortune Global 500 companies—extraordinarily rare anywhere in the world.

五、78岁，去救一家已经破产的公司

V. At 78, Going to Rescue an Already Bankrupt Company

2010年，日本航空（JAL）破产重整。日本政府请他出山，这一年他78岁。

In 2010, Japan Airlines (JAL) went bankrupt and entered restructuring. The Japanese government asked him to step in. That year he was 78.

他答应了，而且提出两个条件：不要报酬；每周只去公司几天。

He agreed—on two conditions: no pay, and only a few days a week at the company.

他用自己那一套经营哲学，重整日航：砍掉亏损航线、重塑成本意识、把“阿米巴”式的经营意识植入组织。不到三年，日航就从破产重整走向重新上市，成为企业经营史上的经典案例。

Using his own management philosophy, he restructured JAL: cutting unprofitable routes, rebuilding a sense of cost discipline, and instilling "amoeba"-style management thinking throughout the organization. In less than three years, JAL went from bankruptcy restructuring to re-listing—a classic case in the history of corporate management.

2022年8月24日，稻盛和夫在家中去世，享年90岁。

On August 24, 2022, Kazuo Inamori died at home at the age of 90.

六、致2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

稻盛和夫的故事，对“我进了一家烂公司”的焦虑，是一个非常有分量的回答。

Kazuo Inamori's story is a weighty answer to the anxiety of "I joined a bad company."

第一，你改变不了环境，但你可以选一个“难题”接住它。他留在松风工业之后做的事，不是“忍气吞声”，而是主动去啃别人不愿啃的技术难关。正是那个难关，让他从一个“找不到工作的毕业生”，变成了业内认可的技术人才。在烂牌里，你要找的是那张能练出你能力的牌。

First, you can't change the environment—but you can choose a "hard problem" and take it on. What he did after staying at Shofu Industries was not to swallow his pride and endure; he volunteered to gnaw at a technical difficulty others would not touch. That difficulty was what turned him from "a graduate who couldn't find a job" into a technically recognized talent. When you're dealt a bad hand, what you're looking for is the card that will sharpen your abilities.

第二，“走不了”有时是好事。他当年如果成功跳槽去当了军官，很可能只是换了另一种平庸的人生。是那次“没走成”，让他被迫留在一个地方，把一件事做透。人生的转折，常常伪装成一次“没得选”。

Second, sometimes "being unable to leave" is a good thing. Had he succeeded back then in jumping ship to become a military officer, he would most likely have traded one mediocre life for another. It was that "failure to leave" that forced him to stay in one place and master one thing thoroughly. Life's turning points often disguise themselves as "I had no choice."

第三，理念是可以变成生产力的。“阿米巴经营”“敬天爱人”这些东西，听起来像口号，但他把它们变成了具体的核算制度和管理方法，让每个员工都知道“我的工作对公司的意义”。空谈价值观没用，把价值观落成制度才有用。

Third, ideas can become productive forces. Things like "amoeba management" and "respect heaven, love people" sound like slogans, but he turned them into concrete accounting systems and management methods, so that every employee knew "what my work means to the company." Empty talk about values is useless; only building values into institutions is useful.

他一生最想逃离的那间破厂房，最后成了他一辈子事业的起点。

The shabby factory building he most wanted to escape his whole life became, in the end, the starting point of his life's work.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

6. “他想跳槽、因户口簿没寄到而未成”——这一段是他本人多次讲述的经历，但细节（报考的目标是“自卫队干部候补生学校”、阻止他的是哥哥等）在不同材料与讲述版本中存在差异，且带有较重的个人叙事色彩。文中已标注“据他本人的叙述”，并以“不同资料在细节上略有差异”作保留。

6. "He wanted to change jobs but didn't, because the household register never arrived." This episode was recounted by Inamori himself on many occasions, but the details—that the school he applied to was the Self-Defense Forces' officer candidate school, that it was his brother who stopped him, and so on—vary across different materials and versions of the telling, and carry a heavy personal-narrative coloring. The text marks it as "by his own account" and hedges with "sources differ slightly on the details."

7. “他入职的公司濒临倒闭”——松风工业当时经营困难、财务状况恶劣，是他本人与众多传记的一致描述；但“濒临倒闭”的程度与时间跨度，各资料表述不一，文中按“在倒闭边缘”处理。

7. "The company he joined was on the brink of bankruptcy." That Shofu Industries was then in financial difficulty with poor business conditions is the consistent description of both Inamori himself and numerous biographers; but how close to collapse it was, and over what span of time, is described differently from source to source. The text treats it as "on the verge of collapse."

8. “镁橄榄石陶瓷是他独自攻克”——该技术突破是京瓷创立的技术基础，也是稻盛和夫本人生涯的关键节点；不过，“独自攻克”更应理解为“他主导并深度参与”（团队与公司资源亦在其中），文中已表述为“他主动接下并住在实验室攻克”。

8. "He cracked the forsterite ceramic alone." This technical breakthrough was the technological foundation on which Kyocera was built, and a key node in Inamori's own career; however, "cracked alone" is better understood as "he led it and was deeply involved in it" (the team and the company's resources were part of it too). The text renders it as "he volunteered to take it on and moved into the laboratory to crack it."

9. “他创办了两家世界500强”——京瓷与KDDI（由第二电电发展而来）确为世界500强企业，稻盛和夫为两家的创办者，此说通行。但需注意KDDI是历经合并重组而成的企业，“他创办的DDI最终成为KDDI的一部分”，不宜简单表述为“他一个人创办了KDDI”。文中已作“创办了第二电电（DDI，后来合并为KDDI）”的处理。

9. "He founded two Fortune Global 500 companies." Kyocera and KDDI (which grew out of Daini Denden) are indeed Fortune Global 500 companies, and Inamori founded both—this is the standard account. But note that KDDI is an enterprise formed through successive mergers and reorganizations; "the DDI he founded ultimately became part of KDDI" should not be simplified into "he alone founded KDDI." The text handles this as "founded Daini Denden (DDI, later merged into KDDI)."

10. “78岁零报酬重整日航”——他出任日航会长并不领取报酬，是他公开承诺的，属实；“每周只去几天”亦为其实际工作方式。文中据实呈现。

10. "At 78, he restructured JAL without pay." That he took the chairmanship of JAL without drawing a salary was his own public pledge and is true; "going in only a few days a week" was likewise his actual working style. The text presents it as it was.
