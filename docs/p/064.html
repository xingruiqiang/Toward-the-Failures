Chapter 064 · Part 4

茨威格：一个亲眼看着自己世界毁灭的人

Zweig: A Man Who Watched His Own World Destroyed Before His Eyes

——“愿他们在漫漫长夜之后，还能看到旭日东升”

— "May they, after the long night, still see the sun rise."

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂64 / 100约 2973 字 · 2973 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，我们这一代人第一次真切地感受到“世界在剧烈变化”：旧的秩序在松动，熟悉的生活方式在改变，很多人心里有一种说不上来的不安。

In 2026, our generation is feeling for the first time, in a real way, that "the world is changing violently": the old order is loosening, familiar ways of life are shifting, and many people carry an unease they cannot quite put into words.

但有一个人，经历的是“整个世界被连根拔起”。他曾经是欧洲最有名的作家之一，作品被译成几十种语言；他生活过的维也纳、萨尔茨堡、巴黎，是人类文明的骄傲。然后，一切在他眼前崩落——两次世界大战、纳粹上台、犹太人的灾难、他被迫离开祖国，最后客死异乡。

But there was one man who lived through the uprooting of an entire world. He had once been one of the most famous writers in Europe, his works translated into dozens of languages; the Vienna, Salzburg, and Paris he had lived in were the pride of human civilization. Then everything collapsed before his eyes—two world wars, the rise of the Nazis, the catastrophe of the Jews, his forced departure from his homeland, and at last a death in a foreign land.

1942年2月22日，他和妻子在巴西的一座小城里，服毒自尽。

On February 22, 1942, in a small town in Brazil, he and his wife took poison and died.

他叫斯蒂芬·茨威格。他留下的遗书里，有一句话成了那个年代最沉重的叹息之一。

His name was Stefan Zweig. In the farewell letter he left behind, one sentence became one of the heaviest sighs of that era.

一、“世界公民”的黄金年代

I. The Golden Age of the "World Citizen"

1881年，茨威格出生在维也纳一个富裕的犹太家庭。

In 1881, Zweig was born into a wealthy Jewish family in Vienna.

那是一个让欧洲人至今怀念的时代。维也纳是音乐之都、艺术之都、思想之都，茨威格在这里读书、写诗、结识名人、周游欧洲。他后来写过一本书，标题就叫《昨日的世界》——他把它写成了对那个已经消失的欧洲的挽歌。

It was an age that Europeans still look back on with longing. Vienna was the capital of music, of art, of thought, and there Zweig studied, wrote poetry, met famous people, and traveled across Europe. He later wrote a book titled The World of Yesterday (Die Welt von Gestern)—he made it an elegy for that vanished Europe.

他出身富家，没有生计压力，可以专心做两件事：收藏与理解人的内心。 他写传记，写《三大师》（巴尔扎克、狄更斯、陀思妥耶夫斯基），写《与魔鬼作斗争》（荷尔德林、克莱斯特、尼采），写《人类群星闪耀时》——那本用一个个历史瞬间照亮人性的名作。

Born into a wealthy family, free from the pressure of making a living, he could devote himself to two things: collecting, and understanding the human heart. He wrote biographies—Three Masters (Drei Meister: Balzac, Dickens, Dostoevsky), The Struggle with the Daemon (Der Kampf mit dem Dämon: Hölderlin, Kleist, Nietzsche)—and Decisive Moments in History (Sternstunden der Menschheit), that celebrated work that illuminates human nature through one historical moment after another.

他也写小说：写一个女人一生的隐秘痴恋（《一个陌生女人的来信》），写一个赌徒的内心的煎熬，写一封长信里烧尽了一个人的灵魂。他擅长的是极其细腻的心理描写，他能钻进人最隐秘的角落，把那些说不出的话写出来。

He wrote fiction too: the secret, lifelong obsession of a woman (Letter from an Unknown Woman); the inner torment of a gambler; a long letter in which a person's soul burns itself out. His gift was an extraordinarily delicate psychological portraiture—he could slip into a person's most hidden corners and put into words the things that could not be said.

那时他大概相信，文明会一直向前，欧洲会一直是世界的心脏。

At that time he probably believed that civilization would keep advancing, that Europe would always be the heart of the world.

二、世界开始崩塌

II. The World Begins to Collapse

第一次世界大战打碎了他的信念。他在战争期间就写过反战作品，呼吁欧洲人彼此理解。战后他定居萨尔茨堡，继续写作，成为当时世界上被翻译最多的作家之一。

The First World War shattered his faith. During the war he had already written anti-war works, calling on Europeans to understand one another. Afterward he settled in Salzburg, kept writing, and became one of the most translated writers in the world at the time.

然后，1933年，希特勒上台。

Then, in 1933, Hitler came to power.

纳粹开始大规模焚烧书籍——茨威格的书也在被烧之列。 作为犹太人，他的作品被禁、被烧，他的生存空间被一寸寸压缩。

The Nazis began burning books on a massive scale—Zweig's own among them. As a Jew, his works were banned and burned, and the space left for him to live shrank inch by inch.

1934年，他离开奥地利，去了英国。1938年，奥地利被纳粹吞并。1940年，他转到美国。1941年，他去了巴西。

In 1934 he left Austria and went to England. In 1938 Austria was annexed by the Nazis. In 1940 he moved on to the United States. In 1941 he went to Brazil.

他成了一个没有国家的人。

He had become a man without a country.

一个把“欧洲精神”当作毕生信仰的人，眼睁睁看着这份精神被暴力、仇恨和野蛮撕碎。最让他痛苦的，不是他个人的流亡，而是文明的自我毁灭——那个他深爱并引以为傲的欧洲，正在亲手烧掉自己。

A man who had made the "spirit of Europe" his lifelong faith watched, with his own eyes, that spirit torn apart by violence, hatred, and savagery. What pained him most was not his own exile but the self-destruction of civilization—the Europe he loved and took such pride in was with its own hands burning itself down.

三、1942年2月的遗书

III. The Farewell Letter of February 1942

在巴西的彼得罗波利斯，他写下了一封简短的遗书。

In Petrópolis, Brazil, he wrote a short farewell letter.

他感谢巴西这个收留他的国家，然后写下了那段让无数人动容的话。大意是：

He thanked Brazil, the country that had taken him in, and then wrote the passage that has moved countless people. In substance it reads:

“我向所有的朋友致意！愿他们在漫漫长夜之后还能看到旭日东升！而我，我这个过于性急的人，要先他们而去了。”

"I send greetings to all my friends! May they see the sunrise after the long night! And I, who am too impatient, go on ahead of them."

1942年2月22日，他和第二任妻子洛特（Lotte Altmann）一起，在住所内服毒身亡。那一年，他61岁，妻子33岁。

On February 22, 1942, he and his second wife, Lotte (Lotte Altmann), took poison together in their residence. He was sixty-one; his wife was thirty-three.

他不是死于贫穷，也不是死于被遗忘——他恰恰是被全世界记住的人。他死于对“人的精神能否被守住”这件事的彻底绝望。

He did not die of poverty, nor of being forgotten—he was, precisely, a man remembered by the whole world. He died of utter despair at the question of whether the human spirit can be preserved.

四、他为什么至今仍被阅读

IV. Why He Is Still Read Today

茨威格在今天依然被大量阅读，原因不只是他写得好。更重要的是，他提供了一种“在极端时代里保持人的温度”的样本。

Zweig is still read widely today, and not only because he wrote well. More important, he offers a model of "keeping human warmth alive in an extreme age."

他不写宏大的战争场面，而是专写人心：一个女人的一生、一个赌徒的崩溃、一个棋手的天才与疯癫。他相信，真正值得书写的，是“人的精神如何在历史中被碾压、又如何挣扎着活下来”。

He did not write grand battle scenes but wrote the human heart: one woman's whole life, a gambler's collapse, a chess player's genius and madness. He believed that what truly deserved to be written was how the human spirit is crushed by history—and how it struggles to survive.

他的《人类群星闪耀时》，写的是一连串“历史瞬间”——一个决定、一分钟的犹豫、一次失败或侥幸，如何改变整个世界。这种写法，后来被无数历史写作和叙事作品借鉴。

His Decisive Moments in History (Sternstunden der Menschheit) is a series of "historical moments"—how a single decision, a minute of hesitation, a failure or a stroke of luck changes the whole world. This way of writing was later borrowed by countless works of history writing and narrative.

他一生都在写“个人的内心”，而时代最终用最残酷的方式，让他成了“个人被时代碾碎”的最好例子。

All his life he wrote of the individual's inner world, and in the end the age, in the cruelest way, made him the finest example of an individual crushed by his time.

五、他最后写下的那本书

V. The Book He Wrote Last

茨威格去世时，留下了一部未完成的手稿——《昨日的世界：一个欧洲人的回忆》。

When Zweig died, he left behind an unfinished manuscript—The World of Yesterday: Memoirs of a European (Die Welt von Gestern: Erinnerungen eines Europäers).

在这本书里，他写下了他经历的那个“最好的欧洲”，也写下了它如何崩塌。他在前方写道，自己1914年前生活在“自由的世界”里，而此后，世界一步步滑向深渊。

In this book he set down the "best Europe" he had known, and how it collapsed. Early in the book he wrote that before 1914 he had lived in a "free world," and that afterward the world slid step by step into the abyss.

这本书的著名开头，大意是：他没有为自己争取某种“世界公民”的荣誉，因为他的身份证明早就在各种手续中被磨掉了；他成了一个“护照上的编号”，一个失去了祖国的人。

The book's famous opening, in substance: he had not sought any honor as a "world citizen," because his papers had long since been worn away in one formality after another; he had become "a number on a passport," a man who had lost his homeland.

有意思的是，这部充满绝望的作品，本身却是一种“反抗”——他用最大的诚意，记录下人类文明曾经达到的高度，仿佛是在为后世留下遗嘱：这样的欧洲，真的存在过。

What is interesting is that this work, so full of despair, is itself a kind of "resistance"—with the greatest sincerity he recorded the heights human civilization had once reached, as if leaving a testament to posterity: such a Europe really did exist.

六、致2026：关于“在长夜中做什么”

VI. To 2026: On "What to Do in the Long Night"

2026年，我们未必经历茨威格那样的浩劫，但很多人心里确实有一种“时代在下沉”的感受。于是就有了两种选择：要么麻木地装作一切正常，要么彻底陷入虚无。

In 2026 we may not have lived through a catastrophe like Zweig's, but many of us do feel something like an age sinking. And so there are two choices: either to go numb and pretend everything is normal, or to fall completely into nihilism.

茨威格的悲剧，恰恰在于他无法在“麻木”和“虚无”之间找到第三条路——一个太过清醒、太过热爱文明的人，最后被文明的崩塌击穿了。

Zweig's tragedy lies precisely in his inability to find a third path between numbness and nihilism—a man too clear-sighted, too devoted to civilization, was in the end pierced through by its collapse.

但他留下的东西，反过来给了我们一条路：

But what he left behind gives us a path in turn:

在长夜之中，可以做一件事——记录。 记录你相信的东西，记录你见过的美好，记录那些“本不该被忘记”的人与事。茨威格没能等到天亮，但《昨日的世界》让后来的人知道，天亮之前，曾经有人这样活过。

In the long night there is one thing you can do—record. Record what you believe, record the beauty you have seen, record the people and things that "should never have been forgotten." Zweig did not live to see the dawn, but The World of Yesterday lets those who come after know that before the dawn, someone once lived like that.

对我们这代人来说，也许最有用的不是“如何熬过黑夜”的技术，而是一种姿态：

For our generation, perhaps the most useful thing is not a technique for "how to get through the night," but an attitude:

哪怕天还没亮，也别停止写你相信的那部分世界。

Even if the day has not yet broken, do not stop writing the part of the world you believe in.

还有一层安慰也许更实在：茨威格最重要的作品，是在他最绝望的时候写下的，却在他死后被一代又一代人读着。一个人写下的东西，往往比他本人活得更久——这大概就是“长夜”里最靠得住的一点光。

There is another consolation, perhaps a more solid one: Zweig's most important work was written when he was in his deepest despair, yet after his death it has been read generation after generation. What a person writes often outlives the person himself—that is probably the most dependable light in the long night.

对我们来说，也许不必像他那样背负一整个时代。但可以在自己的小世界里做同一件事：把仍然相信的东西记下来，留给还没经历长夜的人。哪怕只是写给十年后的自己，也算没有白白熬过这一段黑。

For us, perhaps we need not carry an entire age on our backs as he did. But we can do the same thing in our own small world: write down what we still believe, and leave it for those who have not yet lived through the long night. Even if it is only written to your own self ten years from now, it still means this stretch of darkness was not endured in vain.

Sources and Notes

2. That they died by poison together, he and his wife Lotte Altmann—an accepted historical fact; Lotte was born in 1908 and was about thirty-three when she died.

3. 遗书原文：“我向我所有的朋友致意！愿他们在漫漫长夜之后还能看到旭日东升！而我，我这个过于性急的人，要先他们而去了。”——为通行中译，各版本文字略有差异，本文按大意引用并标注为“大意”。

3. The text of the farewell letter: "I send greetings to all my friends! May they see the sunrise after the long night! And I, who am too impatient, go on ahead of them."—a standard Chinese rendering; the wording varies slightly among versions, and this essay quotes it in substance and marks it as such.

4. “《昨日的世界》为未完成手稿、在其身后出版”“写于流亡期间”——为公开史料。

4. That The World of Yesterday was an unfinished manuscript, published after his death, and written during his exile—a matter of public historical record.

5. “他是当时世界上被翻译最多的作家之一”——为文学史通行评价；亦有说法称他一度是“世界上被翻译最多的作家”，用语略有差异，本文采较稳妥的表述。

5. That he was one of the most translated writers in the world at the time—a common assessment in literary history; some accounts call him, for a period, "the most translated writer in the world." The wording varies slightly, and this essay adopts the more cautious formulation.

6. 需辨析：茨威格与第二任妻子的死因，有时被渲染为“因看到欧洲文明毁灭而绝望”的纯粹精神性事件。较全面的理解应包括：作为一个犹太作家，他确实面临纳粹的追捕与祖国沦陷；但他身处巴西、相对安全，其死亡更多是长期精神绝望与个人抑郁叠加的结果。本文两者并列，不作单一归因。

6. A point to weigh carefully: the death of Zweig and his second wife is sometimes dramatized as a purely spiritual event—despair at seeing European civilization destroyed. A fuller understanding should include this: as a Jewish writer he did indeed face Nazi pursuit and the fall of his homeland; but he was in Brazil, relatively safe, and his death was more a result of long-term spiritual despair compounded by personal depression. This essay presents both and offers no single cause.

7. “《一个陌生女人的来信》《人类群星闪耀时》《三大师》《与魔鬼作斗争》”——均为其真实作品，中译名通行。

7. "Letter from an Unknown Woman," "Decisive Moments in History," "Three Masters," and "The Struggle with the Daemon"—all genuine works of his, with Chinese titles that are in common use.

8. 本文涉及他与第一任妻子弗里德里克的分手等私人细节，因与主题关联不大，未予展开，以避免脱离史实的演绎。

8. Details of his private life, such as the break with his first wife, Friderike, are not elaborated here because they bear little on the theme, to avoid speculation unmoored from the historical record.

9. “世界公民”“护照上的编号”等表述，出自《昨日的世界》相关段落的大意转述，非逐字译文。

9. Expressions such as "world citizen" and "a number on a passport" are paraphrases in substance from the relevant passages of The World of Yesterday, not word-for-word translations.

10. 本文对其心理状态的描写，依据公开传记与其自述性文字，不作超出材料的推断。

10. The descriptions of his psychological state in this essay rest on public biographies and his own autobiographical writings, and make no inferences beyond the material.
