Chapter 091 · Part 5

斯诺：第一个走进红色中国的西方记者

Snow: The First Western Journalist to Enter Red China

——“我爱中国，我愿在我死后把我的一部分留在那里。”

—"I love China, and I want a part of me to stay there after I die."

← 返回主页 Home第五辑　走向未知的人91 / 100约 3040 字 · 3040 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，外国博主在中国拍视频、在海外讲中国故事，已经很常见了。但在九十多年前，一个西方记者要走进中国西北那片被重重封锁的区域，把里面的真实情况告诉世界，是一件几乎等于“自毁前程”的事。

In 2026, foreign vloggers filming in China and telling Chinese stories abroad have become commonplace. But more than ninety years ago, for a Western journalist to walk into that heavily blockaded region of northwest China and tell the world what was really going on there was all but the same as "ruining his own future."

他做到了。他写出了一本书，书名在西方叫《红星照耀中国》，中译本叫《西行漫记》。这本书让全世界第一次知道，在中国的西北，有一群人，正在进行一场改变国家命运的事业。

He did it. He wrote a book, known in the West as Red Star Over China and in its Chinese translation as Xixing Manji (Journey to the West). For the first time the book let the whole world know that in northwest China there was a group of people engaged in an undertaking that would change the fate of the nation.

但正因为这本书，他在自己的祖国，被当成了“可疑的人”。他被调查、被监视、被迫离开，晚年在异国度过。

But precisely because of this book, he was treated as a "suspicious person" in his own country. He was investigated, surveilled, forced to leave, and spent his last years in a foreign land.

他叫埃德加·斯诺。

His name was Edgar Snow.

一、一个本想“待六个星期”的年轻人

I. A Young Man Who Meant to "Stay Six Weeks"

1905年7月19日，斯诺出生在美国密苏里州的堪萨斯城。他后来进了密苏里大学学新闻。

On July 19, 1905, Snow was born in Kansas City, Missouri. He later entered the University of Missouri to study journalism.

1928年，二十三岁的斯诺坐船来到中国。他一开始的计划是“在中国待六个星期”，做个短期的旅行和报道。

In 1928, at twenty-three, Snow sailed to China. His original plan was to "spend six weeks in China," making a short trip and filing a few reports.

结果他在这里待了十三年。

In the end, he stayed thirteen years.

他到中国的时候，看到的是一个内忧外患的国家：北洋军阀混战刚结束，列强在华有各种特权，日本的侵略野心日益明显，国民党的统治又充满腐败。他亲眼看到了这些，也看到了底层中国人生活的艰难。

When he arrived, he found a country beset by troubles within and without: the warlord wars of the Beiyang era had just ended, the foreign powers held every kind of privilege in China, Japan's aggressive ambitions were growing ever more obvious, and the Nationalist government was steeped in corruption. He saw all of this with his own eyes, and he saw too how hard life was for the Chinese at the bottom of society.

这些经历，让斯诺从一个“来撞大运的年轻记者”，变成了一个真正关心中国命运的观察者。1933年到1935年，他还在燕京大学新闻系兼课，跟中国的学生和知识分子有了很多直接的交往。

These experiences turned Snow from "a young reporter out to try his luck" into an observer who genuinely cared about China's fate. From 1933 to 1935 he also taught part-time in the journalism department of Yenching University, where he had a great deal of direct contact with Chinese students and intellectuals.

二、走进“红色中国”

II. Into "Red China"

在1930年代，关于中国共产党领导的地区，外界几乎没有可靠的信息。

In the 1930s, the outside world had almost no reliable information about the areas led by the Chinese Communist Party.

当时国民党的新闻封锁极其严密，所有关于“红军”的消息，要么被禁止，要么被扭曲。全世界对这支队伍的了解，基本停留在传闻的层面——有人说他们是“匪”，有人说他们已经被消灭了。

At the time the Nationalist news blockade was extremely tight: every piece of news about the "Red Army" was either banned or distorted. The world's understanding of this force remained at the level of rumor—some said they were "bandits," others that they had already been wiped out.

斯诺想做一件事：亲眼去看一看，那里到底是什么样子。

Snow wanted to do one thing: see for himself what it was actually like there.

1936年，在宋庆龄等人的安排下，斯诺获得了进入苏区的机会。他先到西安，然后一路北上，进入了陕北的保安（今志丹县）。

In 1936, through arrangements made by Soong Ching-ling and others, Snow obtained the chance to enter the Soviet areas. He went first to Xi'an, then traveled north the whole way, arriving in Bao'an in northern Shaanxi (today's Zhidan County).

在他到达时，当地举行了欢迎大会，街上挂起了用中英文写的“欢迎美国记者来苏区调查”的标语。他是第一个进入红区采访的西方记者。

When he arrived, the local people held a welcoming rally, and the streets were hung with banners written in both Chinese and English, reading "Welcome, American journalist, come investigate the Soviet areas." He was the first Western journalist to enter the Red areas to report.

三、在窑洞里采访毛泽东

III. Interviewing Mao Zedong in a Cave Dwelling

斯诺在苏区待了几个月。他做了大量的采访，其中最重要的是与毛泽东的长谈。

Snow stayed several months in the Soviet areas. He did a great deal of interviewing, and the most important of it was a long conversation with Mao Zedong.

1936年7月，毛泽东连续多日会见斯诺。在那些谈话里，毛泽东第一次系统地回顾了自己的生平——从少年时代到参加革命，从井冈山到长征。斯诺把这些内容记录下来，后来形成了那篇《毛泽东自传》。

In July 1936, Mao met with Snow over several days in succession. In those talks, Mao reviewed his life systematically for the first time—from his boyhood to joining the revolution, from Jinggangshan to the Long March. Snow recorded it all, and it later took shape as the Autobiography of Mao Zedong.

这是外界第一次看到毛泽东完整的个人经历。 在此之前，这位被西方世界称为“传奇”的人物，几乎是一个没有面孔的符号。

This was the first time the outside world saw a complete account of Mao's personal life. Before then, this figure the West called a "legend" had been little more than a faceless symbol.

斯诺还采访了周恩来、彭德怀等人，也记录了大量红军战士的日常生活。他没有把这些人写成“圣人”，而是写成了有血有肉、会开玩笑、会唱歌、也会疲惫的人。

Snow also interviewed Zhou Enlai, Peng Dehuai, and others, and recorded a great deal about the daily life of the Red Army soldiers. He did not write these people as "saints," but as flesh-and-blood human beings who could joke, sing, and grow tired.

正是这种“真实”，让这本书有了穿透力。 它不是外交辞令，是一个记者用自己的眼睛看到的东西。

It was precisely this "truthfulness" that gave the book its power to cut through. It is not diplomatic language; it is what a reporter saw with his own eyes.

四、《红星照耀中国》

IV. Red Star Over China

1937年10月，斯诺根据采访材料写成的《红星照耀中国》（Red Star Over China）在英国出版。

In October 1937, Red Star Over China, which Snow had written from his interview material, was published in Britain.

这本书产生了巨大的影响。它第一次向世界全面报道了中国工农红军，特别是长征的经过。斯诺把长征称为“当今时代无与伦比的一次史诗般的远征”。

The book had an enormous impact. For the first time it gave the world a comprehensive account of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army, and especially of the course of the Long March. Snow called the Long March "a magnificent epic expedition unrivaled in modern times."

它在国际上的作用，是“撕开了一道口子”。 国民党多年来构筑的新闻封锁和信息歪曲，被这本书一举打破。它被翻译成近二十种文字，传遍世界。

Its effect internationally was to "tear open a gap." The news blockade and distortion the Nationalists had built up over the years were broken at a single stroke by this book. It was translated into nearly twenty languages and spread across the world.

1938年，这本书的中译本在上海出版。考虑到要在国民党统治区和敌占区发行，为了躲避书报审查，译本改了个名字——《西行漫记》。

In 1938, the Chinese translation was published in Shanghai. Because it had to circulate both in Nationalist-held areas and in enemy-occupied territory, and to evade the censors, the translation was given a different title—Xixing Manji (Journey to the West).

很多爱国青年和外国友人，就是因为读了这本书，才决定奔赴延安的。白求恩在给友人的信里就说过：要问我为什么去中国，请读埃德加·斯诺的《红星照耀中国》。

Many patriotic young people and foreign friends decided to go to Yan'an precisely because they had read this book. In a letter to a friend, Norman Bethune said: if you ask me why I went to China, read Edgar Snow's Red Star Over China.

五、在祖国的“不自由”

V. "Unfreedom" in His Own Country

1941年，皖南事变发生。斯诺为这件事写下了报道，揭露了部分真相。这让他受到了国民党当局的压制，采访权被取消。1941年2月，他被迫离开中国。

In 1941, the Southern Anhui Incident took place. Snow wrote a report on it, exposing part of the truth. This brought him suppression from the Nationalist authorities, and his right to report was revoked. In February 1941 he was forced to leave China.

回到美国后，他没有停止为中国的抗战和中国的事业发声，也因此受到罗斯福总统的重视——罗斯福读过他的书，先后三次约见斯诺谈中国问题。

After returning to the United States, he did not stop speaking up for China's war of resistance and for China's cause, and for this he drew the regard of President Roosevelt—who had read his book and met with Snow three times to discuss China.

但到了1950年代，美国的“麦卡锡主义”开始蔓延。任何被认为“同情共产党”的人，都可能被调查、被监视、被打压。斯诺也未能幸免——FBI上门，要求他“坦白一切共产主义活动”。

But by the 1950s, "McCarthyism" began to spread in the United States. Anyone thought to "sympathize with the Communists" could be investigated, surveilled, and suppressed. Snow did not escape it—the FBI came to his door and demanded that he "confess to all Communist activities."

他没有屈服。但他发现，自己在美国的生活越来越受限。1959年，他带着全家迁居瑞士日内瓦。

He did not give in. But he found that his life in America grew more and more restricted. In 1959 he moved with his whole family to Geneva, Switzerland.

一个因为报道真相而成名的人，被自己的国家逼着离开了。

A man who had made his name by reporting the truth was driven out by his own country.

六、他最后一次回来

VI. His Last Time Back

在瑞士的日子里，斯诺始终关注着中国。

During his years in Switzerland, Snow never stopped paying attention to China.

1960年、1964年、1970年，他三次回到中国访问。1970年10月，他和夫人登上天安门城楼，参加了中国的国庆观礼，受到了毛泽东和周恩来的接见。

He returned to visit China three times, in 1960, 1964, and 1970. In October 1970, he and his wife climbed the rostrum of Tiananmen Gate to attend China's National Day celebrations, and were received by Mao Zedong and Zhou Enlai.

这次会面，后来被认为对中美关系的解冻产生了重要影响。 1971年，斯诺在美国的《生活》杂志上撰文，透露了相关的重要信息，成为推动中美接近的重要舆论信号。

This meeting was later regarded as having had an important influence on the thaw in Sino-American relations. In 1971, Snow wrote in the American magazine Life, disclosing important related information and becoming an important signal in public opinion urging the two countries toward each other.

但他没能等到最后那一刻。1972年2月15日，斯诺在瑞士日内瓦去世——就在尼克松访华的前一周。

But he did not live to see that final moment. On February 15, 1972, Snow died in Geneva, Switzerland—just one week before Nixon's visit to China.

他临终前说过一句话：“我热爱中国。”按照他的遗嘱，他骨灰的一部分被带到了中国，安葬在北京大学的未名湖畔。

Before he died, he said, "I love China." In accordance with his will, part of his ashes was brought to China and buried by the shore of Weiming Lake at Peking University.

他没能活到亲眼看见中美关系真正破冰的那一天。（宋庆龄曾感叹，这是一个悲剧。）

He did not live to see with his own eyes the day Sino-American relations truly broke the ice. (Soong Ching-ling once lamented that this was a tragedy.)

七、写给2026年的我们

VII. To Us in 2026

斯诺的一生，是一个“诚实的人”的一生。

Snow's life was the life of an "honest man."

他既不是中国的“宣传员”，也不是谁的“特派员”。他做的事情很纯粹：他去了，看了，写了。 他写出的东西里，有赞美，也有批评；有对革命者的敬意，也有对现实的观察。

He was neither China's "propagandist" nor anyone's "special envoy." What he did was very simple: he went, he looked, he wrote. In what he wrote there was praise and there was criticism; there was respect for the revolutionaries and there was observation of reality.

而这恰恰是他最珍贵的地方。在一个信息被不断筛选、被不断站队的世界里，斯诺证明了一件事：一个人最大的力量，是可以“自己去看”，而不是“听别人说”。

And this is precisely what was most precious about him. In a world where information is constantly sifted and constantly forced to take sides, Snow proved one thing: a person's greatest strength is the ability to "go and see for himself," rather than "hear it from others."

2026年，我们的信息比斯诺那个时代多了一万倍，但“自己去看”这件事，反而更难了。算法把每个人固定在各自的立场上，我们越来越少去触碰那些“和自己不一样”的地方。

In 2026, we have ten thousand times more information than in Snow's era, yet "going to see for oneself" has become harder rather than easier. Algorithms pin each of us to our own position, and we less and less often reach out to touch what is "different from ourselves."

斯诺的遗产，也许就是这个朴素的提醒：世界上很多误解，不是因为信息不够，而是因为没有人愿意亲自走进去看一眼。

Snow's legacy is perhaps this plain reminder: many of the world's misunderstandings arise not from a lack of information, but from no one being willing to walk in and take a look for themselves.

史料来源与辨析

Sources and Notes

1. 生平基础事实：埃德加·斯诺（Edgar Snow），1905年7月19日生于美国密苏里州堪萨斯城，1972年2月15日卒于瑞士日内瓦，年66岁。为确证史实。

1. Basic biographical facts: Edgar Snow, born July 19, 1905, in Kansas City, Missouri, USA; died February 15, 1972, in Geneva, Switzerland, aged 66. Well-established historical fact.

2. 来华时间与经历：1928年来华，曾在燕京大学新闻系兼任讲师。为确证史实。

2. Time of arrival in China and experience: came to China in 1928; served as a part-time lecturer in the journalism department of Yenching University. Well-established historical fact.

3. 进入苏区：1936年，在宋庆龄等人的安排与协调下进入陕北苏区，为第一个进入红区采访的西方记者。为确证史实。

3. Entry into the Soviet areas: in 1936, entered the northern Shaanxi Soviet area through the arrangements and coordination of Soong Ching-ling and others; the first Western journalist to enter the Red areas to report. Well-established historical fact.

4. 与毛泽东的长谈：1936年7月，毛泽东多次会见斯诺，其谈话内容形成了后来的《毛泽东自传》。为确证史实。

4. The long conversation with Mao Zedong: in July 1936, Mao met with Snow several times, and the content of these talks formed what later became the Autobiography of Mao Zedong. Well-established historical fact.

5. 《红星照耀中国》：1937年在英国出版；1938年中译本以《西行漫记》为名在上海出版。为确证史实。

5. Red Star Over China: published in Britain in 1937; the Chinese translation, under the title Xixing Manji (Journey to the West), was published in Shanghai in 1938. Well-established historical fact.

6. 被国民党打压与1941年离华：皖南事变后因其报道受国民党压力，1941年被迫离开中国（其外国记者特许证曾被吊销）。为确证史实。

6. Suppression by the Nationalists and departure from China in 1941: after the Southern Anhui Incident he came under Nationalist pressure because of his reporting and was forced to leave China in 1941 (his foreign correspondent's permit was revoked). Well-established historical fact.

7. 麦卡锡主义时期的处境：1950年代在美国受到FBI调查与监视，处境艰难；1959年迁居瑞士。为确证史实。

7. His situation during the McCarthy era: in the 1950s he was investigated and surveilled by the FBI in the United States and faced difficult circumstances; in 1959 he moved to Switzerland. Well-established historical fact.

8. 三次访华与1970年天安门：1960、1964、1970年三次访华；1970年10月登上天安门城楼。为确证史实。

8. Three visits to China and Tiananmen in 1970: visited China three times, in 1960, 1964, and 1970; in October 1970 he climbed the rostrum of Tiananmen Gate. Well-established historical fact.

9. 去世与安葬：1972年2月15日在日内瓦去世（尼克松访华前一周）；1973年10月19日，其骨灰的一部分安葬于北京大学未名湖畔。为确证史实。

9. Death and burial: died in Geneva on February 15, 1972 (one week before Nixon's visit to China); on October 19, 1973, part of his ashes was buried by the shore of Weiming Lake at Peking University. Well-established historical fact.

10. 本文引用的“要问我为什么去中国，请读埃德加·斯诺的《红星照耀中国》”——白求恩的这句话，出自斯诺一书的传播影响相关记载，为通行引述。

10. The line quoted in this text, "if you ask me why I went to China, read Edgar Snow's Red Star Over China"—this remark by Norman Bethune comes from records concerning the influence and circulation of Snow's book; it is a commonly cited quotation.

11. 2009年，斯诺与白求恩、约翰·拉贝等被评为“中国十大国际友人”之一，为确证史实。

11. In 2009, Snow, along with Norman Bethune, John Rabe, and others, was named one of the "Ten Great International Friends of China." Well-established historical fact.
