Chapter 049 · Part 3

史玉柱：从“首富”到“首负”，欠债2.5亿的那个人

Shi Yuzhu: From "Richest Man" to "Most Indebted Man," the One Who Owed 250 Million

——一个35岁破产的人，用十几年时间把“失败”变成了自己最值钱的资产

—A man who went bankrupt at 35, and over a dozen years turned "failure" into his most valuable asset

← 返回主页 Home第三辑　在废墟上重建的人49 / 100约 3630 字 · 3630 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，负债是一件很敏感的事。房贷、车贷、经营贷，很多人每个月睁开眼，先要还一笔钱。一旦创业失败、公司倒闭、背上巨额债务，很多人第一反应是：完了，这辈子完了。

In 2026, debt is a sensitive matter. Mortgages, car loans, business loans—for many people, the first thing they face when they open their eyes each month is a payment due. And once a venture fails, a company folds, and a mountain of debt piles up, the first thought for many is: that's it. My life is over.

但三十年前，有一个人背上了2.5亿元的债务，成了当时全国最有名的“失败者”。他在公众视野里消失了整整三年，爬上珠穆朗玛峰，隐居南京读《毛泽东选集》。三年后，他借了50万元重新开始，用一款保健品翻了身，又用一款网游登上了另一个高峰。

But thirty years ago, one man took on 250 million yuan in debt and became the most famous "failure" in the country at the time. He vanished from public view for three full years, climbed Mount Everest, and lived in seclusion in Nanjing reading the Selected Works of Mao Zedong. Three years later, he borrowed 500,000 yuan to start over, turned his fortunes around with a health supplement, and then reached another peak with an online game.

他叫史玉柱。他后来总结说：“创业者应该少去听一些成功人士的经验报告，相反多看看创业失败者的经验，会更有收获。”

His name is Shi Yuzhu. He later summed it up this way: "Entrepreneurs should spend less time listening to the experience reports of successful people. On the contrary, they should study the experience of failed entrepreneurs more—they'll gain far more from it."

一、“神童”的第一次豪赌

I. A "Child Prodigy's" First Big Gamble

1962年，史玉柱出生在安徽蚌埠怀远县。初二之前他贪玩，成绩不好；1977年恢复高考，他突然开始拼命读书。1980年，他以全县总分第一、数学差1分满分的成绩，考进了浙江大学数学系。

Shi Yuzhu was born in 1962 in Huaiyuan County, Bengbu, Anhui Province. Before his second year of middle school he was playful and his grades were poor; then in 1977, when the college entrance examination was reinstated, he suddenly began studying desperately. In 1980, he was admitted to the mathematics department of Zhejiang University with the highest total score in the whole county—and one point short of a perfect score in mathematics.

刚上一学期，他就放弃了自己“成为陈景润”的理想——因为他发现自己的天赋不足以支撑那个目标。这个“认清自己”的时刻很重要，它让史玉柱从“想当科学家”变成了“想做成事的人”。

After just one semester, he abandoned his dream of "becoming Chen Jingrun"—because he discovered that his talent was not enough to sustain such a goal. This moment of "seeing himself clearly" mattered a great deal: it turned Shi Yuzhu from someone who "wanted to be a scientist" into someone who "wanted to get things done."

1984年，他从浙大毕业，分配到安徽省统计局。因为工作出色，他被作为“第三梯队”（储备干部）送到深圳大学进修，1989年从深圳大学软科学管理系研究生毕业。

In 1984 he graduated from Zhejiang University and was assigned to the Anhui Provincial Bureau of Statistics. Because of his outstanding work, he was sent as part of the "third echelon" (reserve cadres) to Shenzhen University for further study, and in 1989 he completed a master's degree in the Department of Soft Science Management there.

但他决定不回去了。他要下海。

But he decided not to go back. He was going into business.

那时他全部的家当，是4000元钱，和一款自己开发了9个月的桌面排版软件M-6401。

At that point, his entire fortune consisted of 4,000 yuan and M-6401, a desktop publishing program he had spent nine months developing himself.

接下来的操作，是他人生中的第一次豪赌。他找到《计算机世界》报，提出要登一则8400元的广告——条件是：广告先登，半个月后再付钱。他赌的，是自己能在两周内把产品卖出去。

What he did next was the first great gamble of his life. He approached the newspaper China Computer World and proposed to place an 8,400-yuan advertisement—on the condition that the ad run first and payment come half a month later. What he was betting on was his ability to sell the product within two weeks.

广告登出来了，半个版面，只有一行字：“M6401，历史性的突破”。

The ad appeared: half a page, carrying just one line of text—"M6401, a historic breakthrough."

13天后，他的银行账户收到了第一笔汇款。两个月，他赚了10万元。四个月，他成了百万富翁。

Thirteen days later, the first remittance arrived in his bank account. In two months, he earned 100,000 yuan. In four months, he was a millionaire.

二、“巨人”的崛起

II. The Rise of "Giant"

1991年，史玉柱移师珠海，成立了巨人公司。他给公司取名“巨人”，理由很直白：“IBM是国际公认的蓝色巨人，我用'巨人'命名公司，就是要做中国的IBM。”

In 1991, Shi Yuzhu moved his base to Zhuhai and founded the Giant company. His reason for naming it "Giant" was blunt: "IBM is internationally recognized as Big Blue. By naming my company Giant, I mean to build China's IBM."

他推出M-6403巨人汉卡，三个月后销量就位居全国同类产品之首，当年利润3500万元。1993年，巨人成为中国第二大民营高科技企业。

He launched the M-6403 Giant Chinese character card, and within three months it led the country in sales among comparable products, generating a profit of 35 million yuan that year. In 1993, Giant became China's second-largest private high-tech enterprise.

那时的史玉柱，被时代赋予了很高的期待：90年代初，国内人才大量出国、很少回国，北京方面希望树立一个“中国大学生留在本土创业”的典型。媒体把他塑造成“替民族国家争气、攻克科技难关、挑战西方巨头”的人物。他开始相信自己“无所不能”。

At the time, the era placed high expectations on Shi Yuzhu: in the early 1990s, when large numbers of talented people were going abroad and few were returning, Beijing hoped to hold up a model of a "Chinese university graduate who stayed home to start a business." The media portrayed him as a figure who "won honor for the nation, conquered scientific and technological barriers, and challenged Western giants." He began to believe that he was "capable of anything."

而“无所不能”这四个字，正是所有崩塌的开始。

And those words—"capable of anything"—were precisely the beginning of all collapse.

三、一座楼压垮一个帝国

III. One Building Crushes an Empire

1992年，珠海市政府几乎以白送的价格（125元/平方米）批给巨人一块地，史玉柱准备盖一栋办公楼。有领导问他“为何不盖高一点”，于是楼层从18层加到38层，再到54层。后来听说广州要盖63层的中国最高楼，他决定“为珠海争光”，加到64层；又有人觉得“64”不吉利，最终定在70层。

In 1992, the Zhuhai municipal government granted Giant a parcel of land at what amounted to a giveaway price (125 yuan per square meter), and Shi Yuzhu planned to build an office tower. A leader asked him "why not build it a little taller," so the floors went from 18 to 38 to 54. Later, hearing that Guangzhou was going to build China's tallest building at 63 stories, he decided to "win glory for Zhuhai" and added on up to 64 floors; then someone felt that "64" was inauspicious, and the final plan settled on 70 floors.

一座总预算12亿元的巨人大厦，而他手里只有约1亿元的流动资金。

A Giant Building with a total budget of 1.2 billion yuan—while he had only about 100 million yuan in working capital on hand.

更糟的是，大厦的地基建在了三层断裂带上，光地基就多投入了1亿元，工期延误，投资也一路飞涨到12亿。为了解决资金，他开始卖“楼花”，又赶上1994年国家加强宏观调控，最终只卖掉1.8亿元。

Worse still, the building's foundation was laid atop three fault zones; the foundation alone cost an extra 100 million yuan, the schedule slipped, and the investment spiraled to 1.2 billion. To raise funds, he began selling "unbuilt units"; then came the state's tightened macroeconomic controls in 1994, and in the end only 180 million yuan worth was sold.

与此同时，他还同时开辟了保健品、药品、化妆品、服装等多条战线。1997年初，资金链彻底断裂，巨人大厦停工。媒体一篇《巨人大厦濒临破产》的报道，让所有投资人涌上门来要债。

At the same time, he had opened up frontlines in health supplements, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, clothing, and more. In early 1997, the cash chain broke completely and construction on the Giant Building halted. A media report titled "Giant Building on the Brink of Bankruptcy" brought all the investors rushing to his door demanding their money.

一个星期之内，巨人坍塌。

Within a single week, Giant collapsed.

那一年，史玉柱35岁，欠债2.5亿元，被称为“中国首负”、“最著名的失败者”。据说，他当时只差最后1000万元，就有可能渡过难关。但就是这1000万，把英雄汉难倒了。

That year, Shi Yuzhu was 35, owed 250 million yuan, and was called "China's most indebted man" and "the most famous failure." It is said that he was only 10 million yuan short of possibly weathering the storm. But it was that very 10 million that felled the valiant man.

四、消失的三年

IV. The Three Vanished Years

此后，史玉柱在公众视野里消失了整整三年。

Afterward, Shi Yuzhu vanished from public view for a full three years.

这三年，他在大江南北游荡：爬珠穆朗玛峰，隐居南京读《毛泽东选集》。他后来说过一段很坦诚的话：

During those three years, he wandered across the land: climbing Mount Everest and living in seclusion in Nanjing reading the Selected Works of Mao Zedong. He later said something very candid:

“人是这样的，人只有在低谷的时候，一个人在很凄婉的那种环境下，才能去认真琢磨这些事儿。现在那些做得好的，你去跟他们谈，他们的口气跟我当年一样……好像老子天下第一，这个事我也能做，那个事我也能做。事实上每个企业、每个人真正能做的事很少，适合自己、能做成功的事实际更少。”

"It's like this with people: only when you're in a trough, alone in a rather bleak and sorrowful kind of environment, can you seriously think these things through. Talk to those who are doing well now, and their tone is the same as mine back then... as if they were the greatest in the world, as if they could do this and could do that too. In fact, what any company, any person, can truly do is very little; and what actually suits you and can be done successfully is even less."

他也反思过巨人为什么会倒：“表面看是巨人大厦，实际是因为我本人和我们团队的不成熟。如果巨人大厦盖起来了，还要倒，再倒的话，可能摔的跤更重，所以晚倒不如早倒。”

He also reflected on why Giant fell: "On the surface it was the Giant Building, but in fact it was because of the immaturity of myself and my team. If the Giant Building had been completed, we would still have fallen—and falling then might have meant an even harder crash. So a late fall is worse than an early one."

三年后他想明白了：梦还没断，还可以靠做保健品去还债。

Three years later, he had figured it out: the dream was not yet broken, and he could still repay his debts by making health supplements.

五、50万元和一句广告词

V. 500,000 Yuan and a Slogan

1998年，他找朋友借了50万元。在一辆从珠海开往无锡的面包车里，他对二十多个几个月没领到工资的旧部说：“等我有了钱，一定补偿你们。”

In 1998, he borrowed 500,000 yuan from friends. In a van driving from Zhuhai to Wuxi, he told more than twenty old subordinates who had gone months without pay: "When I have money, I promise I'll make it up to you."

他把50万分成四份：5万补发工资，15万给无锡一家公司生产产品，15万作预备资金，剩下的15万，全部砸向江苏江阴的营销市场。吸取了巨人3亿元应收烂账的教训，这一次他坚决不赊账。

He divided the 500,000 into four parts: 50,000 to pay the overdue wages, 150,000 for a Wuxi company to manufacture the product, 150,000 as reserve funds, and the remaining 150,000—all of it—thrown into the marketing battle in Jiangyin, Jiangsu. Having learned from Giant's 300 million yuan in bad receivables, this time he refused to extend credit.

产品叫“脑白金”。广告词沿用了他当年做“脑黄金”时的那句：“今年过节不收礼，收礼只收脑白金。”

The product was called Nao Baijin ("Brain Platinum"). The slogan carried over the line from his earlier "Nao Huangjin" ("Brain Gold"): "This holiday season, we take no gifts—if it's a gift, it has to be Nao Baijin."

这句话后来连续多年被评为“十差广告”。史玉柱的回应是：

For years afterward, this line was voted one of the "Ten Worst Advertisements." Shi Yuzhu's response was:

“'十佳广告'倒是年年换，因为许多公司都倒闭了。评选广告的专家们讲唯美、讲创意、讲社会责任感，就是不讲能不能卖货，而厂商只认销售额。”

"The 'Ten Best Advertisements' change every year, because many of those companies have gone bankrupt. The experts who judge ads talk about aesthetics, creativity, and social responsibility—anything except whether the thing sells. But manufacturers care only about sales figures."

一年半后，脑白金铺遍全国，月销售额过亿。2000年，销售额13亿元，成为保健品单品销售冠军。2001年，他还清了那2.5亿元债务。

A year and a half later, Nao Baijin was sold across the country, with monthly sales topping 100 million yuan. In 2000, sales reached 1.3 billion yuan, making it the top-selling single health supplement product. In 2001, he paid off that 250 million yuan debt.

关于还债，他的解释既不崇高也不龌龊：“我既不像外界说得那么高尚，也没有那么龌龊。我前面的路还很长，欠着人家，走到哪都挺丢人的，心里不踏实。”

Regarding the debt repayment, his explanation was neither noble nor sordid: "I'm not as noble as the outside world makes me out to be, nor as sordid either. I still have a long road ahead. Owing people money, I'd feel ashamed wherever I went, and I wouldn't have peace of mind."

六、第二次登顶与他的“失败学”

VI. A Second Summit and His "Study of Failure"

2004年，42岁的史玉柱又转了一次身：他成立征途网络，进入网游行业。

In 2004, at 42, Shi Yuzhu reinvented himself once more: he founded Zhan Tu Network ("Journey") and entered the online gaming industry.

他做的《征途》首开“永久免费、靠卖道具赚钱”的模式，同时他把“农村包围城市”的打法用在了游戏上，市场锁定二三线城市和农村。2007年11月1日，巨人网络在纽约证券交易所上市，成为当时赴美IPO的中国最大民营企业，公司市值一度接近50亿美元。

His game Zhan Tu ("Journey") pioneered a "permanently free, profits from selling in-game items" model, and he applied the "encircle the cities from the countryside" tactic to gaming, targeting second- and third-tier cities and rural areas. On November 1, 2007, Giant Network went public on the New York Stock Exchange—at the time the largest Chinese private enterprise to hold an IPO in the United States—and the company's market value at one point approached 5 billion U.S. dollars.

但《征途》的成功也带来了巨大的道德争议——把“金钱”引入网游、为让玩家掏钱设置规则，这些质疑至今没有平息。史玉柱当时的回答，是把问题推给整个行业：“我觉得，任何游戏公司都要面临这个追问。”

But the success of Zhan Tu also brought enormous moral controversy—introducing "money" into online games, and designing rules to make players spend. These criticisms have never fully subsided. Shi Yuzhu's answer at the time was to deflect the question onto the entire industry: "I think any gaming company has to face this question."

这些年里，他起起落落，也争议不断。但有一点始终没变：他反复讲的，不是自己怎么成功，而是别人怎么失败。

Over these years he rose and fell, and remained surrounded by controversy. But one thing never changed: what he kept talking about was not how he had succeeded, but how others had failed.

七、致2026：把“失败”当成一种资产

VII. To 2026: Treating "Failure" as an Asset

2026年，很多人对“失败”这两个字过敏。一次创业崩了，一次投资亏了，一次考试没过，就觉得人生被打了折。

In 2026, many people are allergic to the word "failure." A venture collapses, an investment loses money, an exam isn't passed—and they feel their whole life has been marked down.

史玉柱的故事里，最有价值的其实不是“翻身”，而是他对失败的态度。他把巨人那次坍塌，看成一次“体检”：它暴露了他和团队的幼稚，也让他明白了“一个人真正能做的事很少”。

In Shi Yuzhu's story, the most valuable thing is not really the "comeback" but his attitude toward failure. He saw Giant's collapse as a kind of "physical exam": it exposed the naivety of himself and his team, and it made him understand that "there is very little a person can truly do."

从这个角度看，失败不是负债，而是一种资产——只要你别在同一个地方倒第二次。

Seen this way, failure is not a liability but an asset—as long as you don't fall down in the same place twice.

对今天背着各种压力的80后、90后来说，史玉柱至少提供了两个参考：第一，认输可以，认命不行，债务是需要还的，但人生不必一次还清；第二，比“我能不能成功”更重要的，是“我有没有真正想明白自己适合做什么”。

For today's post-80s and post-90s generations, weighed down by all kinds of pressure, Shi Yuzhu offers at least two reference points. First, it's fine to concede, but you can't resign yourself to fate—debts do need to be repaid, but life doesn't have to be paid off all at once. Second, more important than "can I succeed" is "have I truly figured out what I'm suited to do."

他说过一句话，也许比任何励志故事都实在：

He once said something that may be more down-to-earth than any inspirational story:

“少听成功者的经验，多看失败者的教训。”

"Listen less to the experience of winners; study more the lessons of failures."

Biographical timeline (born 1962 in Huaiyuan, Anhui; admitted to the mathematics department of Zhejiang University in 1980; graduated from the Department of Soft Science Management at Shenzhen University in 1989; borrowed 4,000 yuan to start a business in 1989; founded Giant in 1991; cash chain broke and debt of 250 million in 1997; borrowed 500,000 to make Nao Baijin in 1998; repaid the debt in 2001; entered online gaming in 2004; Giant Network listed on the NYSE on November 1, 2007)—compiled from Baidu Baike, China Entrepreneur magazine, the People's Daily app, China Economic Net, and others.

2. “4000元做8400元广告、13天收到汇款、4个月赚100万”——为通行传记细节，出现在多篇纪实报道中，属史玉柱本人多次讲述的经历。

2. "Placing an 8,400-yuan ad with 4,000 yuan, receiving a remittance in 13 days, earning 1 million in 4 months"—a common biographical detail appearing in many documentary reports, and an experience Shi Yuzhu himself has recounted repeatedly.

3. “巨人大厦从18层加到70层”“预算从2亿涨到12亿”“地基建在三层断裂带上”——多篇报道一致；楼层具体演变过程（18→38→54→64→70）有不同版本，本文取较通行的叙述。

3. "The Giant Building grew from 18 to 70 floors," "the budget rose from 200 million to 1.2 billion," "the foundation was laid atop three fault zones"—consistent across many reports; there are different versions of the specific sequence of floor changes (18→38→54→64→70), and this text follows the more common account.

4. “负债2.5亿元”——为公开报道的统一口径。

4. "Debt of 250 million yuan"—the consistent figure in public reports.

5. “只差1000万元就能渡过难关”——报道中多写作“据称”，未见当事人正式确认，故本文以“据说”标注。

5. "Only 10 million yuan short of weathering the storm"—mostly written as "reportedly" in the coverage, without formal confirmation from the person concerned; this text therefore marks it with "it is said."

6. “脑白金2000年销售额13亿”“2001年还清2.5亿债务”——为公开报道数字。

6. "Nao Baijin sales of 1.3 billion in 2000," "the 250 million debt repaid in 2001"—figures from public reports.

7. “巨人网络2007年上市，市值接近50亿美元”——不同报道有“42亿美元市值”“融资8.87亿美元”“市值接近50亿美元”等口径，本文取“接近50亿美元”，属约数。

7. "Giant Network listed in 2007 with a market value approaching 5 billion U.S. dollars"—different reports use such figures as "a market value of 4.2 billion U.S. dollars," "raising 887 million U.S. dollars," and "a market value approaching 5 billion U.S. dollars"; this text adopts "approaching 5 billion U.S. dollars," which is an approximate figure.

8. 需辨析：网上流传的“史玉柱从首富到首负只用了一个星期”等表述为概括性说法；“巨人倒下时他身边人都跑了”亦属简化叙事，实际有多位旧部一直跟随他二次创业。

8. A note of caution: such widely circulated online statements as "It took Shi Yuzhu only a week to go from richest man to most indebted man" are sweeping generalizations; "when Giant fell, everyone around him ran away" is likewise a simplified narrative—in reality several old subordinates followed him through his second venture to the end.

9. 争议提示：《征途》的商业模式、脑白金广告的价值观，长期存在公开争议，本文如实呈现争议本身，不为其辩护。

9. A note on controversy: the business model of Zhan Tu and the values expressed in Nao Baijin's advertising have long been subject to public controversy; this text presents the controversy as it is and does not defend it.

10. 涉及现代企业家的细节多来自公开媒体，本文不做超出公开报道范围的推测。

10. Details concerning contemporary entrepreneurs come mostly from public media; this text does not speculate beyond the scope of public reporting.
