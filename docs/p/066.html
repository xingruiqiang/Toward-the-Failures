傅雷：把巴尔扎克译成中文的人，和妻子一起走了

Fu Lei: The Man Who Translated Balzac into Chinese, Who Left Together with His Wife

——遗书里，他详细交代了53.30元的火葬费

In his suicide note, he accounted in detail for the 53.30 yuan cremation fee.

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂66 / 100约 2963 字 · 2963 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，我们谈“精神内耗”，谈“情绪价值”，谈如何在高压环境里自我调节。这些都是好事。但有些年代的高压，不是“调节”能解决的——它会直接击穿一个人活下去的意愿。

In 2026 we discuss "mental attrition," we discuss "emotional value," we discuss how to regulate ourselves under great pressure. All of this is good. But the pressure of certain eras cannot be solved by "regulation"—it pierces straight through a person's will to live.

1966年9月3日凌晨，上海江苏路的一栋花园洋房里，一位58岁的翻译家，和妻子一起结束了生命。他生前翻译了巴尔扎克、罗曼·罗兰、伏尔泰等人的三十多部名著，被法国巴尔扎克研究会吸收为会员。

In the early hours of September 3, 1966, in a garden house on Jiangsu Road in Shanghai, a fifty-eight-year-old translator ended his life together with his wife. In his lifetime he had translated more than thirty major works by Balzac, Romain Rolland, Voltaire, and others, and had been made a member of the French Society for Balzac Studies.

他叫傅雷。他留下的那封遗书，没有一句控诉，只把身后事一样一样交代清楚。

His name was Fu Lei. The suicide note he left behind contained not a single word of accusation; it simply set out, one item at a time, everything that had to be done after his death.

一、从巴黎带回的“翻译标准”

I. The "Standard of Translation" He Brought Back from Paris

1908年，傅雷出生在上海南汇（今属浦东）。他早年留学法国，在巴黎读书、听音乐、看画展。

Fu Lei was born in 1908 in Nanhui, Shanghai (today part of Pudong). In his early years he studied in France, reading, listening to music, and visiting exhibitions in Paris.

这段经历塑造了他一生的“标准”。他回国后，一边任教，一边从事翻译。他翻译的不只是文字，而是一种“水准”——他要求自己的译文既要忠实原作，又要有中文的韵味。他译罗曼·罗兰的《约翰·克利斯朵夫》、巴尔扎克的《欧也妮·葛朗台》《高老头》、伏尔泰的作品……这些译本成了几代中国读者的共同记忆。

This experience shaped the "standard" by which he lived his whole life. After returning home, he taught and translated at the same time. What he translated was not merely words but a certain "level of excellence"—he required his translations to be both faithful to the original and possessed of the savor of Chinese. He translated Romain Rolland's Jean-Christophe, Balzac's Eugénie Grandet and Le Père Goriot, and works by Voltaire... These translations became a shared memory for generations of Chinese readers.

他有一套很“旧派”的工作方式：为一个词反复推敲，为一段译文抄改多遍。这种“慢”在一个追求效率的时代显得有些过时，但恰恰是这种“过时”，让他的译本在几十年后依然是很多读者的首选。

He had a very "old-fashioned" way of working: weighing a single word again and again, copying and revising a passage many times. This kind of "slowness" seemed out of date in an age that pursued efficiency, but it was precisely this "outdatedness" that made his translations the first choice of many readers decades later.

傅雷不只是翻译家。他还是美术评论家、音乐鉴赏家，对中西文学、音乐、绘画都有很深的见解。他的“博学”不是炫技，而是那种“真的钻进去了”的深。

Fu Lei was not only a translator. He was also an art critic and a connoisseur of music, with deep insight into Chinese and Western literature, music, and painting. His "erudition" was not a display of technique but the depth of someone who had "truly gone deep into it."

1932年，他和朱梅馥结婚。妻子“既具有东方文化素养，又经西方文化洗礼”，主动承担起为他整理文稿、抄录信件的角色。他们的感情很深——深到后来选择“一起走”。

In 1932 he married Zhu Meifu. His wife, "versed in Eastern culture and tempered by Western culture," took on the role of organizing his manuscripts and copying out his letters. Their bond was deep—deep enough that later they chose to "go together."

二、《傅雷家书》：一个父亲的自白

II. Fu Lei's Family Letters: A Father's Confession

让傅雷在普通读者中最广为人知的，是《傅雷家书》。

What made Fu Lei best known among ordinary readers was Fu Lei's Family Letters.

1954年，他的长子傅聪离家赴波兰留学，此后十几年间，傅雷夫妇给儿子和儿媳写了数百封家信。这些信谈艺术、谈音乐、谈做人、谈感情、谈如何面对孤独。1981年，这些信被编成《傅雷家书》出版，成了中国最著名的家教书之一。

In 1954 his eldest son, Fu Cong, left home to study in Poland, and over the following decade and more, Fu Lei and his wife wrote hundreds of family letters to their son and daughter-in-law. The letters discuss art, music, how to conduct oneself, love, and how to face loneliness. In 1981 they were compiled and published as Fu Lei's Family Letters, which became one of the most famous books of family instruction in China.

书里有一段话，被无数人抄在本子上：

One passage in the book has been copied into notebooks by countless people:

“赤子便是不知道孤独的。赤子孤独了，会创造一个世界，创造许多心灵的朋友！永远保持赤子之心，到老也不会落伍。”

"The innocent heart knows no loneliness. When the innocent heart is lonely, it creates a world, and creates many friends of the spirit! Keep the heart of a child always, and even in old age you will never fall behind."

这句话写在1950年代中期。那时傅雷还不知道，自己将在十年后以怎样惨烈的方式，验证这句“赤子孤独了，会创造一个世界”。

This was written in the mid-1950s. At the time Fu Lei did not yet know in what harrowing manner, ten years later, he would prove the truth of those words: "when the innocent heart is lonely, it creates a world."

三、1958年之后：一个“失声”的人

III. After 1958: A Man Who Lost His Voice

1958年，傅雷被划为“右派”。同年12月，留学波兰的傅聪在海外出逃，去了英国。

In 1958 Fu Lei was labeled a "rightist." In December of that year, Fu Cong, then studying in Poland, fled abroad and went to Britain.

从此，傅雷失去了出版资格，经济来源几乎断绝。一个以文字为生、以翻译为使命的人，被禁止发表任何东西——这几乎等于把他从世界上抹掉。

From then on Fu Lei lost the right to publish, and his means of income was all but cut off. A man who lived by the written word and took translation as his mission was forbidden to publish anything—which was almost the same as erasing him from the world.

他闭门不出。虽然人民文学出版社出于照顾为他预支了稿费，但这仅有的收入来源实在难以支撑。1965年，他还写信向当时的文化部副部长求援。但一年之后，自身难保的对方也无能为力。

He shut himself in and rarely went out. Although the People's Literature Publishing House, out of kindness, advanced him royalties, this sole source of income was hardly enough to live on. In 1965 he even wrote to the then vice minister of culture to ask for help. But a year later the man himself was in no position to help, and could do nothing.

傅雷很清楚自己难逃那一劫。据记载，1966年8月23日，他对妻子说过一句话，大意是：“音乐学院可能要来砸，要砸就让他们来砸，最多大不了两条命。”

Fu Lei knew very well that he could not escape the coming calamity. It is recorded that on August 23, 1966, he said to his wife, in words to this effect: "The conservatory may come to smash things up. If they come to smash, let them. At worst it costs two lives."

四、1966年9月3日

IV. September 3, 1966

1966年8月30日，上海音乐学院的红卫兵联合区房管局，对傅宅进行了抄家。他们在阁楼里发现一只箱子，里面有一面小圆镜和一张旧画报——镜背是蒋介石像，画报上是宋美龄。这两件旧物，被定成了“变天罪证”。

On August 30, 1966, Red Guards from the Shanghai Conservatory of Music, together with the district housing bureau, ransacked the Fu home. In the attic they found a trunk containing a small round mirror and an old pictorial—on the back of the mirror was a portrait of Chiang Kai-shek, and in the pictorial was a photograph of Soong Mei-ling. These two old objects were designated "evidence of a plot to restore the old order."

傅雷夫妇被连续批斗四天三夜，遭受下跪、皮带抽打、当众戴高帽等凌辱。花园被翻，地板被撬，四周贴满大字报。

The Fu couple were struggled against for four days and three nights without letup, subjected to such humiliations as being forced to kneel, being beaten with leather belts, and being made to wear tall dunce caps in public. The garden was dug up, the floorboards were pried open, and big-character posters covered everything around them.

9月2日，傅雷写好了遗书。看过遗书手稿的人说：“遗书那么长，没有改过一个字，一口气写下来。” 遗书里，他委托妻子的兄长处理一切后事，甚至交代了一句：“十一，现钞53.30元，作为我们火葬费。”

On September 2 Fu Lei finished writing his suicide note. Someone who saw the manuscript said: "The note was so long, and not a single word was changed—it was written straight through in one breath." In the note he entrusted the handling of all his affairs after death to his wife's elder brother, and even set down this line: "Eleven: 53.30 yuan in cash, as our cremation fee."

9月3日凌晨，夫妇俩用浦东土布被单撕成长条，拧绳打结，系在卧室落地钢窗的铁框上，又在地板上铺好棉被——为的是防止凳子倒下时发出的声响惊动邻里。

In the early hours of September 3, the couple tore a native Pudong cotton bed sheet into long strips, twisted them into a cord and knotted it, and tied it to the iron frame of the floor-to-ceiling steel window in the bedroom; they also spread a quilt on the floor—so that the sound of the stool falling would not alert the neighbors.

第二天，保姆发现了他们。

The next day, their housekeeper found them.

关于傅雷的死因，早期流传“服毒”之说（来自保姆在极度紧张下的表述）。后来研究者查阅了上海公安部门的死亡档案，案别一栏写的是“上吊自杀”。这一档案更正了此前的说法。

As for the cause of Fu Lei's death, an early account circulated that he had "taken poison" (based on the housekeeper's statement made under extreme strain). Later researchers examined the death records of the Shanghai public security authorities, where the entry under "type of case" read "suicide by hanging." These records corrected the earlier account.

他们死后，骨灰一度无人敢认领。最后，是一位热爱傅雷译文的文学女青年，冒着风险以“干女儿”的身份，把骨灰领了出来，寄存时写的是傅雷的字号“傅怒安”。

After their deaths, for a time no one dared claim the ashes. In the end, a young woman of letters who loved Fu Lei's translations took the risk of claiming them as their "goddaughter," and left them in storage under Fu Lei's courtesy name, "Fu Nu'an."

1979年4月，上海市文联和中国作协上海分会为傅雷夫妇举行追悼会，柯灵致悼词，宣布1958年划其为“右派”是错误的，予以改正；“文革”中所受诬陷迫害一律平反。

In April 1979, the Shanghai Federation of Literary and Art Circles and the Shanghai branch of the Chinese Writers Association held a memorial meeting for the Fu couple. Ke Ling delivered the eulogy, announcing that the 1958 designation of Fu Lei as a "rightist" had been wrong and was being corrected, and that all the false charges and persecution they had suffered during the "Cultural Revolution" were fully rehabilitated.

五、致2026：关于“标准”和“体面”

V. To 2026: On "Standard" and "Decency"

2026年，我们讨论“躺平”与“内卷”，讨论如何在系统里活得更舒服一点。傅雷这个人，其实一点都不“舒服”——他太刚、太认真、太不肯妥协。

In 2026 we discuss "lying flat" and "involution," and how to live a little more comfortably within the system. Fu Lei, as a person, was not the least bit "comfortable"—he was too upright, too earnest, too unwilling to compromise.

但正因如此，他留下的东西才格外有分量。

But precisely for that reason, what he left behind carries a special weight.

他一生守的是“标准”。翻译要准、文字要美、做人要有骨气。《傅雷家书》里那些谈艺术的话，谈的都是这个：做一件事，要做到自己挑不出毛病。

What he defended all his life was "standard." Translation must be accurate, writing must be beautiful, and a person must have backbone. The passages on art in Fu Lei's Family Letters are all about this: whatever you do, do it so that you yourself can find no fault with it.

他最后的选择，也是一种“标准”。遗书里不控诉、不抱怨，只把每一笔账、每一件后事交代清楚，连53.30元的火葬费都写得明明白白。一个人在生命最后一刻，仍然不肯潦草。

His final choice, too, was a kind of "standard." The suicide note contains no accusation and no complaint; it simply sets out every account and every after-death matter clearly, down to the 53.30 yuan cremation fee written out in plain detail. To the very last moment of his life, a man refused to be slipshod.

另外值得一提的是，傅雷夫妇留下的《傅雷家书》，后来成了中国最著名的家教书之一。他教儿子的，不只是音乐和艺术，还有怎么面对孤独、怎么在困境里保持体面。书里那句“赤子孤独了，会创造一个世界”，如今被无数人当作座右铭——而写下这句话的人，最终没能为自己创造出一个足够安全的世界。这大概是这本书最沉重、也最动人的地方。

It is also worth noting that Fu Lei's Family Letters, which the couple left behind, later became one of the most famous books of family instruction in China. What he taught his son was not only music and art, but how to face loneliness and how to keep one's dignity in hardship. The line from the book—"when the innocent heart is lonely, it creates a world"—is today taken as a motto by countless people. Yet the man who wrote those words was ultimately unable to create a world safe enough for himself. This is perhaps the heaviest and most moving thing about the book.

对我们这代人来说，傅雷的故事当然不是要我们去效仿他的结局——恰恰相反，它提醒我们：要让这个社会，不再把人逼到必须以生命来维护尊严的地步。

For our generation, Fu Lei's story is certainly not meant to have us imitate his end—quite the opposite: it reminds us to make this society one that no longer drives people to the point where they must defend their dignity with their lives.

但他那种“认真到最后一刻”的姿态，值得记住。

But his bearing—"earnest to the very last moment"—deserves to be remembered.
