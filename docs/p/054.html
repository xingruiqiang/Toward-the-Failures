柳井正：接手父亲的西装店，把店员都“气跑”的接班人

Tadashi Yanai: The Successor Who Took Over His Father's Suit Shop and Drove All the Clerks Away

——“你挑战十次新事物，必定会有九次失败”

— "If you try ten new things, nine of them are bound to fail"

← 返回主页 Home第三辑　在废墟上重建的人54 / 100约 3204 字 · 3204 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，很多年轻人正在面对一个尴尬的处境：接手一份家族生意、一家小公司、一个“老摊子”，想改，却一改就出乱子；不改，又眼看着它一天天衰败下去。

In 2026, many young people face an awkward predicament: they take over a family business, a small company, an "old stall," and want to change it—yet the moment they do, everything falls into chaos; if they don't, they watch it wither day by day.

有一个人，23岁那年回老家接手父亲的小西装店。他一上任就大改流程，结果六名老员工跑得只剩一个。这家店几乎要被他和那个唯一的店员一起干垮。

There was a man who, at 23, went back to his hometown to take over his father's small suit shop. The moment he took charge he overhauled the whole operation, and of the six veteran employees, all but one quit. The shop nearly went under, with only him and that lone clerk left to run it.

他叫柳井正。后来，这家小西装店变成了优衣库，他成了日本首富。他把自己的经营哲学总结成四个字：一胜九败。

His name was Tadashi Yanai. Later, that little suit shop became Uniqlo, and he became the richest man in Japan. He summed up his business philosophy in four words: one win, nine losses.

一、那个“不想接班”的年轻人

I. The Young Man Who "Didn't Want to Take Over"

1949年，柳井正出生在日本山口县。他的家族里很多人做服装生意，父亲柳井等也在1949年开了一家男装店“小郡商事”，主营西装，客户多是希望穿得体面的银行、证券从业者。

In 1949, Tadashi Yanai was born in Yamaguchi Prefecture, Japan. Many in his family were in the clothing business, and his father, Hitoshi Yanai, opened a menswear shop called Ogori Shōji in 1949, dealing mainly in suits; its customers were mostly bankers and stockbrokers eager to dress well.

父亲脾气严厉，从小对他要求极高，口头禅是“要当第一名，当什么都行”。柳井正印象中，只有考上高中和大学时，父亲夸过他。他后来在书里写：“当时我觉得爸爸只会骂我，但现在想起来，那可能是在激励我。”

His father had a stern temper and demanded a great deal of him from childhood; the catchphrase was "Be number one, at whatever you do." In Yanai's memory, the only times his father praised him were when he got into high school and into university. He later wrote in a book: "At the time I felt my father did nothing but scold me, but looking back now, that may have been his way of spurring me on."

他考上早稻田大学政治经济系，去了东京。但大学四年他过得浑浑噩噩，沉迷打麻将和玩游戏。毕业后，父亲托关系让他进了百货公司佳世客（JUSCO，现永旺集团）当理货员。他只干了九个月就辞职了——因为觉得找不到方向和目标。

He got into the Department of Political Science and Economics at Waseda University and went to Tokyo. But he drifted through his four college years, absorbed in mahjong and video games. After graduating, his father used connections to get him a job as a stock clerk at the department-store company JUSCO (now the AEON Group). He quit after only nine months—because he felt he could find neither direction nor purpose.

1972年，他23岁，遵父命回老家结婚，并接手家业。一个“玩心未退”的年轻人，就这样被推上了一家小店的经营位子。

In 1972, at 23, he returned home at his father's bidding to marry and take over the family business. And so a young man still "not done playing" was pushed into the operator's seat of a small shop.

二、一改就散：六个人只剩一个

II. Reform and Collapse: Six People Down to One

接手以后，柳井正发现这家店的问题很多：商品陈列、流程效率都太差，资金周转也太慢。

After taking over, Yanai found the shop riddled with problems: the merchandising and the efficiency of its processes were both poor, and cash turned over far too slowly.

年轻气盛的他，开始用自己的方式大刀阔斧地改革——引进大企业的现代管理制度，还试图从只卖西装、领带，扩展到休闲服饰。

Young and full of fire, he set about reforming it drastically in his own way—importing the modern management systems of big corporations, and trying to expand beyond suits and ties into casual wear.

结果，老员工完全无法接受，纷纷辞职。六名员工，最后只剩一个人留下来。

The result: the veteran employees could not accept it at all and quit one after another. Of the six employees, only one stayed.

从进货、整理库存、销售到打扫，所有事都由他和这唯一的店员包办，每天都忙得脚不沾地。生意一度陷入困境，他也深受打击。

From buying stock and organizing inventory to selling and sweeping the floor, everything fell to him and that single remaining clerk; every day they were run off their feet. The business slid into trouble, and it hit him hard.

但父亲并没有责怪他，而是把账本都交给了他。这一刻，25岁的柳井正决定：豁出去，一定要把这门生意做好。

But his father did not blame him; instead he handed over all the account books. At that moment, the 25-year-old Yanai resolved: throw everything into it, and make this business succeed no matter what.

他后来说，那段最忙碌的日子，反而成了他日后成功的最大关键。

He would later say that those busiest days turned out to be the single greatest key to his later success.

三、1984年，广岛的第一家店

III. 1984: The First Store, in Hiroshima

在经营中，柳井正发现了一个机会。

In the course of running the business, Yanai spotted an opportunity.

父亲原来的生意针对的是“穿着体面”的银行、证券人士，价格昂贵。他想：普通年轻人也想穿得体面，可是买不起。市面上只要带一点设计感，价格就上去了，让年轻人望而却步。

His father's original business had catered to bankers and stockbrokers who wanted to "dress respectably," and its prices were high. He thought: ordinary young people also want to dress well, but they can't afford it. In the market, anything with even a touch of design sense carried a higher price, putting it out of reach of the young.

他特意去美国考察，对“自助式休闲服超市”的形态印象深刻——顾客可以自己挑、自己试，像逛超市一样。他心里理想的店铺模型越来越清晰：“一家随时可以挑选衣服的巨大仓库。”这也就是店名“Unique Clothing Warehouse”的由来。

He made a point of going to the United States to study the market, and was struck by the format of the "self-service casual-wear supermarket"—customers pick and try on clothes themselves, like browsing a supermarket. The ideal store model in his mind grew clearer and clearer: "a giant warehouse where you can pick out clothes anytime." This is also the origin of the store name "Unique Clothing Warehouse."

1984年6月2日，日本广岛，第一家优衣库开业。当时它还不叫优衣库，而是“Unique Clothing Warehouse”，简写作“UNI-CLO”。

On June 2, 1984, in Hiroshima, Japan, the first Uniqlo store opened. At the time it was not yet called Uniqlo but "Unique Clothing Warehouse," abbreviated as "UNI-CLO."

那一年，日本还处在泡沫经济的繁荣中，人人乐于消费，柳井正却断然选择做平价休闲服。他把价格定在1000到1900日元之间，靠自助购物、低价良品吸引顾客。开店首日门庭若市，商品迅速售罄，甚至不得不对客流加以限制。因为人太多，他在接受当地电台采访时居然说：“非常抱歉，现在来排队可能进不了店里，所以请大家不要来。”

That year Japan was still in the boom of the bubble economy, everyone eager to spend, yet Yanai resolutely chose to make inexpensive casual wear. He set prices between 1,000 and 1,900 yen, drawing customers with self-service shopping and good goods at low prices. On the first day the store was packed, the merchandise sold out quickly, and they even had to limit the crowds. Because there were so many people, he actually said in an interview with a local radio station: "I'm very sorry—if you come and line up now you may not get in, so please don't come."

四、一个“香港的错字”

IV. A "Typo from Hong Kong"

关于“UNIQLO”这个名字，有一个很有意思的插曲。

There is a rather interesting episode behind the name "UNIQLO."

1988年，公司在香港设立合资公司，负责注册的文员手误，把“UNI-CLO”写成了“UNI-QLO”。柳井正不但没有纠正，反而觉得“Q”这个字母的视觉效果更好，于是将错就错——回到日本后，把所有店名都改成了“UNIQLO”。

In 1988, the company set up a joint venture in Hong Kong, and the clerk handling the registration misspelled "UNI-CLO" as "UNI-QLO." Rather than correct it, Yanai thought the letter "Q" looked better visually, so he let the mistake stand—and back in Japan he changed all the store names to "UNIQLO."

一个错别字，成了今天全球最知名的服装品牌之一。

A single typo became one of the most famous clothing brands in the world today.

五、一胜九败：他为什么不怕失败

V. One Win, Nine Losses: Why He Isn't Afraid to Fail

优衣库并不是一路顺风。恰恰相反，柳井正把它写成了一本书的书名——《一胜九败》（2003年出版）。

Uniqlo's path was far from smooth. On the contrary, Yanai turned it into the title of a book—One Win, Nine Losses (published in 2003).

他说：“你挑战十次新事物，必定会有九次失败。”“重点在于尝试，错了也没关系，错九次，就有九次经验。”

He said: "If you try ten new things, nine of them are bound to fail." "What matters is trying. It's fine to be wrong—fail nine times, and you gain nine lessons."

这句话不是空谈，而是他真实交过学费的结果：

This line was not empty talk; it was the result of tuition he had genuinely paid:

1994年，优衣库在纽约设子公司，想“在美国收集时尚信息、在日本设计、委托工厂加工”，结果美日信息沟通不畅，北美市场全军覆没，苦撑三年后子公司解散。

1994: Uniqlo set up a subsidiary in New York, hoping to "gather fashion information in America, design in Japan, and have factories do the manufacturing on contract." But communication between the U.S. and Japan was poor, the North American market was a complete rout, and after three hard years the subsidiary was dissolved.

1996年，公司收购东京一家童装公司85%的股权，很快遭遇商标侵权诉讼，八个月后该公司进入破产清算。

1996: The company acquired 85 percent of a Tokyo children's-wear firm, soon ran into a trademark-infringement lawsuit, and eight months later the firm entered bankruptcy liquidation.

1997年，尝试推出运动款、家庭款子品牌，与优衣库主品牌定位区隔不清，开了三十多家店后，柳井正迅速决定关店。

1997: It tried launching sports and family sub-brands, whose positioning blurred with the Uniqlo main brand; after opening more than thirty stores, Yanai quickly decided to close them.

2003年，英国店铺扩张到21家时一直亏损，最终关掉16家。

2003: The British stores kept losing money as they expanded to 21, and in the end 16 were closed.

2001年优衣库新商品开发乏力，销售再次下滑——那是公司上市后的第一次减收减益。

2001: New-product development at Uniqlo faltered and sales slid again—the company's first decline in both revenue and profit since going public.

2015年，一次提价策略失误，净利暴跌56%，他主导的“2020年做到世界第一”的计划宣告失败。

2015: A misjudged price increase sent net profit plunging 56 percent, and the "be number one in the world by 2020" plan he had championed was declared a failure.

但每一次，他都做出了同一个选择：及时止损，然后继续尝试。

But every time, he made the same choice: cut the losses in time, then keep trying.

1998年，是优衣库真正的转折点。那年东京原宿店开张，“优衣库的摇粒绒衫，1900日元”的广告铺满街头——这个价格约相当于日本工薪阶层日薪的十分之一。开业当天，顾客排起长队。这一年，优衣库销售额首次突破1000亿日元。2000年秋冬季，优衣库一口气推出51种颜色的摇粒绒衫，原定销售1200万件，最终卖出2600万件。

1998 was Uniqlo's true turning point. That year the Harajuku store in Tokyo opened, and ads for "Uniqlo fleece, 1,900 yen" covered the streets—a price roughly one-tenth of a Japanese salaried worker's daily wage. On opening day, customers lined up in long queues. That year, Uniqlo's sales topped 100 billion yen for the first time. In the fall-winter of 2000, Uniqlo rolled out fleece in 51 colors at once, originally planning to sell 12 million pieces—and ended up selling 26 million.

同年（2000年），公司销售额突破1000亿日元的目标达成，门店增加到368家。

That same year (2000), the company reached its goal of surpassing 100 billion yen in sales, and its stores grew to 368.

而柳井正最看重的一个对手，是他的父亲。1999年，优衣库母公司迅销在东京证券交易所主板上市。上市消息告诉父亲的第五天，父亲去世了——那天正好是柳井正50岁生日。葬礼上，他第一次在众人面前放声大哭，说：“父亲是我一生中最大的竞争对手。”

And the rival Yanai valued most was his own father. In 1999, Fast Retailing, Uniqlo's parent company, listed on the first section of the Tokyo Stock Exchange. Five days after he told his father the news of the listing, his father died—on the very day of Yanai's fiftieth birthday. At the funeral, he wept aloud in front of a crowd for the first time and said: "My father was the greatest competitor of my life."

六、致2026：把“失败”变成日常口粮

VI. To 2026: Turning "Failure" into Daily Rations

2026年，我们这个时代对“失败”仍然不够宽容，尤其是对“接手老摊子”的年轻人。改，可能被说成“瞎折腾”；不改，被说成“啃老没本事”。

In 2026, our era is still not tolerant enough of "failure," especially for young people who "take over the old stall." Change things, and you may be called a reckless meddler; don't change them, and you're called a sponger with no skills.

柳井正提供的是一种很有韧性的思路：

Yanai offers a remarkably resilient way of thinking:

不要指望一出手就赢。要做好“十次里输九次”的准备，然后确保每一次输了都还能站起来。

Don't expect to win on your first move. Be prepared to "lose nine times out of ten," and then make sure that after each loss you can still stand back up.

他的“一胜九败”里，最关键的不是“胜”，而是那个“九”——九次失败是被提前算进成本里的。 当你把失败当成日常口粮，而不是需要遮掩的耻辱，你才不会在第一次挫折时就把自己判死。

In his "one win, nine losses," the crucial part is not the "win" but the "nine"—the nine failures are built into the cost in advance. When you treat failure as daily rations rather than a shame to be hidden, you won't sentence yourself to death at the first setback.

对我们这代人来说，这大概是一种更务实、也更有用的乐观：不是相信“我一定会成功”，而是相信“我失败得起”。

For our generation, this is probably a more pragmatic and more useful kind of optimism: not the belief that "I will certainly succeed," but the belief that "I can afford to fail."
