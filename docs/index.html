About this book

This is a book about one hundred people who, judged by the standards of their own time, lost. Socrates was voted to death. Confucius was mocked as a “stray dog.” Copernicus did not dare publish until he was dying. Van Gogh sold a single painting. Turing was chemically castrated. Zhang Zhixin was executed for refusing to take back a sentence she believed.

They were misread, ridiculed, exiled — some of them killed. And what they left behind has outlived the age that misjudged them.

Written in Chinese by Raymond Xing in 2026, the book gathers 100 short lives into five parts. This online edition presents every chapter in a paragraph-by-paragraph bilingual format — one paragraph of Chinese, then its English translation — so readers anywhere can move through the text in either language, or both at once. Each chapter ends with Sources & Notes, which sorts what is documented fact from what is legend or still disputed.
