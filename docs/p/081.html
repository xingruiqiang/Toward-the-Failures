八大山人：明朝的皇孙，画了一辈子翻白眼的鸟

Bada Shanren: A Ming Imperial Grandson Who Painted Rolled-Eye Birds All His Life

——“墨点无多泪点多，山河仍是旧山河。”

—"Not many drops of ink, but many drops of tears; the hills and rivers remain the old hills and rivers."

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂81 / 100约 3061 字 · 3061 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，一只“翻白眼的鸟”成了网络热梗。很多人不知道，这个形象的源头，是一个三百多年前的明朝落魄皇孙。

In 2026, a "rolled-eye bird" became an internet meme. Few people know that the source of this image is a down-and-out Ming imperial grandson from more than three hundred years ago.

他出身皇族，十九岁时国破家亡。他出家为僧，又还俗入道，装疯卖哑半辈子。他画的鸟，永远是一只脚站着、缩着脖子、眼睛上翻，一副“谁都别理我”的样子。他落款时把“八大山人”四个字竖着连写，看上去像一个“哭之”，又像一个“笑之”。

He was born into the imperial clan, and at nineteen saw his country overthrown and his family destroyed. He became a Buddhist monk, then returned to lay life and entered Daoism, feigning madness and muteness for half his lifetime. The birds he painted always stand on one leg, neck drawn in, eyes rolled upward, wearing an air of "leave me alone." When he signed his name, he wrote the four characters "八大山人" vertically and joined together, so that they look like a "哭之" (weeping for it) and also like a "笑之" (laughing at it).

他叫朱耷。他活了将近八十岁，是中国写意画的一座高峰。

His name was Zhu Da. He lived to nearly eighty, and stands as one of the great peaks of Chinese freehand painting (xieyi).

一、宁王的后代

I. Descendant of the Prince of Ning

1626年，朱耷出生在江西南昌。他的家族，是明朝宗室里极特殊的一支——他是宁王朱权的九世孙。而朱权，是明太祖朱元璋的儿子，一个博学多才、精通音律的王爷。

In 1626, Zhu Da was born in Nanchang, Jiangxi. His family was an unusual branch of the Ming imperial clan—he was a ninth-generation descendant of Zhu Quan, the Prince of Ning. And Zhu Quan was a son of Zhu Yuanzhang, founding emperor of the Ming, a prince of wide learning and deep mastery of music.

所以，朱耷身上流着的是正正经经的明朝皇族血。而这个家族的文化基因也极强：他的祖父是画家兼诗人，父亲擅长山水花鸟，叔父也是画家。朱耷从小就在书画里长大。

So it was true imperial Ming blood that ran in Zhu Da's veins. The family's cultural inheritance was powerful as well: his grandfather was both painter and poet, his father excelled at landscape and bird-and-flower painting, and his uncle was also a painter. Zhu Da grew up surrounded by brush and ink.

史书上说他八岁就能作诗，十一岁就能画山水，还能写一手漂亮的米芾体小楷。十几岁时，他考中了秀才。

The histories say he could compose poetry at eight, paint landscapes at eleven, and write a beautiful hand of small regular script in the style of Mi Fu. In his teens he earned the degree of xiucai in the imperial examinations.

放在太平年月，这应该是一个贵族少年顺理成章的人生：读书、科考、做官、光耀门楣。

In peacetime, this would have been the natural course of a noble youth's life: study, sit the examinations, take office, and bring glory to the family name.

但1644年来了。

But 1644 came.

二、十九岁那年的崩塌

II. The Collapse in His Nineteenth Year

1644年，李自成攻破北京，崇祯皇帝在煤山自缢。随后清军入关。延续了二百七十多年的大明王朝，一夜之间终结。

In 1644, Li Zicheng broke into Beijing, and the Chongzhen Emperor hanged himself on Coal Hill. Qing troops then crossed the passes. The Ming dynasty, which had lasted more than two hundred and seventy years, ended overnight.

那一年，朱耷十九岁。

That year, Zhu Da was nineteen.

对任何一个读书人来说，改朝换代都是一场精神上的重创。但对朱耷，这多了一层更沉重的含义：他姓朱。他不是“某个朝代的普通百姓”，他是那个已经死去的王朝的直系后人。

For any man of letters, a change of dynasty was a spiritual catastrophe. But for Zhu Da it carried an even heavier meaning: his surname was Zhu. He was not "an ordinary subject of some dynasty"; he was a direct descendant of the dynasty that had just died.

明亡的第二年，他的父亲去世。国家没了，家也没了，那条“读书做官”的路，彻底断了。

The year after the Ming fell, his father died. The state was gone, the family was gone, and that road of "study and take office" was cut off for good.

1648年，二十二岁的朱耷剃发为僧，法名传綮，字刃庵。他在山里一住就是几十年。对一个前朝皇族来说，出家不只是信仰的选择，更是保命的选择。

In 1648, at twenty-two, Zhu Da shaved his head and became a monk, taking the dharma name Chuanqi and the style name Ren'an. He lived in the mountains for decades on end. For a former member of the imperial clan, becoming a monk was not only a matter of faith—it was a way to stay alive.

三、忽笑忽哭的那些年

III. The Years of Sudden Laughter and Sudden Tears

朱耷身上有所谓的“癫疾”——一种间歇性发作的精神症状。

Zhu Da had what was called a "dianji"—a kind of intermittent mental disturbance.

1679年，他应临川知县胡亦堂的邀请，在临川住了将近一年。那段时间，他的状态极不稳定：有时终日不与人说话，只点头作答；有时突然发狂大笑，有时又整日痛哭。有一天夜里，他甚至扯破自己的僧袍，把它烧掉了。

In 1679, at the invitation of Hu Yitang, the magistrate of Linchuan, he stayed in Linchuan for nearly a year. During that time his condition was extremely unstable: sometimes he would go whole days without speaking to anyone, answering only with a nod; sometimes he would suddenly burst into wild laughter, and sometimes weep all day long. One night he even tore his own monk's robe to pieces and burned it.

1680年初春，他一个人从临川徒步走回南昌——两地相距一百二十多里。回到南昌后，他每天在街上晃荡，戴着布帽，穿着长袍，脚上是一双露出脚后跟的破鞋。街上的孩子跟在他身后起哄，他也不在意。

In the early spring of 1680, he walked back to Nanchang alone from Linchuan—the two places more than a hundred and twenty li apart. After returning to Nanchang he drifted through the streets every day, wearing a cloth cap and a long robe, his feet in a pair of broken shoes worn through at the heels. The children on the street followed behind him, hooting, and he paid them no mind.

一个曾经的皇族子弟，变成了街市上的一个怪人。

A one-time scion of the imperial clan had become an oddity of the marketplace.

后来，他的一个侄儿认出了他，把他接回家住了两三年，他的状态才慢慢恢复。

Later, a nephew recognized him and took him home to live for two or three years, and only then did his condition slowly recover.

这里需要说清楚一件事：朱耷的“疯”与“哑”，很可能既有装的部分，也有真的部分。他的家族里确实有精神方面的遗传（他的祖父性格狂狷，父亲是聋哑人）。国破家亡的悲愤，加上真实的病患，两者纠缠在一起，构成了他一生那种“半疯半醒”的状态。

One thing needs to be made clear here: Zhu Da's "madness" and "muteness" very likely were part feigned, part genuine. There was indeed a strain of mental illness in his family (his grandfather was wild and unruly in temperament; his father was deaf and mute). The grief and fury of a fallen state and a broken family, together with a real illness, were tangled up with each other, and together they made up that "half-mad, half-lucid" state of his whole life.

四、八大山人

IV. Bada Shanren

1684年，五十八岁的朱耷，开始使用一个新的别号：八大山人。

In 1684, at fifty-eight, Zhu Da began to use a new sobriquet: Bada Shanren.

这个名号一直用到他去世。他在画上落款时，总是把“八大山人”四个字竖着连着写，写出来有时像“哭之”，有时像“笑之”。“哭之笑之”——故国不再，哭笑不得，这是他一生的注脚。

He went on using this name until his death. When he signed his paintings, he always wrote the four characters "八大山人" vertically and joined together, so that they sometimes look like a "哭之" (weeping for it) and sometimes like a "笑之" (laughing at it). "Weeping for it, laughing at it"—the old country gone, unable to weep or laugh, this was the footnote to his whole life.

他后来还俗，在南昌一带建了一处道院（青云谱），晚年又在北兰寺、寤歌草堂居住，靠卖画为生。他画画很不“商业”：兴之所至就画，画完随手就送人，拿到画的人往往也不知道这些画有多值钱。有朋友看不过去，就发动大家付钱买他的画，好让他能维持生计。

He later returned to lay life, and in the Nanchang area built a Daoist temple (the Qingyun Pu). In his last years he lived at the Beilan Temple and the Wuge Thatched Cottage, supporting himself by selling paintings. He painted in a very uncommercial way: when the mood came he painted, and when he was done he gave the work away on the spot, and those who received the paintings often had no idea how valuable they were. A friend who could not bear to watch organized others to pay him for his paintings, so that he could keep body and soul together.

五、那些翻白眼的鸟

V. Those Rolled-Eye Birds

八大山人最著名的，就是他笔下的动物。

Bada Shanren is most famous for the animals beneath his brush.

《孤禽图》里，一张大白纸上，只有一只黑色的鸟，缩着脖子、弓着背、单脚站着，眼睛上翻，一股冷漠倔强的气，从画里直冲出来。

In "Solitary Fowl," a great sheet of white paper holds only a single black bird—neck drawn in, back hunched, standing on one leg, eyes rolled upward—and a cold, stubborn air rushes straight out of the painting.

《眠鸭图》里，一只黑色的鸭子闭着眼睛趴着，一副“世事与我无关”的样子。

In "Sleeping Duck," a black duck lies with its eyes shut, wearing an air of "the world has nothing to do with me."

《杨柳浴禽图》里，一只乌鸦站在横斜的老树干上，仰头侧立，倔强得让人心疼。

In "Willow and Bathing Bird," a crow stands on a slanting old tree trunk, head tilted back, turned sideways, so stubborn it makes your heart ache.

这些鸟、鱼、鸭、猫，几乎都有一个共同点：不看你。或者低头，或者翻白眼，或者背对着你。 三百多年来，人们对这些画做了无数种解读。但谁也否认不了一件事：那是朱耷自己。

These birds, fish, ducks, cats almost all share one trait: they do not look at you. They lower their heads, or roll their eyes, or turn their backs. For more than three hundred years people have produced countless readings of these paintings. But no one can deny one thing: that was Zhu Da himself.

他就是那只鸟。国没了，家没了，理想没了。他不肯低头，就只能翻着白眼，与整个世界保持距离。

He was that bird. The state gone, the family gone, the ideals gone. Unwilling to bow his head, he could only roll his eyes and keep his distance from the whole world.

他也画山水，但很少画完整性，多画的是“残山剩水”、老树枯枝。那是一种荒寒萧疏的境地，一看就是亡国之人眼里的山河。

He painted landscapes too, but seldom complete ones; mostly he painted "broken hills and leftover waters," old trees and withered branches. It was a bleak, desolate realm—at a glance, the landscape seen through the eyes of a man whose country had fallen.

一处容易被忽略的细节

A Detail Easily Overlooked

他的画上常有一种奇怪的“签押”，形状像一个乌龟。后人研究后发现，那是“三月十九日”几个字的变形——三月十九日，正是崇祯皇帝自缢的日子。

On his paintings there is often a strange "signature cipher," shaped like a turtle. Later scholars, studying it, found that it was a distortion of the characters "三月十九日" (the nineteenth day of the third month)—and the nineteenth day of the third month was exactly the day the Chongzhen Emperor hanged himself.

他把亡国之痛，藏进了一个几乎没人看得懂的符号里，画了一辈子。

He hid the pain of a fallen country inside a symbol almost no one could read, and painted it for the rest of his life.

六、写给2026年的我们

VI. To Us in 2026

八大山人的一生，是一个“旧世界碎了，我该怎么办”的故事。

Bada Shanren's life is the story of "the old world has shattered—what am I to do?"

今天的人，也会遇到类似的时刻：一个行业突然没了，一份干了十几年的工作突然不需要了，一套自己信奉的秩序突然失效了。旧地图作废，新地图还没画出来。

People today meet similar moments too: an industry suddenly vanishes, a job you did for over a decade is suddenly no longer needed, a set of rules you believed in suddenly stops working. The old map is void, and the new map has not yet been drawn.

八大山人的答案是：既然不能在旧世界里活下去，那就用剩下的一生，创造一个新世界。 他没有去反清复明（他做不到），也没有就此烂掉（他没有）。他把所有的痛、所有的怨、所有的不甘，全部变成了纸上的笔墨。

Bada Shanren's answer was: since I cannot live on in the old world, then I will use the life left to me to create a new one. He did not go off to fight the Qing and restore the Ming (he could not), nor did he rot away on the spot (he did not). He turned all his pain, all his resentment, all his unwillingness, into ink and brush on paper.

他用一支笔，给自己造了一个谁也无法入侵的世界。 在这个世界里，他可以翻白眼，可以缩着脖子，可以单脚站着，不看任何人。

With a single brush he built himself a world no one could invade. In that world he could roll his eyes, draw in his neck, stand on one leg, and look at no one.

三百多年后，那只翻着白眼的鸟，成了中国绘画史上最贵的符号之一。而那个当年在街上被孩子嘲笑的怪老头，成了“中国古代十大文化名人”之一。

More than three hundred years later, that rolled-eye bird has become one of the most expensive symbols in the history of Chinese painting. And that odd old man, once mocked by children in the street, has become one of the "Ten Great Cultural Figures of Ancient China."

历史有时候很慢，但它从不缺席。

History is sometimes slow, but it never fails to show up.

还有一点值得说：八大山人并不是一个“躺平”的人。他一直在画，画了几十年，画到七十多岁还“登山如飞”。他的朋友石涛写信给他，说听说他七十四五岁了还能健步登山，真是神仙一样的人。一个背负着亡国之痛的人，没有选择沉沦，而是选择把余生全部投进一件事里——这件事本身，就是一种了不起的生命力。

One more thing worth saying: Bada Shanren was not a man who "lay flat." He painted all along, for decades, and even in his seventies could still "climb mountains as if flying." His friend Shitao wrote him a letter saying he had heard that at seventy-four or seventy-five he could still stride up mountains, truly a man like an immortal. A man carrying the grief of a fallen country did not choose to sink, but chose to pour the rest of his life into one thing—and that in itself is a remarkable vitality.

他没有能力恢复故国，但他有能力让“朱耷”这个名字，以另一种方式，比那个王朝活得更久。

He had no power to restore his old country, but he had the power to make the name "Zhu Da," in another form, outlive that dynasty.

今天，他的一幅画可以拍出天价，被各大博物馆奉为镇馆之宝。但在他的年代，他只是一个在街上被孩子嘲笑的怪老头，靠朋友张罗着卖画糊口。他把一生所有的委屈、不甘和孤傲，都画进了那些翻着白眼的鸟里。那些鸟替他说了三百多年的话，而说到今天，还没说完。

Today one of his paintings can fetch a sky-high price and is treasured by major museums as a prize of their collections. But in his own day he was only an odd old man mocked by children in the street, scraping a living by having friends arrange sales of his paintings. He painted all the grievance, unwillingness, and proud solitude of a lifetime into those rolled-eye birds. Those birds have spoken for him for more than three hundred years, and they are still not finished speaking today.

史料来源与辨析 · Sources & Notes

史料来源与辨析

Sources and Notes

1. 生平基础事实：八大山人，本名朱耷，1626年生于南昌，约1705年卒，年约79—80岁。明宁王朱权九世孙。为通行记载。辨析：其生卒年各资料略有出入（有1626—1705、1626—约1705等），本文用“约”。

1. Basic biographical facts: Bada Shanren, original name Zhu Da, born in 1626 in Nanchang, died about 1705, aged about 79–80. A ninth-generation descendant of Zhu Quan, Prince of Ning of the Ming. This is the standard account. Note: sources differ slightly on his dates of birth and death (1626–1705, 1626–c. 1705, and so on); this essay uses "about."

2. 明亡时间与其年龄：1644年明亡，朱耷19岁。为通行记载。

2. The fall of the Ming and his age: the Ming fell in 1644, and Zhu Da was 19. This is the standard account.

3. 1648年出家：22岁剃发为僧，法名传綮。为通行记载。

3. Becoming a monk in 1648: at 22 he shaved his head and became a monk, with the dharma name Chuanqi. This is the standard account.

4. “八大山人”名号：1684年（58岁）前后开始使用。为通行记载。

4. The name "Bada Shanren": he began to use it around 1684 (aged 58). This is the standard account.

5. “哭之笑之”：落款连写形似“哭之”“笑之”，为后人的读法，其本人是否刻意如此设计，无法确证，本文表述为“看上去像”。

5. "Weeping for it, laughing at it": that the joined signature resembles "哭之" and "笑之" is a later reading; whether he deliberately designed it so cannot be confirmed, and this essay states it as "looks like."

6. 癫疾与装疯：其家族有精神方面的记载（祖父狂狷、父亲聋哑），他本人确有间歇发作的“癫疾”；同时其“佯狂”也带有自我保护的成分。本文采取“真病与装疯并存”的表述，避免简单地把他的一切异常都归结为“装疯”或“真疯”。

6. The madness illness and the feigned madness: there are records of mental illness in his family (a wild and unruly grandfather, a deaf-mute father), and he himself did suffer from an intermittent "dianji"; at the same time his "feigned madness" also had an element of self-protection. This essay adopts the formulation that "real illness and feigned madness coexisted," avoiding the simple reduction of all his abnormalities to either "feigned madness" or "true madness."

7. 青云谱的归属：青云谱道院为南昌著名道观，与八大山人关系密切，但“他亲手创建”一说学界有讨论（一说由其弟或以他名义所建）。本文采用“在南昌一带建有一处道院”的谨慎表述。

7. The attribution of the Qingyun Pu: the Qingyun Pu Daoist temple is a famous temple in Nanchang and closely associated with Bada Shanren, but the claim that "he founded it with his own hands" is debated in scholarly circles (one view holds that it was built by his younger brother or in his name). This essay adopts the cautious formulation "built a Daoist temple in the Nanchang area."

8. “三月十九日”签押：这是学者的研究结论（认为画押由“三月十九日”变形而来），有一定证据支持，但并非铁证，本文表述为“后人研究发现”。

8. The "nineteenth day of the third month" cipher: this is a scholarly conclusion (holding that the cipher derives from "三月十九日"), supported by a certain amount of evidence but not conclusive proof; this essay states it as "later scholars, studying it, found."

9. 四僧之说：与石涛、弘仁、髡残并称“清初四僧”，为后世通行的分类。2004年（一说1985年）入选“中国古代十大文化名人”一类说法，各版本不一，本文不具体坐实年份。

9. The theory of the Four Monks: he is classed with Shitao, Hongren, and Kuncan as the "Four Monks of the Early Qing," a classification standard in later ages. The claim that in 2004 (some say 1985) he was selected as one of the "Ten Great Cultural Figures of Ancient China" and the like varies from version to version; this essay does not pin down a specific year.
