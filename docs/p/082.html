徐渭：九次自杀没死成，最后成了大写意画的鼻祖

Xu Wei: Nine Failed Suicides, and the Man Who Became the Founding Father of Freehand Painting

——“几间东倒西歪屋，一个南腔北调人。”

—"A few crooked, tumbling rooms; a man of mixed southern and northern accents."

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂82 / 100约 3036 字 · 3036 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，如果你在博物馆里看到一幅泼墨淋漓、几乎看不出形状的葡萄或花鸟，标签上很可能写着“徐渭”。

In 2026, if you see a painting in a museum—a grape or a bird-and-flower subject painted in splashed ink, its shape almost impossible to make out—the label is very likely to read "Xu Wei."

他是中国“大写意”花鸟画的开山鼻祖。几百年后，郑板桥刻了一方印，自称“青藤门下走狗”；齐白石说，如果早生三百年，他愿意替徐渭磨墨理纸。

He was the founding father of China's "great freehand" (da xieyi) bird-and-flower painting. Centuries later, Zheng Banqiao carved a seal calling himself "a running dog at the gate of the Qingteng Studio"; and Qi Baishi said that if he had been born three hundred years earlier, he would have been willing to grind ink and spread paper for Xu Wei.

但徐渭本人，一生是个彻底的失败者。 他考了半辈子科举，八次落榜；他做过幕僚，靠山一倒，吓得发了疯；他用斧头砍自己的头、用钉子钉进耳朵，九次自杀都没死成；他杀过自己的妻子，坐了七年牢；晚年穷困潦倒，靠卖字画换米。

But Xu Wei himself was, in his lifetime, a thorough failure. He sat the imperial examinations for half his life and failed eight times; he worked in a governor's staff, and when his patron fell, he was frightened into madness; he hacked at his own head with an axe, drove a nail into his ear—nine suicide attempts, none of which killed him; he killed his own wife and served seven years in prison; in his last years he was destitute, living by selling calligraphy and paintings to buy rice.

他活到七十三岁。他死后，才成为中国美术史上绕不过去的一座山。

He lived to seventy-three. Only after his death did he become a mountain no one in the history of Chinese art can go around.

一、生下来就“多余”的孩子

I. A Child Born 'Superfluous'

1521年，徐渭出生在浙江山阴（今绍兴）。字文长，号青藤老人、天池山人。

In 1521, Xu Wei was born in Shanyin, Zhejiang (present-day Shaoxing). His courtesy name was Wenchang; his sobriquets were the Old Man of Qingteng and the Mountain Man of Tianchi.

他的出身就很苦。父亲徐鏓在他出生一百天时去世。他的生母是父亲晚年的一个侍妾，在家中地位很低。父亲死后，家道败落，他由嫡母抚养长大，十四岁时嫡母也去世了。

His beginnings were hard from the start. His father, Xu Cong, died when he was a hundred days old. His birth mother was a concubine taken by his father in his later years, of very low standing in the household. After his father's death the family fortunes declined; he was raised by his father's principal wife, who herself died when he was fourteen.

一个“庶出”的孤儿，在一个讲究出身的宗族社会里，从出生起就带着一种“多余感”。 他后来的诗里，反复出现“孤”“贱”“畸”这样的字，那不是文人的矫情，而是他从小的真实处境。

A "bastard-born" orphan, in a clan society that set great store by birth, carried from the outset a sense of being "superfluous." In his later poems the words "alone," "lowly," "lopsided" recur again and again—not the affectation of a man of letters, but the real condition of his life from childhood.

他很早就显示出惊人的才华。六岁能读书，八岁能文，二十岁时考中了秀才。周围的人都觉得，这个孩子前途无量。

He showed astonishing talent early. At six he could read, at eight write prose, and at twenty he earned the degree of xiucai in the examinations. Everyone around him believed the boy had a boundless future.

然后，他的人生就卡住了。

And then his life came to a standstill.

二、八次落榜

II. Eight Failed Examinations

考中秀才之后，徐渭接着去考乡试（考举人）。这是通往仕途的下一道门。

After earning the xiucai degree, Xu Wei went on to sit the provincial examination (for the juren degree). This was the next door on the road to office.

他考了八次。

He sat it eight times.

八次，全部落榜。 从二十几岁考到四十几岁，整整二十多年。他不是不努力，也不是没才华——恰恰相反，他的文章在同时代人里是公认的好。问题在于，科举有它自己的标准，而他那种自由、锋利、不合规矩的文风，始终入不了考官的眼。

Eight times, every one a failure. From his twenties into his forties, more than twenty years in all. It was not for lack of effort, nor for lack of talent—quite the opposite: his writing was acknowledged by his contemporaries as excellent. The problem was that the examination had standards of its own, and his free, sharp, rule-breaking style could never win the examiners' eyes.

一个才华横溢的人，被一个僵硬的制度反复判定为“不合格”，连续二十多年。这二十多年，把一个骄傲的年轻人，磨成了一个自我怀疑的中年人。

A man of brilliant talent, judged "unqualified" over and over by a rigid system, for more than twenty years. Those twenty-odd years ground a proud young man into a middle-aged man full of self-doubt.

三、幕府里的短暂高光

III. A Brief Radiance in the Governor's Staff

徐渭三十七岁那年，人生出现了一次转机。他进入了浙闽总督胡宗宪的幕府。

In his thirty-seventh year, a turn came in Xu Wei's life. He entered the staff of Hu Zongxian, governor-general of Zhejiang and Fujian.

胡宗宪是当时抗击倭寇的主帅，手握重兵。他很欣赏徐渭的才华，把他留在身边起草文书。徐渭在幕府里干得不错——他替胡宗宪写的那些奏疏、文告，都写得漂亮，深受赏识。

Hu Zongxian was the commander-in-chief of the war against the Japanese pirates at the time, with a great army under his hand. He admired Xu Wei's talent and kept him close to draft documents. Xu Wei did well in the staff—the memorials and proclamations he wrote for Hu Zongxian were all finely done and highly appreciated.

那是徐渭一生中最意气风发的一段时间。一个屡试不第的落魄书生，终于找到了一个能赏识自己的人。

That was the most buoyant period of Xu Wei's life. A down-and-out scholar who had failed the examinations again and again had finally found someone who could appreciate him.

但这段好日子只持续了几年。1565年，胡宗宪因卷入朝廷斗争被下狱，随后死在狱中。

But the good days lasted only a few years. In 1565, Hu Zongxian was imprisoned for becoming entangled in court factional struggles, and then died in prison.

靠山倒了。徐渭的恐惧，也一下子爆发了。

His patron had fallen. And Xu Wei's terror broke out all at once.

四、九次自杀

IV. Nine Suicides

徐渭的“发狂”，是从胡宗宪死后开始的。他害怕自己会因为与胡宗宪的关系被牵连，精神彻底崩溃。

Xu Wei's "madness" began after Hu Zongxian's death. He was afraid he would be implicated because of his connection to Hu Zongxian, and his mind collapsed entirely.

他选择了自杀。而且不是一次。

He chose to kill himself. And not just once.

据记载，他用斧头砍自己的头，血流满面，头骨都碎了，却没死；他用一根长钉刺进自己的耳朵，深达数寸，依然没死；他还用锤子击打自己的下身……他先后自杀了九次，一次都没有成功。

According to the records, he hacked at his own head with an axe, his face streaming with blood, the skull itself broken—and he did not die; he drove a long nail into his own ear, several inches deep, and still did not die; he also struck his own genitals with a hammer... In all he attempted suicide nine times, and not one attempt succeeded.

这段记载，读起来几乎像一种残酷的黑色幽默：一个人想死，却屡屡被命运拒绝。但对徐渭本人来说，这是实实在在的地狱。

This passage reads almost like a cruel black comedy: a man wants to die, and fate refuses him again and again. But for Xu Wei himself it was a veritable hell.

更不幸的是，1566年，他在一次精神失常中，杀死了自己的继妻张氏。他因此被投入监狱。

More unfortunate still, in 1566, in a fit of mental derangement, he killed his own second wife, née Zhang. For this he was thrown into prison.

七年牢狱

Seven Years in Prison

徐渭在牢里待了七年。到1573年（万历元年）前后，因为友人多方营救，又恰好遇到新帝即位的大赦，他被释放了。

Xu Wei spent seven years in prison. Around 1573 (the first year of the Wanli era), through the efforts of friends to rescue him, and just then a general amnesty on the accession of the new emperor, he was released.

出来时，他五十三岁。

When he came out, he was fifty-three.

五、晚年的潦倒与泼墨

V. Poverty and Splashed Ink in His Last Years

出狱后的徐渭，已经是一个被彻底击碎的人。

After leaving prison, Xu Wei was already a man utterly shattered.

他到处游历，也曾北上、入幕，但都没能安定下来。晚年他回到绍兴，住在几间破败的屋子里，靠卖字画度日，常常吃了上顿没下顿。

He traveled everywhere, and at times went north and served in a staff, but could never settle. In his last years he returned to Shaoxing and lived in a few dilapidated rooms, getting by on the sale of calligraphy and paintings, often not knowing where his next meal would come from.

他有一副著名的对联，写的就是自己：“几间东倒西歪屋，一个南腔北调人。” 屋子是歪的，人是格格不入的——十四个字，写尽了一生的窘迫。

He wrote a famous couplet about himself: "A few crooked, tumbling rooms; a man of mixed southern and northern accents." The rooms lean, the man fits nowhere—fourteen characters that exhaust a lifetime's awkwardness and want.

但恰恰是在这样的晚境里，他画出了中国美术史上最重要的一批画。

Yet it was precisely in such a late, straitened life that he painted some of the most important paintings in the history of Chinese art.

他的画，是“大写意”的极致。他不再追求画得像，而是要用最少的笔墨，泼出最浓的情绪。 他画葡萄，墨点淋漓，像是泪，也像是血。他画花，画鸟，画蟹，画山水，笔走如风，痛快淋漓，完全不理会“工整”和“漂亮”。

His painting is the extreme of "great freehand." He no longer sought to paint things as they looked, but to splash out the thickest emotion with the fewest strokes. He painted grapes in splashed dots of ink that look like tears and like blood. He painted flowers, birds, crabs, landscapes, his brush moving like wind, free and dripping—utterly indifferent to "neatness" and "prettiness."

那幅《墨葡萄》

That Painting, "Ink Grapes"

《墨葡萄图》是徐渭的代表作。画上是一架葡萄，藤蔓纷披，果实零落，笔墨纵横，几乎看不出清晰的轮廓。他在画上题了一首诗：

"Ink Grapes" is Xu Wei's masterpiece. On the painting is a grape trellis, vines scattering and draping, fruit sparse and fallen, brush and ink running freely, almost no clear outline visible. He wrote a poem on the painting:

半生落魄已成翁，独立书斋啸晚风。

Half a lifetime fallen on hard times, and now I am an old man; / standing alone in my study, I whistle into the evening wind.

笔底明珠无处卖，闲抛闲掷野藤中。

The bright pearls beneath my brush have nowhere to be sold; / idly I toss and fling them away among the wild vines.

“笔底明珠无处卖”——他画里那些墨点，明明是他一生最珍贵的东西，却连卖都卖不出去，只能扔在野地里。 这幅画，是他一生的自画像。

"The bright pearls beneath my brush have nowhere to be sold"—those ink dots in his paintings were plainly the most precious thing in his whole life, and yet he could not even sell them; he could only throw them into the wilderness. This painting is the self-portrait of a lifetime.

六、他为什么是“鼻祖”

VI. Why He Is the 'Founding Father'

徐渭的意义，不在于他画得“好”，而在于他打开了一扇门。

Xu Wei's significance lies not in his having painted "well," but in his having opened a door.

在他之前，中国花鸟画讲究的是精致、工整、写实。徐渭用他的墨，把这一切都打破了。他证明：画可以不是“画出来”的，而是“泼出来”的；画里的主角，不必是花鸟，而是画者自己的情绪。

Before him, Chinese bird-and-flower painting prized refinement, neatness, and realism. Xu Wei, with his ink, broke all of it open. He proved: a painting need not be "painted out," but can be "splashed out"; and the protagonist of a painting need not be the flowers and birds, but the painter's own feeling.

这条路，后来被八大山人、石涛、扬州八怪一直走下去。到了近现代，齐白石、潘天寿都把徐渭奉为宗师。可以说，没有徐渭的“大写意”，就没有后来中国文人画的半壁江山。

This road was later followed onward by Bada Shanren, Shitao, and the Eight Eccentrics of Yangzhou. In modern times, Qi Baishi and Pan Tianshou both honored Xu Wei as a master. One can say that without Xu Wei's "great freehand," there would have been no half of the later tradition of Chinese literati painting.

他还写过戏曲——杂剧《四声猿》被誉为明代杂剧的代表作；他写过《南词叙录》，是研究南戏的重要文献；他的诗文也被后人辑为《徐文长全集》。

He also wrote drama—the zaju play Four Cries of a Gibbon (Sisheng yuan) is praised as a representative work of Ming zaju; he wrote the Nanci Xulu (A Catalogue of Southern Song-Lyrics), an important document for the study of Southern drama; and his poetry and prose were later collected by posterity as the Complete Works of Xu Wenchang.

一个科举的失败者，在几乎所有“正经”的道路上都失败了，却在所有“不正经”的道路上，都成了大师。

A failure in the examinations, a failure on nearly every "proper" road, yet on every "improper" road he became a master.

七、写给2026年的我们

VII. To Us in 2026

徐渭的一生，几乎是“不幸运”这个词的极限。

Xu Wei's life is almost the outer limit of the word "unlucky."

他出身低微，屡试不第，靠山倒台，精神崩溃，九次自杀，杀妻入狱，晚年贫困。任何一个环节，都足以压垮一个人。

Low birth, repeated failure in the examinations, the fall of his patron, mental collapse, nine suicides, the killing of his wife and imprisonment, poverty in old age. Any single link in the chain would have been enough to crush a man.

但他没有停下来。他在牢里写戏，在破屋里画画，在最穷的时候，还在泼他的墨。他活着的时候，没人把他的画当回事。他自己也知道——所以他写下“笔底明珠无处卖”。

But he did not stop. He wrote plays in prison, painted in broken rooms, and in his poorest days was still splashing his ink. While he lived, no one took his paintings seriously. He knew it himself—that is why he wrote "The bright pearls beneath my brush have nowhere to be sold."

2026年的我们，常被“成功要趁早”“三十岁前必须做到什么”之类的话推着走。徐渭提供了一个完全相反的样本：一个人可以在世俗意义上彻底失败，可以在六十岁还一无所有，但只要他手里那件事是真的，时间最终会替他说完他想说的话。

We in 2026 are often pushed along by sayings like "success must come early" and "by thirty you must have achieved such and such." Xu Wei offers a completely opposite model: a person can fail utterly in worldly terms, can reach sixty with nothing at all—yet so long as the thing in his hands is true, time will in the end finish speaking the words he meant to say.

他死在穷困里。四百年后，无数人站在他的画前，久久不愿离开。这大概就是“笔底明珠无处卖”之后，最好的答案。

He died in poverty. Four hundred years later, countless people stand before his paintings and do not want to leave for a long time. This is probably the best answer to "The bright pearls beneath my brush have nowhere to be sold."

他还说过一句更“狂”的话：“吾书第一，诗二，文三，画四。”——在他看来，自己最不重要的一项才是画。可历史偏偏把“大写意鼻祖”这个头衔，给了他的“第四名”。这也说明一件事：一个人对自己价值的判断，往往并不准确；真正公正的裁判，是时间。

He also said something even more "arrogant": "My calligraphy first, poetry second, prose third, painting fourth." —In his own view, his least important discipline was painting. And yet history handed the title "founding father of great freehand" precisely to his "fourth place." This too shows one thing: a person's judgment of his own value is often inaccurate; the truly fair judge is time.

史料来源与辨析

Sources and Notes

1. 生平基础事实：徐渭（字文长，号青藤老人、天池山人），1521年生于浙江山阴（今绍兴），1593年卒，年73岁。为确证史实。另有“号青藤道士”“田水月”等别号。

1. Basic biographical facts: Xu Wei (courtesy name Wenchang; sobriquets the Old Man of Qingteng, the Mountain Man of Tianchi), born in 1521 in Shanyin, Zhejiang (present-day Shaoxing), died in 1593, aged 73. This is a well-attested fact. He also had other sobriquets, such as "the Daoist of Qingteng" and "Tian Shui Yue."

2. 出身与家世：父徐鏓于其出生百日后去世；生母为侍妾，嫡母在其14岁时去世。为通行记载（徐渭本人有《畸谱》自述生平，是重要的第一手材料）。

2. Origins and family: his father, Xu Cong, died a hundred days after his birth; his birth mother was a concubine, and his father's principal wife died when he was 14. This is the standard account (Xu Wei himself left an autobiographical work, the Jipu (Record of a Lopsided Life), an important first-hand source).

3. 科举：20岁中秀才，之后八次乡试不中。为确证史实。

3. Examinations: earned the xiucai degree at 20, and thereafter failed the provincial examination eight times. This is a well-attested fact.

4. 入胡宗宪幕府：约37岁（1557年）前后入幕。为通行记载。

4. Entry into Hu Zongxian's staff: around the age of 37 (c. 1557). This is the standard account.

5. 胡宗宪下狱死：1565年，胡宗宪死于狱中，徐渭因此精神崩溃。为确证史实。

5. Hu Zongxian's imprisonment and death: in 1565 Hu Zongxian died in prison, and Xu Wei's mind collapsed because of it. This is a well-attested fact.

6. 九次自杀：史载其以斧击头、以钉刺耳等方式自杀，“凡九自杀，皆不死”（《明史·徐渭传》有类似记载）。辨析：具体方式与次数出自史书与本人自述，其细节的医学合理性无法完全核实，但“多次自杀未遂”这一基本事实可靠。

6. Nine suicides: the histories record that he tried to kill himself by striking his head with an axe, piercing his ear with a nail, and other means, "in all nine attempts at suicide, none of them fatal" (the "Biography of Xu Wei" in the Mingshi (History of the Ming) has a similar record). Note: the specific methods and the number come from the histories and from his own account; the medical plausibility of the details cannot be fully verified, but the basic fact of "multiple failed suicide attempts" is reliable.

7. 杀继妻张氏与下狱七年：1566年前后杀妻，入狱，约1573年获释。为确证史实。辨析：其杀妻时的精神状态，历来有“精神病发作”与“疑其不贞”等不同说法，本文表述为“在一次精神失常中”，以反映其长期的精神问题背景。

7. The killing of his second wife, née Zhang, and seven years in prison: he killed his wife around 1566, was imprisoned, and was released about 1573. This is a well-attested fact. Note: on his mental state at the time of the killing, there have long been differing accounts—"a psychotic episode" and "suspecting her of infidelity"—and this essay states it as "in a fit of mental derangement," to reflect the background of his long-standing mental problems.

8. 《墨葡萄图》题诗：为确证传世画作上的题诗，广为引用。

8. The inscribed poem on "Ink Grapes": the poem is inscribed on a surviving painting and is widely quoted.

9. 后世评价：郑板桥自称“青藤门下走狗”（其印章文字），齐白石有“愿为青藤磨墨理纸”之语。为确证史实。

9. Later evaluations: Zheng Banqiao called himself "a running dog at the gate of the Qingteng Studio" (the text of his seal), and Qi Baishi said he was "willing to grind ink and spread paper for Qingteng." This is a well-attested fact.
