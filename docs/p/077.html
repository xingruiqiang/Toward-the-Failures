王勃：写下“落霞与孤鹜齐飞”的人，27岁溺死在海里

Wang Bo: The Man Who Wrote "The Setting Clouds and the Lonely Duck Fly Together," and Drowned at Twenty-Seven

——“海内存知己，天涯若比邻。”

— "If you have a friend who knows your heart, distance cannot keep you two apart."

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂77 / 100约 3071 字 · 3071 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，只要读到《滕王阁序》，很多人还是会起一身鸡皮疙瘩。那句“落霞与孤鹜齐飞，秋水共长天一色”，几乎是中国古诗文里最著名的一幅画面。

Even in 2026, many readers still get goosebumps when they reach the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng (Tengwang Ge Xu). The line "the setting clouds and the lonely duck fly together, the autumn water and the vast sky share a single hue" is perhaps the most celebrated image in all of classical Chinese verse and prose.

写下它的人，当时据说只有二十几岁。而这个人，一生经历了神童、入仕、被逐、坐牢、连累父亲、最后死在海上。

The man who wrote it was, by all accounts, only in his twenties at the time. And his life would run the whole course of it: a child prodigy, an official career, expulsion, imprisonment, the ruin of his own father, and at last death at sea.

他叫王勃。死时，27岁。

His name was Wang Bo. He was twenty-seven when he died.

一、神童的起点

I. The Starting Point of a Prodigy

约650年，王勃出生在绛州龙门（今山西河津）。他的家族是真正的书香门第——他的祖父王通，是隋代著名的大儒；他的叔祖、兄长，都是当时有名的学者和诗人。

Around 650, Wang Bo was born in Longmen of Jiangzhou (present-day Hejin, Shanxi). His was a genuine family of scholars: his grandfather Wang Tong was a renowned Confucian of the Sui dynasty, and his grand-uncles and elder brothers were noted scholars and poets of their day.

在这样的家庭里，王勃的早慧几乎是必然的。他六岁能文，九岁读《汉书》就敢写文章指出前人的注释错误，十岁通六经。放在任何一个时代，这都是“神童”级别的表现。

In such a household, Wang Bo's precocity was almost inevitable. He could write at six; at nine, reading the Book of Han (Hanshu), he dared to write an essay pointing out errors in earlier commentators' notes; by ten he had mastered the Six Classics. In any age, that would count as prodigy-level performance.

不到二十岁，他就通过科举（幽素科）及第，被授朝散郎。年纪轻轻就进了朝廷，前途看上去无可限量。

Before he was twenty, he passed the imperial examination (the Yusu ke) and was appointed Gentleman of the Court for Miscellaneous Duties (Chaosan Lang). To enter the court so young—his future seemed beyond measure.

二、一篇游戏文字，毁掉前程

II. A Piece of Playwriting That Ruined a Career

王勃的第一次重大挫折，来得极其荒诞。

Wang Bo's first great setback came in the most absurd way imaginable.

当时沛王李贤（唐高宗的儿子）很喜欢他，把他招入王府。有一次，沛王与英王斗鸡，王勃一时兴起，写了一篇《檄英王鸡》——用讨伐敌国的檄文格式，写了一篇讨伐“英王的鸡”的游戏文章。

Prince Pei, Li Xian (a son of Emperor Gaozong of Tang), was fond of him and brought him into his household. Once, when the Prince of Pei and the Prince of Ying staged a cockfight, Wang Bo, on a whim, wrote "A Call to Arms Against the Prince of Ying's Rooster" (Xi Ying Wang Ji)—a mock declaration of war, cast in the format of the military proclamations that summoned the annihilation of an enemy state, but aimed at "the Prince of Ying's rooster."

这在今天看来就是个段子。但在当时，它却触动了皇帝最敏感的神经。唐高宗李治看到后大怒：诸王之间斗鸡，本是儿戏，你一个王府官员，竟然写“檄文”去挑拨，这是什么居心？

To us today it is a joke. But at the time it touched the emperor's most sensitive nerve. When Emperor Gaozong (Li Zhi) read it, he flew into a rage: a cockfight between princes was child's play—yet you, an official of a princely household, would write a "call to arms" to set them against each other? What were your intentions?

王勃就这样被逐出了王府。 一篇玩笑文章，断送了他最好的政治前途。

And so Wang Bo was driven from the princely household. A single joke of an essay had cut short his finest political prospects.

三、一次命案，连累父亲

III. A Homicide That Brought Down His Father

被逐之后，王勃辗转去了四川，游历了几年。后来，他回到长安，做了虢州参军。

After his expulsion, Wang Bo drifted to Sichuan and spent several years traveling. Later he returned to Chang'an and took a post as military aide in Guo Prefecture.

在虢州任上，他卷进了一桩命案。史书说，他杀了一个叫曹达的官奴。至于原因，众说纷纭——有人说是他包庇罪犯后事情败露，为灭口而杀人；也有人认为他是被人构陷。

In office at Guo Prefecture he became entangled in a homicide. The histories say he killed an official slave named Cao Da. As for the reason, accounts differ widely—some say he had shielded a criminal, the affair came to light, and he killed to silence the man; others believe he was framed.

无论真相如何，按律当斩。后来遇到大赦，他被免死除名，但他的父亲因为受他连累，被贬到了交趾（今越南北部）做县令。

Whatever the truth, the law called for execution. A general amnesty later spared him death and struck his name from the rolls, but his father, implicated through him, was demoted to serve as a county magistrate in Jiaozhi (present-day northern Vietnam).

这件事，成了王勃一生最沉重的负担。一个骄傲的年轻人，不仅毁了自己的前程，还把年迈的父亲推到了万里之外的蛮荒之地。

This affair became the heaviest burden of Wang Bo's life. A proud young man had not only wrecked his own prospects but pushed his aging father into a wilderness ten thousand li from home.

四、滕王阁上的那一夜

IV. That Night at the Pavilion of Prince Teng

675年（上元二年）前后，被除名后的王勃，决定南下交趾去看望父亲。

Around 675 (the second year of Shangyuan), with his name struck from the official rolls, Wang Bo decided to travel south to Jiaozhi to visit his father.

他路过洪州（今南昌）。正好，洪州都督阎伯屿重修了滕王阁，大宴宾客，想借机让自己的女婿当众写一篇序文来扬名。按惯例，主人会先假意请客人来写，客人都会推辞。

He passed through Hongzhou (present-day Nanchang). It happened that the prefect of Hongzhou, Yan Boyu, had just restored the Pavilion of Prince Teng and was hosting a great banquet, meaning to use the occasion to have his son-in-law compose a preface in public and win renown. Custom dictated that the host first make a show of asking a guest to write it, and that every guest decline.

但王勃没有推辞。他提笔就写。

But Wang Bo did not decline. He took up his brush and wrote.

于是就有了那篇千古名文《秋日登洪府滕王阁饯别序》，也就是《滕王阁序》。从“豫章故郡，洪都新府”开头，一路写到“落霞与孤鹜齐飞，秋水共长天一色”，最后落到“阁中帝子今何在？槛外长江空自流”。

And so came that text of the ages, "A Farewell Preface Composed on an Autumn Day at the Pavilion of Prince Teng in Hongzhou Prefecture" (Qiuri Deng Hongfu Tengwang Ge Jianbie Xu)—that is, the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng. It opens with "Yuzhang, the ancient commandery; Hongdu, the new prefecture," moves on through "the setting clouds and the lonely duck fly together, the autumn water and the vast sky share a single hue," and closes upon "Where is the prince in the pavilion now? Beyond the rails the Long River flows on, empty and alone."

关于这段故事，有一个流传极广的版本：阎伯屿本来很不高兴，站在屏风后面听人念，听到“落霞与孤鹜齐飞”时，惊得站起来说：“此真天才，当垂不朽！”

There is a very widely circulated version of this episode: Yan Boyu was at first greatly displeased, standing behind a screen to listen as the text was read aloud, and when he heard "the setting clouds and the lonely duck fly together," he sprang up in astonishment and cried: "This is true genius—it will endure forever!"

需要说明的是：这个“阎伯屿屏风后听文”的细节，带有明显的传说色彩，《滕王阁序》的确切成文时间与场合，学界也有不同看法。 但可以确定的是：这篇文字，是中国骈文的巅峰。

It should be made clear: this detail of "Yan Boyu listening behind the screen" bears the plain mark of legend, and scholars also disagree over exactly when and on what occasion the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng was composed. What can be certain is this: the piece is the summit of Chinese parallel prose.

五、海上的那一场风

V. That Storm at Sea

写完《滕王阁序》后，王勃继续南下。

After finishing the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng, Wang Bo continued south.

676年（一说上元三年），他在渡海（一说渡南海，一说在渡北部湾一带海域）时遇到风浪，落水。他被救起，但受到了极大的惊吓。不久之后，他就在惊恐与病痛中去世，年仅27岁。

In 676 (some say the third year of Shangyuan), while crossing the sea (some say the South China Sea, some the waters off the Gulf of Tonkin), he met a storm and fell into the water. He was pulled out, but the shock was immense. Not long after, he died amid terror and illness, barely twenty-seven.

一个写出了“海内存知己，天涯若比邻”的人，最后死在海里。

A man who wrote "If you have a friend who knows your heart, distance cannot keep you two apart" died at last in the sea.

他去世时，他的父亲还在交趾。这个骄傲的儿子，终究没能见到父亲。

At his death, his father was still in Jiaozhi. This proud son never lived to see the old man again.

六、不只是《滕王阁序》

VI. Not Only the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng

王勃的才华，不只在骈文上。

Wang Bo's talent was not confined to parallel prose.

他与杨炯、卢照邻、骆宾王并称“初唐四杰”。这四个人做的，是一件承前启后的大事：他们开始把诗歌从宫廷的绮靡宴饮里拉出来，写到离别、边塞、山川、人生。虽然还带着六朝以来的华丽，但气象已经完全不同。

Together with Yang Jiong, Lu Zhaolin, and Luo Binwang, he was named one of the "Four Talents of the Early Tang." What these four did was a great work of transition: they began to draw poetry out of the court's ornate banqueting halls and turn it toward parting, frontier war, rivers and mountains, and human life. It still carried the splendor inherited from the Six Dynasties, but its spirit was utterly different.

王勃的代表作之一《送杜少府之任蜀州》，如今仍是课本里的名篇：

One of Wang Bo's masterpieces, "Seeing Off Vice-Magistrate Du on His Way to Shuzhou" (Song Du Shaofu zhi Ren Shuzhou), remains a celebrated piece in school readers to this day:

海内存知己，天涯若比邻。

If you have a friend who knows your heart, distance cannot keep you two apart.

无为在歧路，儿女共沾巾。

Do not, at the forking of the road, weep like children, wetting your kerchiefs.

这两句把送别诗从“哭哭啼啼”里解放了出来。 在交通极其不便的时代，说“就算隔着天涯，也像邻居一样”——这句话背后，是一种超越距离的自信和开阔。

These two lines freed the farewell poem from its flood of tears. In an age when travel was unimaginably hard, to say "though the ends of the earth lie between us, we are as near as neighbors"—behind that sentence lies a confidence and an openness that transcend distance.

他短短27年留下的诗文，数量不算多，却几乎每一篇都带着一种“少年气”：不服输、不悲戚、眼界极高。他活得比谁都短，但气魄比谁都大。

In his brief twenty-seven years he left a body of verse and prose not large in quantity, yet almost every piece carries a kind of "youthfulness": refusing to concede, never maudlin, with an extraordinarily high horizon. He lived a shorter life than anyone, yet his spirit was greater than anyone's.

七、他死后，发生了什么

VII. What Happened After He Died

王勃去世后，他的诗文由后人收集整理，编成了《王子安集》。但他的命运并没有因为他的死去而变得温柔。

After Wang Bo's death, his poems and prose were collected and arranged by later hands into the Collected Works of Wang Zian (Wang Zi'an Ji). But his fate did not soften with his death.

他的父亲，当时还在交趾。这个被儿子连累、被贬到万里之外的老人，得知儿子死讯后的情形，史书几乎没有记载。这种沉默，比任何描写都更让人难受。

His father was still in Jiaozhi. How that old man—dragged down by his son, banished ten thousand li from home—received the news of the boy's death is something the histories scarcely record. That silence is harder to bear than any description.

而在文学史上，人们对他的评价却一路走高。杜甫写过“王杨卢骆当时体……尔曹身与名俱灭，不废江河万古流”，明确为“初唐四杰”辩护，说他们的成就像江河一样不可磨灭。到了后世，《滕王阁序》被选入各种古文选本，成了几代中国人共同的文学记忆。

In literary history, however, his standing only rose. Du Fu wrote, "Wang, Yang, Lu, Luo—the style of their day... / You and your names will perish together, / but not the rivers, which flow on through all ages," speaking up plainly for the "Four Talents of the Early Tang" and declaring that their achievements, like rivers, could never be erased. In later ages the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng was chosen for every kind of anthology of classical prose, becoming a shared literary memory for generations of Chinese readers.

一个人的一生可以短到只有27年，但他可以留下一些东西，让一千年的人反复朗读。

A single life can be as short as twenty-seven years, yet a person can leave something behind that a thousand years of readers will recite again and again.

八、写给2026年的我们

VIII. To Us in 2026

王勃的故事，常被概括为“天才薄命”。但如果只记住这四个字，就浪费了他的一生。

Wang Bo's story is often summed up as "a genius of scant fate." But to remember only those four words is to waste his whole life.

他真正值得记住的，是那种“被命运连续打击后，依然能写出最壮丽文字”的能力。他被逐出王府，被罢官，坐过牢，连累过父亲——在经历了这些之后，他还能在滕王阁上，写出那样开阔、那样自信、那样没有一丝怨气的句子。

What truly deserves remembering is his power, after blow upon blow from fate, to still write the most magnificent words. Driven from a princely household, stripped of office, imprisoned, the ruin of his father—after all of that, he could still stand at the Pavilion of Prince Teng and write lines so open, so confident, so utterly free of resentment.

“老当益壮，宁移白首之心？穷且益坚，不坠青云之志。”这两句也出自《滕王阁序》。写下这两句的人，当时正走在自己人生最低谷的路上。

"Though old, one should grow ever stronger—how could one change a white-haired heart? Though poor, one should grow ever firmer—never let fall one's ambition to the blue clouds." These two lines also come from the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng. The man who wrote them was at that moment walking the lowest road of his life.

2026年，我们太容易因为一次失败就给自己“判死刑”——一次没考上、一次被裁员、一次创业失败。王勃的答案很直接：你可能只有27年，但你可以在这27年里，写下一句让一千年后的人还在读的话。

In 2026, we are all too ready to hand ourselves a "death sentence" over a single failure—one failed exam, one layoff, one failed venture. Wang Bo's answer is blunt: you may have only twenty-seven years, but in those twenty-seven years you can write a line that people a thousand years later are still reading.

王勃的一生，还有一层更普遍的启示：“神童”从来不是护身符。 他六岁能文，却在二十几岁接连栽跟头；他被逐、被贬、坐牢，走的每一步都比同龄人更狼狈。天赋给了他极高的起点，却没有给他任何豁免权。真正让他留下来的，不是“聪明”，而是他在最狼狈的时候，依然能写出“穷且益坚，不坠青云之志”的那种心气。这八个字，是他在写别人，也是在写自己。他没能等到“青云之志”兑现的那天，但他把这股气，留给了后来所有在低谷里挣扎的人。

There is a more universal lesson in Wang Bo's life as well: a "child prodigy" is never a talisman. He could write at six, yet in his twenties he stumbled again and again; expelled, demoted, imprisoned, he met every step more wretchedly than his peers. His gifts gave him an extraordinarily high starting point, but granted him no immunity whatever. What truly made him last was not "cleverness" but the spirit that, at his most wretched, could still write "though poor, one should grow ever firmer—never let fall one's ambition to the blue clouds." Those eight characters were written about others; they were also written about himself. He never lived to see that "ambition to the blue clouds" fulfilled, but he left that spirit to everyone who came after and struggled in the low places.

《滕王阁序》里有一句话，几乎可以当作他一生的注脚：“穷且益坚，不坠青云之志。”写下这句话的人，正走在人生最狼狈的路上——被逐、被贬、连累父亲。可他没有把怨气写进文章，他写的是开阔、是壮丽、是“天涯若比邻”的那种自信。一个人能活成这样，已经超越了寿命本身。

There is a line in the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng that could almost stand as the footnote to his whole life: "Though poor, one should grow ever firmer—never let fall one's ambition to the blue clouds." The man who wrote it was on the most wretched road of his life—expelled, demoted, the ruin of his father. Yet he did not write resentment into his work; he wrote openness, grandeur, the confidence of "the ends of the earth as near as neighbors." To live like that is already to surpass the span of one's years.

Sources and Notes

2. 家世：祖父王通为隋代大儒，家族学者众多。为确证史实。

2. Family background: his grandfather Wang Tong was a great Confucian of the Sui dynasty, and the family produced many scholars. This is confirmed historical fact.

3. 早慧与及第：六岁能文、九岁读《汉书》作《指瑕》、未及二十及第授朝散郎。为通行记载（除“九岁指瑕”等具体细节外，其早慧的基本事实可靠）。

3. Precocity and examination success: able to write at six; at nine reading the Book of Han and writing "Pointing Out Flaws" (Zhi Xia); passing the examination and being appointed Gentleman of the Court for Miscellaneous Duties before twenty. This follows the standard accounts (apart from particulars such as "pointing out flaws at nine," the basic fact of his precocity is reliable).

4. 《檄英王鸡》与被逐：为确证史实，见于《新唐书》《旧唐书》本传。

4. "A Call to Arms Against the Prince of Ying's Rooster" and his expulsion: confirmed historical fact, found in his biographies in the New Book of Tang (Xin Tangshu) and the Old Book of Tang (Jiu Tangshu).

5. 虢州参军任上杀官奴：史书有记载，但案情存在疑点（是否被构陷、具体动机），本文表明“众说纷纭”，不作定论。其父因此被贬交趾，为确证史实。

5. The killing of an official slave while serving as military aide in Guo Prefecture: recorded in the histories, but the case has its doubtful points (whether he was framed, what his precise motive was); this article states only that "accounts differ" and passes no final judgment. That his father was demoted to Jiaozhi as a result is confirmed historical fact.

6. 《滕王阁序》的写作时间与场合：辨析——通行的“675年过南昌，即席作序”的说法，源自唐代笔记（如《唐摭言》），带有传说色彩；也有学者认为是在676年，或成文时间更早。而“阎伯屿屏风后惊叹”的情节，更接近民间故事的构造。本文如实指出这一点，但《滕王阁序》为骈文巅峰、为王勃所作，则为定论。

6. The time and occasion of the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng: a critical note—the common account, "passing through Nanchang in 675 and composing the preface on the spot," derives from Tang-dynasty anecdote collections (such as the Tang Zhiyan) and carries the flavor of legend; some scholars hold that it was written in 676, or even earlier. And the episode of "Yan Boyu marveling behind the screen" comes even closer to the construction of a folk tale. This article points this out honestly; but that the Preface is the summit of parallel prose and the work of Wang Bo is settled fact.

7. 卒因：渡海溺水，惊悸而卒。为通行记载。具体海域与细节，史料简略，本文不作过度具体化。

7. Cause of death: drowning while crossing the sea, dying of fright and shock. This follows the standard accounts. As to the precise waters and details, the sources are sketchy, and this article does not over-specify.

8. 与杨炯、卢照邻、骆宾王的“初唐四杰”之称：为后世通行称谓，四人并无正式的结社关系。

8. The title "Four Talents of the Early Tang," shared with Yang Jiong, Lu Zhaolin, and Luo Binwang: a conventional label of later ages; the four had no formal association as a group.
