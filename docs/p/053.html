Chapter 053 · Part 3

贝佐斯：被《巴伦周刊》画成一颗炸弹的人

Jeff Bezos: The Man Barron's Drew as a Bomb

——“倾听，保持开放，但别让任何人告诉你你是谁”

— "Listen, stay open, but don't let anyone tell you who you are"

2026年，如果你正在做一个不被看好的项目，大概率会听到这样的声音：这东西不靠谱、这条路走不通、你这个模式没有盈利可能。负面评价像潮水一样涌来，很多人还没做成事，先被舆论压垮了。

In 2026, if you are working on a project that no one believes in, you will most likely hear voices like these: this thing is unreliable, this road leads nowhere, your model can never turn a profit. Negative judgments surge in like a tide, and many people, before they have accomplished anything, are crushed by public opinion first.

二十多年前，有一个人被美国最权威的财经杂志之一放上了封面——但不是赞美。那期杂志把亚马逊比作一颗即将爆炸的炸弹，标题是“Amazon.bomb”。文章断定：这家公司撑不下去。

More than twenty years ago, a man was put on the cover of one of America's most authoritative financial magazines—but not in praise. That issue likened Amazon to a bomb about to explode; the headline read "Amazon.bomb." The article concluded: this company cannot survive.

他叫杰夫·贝佐斯。那一年，亚马逊的股价腰斩，一年后更是暴跌超过90%。几年之后，他成了世界首富。

His name was Jeff Bezos. That year, Amazon's stock price was cut in half; a year later it plunged by more than 90 percent. A few years after that, he became the richest man in the world.

一、从车库里的小书店开始

I. Starting from a Little Bookstore in a Garage

1994年，贝佐斯在一家纽约的对冲基金做着高薪工作。他注意到一个数字：互联网正以每年2300%的速度增长。

In 1994, Bezos held a well-paid job at a New York hedge fund. He noticed a number: the internet was growing at 2,300 percent a year.

他做了一个决定：辞职，去网上卖东西。他列了一份清单，最终选定了“书”——因为书的品类极多（数百万种），而实体书店受限于货架，最多只能摆十几万种。这是一个在互联网上才有优势的品类。

He made a decision: quit, and go sell things online. He drew up a list, and finally settled on "books"—because the number of book titles is enormous (millions of them), while physical bookstores, limited by their shelves, can carry at most a hundred thousand or so. It was a category that held an advantage only on the internet.

1995年，亚马逊在一间车库里成立。1997年，它在纳斯达克上市。

In 1995, Amazon was founded in a garage. In 1997, it went public on Nasdaq.

上市之后，亚马逊做的事情，用当时的眼光看很“反常识”：它几乎不追求盈利，而是把赚到的每一分钱都投进基础设施、仓储、技术和市场扩张里。它连年亏损，却一路狂奔。

After going public, what Amazon did looked, by the standards of the time, deeply counterintuitive: it barely pursued profit at all, instead pouring every cent it earned into infrastructure, warehousing, technology, and market expansion. It lost money year after year—and yet it sprinted forward without stopping.

二、1999：那颗“炸弹”

II. 1999: That "Bomb"

1999年，是亚马逊最受追捧、也最危险的时刻。

1999 was Amazon's most sought-after and also most dangerous moment.

亚马逊的股价在4月底还在每股105美元左右，到5月底就跌到了60美元以下。“互联网泡沫”正在吹大，而亚马逊是所有人争论的焦点。

At the end of April, Amazon's stock still stood at around $105 a share; by the end of May it had fallen below $60. The "internet bubble" was inflating, and Amazon was the focus of everyone's argument.

就在这个时候，《巴伦周刊》做了一期封面报道，标题叫“Amazon.bomb”（亚马逊炸弹）。封面上，贝佐斯的头像被画在一颗即将引爆的炸弹上。

Just then, Barron's ran a cover story titled "Amazon.bomb." On the cover, Bezos's face was drawn onto a bomb about to go off.

文章的论点很“扎实”：亚马逊本质上只是“另一个中间商”，它不可能与索尼、戴尔这样直接面向消费者的公司竞争；它的股价剧烈波动，恰恰证明它无法改变美国的零售格局。文章还指出，亚马逊的图书销售增速在快速放缓——1997年增长了825%，1998年跌到260%，预计1999年只有90%，“从任何角度看，这都是在减速”。

The article's arguments were "solid": Amazon was essentially just "another middleman," it could never compete with companies like Sony and Dell that sold directly to consumers; and its violently swinging stock price proved precisely that it could not change the American retail landscape. The article also noted that Amazon's book-sales growth was rapidly slowing—825 percent in 1997, down to 260 percent in 1998, and projected at only 90 percent in 1999. "From any angle, this is deceleration."

这些话，在当时看来并不是没有道理。亚马逊前一年在有6.1亿美元收入的情况下净亏了1.25亿美元；1999年第一季度，收入2.94亿，亏损0.62亿。

These words were not, at the time, without merit. The previous year, Amazon had posted $610 million in revenue and a net loss of $125 million; in the first quarter of 1999, revenue was $294 million and the loss $62 million.

当全世界都在问你“什么时候才能盈利”时，你能不能坚持自己认为对的事？

When the whole world is asking you "when will you turn a profit," can you hold fast to what you believe is right?

三、泡沫破裂，跌去90%

III. The Bubble Bursts, Down 90 Percent

2000年，互联网泡沫破裂。

In 2000, the internet bubble burst.

亚马逊的股价一路下挫，到2001年跌破了每股10美元，比此前的高点跌去了90%以上。公司1999年亏7.2亿美元，2000年亏得更多——14.1亿美元，累计债务达到20亿美元。

Amazon's stock slid all the way down, dropping below $10 a share by 2001—more than 90 percent off its earlier peak. The company lost $720 million in 1999, and more in 2000: $1.41 billion, with accumulated debt reaching $2 billion.

批评的声音从四面八方涌来。《纽约时报》说它是“一家建立在一层薄得不能再薄的泡沫上的、市值200亿美元、有2100名员工的公司”；有人把它嘲讽为“Amazon.toast”（亚马逊完蛋）、“Amazon.org”（暗示它永远也赚不到钱，只配用非营利组织的域名）。

Criticism poured in from every direction. The New York Times called it "a $20 billion company with 2,100 employees built on the thinnest conceivable layer of froth"; some mocked it as "Amazon.toast" and "Amazon.org"—the latter hinting that it would never make money and was fit only for a nonprofit's domain.

从任何角度看，那时候的亚马逊都像是一家“即将证明自己是个错误”的公司。

From any angle, Amazon back then looked like a company about to prove itself a mistake.

但贝佐斯坚持了一套后来被反复引用的逻辑：基于长期的市场领导地位，而不是短期的盈利和华尔街的短期反应，来做投资决策。 他继续烧钱建基础设施、抢市场份额。

But Bezos held to a logic that would later be cited again and again: to make investment decisions based on long-term market leadership, not short-term profit or Wall Street's short-term reactions. He kept burning money to build infrastructure and seize market share.

泡沫顶峰时期融到的钱，让亚马逊在2000年末还握着11亿美元现金——这保证它在最低谷时，依然能坚持自己一直以来的战略，继续投入创新。

The money raised at the height of the bubble left Amazon holding $1.1 billion in cash at the end of 2000—which ensured that even in its lowest trough it could still stick to the strategy it had always pursued and keep investing in innovation.

四、二十年后的一次“回怼”

IV. One "Comeback" Twenty Years Later

后来的事情，很多人都知道了。

What happened next, many people already know.

亚马逊发明了Kindle电子阅读器，推出了Prime会员，开创了云计算平台AWS，还做起了原创影视。它从一家卖书的网站，变成了横跨电商和云计算的巨头，长期位居全球市值最高的公司之列。

Amazon invented the Kindle e-reader, launched Prime membership, pioneered the cloud-computing platform AWS, and moved into original film and television. It grew from a website that sold books into a giant spanning e-commerce and cloud computing, long ranked among the most valuable companies in the world.

2021年，贝佐斯在社交平台上贴出了那期22年前的《巴伦周刊》封面，配了一句话：

In 2021, Bezos posted that 22-year-old Barron's cover on social media, with one line of text:

“倾听，保持开放，但别让任何人告诉你你是谁。”

"Listen, stay open, but don't let anyone tell you who you are."

他还补了一句：“当时有很多报道从种种角度说明我们终将失败，这只是其中之一。现在，亚马逊是全球最成功的公司之一，革新了两种截然不同的产业。”

He added one more line: "At the time there were many reports arguing, from all sorts of angles, that we would ultimately fail, and this was just one of them. Today, Amazon is one of the most successful companies in the world, having transformed two utterly different industries."

《巴伦周刊》随后也发了回应：承认亚马逊的股价在1999年确实一路下跌，2001年跌破10美元，从高点蒸发了90%以上；但也承认，在贝佐斯的领导下，亚马逊确实成了电子商务和云计算的先驱。

Barron's later published a response of its own: it acknowledged that Amazon's stock had indeed slid through 1999, dropped below $10 in 2001, and lost more than 90 percent from its peak; but it also acknowledged that under Bezos's leadership, Amazon had genuinely become a pioneer of e-commerce and cloud computing.

一家媒体，用22年，从“亚马逊炸弹”改口成了“全球最佳零售商”。

One publication, over 22 years, changed its tune from "Amazon.bomb" to "the world's best retailer."

五、致2026：如何面对“不被看好”

V. To 2026: How to Face Being Written Off

2026年，每个人都被算法和舆论包围。你做任何一件事，都会立刻收到评价：点赞、唱衰、嘲讽、咒骂。

In 2026, everyone is surrounded by algorithms and public opinion. Do anything at all, and the judgments arrive at once: likes, doom-mongering, mockery, curses.

贝佐斯这个故事里最值得学的，不是“要坚持”这种大道理，而是他面对批评时那个微妙的姿态——“倾听，保持开放”。

The most instructive thing in Bezos's story is not the lofty principle of "you have to persist," but the subtle posture he took toward criticism—"listen, stay open."

他没有把批评者当成敌人。他没有说“媒体都是垃圾”“他们嫉妒我”。他说的是：先听，先开放，然后——不要让别人来定义你是谁。

He did not treat his critics as enemies. He did not say "the media are all garbage" or "they're just jealous of me." What he said was: listen first, stay open first, and then—don't let others define who you are.

这是一种很难的分寸感。绝大多数人在两种极端之间摇摆：要么被负面评价击垮，立刻改弦更张；要么彻底不听，一意孤行，最后撞得头破血流。而贝佐斯的做法是：把批评当信息，但不把批评当判决。

This is a very hard measure to strike. Most people swing between two extremes: either they are crushed by negative judgments and instantly change course, or they stop listening altogether, go their own way stubbornly, and finally crack their skulls against the wall. Bezos's approach was this: treat criticism as information, but not as a verdict.

对我们这代人来说，这可能比“坚持就能成功”更有用：

For our generation, this may prove more useful than "persist and you will succeed":

你可以听所有的声音，但最后那个判断，必须是你自己的。 而且你要为此负责。

You can listen to every voice, but the final judgment must be your own. And you must answer for it.

还有一点常被忽略：贝佐斯并不是靠“嘴上不服”熬过那几年的。1999年到2001年，他一边顶着全世界的唱衰，一边真的在做那些当时看不到回报的事——建仓储、铺物流、投技术、上线第三方卖家平台。等到泡沫破裂、对手纷纷倒下时，他手里握着的基础设施，反而成了别人再也追不上的护城河。

There is one more point often overlooked: Bezos did not survive those years by being stubborn with his words. From 1999 to 2001, even as the whole world predicted his doom, he was genuinely doing the things that showed no return at the time—building warehouses, laying out logistics, investing in technology, launching a third-party seller platform. By the time the bubble burst and rivals fell one after another, the infrastructure he held in his hands had become a moat no one else could ever catch up to.

所以，“不听批评”的前提是，“你真的在做对的事”。 如果只是固执，那叫刚愎；如果一边承受否定、一边把该做的功课做扎实，那才叫远见。这大概是贝佐斯留给我们最实用的一条：用行动回答批评，而不是用嘴。

So the premise of "not listening to criticism" is that "you really are doing the right thing." Mere stubbornness is called obstinacy; enduring rejection while doing the homework you owe, solidly, is called foresight. This is probably the most practical lesson Bezos left us: answer criticism with action, not with words.
