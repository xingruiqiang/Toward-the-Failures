老舍：写下《骆驼祥子》的人，走进了太平湖

Lao She: The Man Who Wrote Luotuo Xiangzi, and Walked into Taiping Lake

——“文艺界尽责的小卒，睡在这里”

"A dutiful common soldier of the literary world, sleeping here."

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂65 / 100约 2971 字 · 2971 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，我们习惯用“情绪稳定”夸人，习惯把委屈往肚子里咽。很多人受到不公，第一反应不是反抗，而是“忍一忍就过去了”。

In 2026, we are used to praising people for being "emotionally stable," and used to swallowing our grievances. When many people meet with injustice, their first instinct is not to resist but to tell themselves, "Just endure it and it will pass."

但有些委屈，是忍不过去的。

But some grievances cannot be endured.

1966年8月24日的夜里，一个67岁的老人，在北京城西北的太平湖边，独自坐了很久，最后走进了湖水里。他是中国第一个获得“人民艺术家”称号的作家，写过《骆驼祥子》《四世同堂》《茶馆》。

On the night of August 24, 1966, a sixty-seven-year-old man sat alone for a long time by the shore of Taiping Lake in northwestern Beijing, and at last walked into the water. He was the first writer in China to be named a "People's Artist," and he wrote Luotuo Xiangzi (Camel Xiangzi / Rickshaw Boy), Four Generations Under One Roof, and Teahouse.

他叫老舍。他生前给自己拟好了墓志铭：“文艺界尽责的小卒，睡在这里。”

His name was Lao She. In his lifetime he drafted his own epitaph: "A dutiful common soldier of the literary world, sleeping here."

一、从“小胡同”里走出来的作家

I. A Writer Who Came Out of the Small Lanes

1899年2月3日，老舍出生在北京一个旗人家庭。原名舒庆春，字舍予——“舍予”，是“舍弃自我”的意思，仿佛预示了他一生那种近乎忘我的投入。

On February 3, 1899, Lao She was born into a Banner family in Beijing. His original name was Shu Qingchun; his courtesy name, Sheyu—"sheyu" means "to give up oneself"—seemed to foreshadow the nearly self-forgetting devotion of his life.

他一岁半那年，八国联军攻进北京。他的父亲、身为护军的永寿，在镇守正阳门时力战殉国。家被劫掠，年幼的他因为被一只倒扣的箱子护住，才幸免于难。此后，他和母亲相依为命，过着清贫的日子。直到9岁，得到一位满族贵族的资助，他才得以进私塾读书。

When he was a year and a half old, the Eight-Nation Alliance marched into Beijing. His father, Yongshou, a guardsman, died fighting while defending Zhengyang Gate. The family was looted; the small child survived only because an overturned box shielded him. After that he and his mother depended on each other, living in poverty. Not until he was nine, with the support of a Manchu nobleman, was he able to enter a private school.

老舍一辈子都记着自己的来处。他的笔下，永远是北京的胡同、拉车的、卖艺的、小商人、巡警——那些被时代碾过、却依然要活下去的普通人。

All his life Lao She remembered where he came from. In his writing there are always the hutongs of Beijing, the rickshaw pullers, the street performers, the small merchants, the policemen—ordinary people crushed by their times yet still obliged to go on living.

1924年，他赴英国，在伦敦大学东方学院任教五年。就是在异国他乡，他写出了《老张的哲学》《赵子曰》，开始以“老舍”为笔名发表小说。

In 1924 he went to Britain and taught for five years at the School of Oriental Studies, University of London. It was there, in a foreign land, that he wrote The Philosophy of Lao Zhang and Zhao Ziyue, and began publishing fiction under the pen name "Lao She."

二、《骆驼祥子》和《茶馆》

II. Luotuo Xiangzi and Teahouse

1936年，他写出《骆驼祥子》。这部小说写的是一个拉车的年轻人祥子——他勤劳、倔强，只有一个愿望：凭力气买一辆属于自己的车。可这个愿望一次次被现实碾碎，最后他彻底沉了下去。

In 1936 he wrote Luotuo Xiangzi (Camel Xiangzi / Rickshaw Boy). The novel tells of a young rickshaw puller named Xiangzi—hardworking and stubborn, with only one wish: to buy a rickshaw of his own with the strength of his own body. But that wish was crushed by reality again and again, and in the end he sank completely.

祥子的悲剧，不只是一个人的悲剧，而是那个时代里所有“努力了却依然没活路”的人的悲剧。 到今天，很多人读到祥子，仍会觉得那里面有自己。

Xiangzi's tragedy was not merely one man's tragedy, but the tragedy of everyone in that era who "worked hard and still had no way to live." Even today, many readers find something of themselves in Xiangzi.

抗战时期，他主持中华全国文艺界抗敌协会的工作，写下了大量宣传抗日的作品。1940年代，他写出了《四世同堂》，写一个北平胡同里的大家族在日寇占领下的挣扎。

During the War of Resistance, he led the work of the All-China Anti-Japanese Association of Writers and Artists and wrote a great deal of work promoting resistance to Japan. In the 1940s he wrote Four Generations Under One Roof, about the struggles of a large family in a Beiping hutong under the Japanese occupation.

1949年新中国成立后，他回到北京。1950年，他写了话剧《龙须沟》——写一条臭水沟被改造、普通人的生活变好的故事。这部戏让他获得了“人民艺术家”的称号，他是新中国第一位获此称号的作家。

After the founding of New China in 1949, he returned to Beijing. In 1950 he wrote the play Dragon Beard Ditch—the story of a filthy waterway being improved and the lives of ordinary people getting better. The play earned him the title of "People's Artist"; he was the first writer in New China to receive it.

1957年，他写出了一生最重要的作品之一——话剧《茶馆》。这部戏借一家茶馆的兴衰，把半个世纪的中国近代史浓缩在三个小时里，出场人物几十个，个个活灵活现。它是中国话剧史上公认的巅峰之作。

In 1957 he wrote one of the most important works of his life—the play Teahouse. Through the rise and fall of a single teahouse, the play condenses half a century of modern Chinese history into three hours; dozens of characters appear, and every one of them is vividly alive. It is recognized as the summit of Chinese spoken drama.

三、1966年8月的那几天

III. Those Days in August 1966

1966年，“文化大革命”开始。

In 1966 the "Cultural Revolution" began.

老舍被红卫兵拉去参加一场又一场批斗会。他的罪名是“反动”、宣扬资本主义复辟。8月23日，他和一批文艺界人士一起遭到批斗和殴打，受尽人格的侮辱。

Lao She was dragged by Red Guards from one struggle session to the next. His crimes were being "reactionary" and promoting a capitalist restoration. On August 23, together with a group of writers and artists, he was struggled against and beaten, and suffered every kind of humiliation to his dignity.

关于他去世前后的细节，说法不一。可以确认的是：1966年8月24日，他没有回家，而是在城北的太平湖边坐了很久。第二天清晨，人们在那里发现了他的遗体。

Accounts differ on the details surrounding his death. What can be confirmed is this: on August 24, 1966, he did not go home, but sat for a long time by the shore of Taiping Lake in the north of the city. The next morning, his body was found there.

他死后没有留下骨灰。据说，他生前留下的最后一句话，是关于“总理最了解我”的。

He left no ashes after his death. It is said that the last words he left in life concerned how "the Premier understands me best."

1978年，老舍得到平反，恢复了“人民艺术家”的称号。他的墓上，刻着他自己写的那句墓志铭：“文艺界尽责的小卒，睡在这里。”

In 1978 Lao She was rehabilitated and his title of "People's Artist" was restored. On his tomb is carved the epitaph he wrote himself: "A dutiful common soldier of the literary world, sleeping here."

四、他为什么是“人民的”

IV. Why He Belongs to "the People"

老舍一生有一个很特别的地方：他始终站在普通人这一边。

There was something very particular about Lao She throughout his life: he always stood on the side of ordinary people.

抗战时期，他主持中华全国文艺界抗敌协会的工作，是当时少有的能被各方都接受的“无党派组织者”。1946年，他应邀赴美讲学；1949年新中国成立后，他回到北京。回国之后，他的创作力不降反升，写出了《龙须沟》《茶馆》这样的高峰之作。

During the War of Resistance he led the work of the All-China Anti-Japanese Association of Writers and Artists, and was one of the rare "nonpartisan organizers" acceptable to every side. In 1946 he was invited to lecture in the United States; after the founding of New China in 1949, he returned to Beijing. Once home, his creative powers did not decline but rose, producing such peaks as Dragon Beard Ditch and Teahouse.

他不写帝王将相，不写才子佳人。他写的是拉车的、卖艺的、开茶馆的、当巡警的。他说自己是“文艺界尽责的小卒”——一个“小卒”，却用最深的同理心，写下了那个时代最广大的中国人的样子。

He did not write of emperors and generals, nor of gifted scholars and beautiful ladies. He wrote of rickshaw pullers, street performers, teahouse keepers, policemen. He called himself "a dutiful common soldier of the literary world"—a "common soldier," yet with the deepest empathy he wrote down what the great majority of Chinese in that era were like.

五、他写的不是“悲惨”，而是“人”

V. He Wrote Not of "Misery," but of "People"

很多人以为，老舍写的都是苦。其实不是。老舍最了不起的地方，是幽默。

Many people assume that all Lao She wrote was suffering. In fact it was not. Lao She's greatest gift was humor.

他是天生的语言大师。他能把最苦的事，写得让你又想笑又想哭。他笔下的北京话活色生香，每一个人物一开口，你就能看见他长什么样、站在哪、在做什么。

He was a born master of language. He could write the bitterest things so that you wanted both to laugh and to cry. The Beijing speech on his pages is vivid and alive; the moment a character opens his mouth, you can see what he looks like, where he stands, what he is doing.

他也从不把自己当成“高高在上的作家”。他写祥子的车，能写清一辆人力车怎么租、怎么修、怎么上油；他写茶馆的掌柜，能写清一壶茶怎么续、一桌客人怎么安顿。这种“贴着地面”的写法，来自他对普通人最深的尊重。

Nor did he ever regard himself as a writer "up on high." Writing of Xiangzi's rickshaw, he could explain exactly how one was rented, repaired, and oiled; writing of a teahouse keeper, he could explain exactly how a pot of tea was refilled and how a table of guests was seated. This "close to the ground" way of writing came from his profound respect for ordinary people.

举一个例子：他写北京的春天，写老北京人怎么过年、怎么喝茶、怎么在院子里种一架葡萄。这种“生活感”，让他的作品不像“控诉”，而更像“保存”——他保存的是一个正在消失的北京，一个已经被时代碾过的民间。 直到今天，很多人读老舍，读到的不是历史，而是自己家里那位老人的口音。

Take one example: he wrote of spring in Beijing, of how old Beijingers celebrated the New Year, drank tea, and grew a trellis of grapes in the courtyard. This "texture of life" makes his work feel less like an "indictment" than like "preservation"—what he preserved was a Beijing that was disappearing, a common world already ground over by the times. Even today, many people who read Lao She find not history, but the accent of an old person in their own family.

六、致2026：关于“尊严”这件事

VI. To 2026: On the Matter of "Dignity"

2026年，我们常常讨论“体面生活”。但老舍的一生提醒我们：比生活条件更基本的，是一个人的尊严。

In 2026 we often discuss "a decent life." But Lao She's life reminds us that more fundamental than living conditions is a person's dignity.

老舍不是不能忍。他一生经历过战乱、贫穷、流亡，也一直在“尽责”。可当侮辱超过了人能承受的限度，他选择用一种决绝的方式，守住了最后边界的完整。

Lao She was not incapable of endurance. He lived through war, poverty, and exile, and he never stopped "doing his duty." But when the humiliation exceeded what a person can bear, he chose a final, resolute way to keep intact the last boundary.

我们当然不希望任何人走到那一步。但老舍的故事里有一点，值得今天的我们记住：

Of course we would not wish anyone to reach that point. But there is one thing in Lao She's story that we today should remember:

一个人可以被要求承担责任、接受批评、改正错误，但不该被剥夺作为一个人的基本尊严。

A person can be asked to bear responsibility, to accept criticism, to correct mistakes—but should never be stripped of basic human dignity.

对2026年正在职场、关系、生活里受着委屈的人来说，老舍的那句墓志铭里有个词很重——“尽责”。他一生都在尽责：对语言尽责，对笔下的人物尽责，对时代尽责。他最后的选择，不是逃避责任，而是拒绝以失去尊严的方式继续“活着”。

For those in 2026 who are suffering grievances at work, in relationships, in life, there is a heavy word in Lao She's epitaph—"dutiful." All his life he did his duty: to language, to the people he wrote about, to his times. His final choice was not to escape responsibility, but to refuse to go on "living" in a way that cost him his dignity.

这大概就是一种最朴素、也最了不起的骨气。

This is perhaps the plainest and also the most remarkable kind of backbone.

今天，我们很难再去追究1966年那个夜晚的全部细节。但有一件事是可以确定的：老舍不是因为“活得不够好”而死，而是因为“被剥夺了做人的体面”而死。 记住这一点，比记住任何作品名都重要——它提醒我们，在任何时代，都要给身边的人留一点体面。这也许是纪念他最好的方式。

Today it is hard for us to recover every detail of that night in 1966. But one thing is certain: Lao She did not die because he "failed to live well enough," but because "the decency of being human was taken from him." Remembering this matters more than remembering the title of any work—it reminds us that in any era, we must leave those around us a little decency. Perhaps that is the best way to honor him.
