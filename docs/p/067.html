萧红：31岁死在香港的“文学洛神”

Xiao Hong: The 'Goddess of Literature' Who Died in Hong Kong at Thirty-One

——“半生尽遭白眼冷遇……身先死，不甘，不甘。”

—“Scorned and slighted all my life… to die before my time—I am not resigned, not resigned.”

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂67 / 100约 2967 字 · 2967 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，很多年轻人正在大城市里漂泊。租来的房子、换过好几份工作、关系里总是受伤、身体也开始亮红灯——我们管这种状态叫“漂着”。

In 2026, many young people are drifting through the big cities. A rented apartment, a string of different jobs, wounds that keep piling up in relationships, a body starting to flash its warnings—we call this condition “drifting.”

有一个人，漂了整整一生。从黑龙江到哈尔滨，从北京到青岛，从上海到东京，从武汉到重庆，最后落脚香港。她一生有过十几次“离开”，从来没有在一个地方真正住满过两年。

There was one person who drifted her whole life long. From Heilongjiang to Harbin, from Beijing to Qingdao, from Shanghai to Tokyo, from Wuhan to Chongqing, and at last settling in Hong Kong. In her lifetime she “left” more than a dozen times, and never once stayed in a single place for a full two years.

她31岁病死在战火中的香港。她叫萧红。她被后人称为“二十世纪三十年代的文学洛神”。

She died of illness at thirty-one, in a Hong Kong engulfed by war. Her name was Xiao Hong. Later generations called her “the Goddess of Literature of the 1930s.”

一、从呼兰河逃出来的女孩

I. The Girl Who Ran Away from the Hulan River

1911年6月1日，萧红出生在黑龙江省呼兰县（今哈尔滨市呼兰区）。她原名张廼莹。

On June 1, 1911, Xiao Hong was born in Hulan County, Heilongjiang Province (today Hulan District of Harbin). Her original name was Zhang Naiying.

她的童年并不幸福。生母在她8岁时病故，父亲续娶。唯一真正疼爱她的人，是祖父。祖父去世后，她对那个家就再没有留恋了。

Her childhood was not a happy one. Her birth mother died of illness when she was eight, and her father remarried. The only person who truly loved her was her grandfather. After he died, she had no attachment left to that home.

1930年，初中毕业的她不顾家庭反对，为抗婚离家出走，去了北平读书。此后，她的人生就和“漂泊”这两个字绑在了一起。

In 1930, having finished middle school, she defied her family's opposition and ran away from home to resist an arranged marriage, going to Beiping to study. From then on, her life was bound to the word “wandering.”

1932年，她在哈尔滨遇到萧军。两人一起生活，一起写作。因为欠了旅馆的钱，她还被扣在旅馆里，最后是松花江发大水那晚，萧军趁夜划着小船把她救出来的。

In 1932 she met Xiao Jun in Harbin. The two lived together and wrote together. Because she owed money to a hotel, she was held there; in the end, on the night the Songhua River flooded, Xiao Jun rowed a small boat out in the dark and rescued her.

1933年，她以“悄吟”为笔名发表了第一篇小说《弃儿》，从此踏上文学之路。

In 1933, under the pen name Qiaoyin, she published her first short story, “The Abandoned Child” (Qier), and set foot on the path of literature.

二、鲁迅为她作序

II. Lu Xun Wrote the Preface for Her

1934年，因为作品触及日伪统治，萧红和萧军被迫逃离哈尔滨，辗转到了青岛，之后又去了上海。

In 1934, because their work touched on life under the Japanese puppet regime, Xiao Hong and Xiao Jun were forced to flee Harbin. They made their way to Qingdao, and then on to Shanghai.

在上海，他们得到了鲁迅的帮助。鲁迅不仅指点他们的创作，还关心他们的生活，把他们介绍给茅盾、聂绀弩、叶紫、胡风等人。

In Shanghai they received Lu Xun's help. He not only guided their writing but took an interest in their daily lives, introducing them to Mao Dun, Nie Gannu, Ye Zi, Hu Feng, and others.

1935年，萧红的中篇小说《生死场》出版，鲁迅亲自为她作序，胡风写后记。这本书写的是北方农村在“九一八”前后的真实图景，引起了巨大反响。萧红一举成名。

In 1935, Xiao Hong's novella Shengsi Chang (The Field of Life and Death) was published, with a preface written by Lu Xun himself and an afterword by Hu Feng. The book painted a true picture of rural northern China around the time of the “September 18” Incident, and it caused a great stir. Xiao Hong became famous overnight.

鲁迅是她一生中最重要的“精神依靠”。1936年鲁迅去世时，她正在日本，写下《海外的悲悼》；此后多年，她多次撰文回忆鲁迅——《回忆鲁迅先生》《记忆中的鲁迅先生》……

Lu Xun was the most important “spiritual anchor” of her life. When he died in 1936, she was in Japan, and she wrote “Mourning Abroad” (Haiwai de Beidao); in the years that followed she wrote about him again and again—“Recalling Mr. Lu Xun” (Huiyi Lu Xun Xiansheng), “Mr. Lu Xun in My Memory” (Jiyi zhong de Lu Xun Xiansheng)…

三、一生中的那些“离开”

III. All Those “Leavings” in a Lifetime

萧红的感情生活，是她传记里最让人心疼的部分。

Xiao Hong's emotional life is the most heartbreaking part of her biography.

她和萧军一起生活了六年，从哈尔滨到上海，从上海到武汉、临汾、西安，最终还是分开了——两人在感情上都受过伤，这段关系里既有相互成就，也有彼此消耗。

She and Xiao Jun lived together for six years, from Harbin to Shanghai, from Shanghai to Wuhan, Linfen, and Xi'an, and in the end they parted. Both were wounded in the relationship; it was a bond that both made each of them and wore each of them down.

1938年，她和端木蕻良结婚。在婚礼上，有人提议新人讲恋爱经过，她说了一段很低调、也很心酸的话，大意是：她只想过“正常的老百姓式的夫妻生活”——没有争吵、没有打闹、没有不忠、没有讥笑，有的只是互相谅解、爱护、体贴。

In 1938 she married Duanmu Hongliang. At the wedding, when someone suggested the newlyweds tell the story of their courtship, she said something very modest and very sad, to the effect that she wanted only a “normal, ordinary sort of married life”—no quarreling, no fighting, no infidelity, no mockery; only mutual understanding, care, and tenderness.

但现实并没有给她这样的生活。婚后不久，日军轰炸武汉，端木蕻良一人乘船去了重庆，把大着肚子的她留在了武汉。她历尽艰难才辗转到达重庆，在友人家中生下一个孩子，孩子不久夭折。

But reality did not grant her such a life. Not long after the wedding, the Japanese bombed Wuhan, and Duanmu Hongliang took a boat to Chongqing alone, leaving her, heavily pregnant, behind in Wuhan. After great hardship she made her way to Chongqing, where she gave birth to a child at a friend's home; the child died soon after.

1940年，她和端木蕻良到了香港，住在九龙。

In 1940 she and Duanmu Hongliang arrived in Hong Kong and lived in Kowloon.

从1927年到1942年，她有过15次以上的“离开”，住过哈尔滨、北京、青岛、上海、东京、武汉、临汾、西安、重庆、香港等地。她从来没有在一个地方真正住过两年以上。1934年到1936年，她在上海就至少换过7个住处。

Between 1927 and 1942 she “left” more than fifteen times, living in Harbin, Beijing, Qingdao, Shanghai, Tokyo, Wuhan, Linfen, Xi'an, Chongqing, Hong Kong, and elsewhere. She never truly lived in one place for more than two years. From 1934 to 1936 alone, she moved at least seven times in Shanghai.

文艺批评家李健吾曾用一段话形容她，说她“好像一个嫩芽，有希望长成一棵大树，但是虫咬了根，一直就在挣扎之中过活”。

The literary critic Li Jianwu once described her this way: she was “like a tender shoot that held the promise of growing into a great tree, but a worm has bitten its root, and it lives on in a constant struggle.”

四、1942年1月，香港

IV. January 1942, Hong Kong

在香港，萧红写完了她最重要的作品——《呼兰河传》（1940年12月完稿）。这部小说写她童年的故乡，写呼兰河边的风物与人，写那些被时代和贫穷困住的人们。它是中国现代文学中极特别的一部作品，介于小说、散文与回忆之间。

In Hong Kong, Xiao Hong finished her most important work, Hulanhe Zhuan (Tales of Hulan River) (completed in December 1940). The novel is about the hometown of her childhood, the landscape and the people along the Hulan River, and those trapped by their times and by poverty. It is a singular work in modern Chinese literature, standing somewhere between fiction, essay, and memoir.

她的身体越来越差。1941年，她被发现患有肺结核。

Her health grew steadily worse. In 1941 she was found to be suffering from tuberculosis.

1941年12月8日，太平洋战争爆发，日军占领九龙。1942年1月12日，日军占领香港。萧红病情急剧加重，被送进跑马地的养和医院。

On December 8, 1941, the Pacific War broke out and the Japanese army occupied Kowloon. On January 12, 1942, the Japanese took Hong Kong. Xiao Hong's condition worsened sharply, and she was taken to the Hong Kong Sanatorium and Hospital in Happy Valley.

接下来发生了一件令人扼腕的事：医生将她误诊为“喉瘤”（亦有写作“喉癌”），给她做了喉管手术。手术之后，她不能发声，也无法正常进食，身体迅速衰弱。

Then came a deeply regrettable turn: the doctors misdiagnosed her with a “throat tumor” (sometimes recorded as “throat cancer”) and operated on her windpipe. After the surgery she could not speak and could not eat properly, and her body failed rapidly.

1月18日，她被转入玛丽医院。后来玛丽医院被日军接管，她又转到了临时医院。

On January 18 she was transferred to Queen Mary Hospital. Later, when Queen Mary Hospital was taken over by the Japanese, she was moved again to a temporary hospital.

1942年1月22日上午，萧红在香港病逝，年仅31岁。

On the morning of January 22, 1942, Xiao Hong died of illness in Hong Kong. She was only thirty-one.

临终前，她在纸上写下（据骆宾基《萧红小传》记载）这样的话：

Shortly before she died, she wrote these words on paper (as recorded in Luo Binji's A Short Biography of Xiao Hong):

“我将与蓝天碧水永处，留下那半部《红楼》给别人写了。”

“I shall dwell forever with the blue sky and green waters, and leave that unfinished half of Dream of the Red Chamber for others to write.”

“半生尽遭白眼冷遇……身先死，不甘，不甘。”

“Scorned and slighted all my life… to die before my time—I am not resigned, not resigned.”

她去世前的44天里，一直守在她身边的，是小她六岁的作家骆宾基。

In the forty-four days before her death, the one who stayed at her side was the writer Luo Binji, six years her junior.

她最初葬在香港浅水湾。新中国成立后，她的骨灰被迁至广州银河公墓，实现了她“回到祖国大陆”的遗愿。在她的家乡呼兰河畔，后来建起了萧红故居纪念馆。

She was first buried at Repulse Bay in Hong Kong. After the founding of New China, her ashes were moved to the Yinhe Cemetery in Guangzhou, fulfilling her dying wish to “return to the mainland.” By the Hulan River in her hometown, a memorial hall at Xiao Hong's former residence was later built.

五、致2026：关于“挣扎着活过”

V. To 2026: On “Having Struggled to Live”

2026年，我们习惯用“成功”或“失败”来给人分类。按这个标准，萧红的一生几乎全是“失败”：没读完书、感情一再受伤、孩子没能留住、穷、病、31岁客死他乡，写下的大作品还没来得及被世界充分认识。

In 2026 we are used to sorting people into “successes” and “failures.” By that standard, Xiao Hong's life was almost entirely a “failure”: she never finished school, was wounded again and again in love, could not keep her children, was poor, was ill, died in a foreign place at thirty-one, and her great works had not yet been fully recognized by the world.

但她留下的近百万字，写的是另一种东西：一个人在最动荡、最匮乏、最不被善待的处境里，仍然坚持写。

But the nearly one million words she left behind are about something else: a person who, in the most turbulent, the most deprived, the most unkind of circumstances, still kept writing.

她写的不是“我多苦”，而是呼兰河边的风、祖父的园子、小城里的婚丧嫁娶、那些在尘土里挣扎的普通人。她把一生的漂泊与疼痛，酿成了文字的质地。

What she wrote was not “how I suffered,” but the wind by the Hulan River, her grandfather's garden, the weddings and funerals of a small town, the ordinary people struggling in the dust. She distilled a lifetime of wandering and pain into the very texture of her prose.

对我们这代人来说，萧红的故事不该被读成“才女薄命”的哀叹。它更接近一种提醒：

For our generation, Xiao Hong's story should not be read as a lament over the short life of a gifted woman. It is closer to a reminder:

人生的价值，不一定要用“活得顺不顺”来衡量。 有些人终身颠沛，却把颠沛变成了作品；有些人一生安稳，却什么也没留下。

The value of a life need not be measured by how smoothly it went. Some people are uprooted all their lives, yet turn that uprooting into their work; others live in comfort their whole lives, yet leave nothing behind.

她临终那句“不甘，不甘”，当然是对命运的抗争。但换个角度看，一个人能在31年的生命里，写下《生死场》《呼兰河传》这样的人，她已经站在“不甘”之上了。

Her dying words, “not resigned, not resigned,” were of course a defiance of fate. But seen another way, a person who within thirty-one years of life could write works like Shengsi Chang (The Field of Life and Death) and Hulanhe Zhuan (Tales of Hulan River) has already risen above that “not resigning.”

她只活了31年，却写了近百万字。这个体量本身就说明：她是在怎样逼着自己写。

She lived only thirty-one years, yet wrote nearly a million words. The sheer volume tells us how hard she drove herself to write.

Sources and Notes

2. “原名张廼莹（一作张迺莹）”“笔名萧红、悄吟等”——为公开资料；原名用字在不同材料中有异体写法，本文两说并存。

3. “1932年松花江决堤夜、萧军划船把她从旅馆救出”——出自公开传记。

3. “The night the Songhua River burst its banks in 1932, Xiao Jun rowed a boat out and rescued her from the hotel”—from published biographies.

4. “鲁迅为《生死场》作序、胡风写后记”——为公认史实。

4. “Lu Xun wrote the preface to Shengsi Chang (The Field of Life and Death) and Hu Feng wrote the afterword”—an accepted historical fact.

5. “1938年与端木蕻良婚后、武汉遭轰炸时他先行离开”——综合公开报道与传记资料（张耀杰《民国红粉》相关章节等）。注：关于端木蕻良当时离开的具体原因与责任，文学史上评价不一，本文只陈述“他先行离开”这一事实，不作道德定罪。

5. “After her marriage to Duanmu Hongliang in 1938, he left first when Wuhan came under bombing”—compiled from public reports and biographical materials (the relevant chapter of Zhang Yaojie's Minguo Hongfen (Beauties of the Republic), among others). Note: literary historians disagree about the specific reasons for, and the blame attached to, Duanmu Hongliang's departure at the time; this essay states only the fact that “he left first” and does not pass moral judgment.

6. “孩子生下后夭折”——不同材料对第一个孩子（与汪恩甲所生）与第二个孩子（与端木蕻良所生）的结局记述有差异，本文采用较通行的叙述（送人/夭折），并承认细节存在争议。

6. “The child died after birth”—accounts differ on the fates of her first child (with Wang Enjia) and her second child (with Duanmu Hongliang); this essay follows the more common account (given away / died young) and acknowledges that the details are disputed.

7. 需重点辨析：萧红去世日期，多数权威资料记为1942年1月22日，但也有材料写作1月23日（如部分军网资料）。本文采用“1月22日”这一主流说法。

7. A point requiring careful scrutiny: most authoritative sources give her death date as January 22, 1942, but some materials write January 23 (for example, certain China Military Online articles). This essay adopts the mainstream account of “January 22.”

8. “被误诊为喉瘤（一说喉癌）并接受喉管手术”——为多家资料一致的叙述；“误诊”是通行描述，具体诊断名称在不同材料中有差异。

8. “Misdiagnosed with a throat tumor (some say throat cancer) and undergoing surgery on her windpipe”—a narrative consistent across multiple sources; “misdiagnosis” is the widespread description, though the exact diagnosis is given differently in different materials.

9. “临终遗言”——引自骆宾基《萧红小传》，属当事人记录，是流传最广的版本；文字在不同转述中略有差异。

9. “Her dying words”—quoted from Luo Binji's A Short Biography of Xiao Hong (Xiao Hong Xiaozhuan); as a first-hand account by someone who was present, it is the most widely circulated version, though the wording differs slightly in various retellings.

10. “15次以上'离开'”“在上海至少换过7个住处”“从未在一个地方住满两年”——出自张耀杰相关文章，属对传记材料的统计性概括。本文按此引用并标注来源属性。

10. “More than fifteen ‘leavings’,” “moving at least seven times in Shanghai,” and “never living in one place for a full two years”—from a relevant article by Zhang Yaojie, and a statistical summary of biographical materials. This essay cites it as such and notes the nature of the source.

11. “骨灰由香港浅水湾迁至广州银河公墓”——为公开史实。

11. “Her ashes were moved from Repulse Bay in Hong Kong to the Yinhe Cemetery in Guangzhou”—a public historical fact.
