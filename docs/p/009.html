爱因斯坦：专利局里的“三级技术员”

Einstein: The “Third-Class Technician” in the Patent Office

——在最不被看好的年份，他一个人改写了时间

— In his least promising years, he alone rewrote time

← 返回主页 Home第一辑　生不逢时的先行者9 / 100约 3034 字 · 3034 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

在2026年的今天，爱因斯坦是“天才”两个字的同义词。他的吐舌照片被印在T恤上，他的名字被用来形容一切极其聪明的人。可如果你穿越回1902年的瑞士伯尔尼，你会看到一个完全不同的人：一个二十三岁、刚毕业就失业、连中学教师都当不上的年轻人，靠朋友的父亲走后门，才在专利局谋到一个低级职位——三级技术员。

In 2026, Einstein is synonymous with the word “genius.” His photo sticking out his tongue is printed on T-shirts; his name is used to describe anyone of extraordinary intelligence. But if you traveled back to Bern, Switzerland, in 1902, you would see a completely different person: a twenty-three-year-old who was unemployed the moment he graduated, unable even to become a high school teacher, and who only landed a low-level post at the patent office—that of third-class technician—thanks to a friend's father pulling strings.

他的名片上不会写“未来的物理学革命者”。他的日常，是坐在办公桌前，一份一份地审查别人的电磁专利申请。

His business card would not read “future revolutionary of physics.” His daily routine was to sit at a desk and review other people's electromagnetic patent applications, one after another.

而就在这个“没人看得起”的位置上，他一个人，改写了时间。

And it was precisely in this position that “nobody thought much of” that he, all by himself, rewrote time.

一、一个“说话很晚”的孩子

I. A Child Who “Spoke Late”

1879年3月14日，爱因斯坦出生在德国乌尔姆的一个犹太家庭。

On March 14, 1879, Einstein was born into a Jewish family in Ulm, Germany.

关于他的童年，流传最广的说法是：他说话很晚。家里人回忆，他到三四岁才开口，以至于父母一度担心他智力有问题。（这属于家人回忆，并非医学诊断；但这个“笨孩子”的印象，跟了他一辈子。）

The most widely repeated story about his childhood is that he spoke late. Family members recalled that he did not begin to talk until he was three or four, so that his parents briefly worried he had an intellectual disability. (This is a family recollection, not a medical diagnosis; but the impression of a “slow child” followed him all his life.)

他在慕尼黑读中学时，极其厌恶那种死记硬背、靠纪律和服从维持的普鲁士式教育。老师说他不守规矩，他也确实不合群——他更愿意一个人读物理和数学。

While attending secondary school in Munich, he hated the Prussian style of education—rote memorization upheld by discipline and obedience. His teachers called him unruly, and he was indeed a loner: he preferred to study physics and mathematics on his own.

十五岁那年，他随家人迁往意大利米兰，索性离开了德国。十六岁，他干脆放弃德国国籍，成了一个无国籍者——直到1901年才加入瑞士国籍。

At fifteen, he moved with his family to Milan, Italy, and left Germany for good. At sixteen, he simply renounced his German citizenship and became a stateless person—only in 1901 did he take Swiss citizenship.

一个十几岁的孩子，主动选择了“没有祖国”。这件事本身就说明了他的性格：他从来不在乎“归属”，只在乎自己想弄清楚的问题。

A teenager who deliberately chose to have “no homeland.” That fact alone reveals his character: he never cared about “belonging,” only about the questions he wanted to puzzle out.

二、毕业即失业：他连一个教职都争不到

II. Graduation and Unemployment: He Couldn't Win Even a Teaching Post

1895年，十六岁的爱因斯坦报考苏黎世联邦理工学院（ETH），因为年龄不够、又缺中学毕业证书，落榜了。他只好去瑞士阿劳的一所中学补习了一年，次年才考进去。

In 1895, at sixteen, Einstein sat the entrance exam for the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology (ETH) in Zurich, but failed: he was too young and lacked a secondary-school diploma. He had to spend a year at a school in Aarau, Switzerland, to catch up, and was admitted the following year.

1900年，他毕业了。成绩中等。而更致命的是：因为他性格独立、看不惯权威，和教授（尤其是物理学教授韦伯）关系很差。

In 1900, he graduated, with middling grades. More damaging still: because he was independent-minded and had no patience for authority, he got along badly with his professors—especially the physics professor Weber.

结果就是——他留不了校，也找不到任何大学的教职。

The result: he could not stay on at the school, and could not find a university teaching post anywhere.

他到处求职：做家教，去中学代课，甚至在考虑要不要放弃物理、改行去卖保险。一个后来被奉为“世纪伟人”的人，二十出头时，是学术界一个标准的“失败者”。

He hunted for work everywhere: tutoring, substitute teaching at secondary schools, even considering whether to give up physics and go into selling insurance. A man later revered as a “great man of the century” was, in his early twenties, a textbook “failure” in academia.

1902年，靠好友马塞尔·格罗斯曼的父亲出面推荐，他终于进了伯尔尼的瑞士专利局，当上了一名“三级技术员”，负责审查电磁类专利申请。

In 1902, on the recommendation of the father of his close friend Marcel Grossmann, he finally entered the Swiss Patent Office in Bern as a “third-class technician,” examining electromagnetic patent applications.

在当时任何一个正统学者看来，这都是一个没有前途的“闲职”。它意味着：你被主流科学界淘汰了。

To any orthodox scholar of the day, this was a dead-end “sinecure.” It meant one thing: you had been cast out of mainstream science.

可爱因斯坦后来的很多研究，恰恰和他在专利局的工作有关——他要日复一日地思考“时间”“同时性”“信号传输”这些看似技术、实则哲学的问题。后来有人总结：相对论的种子，也许就埋在那张审查专利的办公桌上。

Yet much of Einstein's later work was intimately connected to his patent-office job. Day after day he had to think about “time,” “simultaneity,” and “signal transmission”—questions that seemed technical but were really philosophical. Someone later concluded: the seeds of relativity may have been buried right there, on that patent-examining desk.

三、1905：一个人，一支笔，一年四篇

III. 1905: One Man, One Pen, Four Papers in a Year

他在专利局待了七年（1902—1909）。白天上班，晚上写作。

He stayed at the patent office for seven years (1902–1909). By day he worked; by night he wrote.

1905年，被后世称为他的“奇迹年”。这一年，他在《物理学纪事》上接连发表了几篇论文，每一篇，后来都成了物理学的里程碑：

1905 is known to posterity as his “miracle year.” That year he published several papers in succession in the Annalen der Physik, and every one of them later became a milestone of physics:

· 一篇讲光电效应，直接催生了量子论（他因此获了诺贝尔奖）；

· One on the photoelectric effect, which directly gave rise to quantum theory (and earned him the Nobel Prize);

· 一篇讲布朗运动，用数学证明了分子、原子是真实存在的实体；

· One on Brownian motion, which used mathematics to prove that molecules and atoms are real, existing entities;

· 一篇讲狭义相对论，推翻了牛顿式的绝对时间与空间；

· One on special relativity, which overturned the Newtonian conception of absolute time and space;

· 还有一篇，给出了那个后来家喻户晓的公式：E=mc²（质能等价）。

· And one more, which gave the formula that later became a household phrase: E=mc² (mass–energy equivalence).

一个人，一年，四篇论文，四个方向。物理学被整个改写了一遍。

One person, one year, four papers, four directions. Physics was rewritten from top to bottom.

而最令人感慨的细节是：这几篇论文，尤其那篇狭义相对论，几乎不引用任何人的文献——几乎没有一个脚注。因为当时他不在任何学术圈里，没有图书馆的便利，也没有人跟他讨论。他一个人在伯尔尼的出租屋里，从最基本的假设出发，重新推导了整个宇宙。

Most striking of all: these papers—especially the one on special relativity—cite almost no one, with hardly a single footnote. For at the time he belonged to no academic circle, had no convenient library, and had no one to discuss things with. Alone in his rented room in Bern, starting from the most basic assumptions, he re-derived the entire universe.

（顺便澄清一个著名的传说：他的博士论文“因为太短被退回，只加了一句话才通过”。更接近事实的版本是——1901年他确实提交过一版论文，但1902年2月主动撤回了；1905年那篇《分子大小的新测定法》约24页，顺利通过，两位评审都给了很高评价。至于“太短、退回来、加一句话才过”，是爱因斯坦本人后来当笑话讲的一则轶事，见于他的传记。）

(A well-known legend deserves clarification here: that his doctoral dissertation was “sent back for being too short, and passed only after he added a single sentence.” The version closer to the facts: in 1901 he did submit a version of the dissertation, but withdrew it of his own accord in February 1902; the 1905 paper, “A New Determination of Molecular Dimensions,” ran to about 24 pages, passed smoothly, and was highly praised by both reviewers. The tale of “too short, sent back, passed after adding one sentence” is an anecdote Einstein himself later told as a joke, recorded in his biographies.)

四、成名之前：他一直在被拒绝

IV. Before Fame: He Was Rejected Again and Again

即便写出了那四篇论文，他也没有一夜成名——恰恰相反。

Even after writing those four papers, he did not become famous overnight—quite the opposite.

1905年之后的好几年，他的论文反响寥寥，学术界几乎没人在意。他继续待在专利局，继续在业余时间搞物理，继续申请大学教职，继续屡屡碰壁。

For several years after 1905, his papers drew little response; the academic world barely took notice. He stayed on at the patent office, kept doing physics in his spare time, kept applying for university posts, and kept running into walls.

直到1909年，他才终于离开专利局，去苏黎世大学当上副教授——距离他那“奇迹年”，已经过去了四年。

Only in 1909 did he finally leave the patent office to become an associate professor at the University of Zurich—four years after his “miracle year.”

真正的转折是1919年。那一年，英国天文学家通过日食观测，证实了广义相对论的预言。消息传遍世界，爱因斯坦一夜之间成了家喻户晓的名字。此时距离他发表狭义相对论，已经十四年。

The real turning point came in 1919. That year, British astronomers confirmed the predictions of general relativity through observations of a solar eclipse. The news spread around the world, and Einstein became a household name overnight. By then, fourteen years had passed since he published special relativity.

十四年。他等了十四年，才等到世界转过身来，看见他。

Fourteen years. He waited fourteen years for the world to turn around and see him.

（特别要说一句：他的成名，还带着一层荒诞——他讨厌被当成“天才偶像”。他说过：“我没有什么特别的才能，我只是极其好奇。”）

(One more thing worth saying: there was something absurd about his fame—he hated being treated as a “genius idol.” He once said: “I have no special talent. I am only passionately curious.”)

五、他到底“失败”在哪里

V. Where Exactly He “Failed”

如果用世俗标尺来衡量年轻的爱因斯坦，他几乎是“全败”：

Measured by worldly standards, the young Einstein was almost a total failure:

他考大学落榜；

He failed his university entrance exam;

他毕业即失业，连个中学老师都当不稳；

He was unemployed the moment he graduated, unable even to hold down a high school teaching job;

他进不了学术圈，只能在专利局当一个低级技术员；

He could not get into academic circles and could only be a low-level technician at the patent office;

他写出改变世界的论文，却好几年无人问津；

He wrote world-changing papers, and for years no one paid them any attention;

他申请教职，一次次被拒。

He applied for teaching posts, and was rejected again and again.

他的“失败”，不是能力不够，而是他不肯把自己塞进那个时代的模子里——不肯讨好教授，不肯服从权威，不肯为了一个位置而改变自己看问题的方式。

His “failure” was not a matter of insufficient ability. It was that he refused to squeeze himself into the mold of his era—refused to flatter professors, refused to obey authority, refused to change the way he saw problems just to obtain a position.

而恰恰是这份“不合时宜”，让他在没有学术圈、没有实验室、没有经费的地方，推开了那扇没人敢碰的门。

And it was precisely this “out-of-step” quality that let him, in a place with no academic circle, no laboratory, and no funding, push open the door that no one else dared to touch.

六、结语：致2026年，每一个“找不到位置”的人

VI. Conclusion: To Everyone in 2026 Who “Can't Find Their Place”

2026年，我们的处境，其实和1902年的爱因斯坦有某种相似：学历通胀，岗位收缩，“对口”的机会越来越少；有人名校毕业却找不到工作，有人三十多岁被优化，有人做着一份“没前途”的工作，怀疑自己是不是废了。

In 2026, our situation is in some ways similar to Einstein's in 1902: credentials are inflated, jobs are shrinking, and chances to work in one's own field grow ever rarer. Some graduate from elite schools and still can't find work; some are “optimized out” in their thirties; some hold a job with “no future” and wonder whether they are simply useless.

爱因斯坦当年，就在那间专利局里。他的头衔是“三级技术员”，一个听上去毫无未来的职位。可他没有停止思考。

Back then, Einstein was in that patent office. His title was “third-class technician,” a post that sounded utterly without a future. But he never stopped thinking.

他给了这个世界两个答案：

He gave the world two answers:

第一，现在的位置，不决定你能走多远。真正的创造力，往往发生在体制的边缘，而不是中心。

First, your present position does not determine how far you can go. Real creativity often happens at the margins of a system, not at its center.

第二，重要的不是被人看见，而是你有没有一直在想那个真正让你着迷的问题。

Second, what matters is not being seen, but whether you keep thinking about the question that truly fascinates you.

所以，如果你此刻也觉得自己“找不到位置”——请记住，那个在伯尔尼专利局里埋头审专利的年轻人，后来在全世界每一本物理课本里，都占据着整整一章。

So if you, right now, also feel that you “can't find your place”—remember that the young man who buried himself in patent reviews in the Bern patent office went on to occupy a full chapter in every physics textbook in the world.

他成名的速度很慢。但他从没有停下。

His rise to fame was slow. But he never stopped.
