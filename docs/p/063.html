海明威：写了一辈子“硬汉”，最后却举枪对准自己

Hemingway: He Wrote Tough Guys All His Life, and in the End He Turned the Gun on Himself

——“一个人可以被毁灭，但不能被打败”

— "A man can be destroyed but not defeated."

← 返回主页 Home第四辑　用痛苦喂养的灵魂63 / 100约 2975 字 · 2975 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，我们开始越来越多地谈论“心理健康”。曾经被视为羞耻的抑郁、焦虑、创伤后应激，正在被越来越多人理解和正视。

In 2026, we are talking more and more about "mental health." Depression, anxiety, and post-traumatic stress, once looked upon as a shameful thing, are now understood and faced squarely by more and more people.

但有一个人，活在“硬汉”这两个字被神化的年代。他一生都在写“什么是勇敢、什么是尊严、什么是打不败的人”，他用拳头、猎枪、鱼叉和酒，塑造了一个近乎不朽的男性形象。

But there was one man who lived in an age when the word "tough guy" was made into an idol. All his life he wrote about what courage is, what dignity is, what an unbeatable man is. With his fists, his hunting rifle, his harpoon, and his liquor, he shaped a male image that was all but immortal.

最后，1961年7月2日清晨，他在美国爱达荷州的家中，用一把猎枪结束了自己的生命。

In the end, in the early morning of July 2, 1961, in his home in the American state of Idaho, he ended his own life with a shotgun.

他叫欧内斯特·海明威。他的一生，是“硬汉神话”最耀眼、也最残酷的注解。

His name was Ernest Hemingway. His life was the most dazzling—and the most cruel—footnote to the myth of the tough guy.

一、从战场上带回的伤口

I. The Wounds He Brought Back from the War

1899年，海明威出生在美国伊利诺伊州的奥克帕克。

In 1899, Hemingway was born in Oak Park, Illinois.

1918年，第一次世界大战期间，19岁的他作为红十字会的救护车司机来到意大利前线。一枚迫击炮弹在他附近爆炸，他身负重伤，身上嵌入了两百多块弹片，但他在受伤的情况下还拖着另一名伤员后撤。他因此获得了意大利的勋章。

In 1918, during the First World War, at nineteen he came to the Italian front as an ambulance driver for the Red Cross. A mortar shell exploded near him, and he was gravely wounded, with more than two hundred pieces of shrapnel embedded in his body. Yet even while wounded he dragged another injured man back to safety. For this he received a decoration from Italy.

战争给了他两样东西：一枚奖章，和一辈子都没能彻底愈合的伤。弹片、失眠、噩梦、“对死亡的近距离凝视”，这些东西后来一直跟着他。

The war gave him two things: a medal, and a wound that never entirely healed for the rest of his life. The shrapnel, the sleeplessness, the nightmares, the close-up stare at death—all of it followed him ever after.

战后，他作为记者去了巴黎，认识了一批文学青年——菲茨杰拉德、庞德、乔伊斯……他成了“迷惘的一代”的代表作家。

After the war he went to Paris as a journalist and met a circle of young writers—Fitzgerald, Pound, Joyce... He became a representative writer of the "Lost Generation."

二、“硬汉”的诞生

II. The Birth of the "Tough Guy"

海明威的写作，是一种极具辨识度的“减法”。他不写华丽的形容词，句子短、硬、克制，像拳击一样一下一下砸过来。他提出的“冰山理论”至今仍是写作课的经典：只写露出水面的八分之一，剩下的让读者自己去感受。

Hemingway's writing is a kind of subtraction, unmistakable at a glance. He used no ornate adjectives; his sentences are short, hard, restrained, coming at you blow after blow like a boxer's punches. His "iceberg theory" is still a classic of the writing class: show only the one-eighth that floats above the water, and let the reader feel the rest.

他写的东西也很“硬”：《太阳照常升起》写迷惘的一代；《永别了，武器》写战争与爱情；《丧钟为谁而鸣》写西班牙内战；《老人与海》写一个老渔民和一条大鱼搏斗了三天三夜。

The things he wrote about were "tough" as well: The Sun Also Rises, about the Lost Generation; A Farewell to Arms, about war and love; For Whom the Bell Tolls, about the Spanish Civil War; and The Old Man and the Sea, about an old fisherman who battles a great fish for three days and three nights.

1952年，《老人与海》出版，两年后，他因此获得诺贝尔文学奖。这本书里那句台词，成了他一生的标签：

In 1952 The Old Man and the Sea was published, and two years later it won him the Nobel Prize in Literature. A single line from the book became the label of his whole life:

“一个人可以被毁灭，但不能被打败。”

"A man can be destroyed but not defeated."

他也活得像自己笔下的主角：拳击、狩猎、深海钓鱼。他一生痴迷于“面对危险时的尊严”。他去非洲猎狮，去古巴海钓，经历过两次飞机失事（1954年），头部、脊椎、肝脏、脾脏严重受伤，报纸甚至一度刊出他的“讣告”。

He also lived like the protagonists of his own books: boxing, hunting, deep-sea fishing. All his life he was obsessed with "dignity in the face of danger." He went to Africa to hunt lions and to Cuba to fish the deep sea; he survived two plane crashes (in 1954), taking severe injuries to his head, spine, liver, and spleen. Newspapers even ran his "obituary."

他把“硬汉”活成了一种信仰——受伤不算什么，痛苦不算什么，不能垮。

He turned the "tough guy" into a kind of faith—being hurt counts for nothing, being in pain counts for nothing, you must not break.

三、那些“硬汉”背后的事

III. What Lay Behind the "Tough Guy"

但海明威的人生从来不是“英雄故事”。

But Hemingway's life was never a "hero's story."

他有过四次婚姻，一次又一次地爱上、离开、伤害那些爱他的人。他酗酒，喝到身体垮掉。他的父亲在1928年也是自杀身亡的——这个家族的阴影，一直悬在他头上。

He married four times, falling in love over and over, leaving over and over, wounding the people who loved him. He drank heavily, until his body gave out. His father, too, died by suicide, in 1928—the shadow of that family hung over him the whole way.

到了晚年，各种伤病一起找上来：旧伤、酒精肝、高血压、糖尿病，还有严重的抑郁症。他一度接受了电击治疗（ECT），这被认为对他的记忆和写作能力造成了损伤——而“写不出来”这件事，对一个作家来说，比什么都致命。

In his later years, injuries and illnesses closed in all at once: old wounds, alcoholic liver disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, and severe depression. For a time he underwent electroconvulsive therapy (ECT), which is thought to have damaged his memory and his ability to write—and for a writer, being unable to write is more fatal than anything.

他曾经说过一句话：“这个世界如此美好，值得人们为它奋斗。” 说这话的人，最后却在痛苦的自白里写道，他感觉自己像一个“被击倒的人”，再也站不起来。

He once said: "The world is a fine place and worth the fighting for." And yet the man who said this, in his final, anguished confessions, wrote that he felt like a "beaten man" who could not rise again.

1961年7月2日清晨，他在爱达荷州凯彻姆的家中，用猎枪结束了自己的生命，享年61岁。

In the early morning of July 2, 1961, at his home in Ketchum, Idaho, he ended his own life with a shotgun, at the age of sixty-one.

四、《老人与海》：他写给自己的答案

IV. The Old Man and the Sea: The Answer He Wrote for Himself

1950年代初，海明威已经很久没写出让自己满意的作品，评论界开始有人说他“过气了”。

By the early 1950s, Hemingway had not written anything that satisfied him for a long time, and critics had begun to say he was "finished."

他用大约八周时间，写出了《老人与海》。故事很简单：古巴老渔夫圣地亚哥连续84天没有捕到鱼，第85天独自出海，钓到一条巨大的马林鱼，在海上与它搏斗了三天三夜。他最终把鱼杀死，却在返航途中被鲨鱼把鱼肉啃食殆尽，只拖回一副巨大的鱼骨架。

In about eight weeks, he wrote The Old Man and the Sea. The story is simple: Santiago, an old Cuban fisherman, has gone eighty-four days without a catch. On the eighty-fifth day he puts to sea alone, hooks a huge marlin, and battles it for three days and three nights. In the end he kills the fish, but on the way home sharks strip the flesh from it, and he tows back only an enormous fish skeleton.

这本书很短，却让他重新站上世界文坛的顶端。1953年它获得普利策奖，1954年海明威获得诺贝尔文学奖。

The book is short, yet it lifted him back to the top of world literature. It won the Pulitzer Prize in 1953, and in 1954 Hemingway received the Nobel Prize in Literature.

那句“一个人可以被毁灭，但不能被打败”，其实是他写给自己的。老渔夫最终什么也没带回来，但他没有输——因为他从未放弃搏斗。

That line—"A man can be destroyed but not defeated"—was in fact written to himself. The old fisherman brings nothing back in the end, but he has not lost—because he never gave up the fight.

这正是海明威一生的信念：结果可以被夺走，但姿态不能被夺走。

This was the creed of Hemingway's whole life: the outcome can be taken from you, but the posture cannot.

五、他真正留下的东西

V. What He Truly Left Behind

今天回看海明威，他真正的影响也许不在“他有多硬”，而在两件事。

Looking back at Hemingway today, his real influence may lie not in how tough he was, but in two things.

第一，他改变了现代小说的写法。那种短句、克制、只说八分之一的风格，影响了后来无数作家——他把“少说”变成了一种力量。

First, he changed the way the modern novel is written. That style of short sentences, of restraint, of telling only one-eighth, influenced countless writers after him—he turned "saying less" into a kind of power.

第二，他留下了“直面困境”的姿态。在一个推崇体面和安全的年代，他坚持写人如何面对失败、疼痛和死亡。

Second, he left behind a posture of facing hardship head-on. In an age that prized respectability and safety, he insisted on writing about how people confront failure, pain, and death.

但他自己，最终没能活成自己笔下的句子。这不是他的“失败”，而是“硬汉”这个标准本身的苛刻——它要求一个人永远不能垮，苛刻到一个亲手写出它的人，也承受不住。

But he himself, in the end, could not live up to the sentence he had written. This was not his "failure"; it was the cruelty of the standard of the "tough guy" itself—it demands that a person never break, and it is so cruel that even the man who wrote it with his own hand could not bear it.

值得一提的是，海明威在中国的读者非常多。《老人与海》自上世纪五十年代起被多次译成中文——张爱玲曾在香港翻译过这部作品——而那句“一个人可以被毁灭，但不能被打败”，几乎成了几代中文读者共同的记忆。

It is worth noting that Hemingway has a great many readers in China. The Old Man and the Sea has been translated into Chinese many times since the 1950s—Eileen Chang once translated it in Hong Kong—and that line, "A man can be destroyed but not defeated," has become a shared memory for several generations of Chinese readers.

六、致2026：关于“允许自己被打败”

VI. To 2026: On "Allowing Yourself to Be Defeated"

2026年，我们仍然生活在一种“必须坚强”的语境里。职场要求你抗压，生活要求你乐观，朋友圈要求你积极向上。示弱、求助、承认“我撑不住了”，仍然被很多人视为丢脸。

In 2026, we still live in a climate of "you must be strong." The workplace demands that you withstand pressure, life demands that you stay optimistic, your social feed demands that you be positive. Showing weakness, asking for help, admitting "I can't hold on anymore" is still seen by many as a disgrace.

海明威的悲剧，恰恰向他那一生宣扬的“硬汉精神”，提出了一个尖锐的问题：

Hemingway's tragedy puts a sharp question to the very "tough-guy spirit" he preached all his life:

如果“不许失败”“不许软弱”变成一种绝对的信仰，那它究竟是在塑造一个人，还是在摧毁一个人？

If "no failure allowed" and "no weakness allowed" become an absolute faith, then is it shaping a person, or destroying one?

他一生都在写“人不能被击败”，可他自己最终还是被击垮了。这不是对他作品的否定——那些关于勇气和尊严的书写，依然真实而有力量。但它提醒我们：

All his life he wrote that a man must not be defeated, and yet in the end he was broken. This is not a repudiation of his work—those writings about courage and dignity are still true and still powerful. But it is a reminder:

硬汉不是“不会痛的人”，而是“敢于承认自己会痛的人”。

A tough guy is not someone who never feels pain, but someone who dares to admit that he does.

对我们这代人来说，海明威的故事里最有用的一课，也许不是“要坚强”，而是反过来：真正的坚强，包括在撑不住的时候，愿意让别人知道。

For our generation, the most useful lesson in Hemingway's story may not be "be strong," but the reverse: true strength includes being willing to let others know when you cannot hold on.

如果你的身边有人在硬撑，请记得，很多人倒下之前，看起来都比谁都硬。

If someone near you is holding on by sheer force of will, remember: many people, before they fall, look tougher than anyone.

再说一句更实在的话：海明威值得被重新阅读，不是因为他的“硬”，而是因为他的“诚实”。他写战争，不回避懦弱；写爱情，不回避背叛；写英雄，也不回避他们的疲惫。真正的勇气叙事，从来不是“不会怕”，而是“怕着也上”。 这一点，比任何“硬汉”标签都更接近真实的他。他没能打败自己，但他写下的那些关于“如何面对一场注定赢不了的搏斗”的文字，至今仍在帮别人打他们自己的仗。

One more plain word: Hemingway deserves to be read again, not for his toughness, but for his honesty. He wrote of war without dodging cowardice; of love without dodging betrayal; of heroes without dodging their exhaustion. A true narrative of courage is never "fearlessness," but "going up even while afraid." That is closer to the real man than any "tough guy" label. He could not defeat himself, but the words he wrote about how to face a fight you are bound to lose are still helping others fight their own.
