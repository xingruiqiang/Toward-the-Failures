吴敬梓：散尽家产的“败家子”

Wu Jingzi: The “Prodigal” Who Squandered the Family Fortune

——他穷得冬天没钱取暖，只能和朋友绕着城墙走路

—He was so poor he could not afford heating in winter, and could only walk round the city wall with friends to keep warm

2026年，“把一手好牌打烂”是网络上最常见的一种惋惜。人们用这四个字，形容那些出身优越却最终落魄的人。

In 2026, “playing a winning hand badly” is one of the most common laments on the internet. With those few words people describe those who were born into privilege but ended in ruin.

而这个短语，几乎可以一字不改地用在一个人身上。他叫吴敬梓。他出身于安徽全椒一个累世做官的科举世家，早年继承了相当可观的家产，然后——被他花光了。族人骂他败家，乡里说他是个疯子。他后半生穷到冬天没钱生火，只能和朋友沿着城墙快步走圈取暖，戏称“暖足”。

And that phrase could be applied, almost word for word, to one man. His name was Wu Jingzi. He came from a family of examination-success bureaucrats in Quanjiao, Anhui, who had held office for generations; early in life he inherited a considerable fortune, and then—he spent it all. His kinsmen cursed him as a squanderer, the local people called him a madman. In the second half of his life he was so poor that in winter he had no money for a fire, and could only walk briskly round the city wall with friends to keep warm, a practice they jokingly called “warming the feet.”

可就是这个人，用后半生的潦倒，写出了《儒林外史》——中国最伟大的讽刺小说。

And yet it was this man who, out of the destitution of his later years, wrote Rulin Waishi (The Scholars)—China's greatest satirical novel.

他“败”掉了祖业，却“立”起了一部书。

He “ruined” the family estate, but he “raised up” a book.

一、名门公子，散尽千金

I. A Gentleman of a Great House Who Squandered His Fortune

1701年，吴敬梓出生在安徽全椒。他的家族很显赫：从曾祖到父辈，几代人考中进士、做过官，是当地有头有脸的“科举世家”。

Wu Jingzi was born in Quanjiao, Anhui, in 1701. His family was illustrious: from his great-grandfather down through his father's generation, several generations had passed the jinshi examination and held office—a locally prominent “family of examination success.”

吴敬梓年轻时，性格非常豪爽——甚至可以说是挥霍。他父亲去世后，他继承了一笔相当大的遗产：田地、房产、钱财。但他对“守财”毫无兴趣。他喜欢结交文人朋友，喜欢喝酒、宴饮、行侠仗义，只要朋友有难，他就掏钱。

In his youth Wu Jingzi was generous to a fault—one might even say extravagant. After his father died he inherited a quite substantial legacy: land, houses, money. But he had no interest whatever in “guarding wealth.” He liked to make friends among men of letters, to drink and feast and play the chivalrous hero; whenever a friend was in trouble, he opened his purse.

族人很不满：这是祖上几代攒下的家业，你怎么能这样花？渐渐地，“败家子”“疯子”的名声就牢牢贴在了他身上。

His kinsmen were deeply displeased: this was a fortune accumulated over generations—how could he spend it like that? Gradually the reputation of “prodigal” and “madman” stuck fast to him.

更麻烦的是，他在家族内部的争产纠纷里也吃了亏——他父亲是过继来的，家产的分配本就复杂。到最后，他名下的财产被瓜分、被消耗，三十岁出头时，他已经相当窘迫了。

Worse still, he also came off the loser in the family's internal disputes over the division of property—his father had been adopted into the family, and the distribution of the estate was complicated to begin with. In the end, the property in his name was carved up and used up; by the time he was in his early thirties he was already in considerable straits.

二、三十三岁，搬到南京

II. At Thirty-three, Moving to Nanjing

约1733年，三十三岁的吴敬梓做了一个决定：离开全椒，搬到南京。

Around 1733, Wu Jingzi, then thirty-three, made a decision: he left Quanjiao and moved to Nanjing.

他在南京秦淮河畔（一说大中桥一带）安了家。在这里，他成了一个真正意义上的“卖文为生”的读书人——没有官职，没有产业，靠写文章、帮人写信、朋友接济过日子。

He settled on the bank of the Qinhuai River in Nanjing (some say in the Dazhongqiao area). Here he became a scholar in the true sense of one who “lives by his pen”—no official post, no property, getting by on writing essays, writing letters for others, and the help of friends.

在最困难的时候，他的日子是这样的：冬天没有炭火取暖。于是，他和几个相熟的朋友，在夜里沿着城墙快步绕行，一边走一边聊天，走到身体发热，就当是取到了暖。他们给这件事起了个自嘲的名字——“暖足”。

In his hardest times, his days went like this: in winter there was no charcoal for heating. So he and a few close friends would walk briskly around the city wall at night, talking as they went, walking until their bodies grew warm, which they counted as having got their heat. They gave the practice a self-mocking name—“warming the feet.”

一边绕城走暖身子，一边谈文论诗——这就是一个“败家子”的晚年日常。

Walking round the city to warm up, and all the while talking of literature and poetry—such was the daily life of a “prodigal's” later years.

三、一边挨穷，一边写《儒林外史》

III. Enduring Poverty While Writing The Scholars

吴敬梓见过太多读书人。

Wu Jingzi had seen far too many men of letters.

他生在科举世家，自己也是考过秀才的人，还曾被人举荐去参加“博学鸿词”科的考试（这类考试是通过推荐选拔的特殊科举途径，他被荐后并未真正参加或未成行）。他太清楚这条路上的人，会为了一个功名，把自己扭曲成什么样子。

He was born into a family of examination success, had himself passed the xiucai examination, and had at one point been recommended to sit the “erudite and broadly learned” (boxue hongci) special examination (a special route into the examinations by recommendation; after being recommended, he did not in fact sit it, or did not go). He knew only too well what men on this road would twist themselves into for the sake of a degree.

于是，他决定把这些写下来。

So he decided to write it all down.

《儒林外史》一书，大约成书于他晚年在南京的这段时间（学界一般认为约在1750年前后成书）。它没有一条贯穿始终的主线，而是用一个个独立的故事，串起了清代读书人的众生相：

The Scholars was written roughly during these later years in Nanjing (scholars generally date its completion to around 1750). It has no single thread running through it; instead, story by story, it strings together a panorama of Qing-dynasty men of letters:

- 中举后高兴到发疯的范进；

- Fan Jin, who goes mad with joy after passing the provincial examination;

- 临死前为了两根灯芯挑不下眼、迟迟不肯断气的严监生；

- Yan Jiansheng, who on his deathbed will not close his eyes or breathe his last over the matter of two lamp-wicks;

- 表面道貌岸然、内里虚伪算计的一批“名士”……

- a whole set of “eminent gentlemen,” outwardly respectable and inwardly false and calculating…

他用极其克制的笔法，把科举制度下的荒唐和人性，一刀一刀地剖开来。鲁迅后来评价《儒林外史》，大意是它“秉持公心，指擿时弊”，是中国讽刺小说的巅峰。

With the most restrained of brushes he dissected, stroke by stroke, the absurdity and the human nature beneath the examination system. Lu Xun later said of The Scholars, in substance, that it “upholds a public spirit and points out the ills of the age,” and that it is the summit of Chinese satirical fiction.

四、书写成，人也走了

IV. The Book Finished, and the Man Gone

1754年，吴敬梓在扬州去世，年五十三岁。

In 1754, Wu Jingzi died in Yangzhou, aged fifty-three.

关于他的死，他的朋友程晋芳在《文木先生传》里记了一笔：他是到扬州访友，酒后又谈笑如常，夜里回去就病了，随后去世。他一生和朋友饮酒、清谈，最后也是在这样一场访友中走的。

About his death, his friend Cheng Jinfang recorded a line in “The Life of Mr. Wenmu”: he had gone to Yangzhou to visit a friend, and after wine chatted and laughed as usual; when he went back that night he fell ill, and soon died. All his life he had drunk and talked of things with friends, and at the last he went in just such a visit.

更让人感慨的是，《儒林外史》在他生前没有印行。这部书，是他死后由朋友、后人陆续整理刊刻，才流传开来的。程晋芳说他是“书成而卒”——书写成了，人也几乎就走了。

What is more moving still: The Scholars was not printed in his lifetime. It was only after his death, as friends and later generations gathered and printed it piece by piece, that it spread abroad. Cheng Jinfang said of him that “the book was completed and he died”—the book finished, the man all but gone.

他一生没有功名，没有做过官，没有商业成就，连家产都花光了。从一个世俗的眼光看，他就是一个彻彻底底的“败家子”。

In all his life he held no degree, served in no office, achieved nothing in business, and spent even the family fortune. By any worldly measure, he was a thoroughgoing “prodigal.”

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

吴敬梓的故事，对“把一手好牌打烂”的焦虑，给出了一种完全不同的解读。

Wu Jingzi's story offers a wholly different reading of the anxiety about “playing a winning hand badly.”

第一，他花掉的，可能不（只）是钱，是一个他不想要的活法。他的家族寄望他走的路是：守好家业、考取功名、光耀门楣。而他花了半生，主动从这个轨道上“下车”了。看起来是“败家”，实际上是一种代价极高的选择。他放弃了遗产，换取了自由——以及旁观者的清醒位置。

First: what he spent may have been not (only) money, but a way of living he did not want. What his family expected of him was this: guard the estate, win the degrees, bring glory to the house. And he spent half his life deliberately getting “off the train,” stepping down from that track. It looks like “wrecking the family,” but in fact it was a choice made at an extremely high price. He gave up his inheritance and in exchange bought freedom—and the clear-eyed vantage point of an outsider.

第二，只有站在体系之外的人，才能把这个体系看透。如果他一直是个顺风顺水的科举成功者，他写不出《儒林外史》。正因为他既见识过这个体系的顶端，又落到了它的外面，他才能把那层“功名”的皮，一刀剥下来。

Second: only one who stands outside the system can see all the way through it. Had he remained a smooth, successful product of the examination system, he could never have written The Scholars. It was precisely because he had seen the very top of that system and also fallen outside it that he could peel away, in one clean cut, that skin of “scholarly honor.”

所以，如果你此刻正被“我把事情搞砸了”的念头缠住——不妨换个角度想：你搞砸的，可能恰恰是别人替你安排的、而你并不想要的那条路。

So if you are right now entangled in the thought “I have wrecked everything”—try looking at it from another angle: what you wrecked may be exactly the road someone else laid out for you, the one you never wanted.

他失去的是一份家产，留下的却是一面照见几百年的镜子。

What he lost was a family fortune; what he left behind was a mirror that reflects several hundred years.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “吴敬梓把家产全花光了”——其家产确有大量消耗与散失，但成因复杂：既有他本人豪爽挥霍、乐善好施的因素，也与他父亲过继身份带来的族内争产纠纷有关。文中已并列这两方面，未将其简单归为“个人挥霍”。

7. “Wu Jingzi spent the entire family fortune”—his estate was indeed heavily depleted and scattered, but the causes are complex: both his own generous extravagance and love of doing good, and the disputes within the clan brought on by his father's adopted status. The text sets out both sides and does not reduce it simply to “personal extravagance.”

8. “他参加过博学鸿词科考试”——他被荐参加（或获得荐举资格），但需注意：他并未走完成功进入仕途的路径，相关记载细节（是否实际应试、过程如何）在史料中不够清晰，文中表述为“被举荐、但未成行/未成”，并保持概括。

8. “He sat the boxue hongci examination”—he was recommended (or obtained the qualification for recommendation), but note: he did not complete the path to successful entry into officialdom, and the details in the records (whether he actually sat it, how it went) are not clear; the text phrases it as “recommended but did not go / did not succeed,” keeping it general.

9. “《儒林外史》全部由他独立完成、成书时间明确”——学界对其成书时间与增补过程有不同讨论，且不排除友人的参与（如评点、整理）。文中以“约1750年前后成书”表述，未作精确断代。

9. “The Scholars was written entirely by him alone, with a definite date of completion”—scholars differ over its date of completion and the process of additions, and the participation of friends (commentary, arrangement) cannot be ruled out. The text uses “completed around 1750” and makes no precise dating.

10. “范进中举发疯'是他写的”——范进、严监生等均为《儒林外史》中人物，此处作为书中情节举例，非其个人经历，特此说明以免混淆。

10. “‘Fan Jin going mad after passing’ was his own experience”—Fan Jin and Yan Jiansheng are characters in The Scholars; they are cited here as examples of the book's plot, not as his personal experience, and this is noted to avoid confusion.
