格兰特：打仗是天才、做生意是笨蛋的“破产总统”

Grant: The "Bankrupt President"—A Genius at War, a Fool at Business

——他在战场上赢下了一切，却在钱上输光了一生

— He won everything on the battlefield, and lost a lifetime's money

← 返回主页 Home第二辑　屡败屡战的坚韧行者25 / 100约 3374 字 · 3374 chars中英对照 ✓ Bilingual

2026年，我们太熟悉一种人了：在公司里是顶梁柱，自己出去创业却一败涂地；在专业上无可替代，一碰理财就赔得底朝天。

In 2026, we know this kind of person all too well: a pillar of the company who is ruined the moment he tries to start something of his own; irreplaceable in his profession, yet wiped out the instant he touches an investment.

一百五十年前，有一个人把这种“偏科”演到了极致。他叫尤利西斯·辛普森·格兰特。他率领北军打赢了南北战争，逼降了南方名将李将军，当了两任美国总统。可他在打仗之外的世界里，几乎是灾难级的：种地失败、开店失败、投资失败、被合伙人骗到破产。他最后的几年，是一位身患绝症的老人，为了不让妻子一贫如洗，拼命写书。

A hundred and fifty years ago, one man took this kind of lopsidedness to its extreme. His name was Ulysses S. Grant. He led the Union Army to victory in the Civil War, forced the surrender of the great Confederate general Robert E. Lee, and served two terms as President of the United States. Yet in every world outside of war he was a walking catastrophe: he failed at farming, failed at shopkeeping, failed at investing, and was swindled into bankruptcy by a business partner. In his last years he was a dying old man, writing desperately to keep his wife from destitution.

他的回忆录，最终成了英语世界最伟大的军事回忆录之一。他用一支笔，赢下了人生最后一场战役。

His memoir would become one of the greatest military memoirs in the English language. With a single pen, he won the last battle of his life.

一、和平年代，他什么都干不好

I. In Peacetime, He Was Good at Nothing

1822年，格兰特出生在俄亥俄州。他本不想当兵，是因为父亲不愿意他接手家里的制革生意，才把他送进了西点军校。1843年毕业时，成绩平平。

Grant was born in Ohio in 1822. He had no wish to be a soldier. It was only because his father would not let him take over the family tanning business that he was sent to West Point instead. He graduated in 1843 with a thoroughly undistinguished record.

他打过美墨战争——尽管他内心并不认同那场战争。1854年，他因饮酒问题从军队辞职。（关于他“嗜酒”，后面会专门说明，这里面有相当程度的政敌抹黑。）

He fought in the Mexican-American War—though he did not believe in it. In 1854 he resigned his commission over his drinking. (His supposed "drinking problem" is discussed in detail later; much of it was smearing by his political enemies.)

脱下军装后，格兰特开始了他的“失败中年”：

Once out of uniform, Grant began his "failed middle years":

- 他当农民，在圣路易斯附近种地，住在一间他自嘲为“苦日子”（Hardscrabble）的小木屋里，做得一塌糊涂；

- He became a farmer outside St. Louis, living in a cabin he wryly named "Hardscrabble," and made a mess of it;

- 他在圣路易斯街头卖柴火贴补家用；

- He sold firewood on the streets of St. Louis to make ends meet;

- 他做过收租、经营过父亲与合伙人开的分店，也不擅长；

- He collected rents and ran a branch store opened by his father and a partner, and was no good at that either;

- 到内战爆发前，他在伊利诺伊州父亲的皮革店里当店员，几乎一无所有。

- By the eve of the Civil War he was a clerk in his father's leather shop in Illinois, with almost nothing to his name.

一个后来的军队统帅，在35岁那年，是别人店里一个卖皮具的伙计。如果人生在那一刻定格，他就是标准意义上的“混得最差的那种老兵”。

The man who would one day command an army was, at thirty-five, a clerk selling leather goods in another man's store. Had his life frozen at that moment, he would have been, by any standard, the worst-off kind of veteran there is.

二、战争，让一个人找回了自己

II. War Gave a Man Back to Himself

1861年，南北战争爆发。格兰特重新入伍。

In 1861 the Civil War broke out. Grant rejoined the army.

接下来的四年，他像是换了一个人。他先是拿下亨利堡、多纳尔森堡——在多纳尔森，他提出“无条件投降”（Unconditional Surrender），恰好与他名字的缩写“U.S.”对上了，从此多了一个绰号。1862年希洛战役，虽然血流成河、颇受争议，但林肯没有撤他，说了一句意思很明确的话：我不能因为他不肯为我打仗而换掉他，我缺的正是肯打仗的人。

Over the next four years he seemed to become another man. He first took Fort Henry and Fort Donelson—at Donelson he demanded "unconditional surrender," which happened to match his initials, "U.S.," and earned him a new nickname. At Shiloh in 1862, though the fighting was bloody and the battle much disputed, Lincoln refused to remove him, saying something unmistakable in meaning: I cannot drop a man for not fighting for me—the very thing I lack is men who will fight.

1863年，格兰特攻克维克斯堡，打通了密西西比河，把南方一分为二。1864年，他被任命为联邦军总司令，用持续不断的会战拖垮了李将军的军队。1865年4月9日，李在阿波马托克斯向格兰特投降。

In 1863 Grant took Vicksburg, opening the Mississippi and cutting the South in two. In 1864 he was appointed general-in-chief of the Union armies, grinding down Lee's army through relentless battle. On April 9, 1865, Lee surrendered to Grant at Appomattox.

受降仪式上，格兰特没有羞辱对手。他允许南方军官保留佩剑，让士兵带马匹回家去耕种。这种克制，为战后的和解留下了余地。战后，他成了与林肯并肩的民族英雄。

At the surrender, Grant did not humiliate his opponent. He let the Southern officers keep their swords and allowed the soldiers to take their horses home to their fields. That restraint left room for reconciliation after the war. When it was over, he stood as a national hero alongside Lincoln.

1869年，他当选总统，连任两届。任内他确实推动了重建、保护了黑人的权利（这是他被低估的一面），但他的政府卷入了大量腐败丑闻——不是他贪，而是他太轻信身边的人，且对金钱毫无判断力。

In 1869 he was elected president and served two terms. He did advance Reconstruction and protect the rights of Black Americans—an underrated side of him—but his administration was mired in a host of corruption scandals. Not because he was greedy, but because he trusted the people around him too easily and had no judgment whatever about money.

三、离开白宫后，他被骗到“只剩80美元”

III. After the White House, He Was Swindled Down to "His Last Eighty Dollars"

卸任后，格兰特做了一件极具讽刺意味的事：他想靠投资发家，也想借此融入他从政时结交的富豪圈子。

After leaving office, Grant did something deeply ironic: he wanted to make his fortune through investment, and to use it to fit into the circle of wealthy men he had come to know in politics.

结果，他的商业“战绩”继续惨不忍睹：投资墨西哥铁路，失败；几处房地产，失败；最后，他最大的坑来了。

The result: his business "record" stayed just as dismal. He invested in a Mexican railroad—failure. Several real-estate ventures—failure. And then came his greatest pitfall of all.

他的儿子与一个叫费迪南德·沃德的金融“神童”合开了“格兰特与沃德公司”。沃德看中的不是格兰特的钱，而是他这个名字的分量——只要这位前总统是合伙人，公司就能轻易吸引投资。格兰特被“华尔街的年轻拿破仑”的光环所惑，投入了自己几乎全部积蓄（其中相当一部分是借来的）。

His son went into partnership with a financial "wonder boy" named Ferdinand Ward to found Grant & Ward. What Ward wanted was not Grant's money but the weight of his name—with a former president as a partner, the firm drew investment with ease. Dazzled by the aura of "the young Napoleon of Wall Street," Grant put in nearly all his savings (a considerable part of it borrowed).

1884年5月，公司崩盘。沃德经营的是一个规模惊人的庞氏骗局，他携款潜逃。格兰特一夜之间几乎破产：他名下的钱只剩约80美元，妻子那边还有约130美元。

In May 1884 the firm collapsed. Ward had been running a Ponzi scheme on an astonishing scale, and he fled with the money. Overnight Grant was nearly destitute: about eighty dollars was left in his own name, and about a hundred and thirty in his wife's.

消息传开后，许多敬重他的退伍军人和普通民众给他寄钱。他收下了——不是因为不想硬气，而是因为他真的需要钱还债。他拒绝了朋友范德比尔特免除债务的好意，坚持变卖房产和收藏来还钱。

When the news spread, many veterans and ordinary people who admired him sent money. He accepted it—not because he lacked the spine to refuse, but because he genuinely needed the money to pay his debts. He turned down his friend Vanderbilt's offer to forgive the debt, insisting on selling his houses and his collection to make good on it.

就在破产后不久，他感到喉咙剧痛，去看医生。1884年秋，他被确诊为喉癌晚期——大多数学者认为，这与他几十年来一根接一根的雪茄有关。

Not long after the bankruptcy, he felt a severe pain in his throat and went to a doctor. In the fall of 1884 he was diagnosed with advanced cancer of the throat—most scholars believe it was tied to the cigars he had smoked one after another for decades.

四、最后一年，他用写作打完了最后一场仗

IV. In His Last Year, He Fought His Final Battle with a Pen

摆在63岁的格兰特面前的现实是：他快死了，而他的妻子朱莉娅将一文不名。

The reality facing the sixty-three-year-old Grant was this: he was dying, and his wife, Julia, would be left penniless.

这时，他的朋友马克·吐温找上门来。吐温多年前就劝他写回忆录，如今终于说服了他，并给了他一份远好于其他出版商的合同（作者分成约70%）。吐温后来回忆说，他这么做，很大程度上是因为不忍心看老朋友做出又一个“灾难性的商业决定”。

Then his friend Mark Twain came to him. Twain had urged him for years to write his memoirs; now he finally persuaded him, and offered a contract far better than any other publisher's (roughly a seventy percent author's share). Twain later recalled that he had done so largely because he could not bear to watch his old friend make yet another "disastrous business decision."

于是，格兰特开始了与死亡的赛跑。他每天写作四到六个小时，最多时一天能写数千字，全凭军人的效率与条理。他在家中堆满作战命令和地图，力求对每一场战役的细节都准确还原。嗓子哑了，他就口述；连口述都困难了，他就用纸条写字条传递，继续写。

And so Grant began his race with death. He wrote four to six hours a day, and on his best days several thousand words, driven by a soldier's efficiency and method. He filled his house with orders and maps, determined to reproduce the details of every battle accurately. When his voice failed he dictated; when even dictating grew too hard, he passed written slips of paper back and forth and kept writing.

1885年7月23日，格兰特在纽约州的一处山庄去世。他刚刚完成手稿没几天。

On July 23, 1885, Grant died at a retreat in upstate New York, only days after finishing his manuscript.

他的两卷本《回忆录》在他身后出版，成了当时的畅销书，为家人带来了约45万美元的收入（相当于今天的近千万美元），也为他赢得了文学上的不朽声誉。直到今天，很多历史学家仍认为，它是英语世界最出色的军事回忆录之一。

His two-volume Memoirs was published after his death, became a bestseller of its day, brought his family roughly $450,000 (close to ten million dollars today), and won him an enduring literary reputation. To this day many historians still rank it among the finest military memoirs in the English language.

一个在财务上失败了一辈子的人，用最后一年，凭一本书救下了他的家人，也完成了自己。

A man who had failed at money his whole life, in his final year, with a single book, saved his family—and completed himself.

五、致2026年的我们

V. To Us in 2026

格兰特的故事，讲的是一件很反常识的事：一个人可以在某个领域是天才，同时在另一个领域是彻底的笨蛋。

Grant's story tells us something quite counterintuitive: a person can be a genius in one field and a complete fool in another.

他打仗近乎天才，做买卖近乎灾难。这不是虚伪，也不是“聪明人装傻”，而是能力的边界真实存在——他在战场上能读懂地形、时机和人心，在生意场上却读不懂账本、合同和骗子。他最重要的失败，从来不是“不努力”，而是在自己不擅长的地方，用错了自己的相信。

He was nearly a genius at war and nearly a disaster at business. This was not hypocrisy, nor a clever man playing dumb—it was that the boundaries of ability are real. On the battlefield he could read terrain, timing, and men; in business he could not read a ledger, a contract, or a swindler. His most important failure was never "not trying hard enough"; it was misplacing his trust in the very places he was least suited to.

对2026年的我们，有两点提醒。

For us in 2026, there are two reminders.

第一，要认得出自己“能赢的地方”和“会输的地方”。格兰特一生最大的幸运，是内战把他放回了自己擅长的地方；他一生最大的教训，是退役后一次又一次闯进自己不擅长的地方。今天也一样：不是所有领域都值得你去拼，把劲用在你会赢的赛道上，比在错的赛道上硬扛重要得多。

First, recognize the places where you can win and the places where you will lose. Grant's greatest piece of luck was that the Civil War put him back where he was strong; his greatest lesson was that after leaving the army he wandered again and again into the places where he was not. The same holds today: not every field is worth fighting for. Spending your strength on the track where you can win matters far more than gritting your teeth on the wrong one.

第二，一个人真正的底色，是他在最后关头怎么选。破产、绝症、63岁——他本可以就这样垮掉。但他选择坐在书桌前，拼尽最后一口气，把能留给家人的东西写出来。他最后赢下的，不是战争，是一份对家人的责任。

Second, a person's true character shows in how they choose at the very end. Bankruptcy, a terminal illness, sixty-three years old—he could have simply collapsed. Instead he chose to sit at his desk and, with his last breath, write down what he could leave his family. What he won in the end was not a war but a duty to his family.

他这一生最后一次“战役”，没有枪声，只有一支笔。

The last "battle" of his life had no gunfire—only a pen.

需辨析者（已在正文中说明）

Points Requiring Clarification (Addressed in the Main Text)

7. “格兰特是酒鬼”——需谨慎。战前及内战期间确有关于他饮酒的记载，但这些叙述被其政敌大量夸大，且常常被用来构建一个不准确的“习惯性酗酒者”形象。现代传记（如切尔诺、Ronald C. White等）多认为他的饮酒是间歇性的，并未显著影响其军事与总统履职。文中按“存在争议、多被政敌夸大”处理，未将其定性为酗酒者。

7. "Grant was a drunkard"—treat with caution. There are indeed records of his drinking before and during the Civil War, but these accounts were much exaggerated by his political enemies and were often used to build an inaccurate image of a "habitual alcoholic." Modern biographies (Chernow, Ronald C. White, and others) generally hold that his drinking was intermittent and did not significantly affect his conduct as a commander or president. This chapter treats the matter as "disputed and much exaggerated by political enemies" and does not label him an alcoholic.

8. “格兰特当总统是个失败的总统、只懂打仗”——需分层。传统评价偏低，但其政府在保护黑人权利与推动重建方面有重要贡献，近几十年评价有所回升；其任内最大的问题确实是政府内的腐败丑闻（多与其识人不明有关）。

8. "Grant was a failed president who only knew how to fight"—this needs nuance. The traditional assessment was low, but his administration made important contributions to protecting Black rights and advancing Reconstruction, and its standing has risen in recent decades; the greatest problem of his tenure was indeed the corruption scandals within his government (largely a matter of his poor judgment about people).

9. 关于破产时其名下现金“只剩80美元、妻子另有130美元”的具体数字，来自常见史料转述，细节或有差异，文中已加“约”字。

9. The specific figures for the cash he held at the bankruptcy—"only eighty dollars left in his name, plus a hundred and thirty of his wife's"—come from commonly cited sources; details may vary, and the main text uses "about."

10. “回忆录是格兰特亲笔、无人代笔”——出版前曾有争议，格兰特与马克·吐温公开反驳了“由秘书巴多代写”的指控；现代主流看法认为该书是格兰特本人撰写的，助手在核对史实与提供材料上有协助。文中表述为“他本人写作、助手协助核查史实”。另：他那位战争部长兼作家亚当·巴多（Badeau）在后期被解雇，与回忆录写作的纠纷有关。

10. "The memoirs were written by Grant's own hand, with no ghostwriter"—there was controversy before publication, and Grant and Mark Twain publicly refuted the charge that his secretary Badeau had written the book; the modern mainstream view holds that Grant wrote it himself, with assistants helping to verify facts and supply material. The main text states it as "written by Grant himself, with assistants helping to check the facts." Note too: Adam Badeau, his Secretary of War and a writer, was dismissed late in the process, in connection with a dispute over the writing of the memoirs.
