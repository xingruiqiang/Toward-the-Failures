马云：高考考了三次、求职被拒了三十次的人

Jack Ma: The Man Who Failed the College Entrance Exam Three Times and Was Rejected Thirty Times Applying for Jobs

——“如果有一天我真要写一本书，书名就叫《阿里巴巴和1001个错误》”

—"If one day I truly write a book, the title will be Alibaba and the 1,001 Mistakes."

2026年，年轻人的焦虑有一大半来自“筛选”：学历筛选、简历筛选、面试筛选。一次被拒，就足以让人怀疑自己。

In 2026, more than half of young people's anxiety comes from "screening": credential screening, résumé screening, interview screening. A single rejection is enough to make a person doubt themselves.

有一个人，被拒的经历多到可以列成清单：中学考了三年、大学考了三次、求职被拒了大概三十次，连去快餐店应聘服务员都被刷掉。他后来创办的公司，改变了中国人的交易方式。

There is one man whose rejections are numerous enough to be written into a list: three years of exams for middle school, three attempts at university, roughly thirty job rejections—he was even turned down applying to be a waiter at a fast-food restaurant. The company he later founded changed the way Chinese people do business.

他叫马云。他自己说：“是错误使我们与众不同。”

His name is Jack Ma. In his own words: "It is our mistakes that set us apart."

一、一个“差生”的前二十年

I. The First Twenty Years of a "Poor Student"

1964年，马云出生在杭州。他小时候身材瘦小，却不肯被欺负，是学校里的“打架大王”，曾经因为打破头缝了13针。父亲为了他打架的事，像孟母一样给他转过三次学。

Jack Ma was born in Hangzhou in 1964. As a boy he was small and slight, but refused to be bullied; he was the school's "king of fights," and once needed thirteen stitches after a blow that split his head open. Because of his fighting, his father moved him to three different schools, like the mother of Mencius of old.

他的求学经历称得上坎坷。小学时期就有两次期末考试不及格；想进重点初中、重点高中，都失败了。1982年，18岁的他第一次参加高考，数学只考了1分，北大梦碎。1983年第二次高考，数学考了19分——比上次提高了18倍，但依然落榜。

His path through school was nothing if not rough. In primary school he failed two final exams; he tried and failed to get into a key middle school and a key high school. In 1982, at eighteen, he sat the college entrance exam for the first time and scored just 1 point in math, shattering his dream of Peking University. On his second attempt in 1983 he scored 19 in math—an eighteenfold improvement—and still failed.

但英语是他的救命稻草。12、13岁那年，他对英语产生了浓厚兴趣。当时杭州没有好的英语环境，他就每天早上跑到香格里拉饭店门口，给外国游客当义务导游，请他们教自己英语。这样坚持了九年。

But English was his lifeline. At twelve or thirteen he developed a deep interest in the language. Hangzhou had no good English environment then, so every morning he ran to the entrance of the Shangri-La Hotel to serve as a volunteer guide for foreign tourists, asking them to teach him English. He kept this up for nine years.

1984年，他第三次高考，数学考到79分。虽然总分离本科线还差5分，但那年杭州师范学院英语专业本科没招满，学校决定降分录取几名英语好的学生——他以专科的分数，上了本科的专业。

In 1984, on his third attempt, he scored 79 in math. Though his total was still 5 points short of the undergraduate cutoff, the English department at Hangzhou Normal College had not filled its quota that year, so the school decided to lower the cutoff and admit a few students with strong English—he entered a four-year undergraduate program with a score that would normally qualify only for a two-year one.

二、“被拒三十次”

II. "Rejected Thirty Times"

大学里的马云如鱼得水。他凭借苦练多年的英语成为优等生，还当上了校学生会主席和杭州市学联主席。1988年毕业后，他被分配到杭州电子工学院（今杭州电子科技大学）当老师，“马老师”这个称呼，从此伴随他一生。

At university Jack Ma thrived. On the strength of the English he had drilled for years, he became a top student and was elected chairman of the student union and of the Hangzhou student federation. After graduating in 1988 he was assigned to teach at the Hangzhou Institute of Electronic Engineering (now Hangzhou Dianzi University), and the name "Teacher Ma" stayed with him for the rest of his life.

但求职的挫折并没有结束。

But the setbacks of job hunting were not over.

他多次讲过这样一段经历：当年去应聘肯德基，二十四个人一起去，二十三个人被录取，只有他一个人被淘汰。他还去考过警察，五个人去，四个人被录取，又只有他没被录取。

He has told one story many times: when he applied to work at KFC, twenty-four people went together, twenty-three were hired, and he alone was turned down. He also applied to be a policeman: five went, four were accepted, and again he was the only one left out.

他后来在演讲里总结：“我上了7年才完成中学，人家用了5年；我想进重点初中、重点高中都失败了，考大学我失败了3次；然后申请工作我失败了差不多30次。”

Later, in a speech, he summed it up: "It took me seven years to finish secondary school; others did it in five. I failed to get into a key middle school and a key high school. I failed the college entrance exam three times. And then I failed at applying for jobs almost thirty times."

关于“肯德基”这件事，还有一个需要说明的细节：很多文章说他“高中毕业去肯德基应聘被拒”，但1983年杭州还没有肯德基。更可靠的说法是，这件事发生在大约十年后，他已经在杭州电子工学院当老师时，带着二十多名学生去应聘，学生都被录取了，只有他落选。

There is one detail about the "KFC" incident that needs clarifying: many articles say he was "rejected by KFC after graduating from high school," but in 1983 Hangzhou had no KFC. The more reliable account is that it happened about a decade later, when he was already teaching at the Hangzhou Institute of Electronic Engineering and took more than twenty students to apply—the students were all hired, and only he was not.

这些挫折真正留下的，不是“逆袭”的素材，而是一种对“被拒绝”的钝感力。 他后来谈起这些时说：“每一次失败，每一次被别人拒绝，我都把它当作一次训练。对于我来说，如果被人拒绝，这是很正常的事情；你被别人接受才是并非顺理成章的事情。”

What these setbacks truly left behind was not material for a "comeback story" but a kind of thick skin toward rejection. Looking back on them later, he said: "Every failure, every time I was rejected by others, I treated as a training session. To me, being rejected is a perfectly normal thing; being accepted by others is what doesn't come naturally."

三、中国黄页：被当成“骗子”

III. China Pages: Taken for a "Swindler"

1995年，30岁的马云接触到互联网，敏锐地意识到这是一个巨大的机会。

In 1995, at thirty, Jack Ma encountered the internet and keenly sensed it was a tremendous opportunity.

他辞掉了大学老师的工作——在那个年代，这是一份含金量极高的“金饭碗”。1995年4月，他和妻子张瑛、同事何一兵在杭州租下一间12平方米的房子，成立了“中国黄页”，这是互联网上最早的中国网站之一。

He resigned his university teaching post—in those days a "golden rice bowl" of the highest value. In April 1995, he and his wife, Zhang Ying, and his colleague He Yibing rented a twelve-square-meter room in Hangzhou and founded China Pages, one of the earliest Chinese websites on the internet.

但那时候的中国人，还不知道互联网对自己有什么用。马云印了一张“营销总监”的名片四处推销，所到之处，人们几乎都把他当成骗子。 他后来去北京找关系，到各部委推销理念，吃尽闭门羹。有一次在回去的公共汽车上，他把头靠在窗玻璃上，喃喃地说：

But at the time Chinese people had no idea what the internet could do for them. Jack Ma had a business card printed reading "Marketing Director" and went everywhere pitching his idea, and almost everywhere people took him for a swindler. He later went to Beijing to work his connections, visiting ministry after ministry to sell the concept, and met with one closed door after another. Once, on the bus back, he leaned his head against the window and murmured:

“再过几年，北京就不会这么对我；再过几年，你们都得知道我是干什么的。”

"In a few years, Beijing won't treat me like this. In a few years, you'll all know what it is I do."

由于没有资金、没有市场认知，中国黄页最终没能做大，他在1997年离开了这家公司。

With no capital and no market awareness, China Pages never grew large, and he left the company in 1997.

四、阿里巴巴：见了三十多个投资人都没人投

IV. Alibaba: More Than Thirty Investors, and Not One Would Put In a Cent

1999年，马云和“十八罗汉”在杭州湖畔花园的一间公寓里，创立了阿里巴巴。

In 1999, Jack Ma and the "Eighteen Arhats" founded Alibaba in an apartment in Hangzhou's Lakeside Garden.

创业初期，最大的难题是钱。他后来说，自己“见了超过30个投资人，没有一个愿意投给我们”。那时的中国市场，几乎没有人相信一家做B2B电子商务的公司能成。

In the early days, the biggest problem was money. He later said he "met more than thirty investors, and not one was willing to invest in us." In the Chinese market of that time, almost no one believed a B2B e-commerce company could succeed.

但阿里巴巴活了下来，并在此后成长为全球最大的电商平台之一。

But Alibaba survived, and went on to grow into one of the world's largest e-commerce platforms.

而马云最常讲的，不是阿里巴巴有多成功，而是它犯过多少错。他说过一句很有意思的话：

And what Jack Ma talked about most was not how successful Alibaba was, but how many mistakes it had made. He once said something quite memorable:

“有许多关于马云的书，关于阿里巴巴的书，但所有这些书都不是我写的……如果有一天，我真的想写一本书，书名将是《阿里巴巴和1001个错误》。”

"There are many books about Jack Ma, books about Alibaba, but none of those books was written by me... If one day I truly want to write a book, the title will be Alibaba and the 1,001 Mistakes."

他把“从失败中学习”当成自己的方法论。在中国，他办湖畔大学培养企业家，用的案例大部分是“失败的故事”。他的理由很强硬：

He made "learning from failure" his methodology. In China he founded Hupan University to train entrepreneurs, and most of the cases it used were "stories of failure." His reasoning was blunt:

“成功的原因千千万万，失败的原因就几个。大多数人都会失败。如同在战场上，生存下来的人才是赢家。”

"There are thousands of reasons for success, but only a handful for failure. Most people fail. It's like a battlefield—only those who survive come out winners."

五、致2026：被拒绝不是判决书

V. To 2026: A Rejection Is Not a Verdict

2026年，我们习惯了用一张简历、一次面试、一个学历，去定义一个人的价值。被拒一次，很多人就给自己贴上“我不行”的标签。

In 2026, we are used to defining a person's worth by a résumé, an interview, a degree. One rejection, and many people slap on themselves the label "I'm not good enough."

马云的故事，当然不是“被拒三十次就能成功”这么简单——他有英语这个稀缺能力，赶上了互联网这个时代窗口，也有一群愿意跟着他的伙伴。这些条件缺一不可。

Jack Ma's story is, of course, not simply "get rejected thirty times and you'll succeed"—he had English, a scarce skill; he caught the historical window of the internet; and he had a group of partners willing to follow him. Not one of these conditions could have been missing.

但至少有一点，是他反复强调、也确实值得今天的人学的：

But there is at least one thing he stressed again and again, and that today's readers would do well to learn:

不要把“被拒绝”当成对自己的判决。

Don't treat a rejection as a verdict on yourself.

他二十出头的时候，被中学拒、被大学拒、被工作拒、被投资人拒。每一次拒绝在当时都是真的打击。但他没有因此认定“我就是个失败者”——他认定的是“这件事暂时不行，我再想办法”。

In his early twenties he was rejected by a middle school, by a university, by employers, by investors. Every rejection was a real blow at the time. But he never concluded "I'm a failure"—what he concluded was "This particular thing isn't working yet, so I'll find another way."

对我们来说，也许最该学的是他那种“把拒绝当训练”的姿势：

For us, perhaps the most worth learning is his stance of "treating rejection as training":

你被拒绝的，是某一个具体的申请、某一个具体的岗位、某一个具体的方案，不是你这个人的全部。

What gets rejected is one specific application, one specific position, one specific plan—not the whole of who you are.

还有一点也许更重要。马云之所以能在被拒三十次之后还往前走，是因为他手里始终握着一样别人替代不了的东西——九年时间练出来的英语，和他在西湖边跟外国人聊天时打开的那个“更大的世界”。被拒绝不会毁掉一个人，但如果没有一样真正拿得出手的本事，被拒绝就真的很难翻身。 所以与其纠结“他们凭什么拒绝我”，不如先把那件“别人没法拒绝你”的事，练到足够硬。

There is one more point, perhaps more important. The reason Jack Ma could keep moving forward after thirty rejections is that he always held something no one else could replace—the English he had honed over nine years, and the "larger world" it opened up as he chatted with foreigners by West Lake. Rejection won't destroy a person, but without a single skill you can truly show for yourself, rejection really can be hard to recover from. So rather than agonizing over "What gives them the right to reject me?", it is better to first get that one thing "no one can reject you for" solid enough to hold.
